Verification & benchmarks reference

TENRYU verification progresses from small checks of individual physics kernels against closed-form results, through golden regressions and reproducibility audits, to integrated 2D RZ (axisymmetric (R, Z) coordinates) test suites and cross-code comparisons. Higher test classes exercise more physics but do not replace the analytic gates below them. Each kind of check has its own oracle and acceptance band, so that a failure shows which requirement is no longer met. The legend defines the terms this page uses (golden, seed corona, chord ray tracing, and others) and the tags that appear in test names.

At a glance

  • Analytic gates compare Sedov and Noh hydrodynamics, the multigroup Marshak spectrum, the SNB (Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet) Epperlein–Short dispersion band, and analytic laser refraction and absorption laws with references independent of the code itself.
  • The GXII (GEKKO XII) 1D FLD (flux-limited diffusion) regression compares implosion history metrics with a golden. A golden is not a value to move so that a test passes; it is re-baselined only for justified physics or numerical changes, with quantitative review.
  • Deterministic 1D paths require run-to-run bitwise identity. In 2D RZ the floating-point sums within a step also run in a fixed order, and two runs of the same configuration must agree bitwise.
  • The I-series (the CTest tests whose names contain I1, I2, I3, I5, or I6; see the legend) raises gray radiative shocks (gray: frequency-independent opacity), multigroup FLD gray collapse, SN (discrete ordinates) radiative shocks, two-temperature radiative shocks, and SN multigroup gray collapse into acceptance tests under coupled 2D RZ conditions.

Verification hierarchy

The hierarchy accumulates evidence in order: one operator, a fixed configuration, execution reproducibility, multi-operator coupling, and comparison with another implementation. Small gates localize sign, unit, coefficient, and boundary-condition errors. Integrated test suites ask whether individually correct parts remain consistent in the production time loop, mesh, and energy ledgers. Cross-code results support validation; they do not replace internal verification.

Gate classWhat it checksWhere it runs
1. Unit / analyticOne physics kernel, discrete operator, or conservation identity against a closed form or independent implementation.ctest targets such as verify_sedov, verify_noh, and the Marshak, SNB, and laser patterns. Many also run as tenryu verify <name>.
2. Golden regressionHistory metrics from a fixed deck against an approved JSON baseline and metric-specific tolerances.ctest -R verify_gxii_1d_fld_regression or tenryu verify gxii_1d_fld_regression.
3. ReproducibilityReplicas of the same input, and where applicable different rank counts, under bitwise, reduction-order-band, or noise-band criteria (defined below).ctest -L mpi_gate (rank-count comparisons with P=1) and replica or feature-off legs inside coupled test suites.
4. Integrated test suiteHydrodynamics, radiation, material coupling, mesh behavior, and boundary energy ledgers together in 2D RZ.The CTest I1, I2, I3, I5, and I6 suites (I-series table); full coupled tests carry strict in their names, and long tests carry the expensive label.
5. Cross-code benchmarkTrends and absorption under physically matched inputs in a separate code.The comparison with HELIOS under matched irradiation (LTE, local thermodynamic equilibrium), recorded as a benchmark rather than a routine per-commit gate.

Analytic and unit-gate catalog

An analytic gate owns an oracle that a wrong implementation would not reproduce. Where practical, its reference avoids the helpers of the production calculation and uses an independent integral, discrete eigenvalue, or published solution.

AreaReference and principal observablesRepresentative gate names
HydrodynamicsSedov–Taylor compares shock radius, density profile, and energy with the similarity solution. Noh checks shock position, compression plateau, upstream flow, and conservation in each geometry.sedov, noh, noh_planar, noh_cylindrical
Multigroup FLDAn eight-group Marshak plateau is compared with a spectrum from independent Planck integration. Frequency-dependent relaxation checks group-opacity wiring through a closed-form backward-Euler response.fld_1d_mg_planar_marshak_spectrum, fld_1d_mg_planar_freqdep_relaxation
SNB conductionA tight comparison with the discrete dispersion relation is separated from a physical band based on Epperlein–Short / Vlasov–Fokker–Planck (VFP) kinetic data [4,5]. Sequences of calculations that vary the grid and the group count, monotonicity, the local limit, and conservation complete the suite.snb_local_limit_1d, snb_dispersion_1d, snb_conservation_1d
Laser / opacityBeer–Lambert attenuation in uniform matter, the turning point in an analytic refractive-index profile, 3D-ray absorption, and the deposition ledger expose errors such as missing or double application of rho*kappa.laser_beer_lambert, laser_3d_beer_lambert, laser_3d_refraction
Radiation frontThe Hammer–Rosen supersonic Marshak-wave test [3] compares front isotherms and the incoming/escaping energy ledger with the perturbative analytic solution.hammer_rosen_supersonic

Tolerances are not uniform. A deterministic Beer–Lambert ray trace has no sampling noise and uses a tight relative error. The SNB band from published data remains separate from the operator gate; its high-wavenumber model limit is not certified by widening the band.

The GXII benchmark

Throughout this documentation, GXII is GEKKO XII — the 12-beam Nd:glass laser facility at the Institute of Laser Engineering, The University of Osaka. TENRYU's standing application benchmark is a GXII-equivalent direct-drive implosion: twelve beams arranged on the faces of a regular dodecahedron (implemented as the twelve icosahedron-vertex directions) irradiate a 500 µm capsule. The regression deck examples/verification/gxii_1d_fld_regression.py fixes this configuration:

ItemValue in the regression deck

Seed coronaA low-density layer placed outside the shell's outer surface as an initial condition (legend). The exponentially decaying ramp \(\rho=0.05\,e^{-(r-250\,\mu m)/2\,\mu m}\) g/cm³ extends 10 µm from the outer radius, with a density floor of \(3\times10^{-4}\) g/cm³. The 351 nm CH critical density (0.028 g/cm³) is reached at a radius of 251.2 µm, inside the innermost ramp cell (250–252.5 µm; Figure A).
DriveNominal 10 kJ in a 1 ns square pulse (10 TW total over the 12 beams, with linear 10 ps ramps at both ends, so the delivered energy is 9.9 kJ) at \(\lambda=351\) nm (third harmonic, 3ω); 12 beams at f/3 with super-Gaussian spots (\(w_0=200\) µm, \(m=4\)), 1000 rays per beam, chord ray tracing (legend).

Physics-kernel pages that quote “GXII” conditions — golden absorption, corona substep counts, S8/S16 comparisons, “GXII-class” Studio presets — refer to this deck or a stated variant of it. Its target and drive parameters double as the physical definition wherever “GXII-class” appears.

Golden regression and re-baselining

verify_gxii_1d_fld_regression covers a 1D spherical implosion with laser deposition, hydrodynamics, conduction, two-temperature matter, and FLD. The absorbed laser energy is taken as the cumulative value at the end of the run; bang time (bang_time_s), rho_peak_max, areal density rhoR_max, and central temperature Tc_max are taken from the history time series rather than the final snapshot. Bang time is the time at which the mass-weighted mean radius of the cells whose density is at least 10% of the peak density is smallest. rho_peak_max, rhoR_max, and Tc_max are the maxima over the whole run of the peak density, the areal density (for the cells with at least 10% of the peak density, the sum of the density times the radial width of the cell), and the central temperature (the electron temperature of the cell nearest the center). The absorbed energy, rho_peak_max, rhoR_max, and Tc_max must lie within 10% of their goldens, and bang time within 5%. shell_r_min (the mean radius above, at bang time) is informational. A mismatched run_profile (the record of run conditions such as the grid and run length) fails the comparison itself, so a shortened run or a different grid cannot be passed off as meeting the same goldens.

A golden detects changes on the same grid and run profile; it does not prove exactness or grid convergence. Re-baselining requires a known bug fix, a physics-algorithm or baseline-parameter change, or code-owner approval. The update must state the reason, quantify old-to-new differences, provide convergence data across resolutions, and receive review. A failing run alone is not a rationale. Re-baselining is done with tenryu verify gxii_1d_fld_regression --generate-golden, and this is the only test that supports --generate-golden.

Reproducibility checks

Each reproducibility check is judged under one of the following criteria:

CriterionRequirement
BitwiseBitwise identity between replicas (and across rank counts where declared).
Reduction-order bandOnly the addition order of an MPI reduction (Allreduce(SUM) and the like) may differ. For each rank's owned region of a field, the largest difference from the P=1 run, divided by the largest value of that field over the whole P=1 run, must not exceed the band width. The width is set per test (--band-default of tests/parallel/run_parallel_gate.py, default \(10^{-13}\)). The registered tests use \(10^{-13}\) for 2D SN and 2D burn, and \(10^{-12}\) for the fields other than the deposition in the 2D laser-mesh test (4 ranks).
Noise bandA difference is accepted if it lies inside a band determined from the differences between replicas of the same configuration (a self-calibrated band, determined by running replicas inside the check, or a band determined from several runs and recorded). It applies only to paths whose replicas differ, and is set per deck and metric. The sums within a 2D RZ step run in a fixed order, so the band is empty for the decks whose replicas were confirmed to agree exactly.
Feature-off identityWith a feature disabled or never triggered, the run must be bitwise identical to the path without the feature.
EnvelopeUsed only for the laser deposition field. From the differences among three P=1 runs, three statistics are formed: the largest difference in units in the last place (ULP), the number of cells that differ, and the fraction of the deposition carried by those cells. A P>1 run must stay below the maximum of each statistic multiplied by a margin of 4 (with floors of 4 ULP, 8 cells, and \(10^{-12}\)) and below the recorded caps (tests/parallel/gate_caps.json). Currently only the 2D laser-mesh test (4 ranks) uses it.

Deterministic 1D runs with the same binary, input, and GPU configuration must produce bitwise-identical fields and diagnostics. GXII 1D uses per-ray private laser deposition followed by fixed-order reduction; the complete trajectory must reproduce bitwise. Physical tolerances retained for toolchain robustness do not permit same-configuration replica drift.

In 2D RZ hydrodynamics, the floating-point sums within one step are computed so that the addition order does not depend on thread timing. A sum onto a node or cell is formed by one thread per target, adding the contributions in ascending key order of the connectivity table. A scalar total is formed from one private partial per item and a fixed two-stage reduction. The laser deposition is an exact 128-bit fixed-point integer sum, rounded once. No order-dependent floating-point atomicAdd (an atomic add whose sum follows the order in which threads run) remains in a sum that enters the state, the time step, the ledgers, or the diagnostics. The remaining atomicAdd calls are either order-independent (integer counts, maxima and minima, additions with one writer per address) or in 1D-only or uncompiled paths. ctest -R atomic_add_allowlist rejects any call missing from the list that records the reason. For this reason two runs of the same configuration must agree bitwise in the state, the time step, the ledgers, and the diagnostics in 2D RZ as well; a disagreement signals a sum that is not yet in fixed order, or another source of nondeterminism. The agreement is confirmed deck by deck by comparing two runs.

I-series integrated 2D RZ test suites

The I-series promotes reference problems into the coupled production path. Each harness (a script that runs the deck and judges the result) executes the deck with several angular counts, opacities, grids, or solver settings, and passes only when every required item in its summary JSON is satisfied.

Test suiteWhat it checksHow to find it in CTest
I1: gray radshock reference solutionsThe suite checks shock and precursor behavior, conservation, and agreement with reference profiles against semi-analytic gray radiative-shock reference solutions — LR07 and LE08 [6,7] plus a limiter-off FLD-CED ordinary-differential-equation (ODE) reference solution (see the legend) — in 1D and in a 2D RZ z-slab leg (a planar shock along z in a radially thin slab, gated for radial invariance).ctest --test-dir build -R "I1|i1"
I2: multigroup FLD gray collapseWith frequency-flat opacity, group-summed multigroup FLD must collapse to the gray coupled solution. The suite also checks that the front position converges as the group count increases (Cauchy contraction) and as the grid is refined (Richardson sequence), sanity audits (the Newton iterations of the material solve all converge, no emergency treatment such as a floor hit or a cell deactivation ever fires, and radial invariance holds), and conservation.ctest --test-dir build -R "I2|i2.*mgfld"
I3: SN radshockOver sequences of calculations that vary the SN order (S4, S8, S16), the opacity multiplier (1, 20, 200), and the grid, the suite checks radiation profiles and flux, the precursor and matter shock, conservation, ordering against FLD, and radial invariance.ctest --test-dir build -R "I3|i3.*radshock"
I5: two-temperature radshockA two-temperature calculation advances the electron temperature \(T_e\) and the ion temperature \(T_i\) separately. It solves a gray radiative shock in a 2D RZ z-slab, where \(T_i\) and \(T_e\) separate behind the shock because the electron–ion energy exchange has a finite rate, and compares the result with the two-temperature FLD-CED ODE reference solution. In a window around the shock, the suite requires the relative L2 differences of \(T_e\) and \(T_i\) to be at most 0.05, the relative difference of the peak of \(T_i/T_e-1\) at most 0.10, and the radial invariance at most \(10^{-6}\).ctest --test-dir build -R "I5|i5" (the name contains expensive; the timeout is 12 hours)
I6: SN multigroup gray collapseThe same SN radiative-shock deck as I3 is run with two or more groups and must agree with the one-group (gray) SN calculation. For the group-summed radiation energy density and z-direction flux, taken as z profiles, the criterion for the relative L2 difference from the one-group calculation is \(10^{-3}\), widened by the spread of three replicas.ctest --test-dir build -R "I6|i6" (the name contains expensive)

The coupled I1 test and the full I2 and I3 acceptance tests include strict in their names: they evaluate the complete mandatory harness rather than a partial smoke test. Their Catch2 [.expensive] tag is exported as the CTest expensive label, keeping long GPU test suites separate from the short development loop. The label classifies runtime and computational scale; it does not weaken acceptance.

Test tags and terms

CTest names and physics-kernel pages identify families of checks by short tags. The tags are names, not a strength ranking. A “rung” is one run of the sequence of calculations inside one test suite that varies a condition in turn, such as the angular count, the opacity multiplier, or the grid. Besides the tags, the table defines the terms that this page and the physics-kernel pages use (seed corona, chord ray tracing, golden, reference solution).

Tag or termMeaning
L1 / L2 (laser)Two levels of laser checks: L1 = checks of the laser physics alone (refraction, Beer–Lambert attenuation, energy ledger, convergence with ray count); L2 = checks of the coupled laser and hydrodynamics. Unrelated to the \(L_2\)/\(L_\infty\) error norms.
G1–G5 (conduction)The SNB nonlocal-conduction gate series: G1 bitwise identity with the local model when SNB is off, G2 local limit and quadratic asymptote, G3 Epperlein–Short dispersion, G4 conservation, G5 maximum principle.
G-R / G-K / G-P (burn)Burn gate series by area (section names inside the burn unit tests): R = reactivities and depletion, K = Fraley range and deposition kernel, P = electron–ion partition.
H1 / H2 (2D hydro)2D RZ hydro reference decks: H1 planar Sod, H2 cylindrical Noh.
I1 / I2 / I3 / I5 / I6Integrated 2D RZ test suites (I-series table): I1 gray radiative shock, I2 multigroup-FLD gray collapse, I3 SN radiative shock, I5 two-temperature radiative shock with separately advanced electron and ion temperatures, I6 SN multigroup gray-collapse acceptance.
R2 (SN)The 2D RZ SN radiation test group. It checks convergence as the SN order rises through S4, S8, and S16 (relative L2 difference between S4 and S16 at most 0.10, between S8 and S16 at most 0.05, with the S8 difference the smaller), agreement with FLD within 5% in the optically thick limit, and similar properties. Unrelated to the EOS charge moment \(r_2\).
LR07 / LE08 / FLD-CED (I1 reference solutions)The reference solutions against which I1 compares: the Lowrie–Rauenzahn 2007 (LR07) and Lowrie–Edwards 2008 (LE08) semi-analytic gray radiative shocks [6,7], and a reference solution obtained by integrating gray FLD as an ODE with the flux limiter off, that is, with a constant Eddington factor 1/3 (FLD-CED, constant-Eddington FLD).
Seed coronaThe low-density layer that the regression deck places outside the shell's outer surface (outer radius 250 µm) as an initial condition, with a density that thins out exponentially. The density is \(\rho=0.05\,e^{-(r-250\,\mu m)/2\,\mu m}\) g/cm³ (deck variables RHO_CORONA0 = 0.05 g/cm³, L_CORONA = 2 µm) over 10 µm from the outer radius (R_CORONA_END), with a density floor of \(3\times10^{-4}\) g/cm³ (RHO_CORONA_MIN). The initial density distribution is shown in Figure A, together with the radius at which the critical density is reached and the cell boundaries. The layer adds about 0.5% of the mass of the CH shell (less than 1%). Without it, the cold solid surface is a one-cell density step, the 351 nm rays reflect at the critical surface with almost no inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption path, the absorbed fraction over the whole pulse stays at \(4\times10^{-4}\), and the corona that grows by heating cannot start by itself (an earlier measurement recorded in the deck comments and not re-measured). The layer matters for laser absorption and for the formation of the corona. It is unrelated to the “seed” of the CBET page (the state that supplies the sample points when the coupling coefficient is computed) and to the random-number seed Main.seed.
Chord ray tracingA method for 1D spherical fluids. For each beam, the aperture is divided into rings of equal width (Laser.rays_per_beam of them; the 1D default is 1000), and one ray per ring is followed, with refraction, in the (R, Z) plane that passes through the target center and is fixed to the beam direction. Because the fluid is spherically symmetric, the orientation of the plane does not change the absorption profile. The regression deck uses Laser.mode="raytrace_2d" and rays_per_beam=1000. See the laser page for details.
GoldenThe reference values saved from an approved run. The golden of the regression deck is examples/verification/gxii_1d_fld_regression/golden.json, which holds the absorbed energy, bang time, rho_peak_max, shell_r_min_cm, rhoR_max, Tc_max_eV, and the run conditions run_profile. It is a detector of change, not a proof of correctness. The conditions and the procedure for re-baselining are in the section Golden regression and re-baselining.
Reference solutionA solution prepared independently as the counterpart of a check: a closed-form solution, a semi-analytic solution (LR07, LE08), or a numerical ODE solution (FLD-CED). It is distinct from the golden.

HELIOS 1D cross-code benchmark

For LTE comparison with HELIOS — a commercial 1D Lagrangian radiation-hydrodynamics code from Prism Computational Sciences — matched-irradiation cases, rather than merely equal nominal laser power, were established as the meaningful basis: a ≈113 µm Gaussian spot delivered by a nearly collimated f/191 beam, with LTE table opacities on both sides. Across those matched cases, measured absorbed fractions (July 2026) were about 45% in TENRYU and about 55–73% in HELIOS, with attribution work recorded for the difference. These numbers are deliberately not comparable with the regression deck's ≈96% absorption (the absorbed energy of the 2026-09-29 golden, 9.53×10¹⁰ erg, divided by the 9.9×10¹⁰ erg delivered by the pulse): the regression deck's twelve f/3 beams with 200 µm super-Gaussian spots overfill the 250 µm-radius target, a much broader drive geometry (the Examples page collects the three absorption figures and their conditions). This comparison alone does not establish that either implementation is correct or validated against experiment.

How to run

CTest selects registered gates from the build tree by pattern; tenryu verify invokes one verification driver directly.

ctest --test-dir build -R <pattern>
./build/tenryu verify <name>

# Examples
ctest --test-dir build -R "sedov|noh|snb|gxii_1d_fld"
./build/tenryu verify snb_dispersion_1d

Use ctest --test-dir build -N to list available names. Select long test suites explicitly with -L expensive, or omit them from a routine pass with -LE expensive. Gates depend on build options and, in some cases, HDF5, MPI, and a usable GPU. Do not interpret a skip as a pass; retain the CTest labels and execution log with the result.
