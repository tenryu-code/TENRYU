Nomenclature & conventions reference

This page collects the vocabulary the rest of the documentation relies on: the version labels, the abbreviations, the external code and facility names, the shared physical constants of the fixed cgs + eV unit system, and disambiguation tables for symbols and words that carry different meanings on different pages. Physics-kernel pages define their own symbols locally; come here when a symbol or acronym appears without a nearby definition.

Version vocabulary

This documentation describes TENRYU v1 (also written v1.0), which corresponds to the public release v1.0.0-beta.1 (the string 1.0.0-beta.1 that tenryu --version prints). The labels v2-E, MPI v1, and M1 v1 in the table below name features, not releases of the whole code, and appear in the error messages of the code.

LabelMeaning
v1 / v1.0The TENRYU version documented by this site; it corresponds to the public release v1.0.0-beta.1. Statements such as “v1 omits radiation pressure force” scope a decision to this version.
v2-EThe name of the neutron-heating model of the burn kernel, Burn.neutron_heating: the heating by the two neutron spectral lines (14.049 MeV from DT reactions, 2.449 MeV from DD reactions) at their first collision with the fuel D and T, evaluated in a single flight. In 2D RZ the name appears in the error message that rejects Burn.neutron_heating=True.
MPI v1The name in the error messages that reject features not yet supported when the number of MPI (Message Passing Interface) ranks is 2 or more: 1D void regions, adaptive artificial viscosity, and Braginskii viscosity (source: src/hydro/hydro_1d.cu). The features that cannot run on multiple ranks are listed in the section on scope and deferral guards of the MPI page.
M1 v1The name in the input-validation and run-time error messages of the ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian) rezone solver Numerics.ale.rezone_solver="m1_tmop" (M1/TMOP; TMOP stands for target-matrix optimization paradigm). It covers polar-family logical meshes only: when Mesh.logical_mesh_2d is rectangular_rz or cone_shell, input validation rejects the deck with “not supported in M1 v1”, and at run time the solver accepts quadrilateral meshes only (source: src/hydro/ale_driver.cu).
pc_v0, midpoint_v1, bbsw_radial_v0, kinematic_basis_rz_v1Namelist values that end in _v0 or _v1. The suffix is part of the value's name, not a version label. Numerics.hydro.time_integration takes pc_v0 (predictor–corrector method) or midpoint_v1 (midpoint method), and Numerics.hydro.corner_mass_convention takes bbsw_radial_v0 (the earlier radially linear lump of the corner masses) or kinematic_basis_rz_v1 (the lump of the bilinear kinematic basis weighted by R). The defaults and their use are described in the sections on time integration and corner masses of the Lagrangian hydrodynamics page.

Shared constants of the cgs + eV system

The unit system is fixed: lengths in cm, time in s, mass in g, energy in erg, density in g/cm³, temperatures and photon-group bounds in eV. Three conventions recur on every physics page:

  • \(k_B\) means eV_to_erg \(=1.6022\times10^{-12}\) erg/eV. Because temperatures are stored in eV, every \(k_BT\) in this documentation converts eV to erg; the Kelvin Boltzmann constant never appears.
  • \(a_{\rm eV}=1.3720\times10^{2}\) erg cm⁻³ eV⁻⁴ is the radiation constant expressed in eV units. Equilibrium radiation energy density is therefore \(a_{\rm eV}T^4\) with \(T\) in eV.
  • \(m_p=1.6726\times10^{-24}\) g is the proton mass. Number densities use \(n_i=\rho/(A\,m_p)\) with \(A\) in amu — TENRYU deliberately treats the atomic mass unit as \(m_p\); the ≈0.7% difference from the true amu is a fixed, documented convention absorbed into the material's effective \(A\).

Symbols and words with more than one meaning

Kernel pages define symbols where they use them, and several letters are standard in more than one subfield. The table lists such symbols and the page context that selects each meaning.

SymbolMeanings by context
\(\alpha\)Fleck implicitness parameter, default 1.0 (FLD); the curvilinear hoop-term geometry factor, 1 cylindrical / 2 spherical (viscosity); a Winslow metric coefficient (ALE); and in \(\alpha_{iaw}\), the CBET ion-acoustic damping rate.
\(\beta\)Radiation–matter stiffness ratio \(4a_{\rm eV}T_e^3/C_{v,e}\) (FLD); the SNB normalized electron energy \(E/(k_BT_e)\) (with \(\hat\beta\) the group-boundary values normalized by the global max \(T_e\)); a Winslow metric coefficient and the M1 barrier weight (ALE); and, in CBET, the impact-parameter bin number in the state \(A=(i,\sigma,\beta)\) (written \(a\) in the tally group).
\(\lambda\)FLD flux-limiter function \(\lambda(R)\); SNB group stopping length \(\lambda_g\) (with abs/tr variants); the ALE line-search blend factor; and \(\lambda_0\), the laser vacuum wavelength.
\(\theta\)Conduction harmonic flux-limit factor \(1/(1+|q|/q_{max})\); the CBET donor cap theta_cap; the \(S_N\) donor-\(\theta\) export limiter (defined on the SN page); and the polar angle of polar meshes and beam geometry.
\(\kappa\)Mass opacity [cm²/g] (radiation, EOS/opacity). In the conduction context, the electron thermal conductivity (\(\kappa\propto T_e^{5/2}\)). \(\kappa_{\rm IB}\) is the inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption coefficient (laser).
\(\sigma\)Length opacity \(\sigma=\rho\kappa\) [cm⁻¹] (subscripts: a absorption, s scattering, t total extinction, R Rosseland mean, P Planck mean; radiation, EOS/opacity); the standard deviation (the coefficient of variation \(\sigma/|\mu|\)); the nuclear cross section in \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\) (burn); in CBET, the index of the sheet (0 = incoming, 1 = outgoing); \(\sigma_{\rm sg}\) on the 1D mesh page is the width of the super-Gaussian weight (grading.sg_sigma); and \(\sigma_b\) on the ALE page is the Jacobian ratio (0.1) below which the barrier term acts.
\(\chi\)In CBET, the coupling coefficient, the proportionality factor of the pair exchange \(\Delta P_{A\leftarrow B,c}=\chi\,L_{A,c}L_{B,c}/V_c\); \(\chi_e\) on the ALE page is the short-edge weld measure \(\ell_e^2/(\delta_e h_e)\), the squared edge length divided by the product of the coordinate representation spacing \(\delta_e\) and the local scale \(h_e\) (an edge with a value of at most 4 is welded).
\(\psi\)The directional radiation intensity \(\psi_{g,m}\) of group \(g\) and direction \(m\) (\(S_N\)); Christensen's limiter function in the CSW-type artificial viscosity (0 at the foot and tail of a shock, 1 in a compression linear in velocity; hydrodynamics); and the crossing angle of two beams (\(\cos\psi=\hat k_q\cdot\hat k_p\); CBET).
\(g\)Photon- or electron-energy group index (radiation, SNB, hot electrons, burn); in CBET, the normalized detuning of the gain curve \(P(g)\) — the CBET page's tally “group” index \(g=b(2N_{bin})+\sigma N_{bin}+a\) is a third, unrelated bookkeeping index.
\(f\)The Fleck factor \(f_{c,g}\) (FLD); the conduction free-streaming fraction f_lim; the explicit-source time-step fraction \(f_E\) (explicit_source_limit); the CBET calibration factor f_cbet; the hot-electron capture fraction \(f_s\) (source_nc_fraction); the burn ion-heating fraction \(f_i\); and the CBET donor-cap factor \(f_g\).
\(R\)The FLD limiter argument \(|\nabla E_g|/(\sigma_{R,g}E_g)\); the radial coordinate in \((R,Z)\); the flux-response ratio of the SNB local-limit test (tag G2); and the viscosity channel ratio \(\eta_e/\eta_i\).
\(E\)Group radiation energy density \(E_{c,g}\) (radiation pages); particle kinetic energy (hot-electron and burn group grids); conservative cell thermal energy \(E_c\) (2D conduction update); \(\mathcal E\) (script) is the electrostatic field in the SNB E-field option.
\(e\)Specific internal energy \(e_i,e_e\) [erg/g] (subscripts: ion and electron species); the elementary charge in collision-rate and critical-density formulas such as \(m_e\omega_0^2/(4\pi e^2)\); and the exponential base, as in the \(e^{-2}\) spot radius.
\(a\)In CBET, the number of the impact-parameter bin (the tally-group index \(g=b(2N_{bin})+\sigma N_{bin}+a\)); \(a_{\rm eV}\) is the radiation constant (see the constants below); a subscript a means acoustic in \(\mathbf{k}_a\), \(c_a\), and \(\nu_a\), and absorption in the subscript of \(\sigma\); on the ALE page, the edge \(e=(a,b)\) joins the nodes \(a\) and \(b\); and in the hot-electron saturation form \(1-e^{-a\sqrt{g-1}}\), a coefficient.
\(u\)Fluid velocity \(\mathbf u\); in the hot-electron spectrum equations, the normalized energy \(u=E/T_h\) (with \(u_{g\pm1/2}\) the group edges and \(u_{min},u_{max}\) the window edges).
\(k\)Boltzmann conversion in \(k_BT\) (see constants above); the perturbation wavenumber in dispersion and decay-rate formulas; \(\mathbf k\) the optical wavevector in CBET.
\(m_i\)Cell mass of cell \(i\) in the hot-electron time-step limiter; \(\bar m_i=A_{eff}m_p\) is the mean ion mass in the CBET acoustic speed.
Subscripts \(i\), \(j\)In \(T_i,e_i,P_i\), the ion species; in the 1D discretization, the cell index \(i\) (\(V_i,\Delta M_i\)) and node index \(j\) (\(r_j,u_j\)). Context — species or grid — selects the meaning.
\(M\), \(M_c\), \(\Delta M_i\)All denote Lagrangian cell mass: \(\Delta M_i\) in 1D cell-mass form, \(M_c\) per 2D cell; burn's \(M_{req}/M_{max}\) are instead subcycle counts (requested versus the subcycle_max cap).
\(\Delta l_c\)The cell size measure used by dt limits: the cell width in 1D, and \(\sqrt{A_c}\) in 2D with \(A_c\) the cell's meridian-plane area.
\(T_h\) (= \(T_{hot}\))The hot-electron exponential-spectrum temperature (T_hot_eV, converted to erg internally).
\(n_c\), \(n_{\rm crit}\)The same quantity — the laser critical density \(m_e\omega_0^2/(4\pi e^2)\); the hot-electron page writes \(n_c\), the laser page \(n_{\rm crit}\).
\(Z_{\rm eff}\), \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}\)\(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Z r_2\) is the collisional effective charge from charge-state moments (with \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\)); \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}\) (and \(A_{\rm eff}\)) are mixed-cell composition averages. The test tag R2 (the 2D RZ SN radiation test group) is unrelated to \(r_2\).
L1 / L2Two levels of laser checks (see the tag legend); \(L_2\)/\(L_\infty\) (also \(\varepsilon_{L2}\), \(\varepsilon_{L\infty}\)) are relative error norms — root-mean-square and maximum-magnitude relative differences over a field.
\(w_m\), \(w_k\), \(w_0\)\(w_m\): \(S_N\) ordinate weights, normalized to the angular measure (the 1D Gauss–Legendre set sums to 2, \(\int_{-1}^1d\mu\), with azimuth folded into \(\psi\); hence \(\sum_{\mu_m>0}w_m\mu_m=1/2\)); \(w_k\): laser annulus ray weights (profile × exact area, normalized to 1); \(w_0\): the \(e^{-2}\) spot radius of the beam profile.

Words with more than one meaning

Besides symbols, a word can name different things on different pages. The table gives the meanings and the way this documentation writes them apart.

WordMeanings by context
KernelA physics kernel is one physics operator and its page (defined on the Physics kernels page). A CUDA kernel is a function that runs in parallel on the GPU. An integral kernel is a mathematical kernel function, such as the point-source-sphere kernel used for the Fraley retention on the burn page. This documentation writes “physics kernel” for the first and “CUDA kernel” for the second.
FreezeTo freeze is to fix the contents of the deck, before the time loop starts, in a form that does not change inside the loop (see the term on the running page). It means replacing the Python functions of the deck with numerical tables, and turning the whole configuration, with the defaults filled in, into one JSON (the frozen configuration). The frozen configuration is also stored in metadata/frozen_config of the HDF5 outputs, and tenryu freeze writes only the frozen configuration. Keeping a value unchanged (for example, a coefficient across the stages of one step) is written “fix” or “hold”, not “freeze”.
SeedOn the CBET page, the seed is the state of a pair that supplies the sample points when the pair coupling coefficient is evaluated; the other state is the pump (sections, seed and pump, shadow). The random seed is the seed of the random numbers and is given by Main.seed. The seed corona is the low-density exponential ramp that the GXII regression deck places outside the shell (test tags and terms legend).
GoldenA golden is a reference value stored from an approved run, a detector of change for the same grid and run conditions (legend). A solution prepared independently — a closed-form solution, a semi-analytic solution, or a numerical ODE solution — is called a reference solution, and is kept distinct from the golden.
SubcycleSubcycling is advancing one time step \(\Delta t\) in smaller steps. In burn, it is the number of divisions \(M_{req}\) (capped by Burn.subcycle_max) that the explicit midpoint method needs to advance the reaction network. In electron conduction, it is the number of steps of the explicit stability limit \(\Delta t_{exp}\) needed to advance \(\Delta t\). They are different quantities under one name, so read the meaning on each page.
ClosureA closure is a relation or model that determines the unknowns of an equation. The material closure is the relation that gives the temperature \(T\), pressure \(P\), and specific heat \(c_v\) from the density \(\rho\) and the specific internal energy \(e\) through the EOS, and re-closure is computing it again whenever an operator changes the state (operator splitting and re-closure). Transport closures include the rule that builds a face conductivity from the two neighboring cells, the flux limiters of diffusion, and Eddington factors. That a budget such as the energy budget holds to within roundoff is written “the budget closes”, not “closure”.

Test tags and reproducibility criteria

The tags that appear in test names and on physics-kernel pages — L1/L2 (laser), G1–G5 (SNB conduction), G-R/G-K/G-P (burn), H1/H2 (2D hydrodynamics), I1/I2/I3/I5/I6 (integrated 2D RZ test suites), R2 (the 2D RZ SN radiation test group), and LR07/LE08/FLD-CED (the reference solutions for I1) — and the reproducibility criteria (bitwise, reduction-order band, noise band, feature-off identity, and envelope) are defined on the Verification & benchmarks page, in its test tags and terms legend and its reproducibility checks.
