Time-step control & CFL reference

All TENRYU physics kernels (a physics kernel is one physical operator and its page) share one global time step \(\Delta t\). Every step, each kernel proposes a cap — "no further than this" — from its own stability or accuracy condition, and the driver takes the minimum. The constraints of the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) type therefore differ kernel by kernel, and which kernel's cap sets \(\Delta t\) changes from step to step. This page collects the full decision rule and every per-kernel \(\Delta t\) formula in one place (the canonical source for the equations is docs/NUMERICS.md §2.2 in the repository).

At a glance

  • The global rule is \(\Delta t_{phys}=\min(\Delta t_{hydro},\Delta t_{cond},\Delta t_{visc},\Delta t_{burn},\Delta t_{hot\,e},\Delta t_{mixed})\), further capped by the growth limit, dt.max_s, landing on output times, and the remaining time. The name of the constraint that set \(\Delta t\) (hydro, conduction, burn, … — the full list is in The decision rule) is recorded in the log (with Main.verbosity="verbose") and in the diagnostics.
  • Hydro is the acoustic CFL (\(C_{CFL}=0.3\)). Conduction proposes the value relaxed by super-time-stepping (STS) — \(s_{max}(s_{max}+1)/2\) times the explicit limit, a factor 820 at the default \(s_{max}=40\) — and drops out entirely (\(\infty\)) with the implicit electron solvers ("implicit" in 1D, "dmp_implicit" in 2D; see the conduction section). Radiation (flux-limited diffusion, FLD, and discrete ordinates, SN) proposes no cap (\(\infty\)).
  • Laser and CBET (cross-beam energy transfer) have no independent \(\Delta t\). Burn and hot electrons are trailing limits (below): the power deposited in one step caps the next step. In 2D, a burn step whose subcycle count exceeds its cap is rejected and retried.
  • Under MPI, rank-local minima are combined into the shared \(\Delta t\) by a single MPI_Allreduce(MPI_MIN).

The decision rule

Choosing the step is a single gather-and-minimize. First the kernels' caps are combined,

$$ \Delta t_{phys}=\min\bigl(\Delta t_{hydro},\;\Delta t_{cond},\;\Delta t_{visc},\; \Delta t_{burn},\;\Delta t_{hot\,e},\;\Delta t_{mixed}\bigr) $$

The subscripts name the kernels that propose a cap (visc is Braginskii viscosity, mixed is the pressure relaxation of 2D mixed cells), and the formula of each cap is in the table of the next section. The physics-independent operational caps are applied on top:

$$ \Delta t=\min\bigl(\Delta t_{phys},\; g_{dt}\,\Delta t^{n},\; \texttt{dt.max_s},\; \Delta t_{output},\; t_{end}-t\bigr),\qquad g_{dt}=1.2\;(\text{default}) $$

The pieces mean the following.

  • Growth limit \(g_{dt}\): caps sudden expansion relative to the previous step (Numerics.dt.growth_factor, default 1.2, allowed range 1 to 2). Even when the physics caps loosen, \(\Delta t\) grows by at most 20% per step. \(\Delta t^{n}\) in the formula is the growth reference: the previous step's \(\Delta t\) before it was shortened to land on an output time or \(t_{end}\). Landing on an output therefore does not hold the following steps back.
  • Ceiling and floor: dt.max_s is the user ceiling (default \(10^{-9}\) s when the deck does not set it, \(2\times10^{-12}\) s in Studio). What happens when \(\Delta t\) falls below the floor dt.min_s (default \(10^{-20}\) s when the deck does not set it, \(10^{-22}\) s in Studio) is described in Floors and stall detection.
  • Initial value: dt.initial_s (default \(10^{-15}\) s when the deck does not set it, \(10^{-14}\) s in Studio) is used only on the first step (there is no previous step, so the growth limit cannot apply). The first step's \(\Delta t\) is the minimum of initial_s and every cap except the growth limit. With initial_s=None, 0.1 times the hydro \(\Delta t\) (dt.max_s when hydro is disabled) replaces initial_s. From the second step on, initial_s is ignored. A restart continues from the growth reference stored in the checkpoint (time_state/dt_growth_ref).
  • Landing on outputs and the end time: with seconds-based output cadences active, \(\Delta t_{output}\) keeps the step from jumping past the next scheduled output time (below); the remaining time \(t_{end}-t\) works the same way.
  • Retry: in configurations with full-step retry (Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled, default False when the deck does not set it, True in the 1D decks that Studio writes (Studio does not write the key in 2D decks); at most driver_full_step_retry_max_attempts retries per step, default 3), when the hydro corrector, burn, radiation, or conduction rejects the attempt at a step, the driver restores the entry snapshot, shortens \(\Delta t\), and reruns the whole operator-split sequence. The reduction is set by the rejecting check: usually one half, but the 2D geometric checks (such as the trial-volume floor) and the 2D burn (trailing limits) use the step that check asks for. The constraint that set \(\Delta t\) on the rerun step is recorded as driver_retry.

The candidate that the adopted \(\Delta t\) matched (the constraint that set \(\Delta t\)) is recorded under one of these names: hydro, conduction, burn, hot_electron, mixed_cell, braginskii (viscosity), growth, output, t_end, max, init (dt.initial_s on the first step), and driver_retry. When several candidates have the same value, driver_retry comes first and the others are tried in the order listed (from hydro to init); the first match is recorded. If none matches, the name is unknown. The name appears in the log (with Main.verbosity="verbose", in dt_winner= of the [dt_breakdown] line printed every step) and in the history's dt_winner. When "\(\Delta t\) keeps shrinking", the first move is to read this name and identify the kernel that sets \(\Delta t\) (see below). In mesh_mode="reale_v2" runs, the history's dt_winner can also record rezone_reanchor, only on the first step after a committed rezone; it marks a \(\Delta t\) raised above the growth limit (it does not appear in the log's [dt_breakdown] line; see the page on reconnection-based ALE (reale_v2)).

Under MPI, each CFL's \(\min_c\) runs over rank-local cells and one MPI_Allreduce(MPI_MIN) makes the global step. Time and the scheduled output times are identical on every rank, so \(\Delta t_{output}\) needs no extra communication.

The hydro CFL and its additional constraints

The base form is the acoustic CFL: information — sound waves and material motion — must not skip a cell in one step. In the Lagrangian stage the mesh moves with the material, so no flow speed enters the denominator.

$$ \Delta t_{acoustic}=C_{CFL}\cdot\min_c\!\left(\frac{\Delta l_c}{c_{s,c}+w_c}\right), \qquad C_{CFL}=0.3\;(\text{default}) $$

\(\Delta l_c\) is the cell length scale: the cell width \(\Delta r_i\) in 1D, the square root of the meridian-plane cell area \(\sqrt{A_c}\) in 2D. \(c_{s,c}\) is the cell sound speed and \(w_c\) is the speed that the artificial viscosity adds to the denominator. For the von Neumann–Richtmyer (VNR) and Caramana–Shashkov–Whalen (CSW) forms in 1D (av_type equal to vnr or csw), \(w_c=C_1c_{s,c}+C_2\,\Delta l_c\,\chi_c\); in 2D, \(w_c=C_1c_{s,c}\). Here \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are the linear and quadratic artificial-viscosity coefficients (in 1D, av_linear = 0.1 and av_quadratic = 1.5 when av_type is vnr, and csw_C1 = 0.5 and csw_C2 = 2.0 when it is csw; in 2D, av_linear, which the deck may omit: the default is then 1.0 for the 2D RZ CSW edge models (including the case where the deck omits av_model too) and 0.1 otherwise), and \(\chi_c\) is the compression rate [1/s] of the cell, built from the cell-edge velocities reconstructed with limited nodal slopes. In 1D, the \(C_1c_s\) term is added only in compressing cells, where the viscosity acts. In the 1D Riemann forms (riemann, riemann_compatible), the compressive nodal velocity jump enters \(w_c\). The minimum runs over hydro-active cells only; \(c_s=0\) cells (unheated fill regions) count as \(\Delta t_{hydro}\to\infty\) and leave the setting of \(\Delta t_{hydro}\) to the other cells. If every cell is inactive, hydro drops out of the time-step constraints.

Depending on the configuration, the following constraints join the base form. All of them act only to shrink \(\Delta t\) (or to force a rezone). In 1D, the caps together give \(\Delta t_{hydro}=\min(\Delta t_{acoustic},\Delta t_{cross},\Delta t_H,\Delta t_{ps})\).

  • Node-crossing cap (1D, on by default): with Numerics.hydro.crossing_dt_safety (\(C_{cross}\), default 0.5; 0 disables it) positive, the cells whose faces are closing (\(u_i>u_{i+1}\)) add \(\Delta t_{cross}=C_{cross}\min_i[\Delta r_i/(u_i-u_{i+1})]\), where \(u_i\) is the velocity of node \(i\). The cap keeps a face from moving more than \(C_{cross}\) cell widths in one step; it prevents node crossing when the denominator of the acoustic CFL approaches zero, as in a cold compression with a sound speed near zero. cfl_hydro is not applied to it.
  • Artificial-heat-flux cap (1D, VNR form, off by default): with Numerics.hydro.av_heat_C (\(C_H\), default 0) positive and av_type="vnr", the explicit stability condition of the artificial heat flux adds \(\Delta t_H=0.5\,m_i/\sum_fG_f\). Here \(m_i\) is the cell mass, the sum runs over the two faces of the cell, and \(G_f=A_fC_H\rho_fl_f\chi_f\) is the conductance of face \(f\) (\(A_f\) is the face area, \(\rho_f\) and \(l_f\) are the means of the density and width of the two adjoining cells, and \(\chi_f\) is the larger of their compression rates).
  • Post-shock heat (1D, off by default): with post_shock_heat=True (default False), an artificial heat flux \(H^{ps}=-C_{ps}\rho\,c_s\,l\,\psi\,\partial e_{tot}/\partial r\) is added in the cells that a shock passed recently (\(C_{ps}\) is post_shock_heat_C, default 0.1; \(e_{tot}=e_e+e_i\) is the total specific internal energy of the cell; \(l\) is the mean of the widths of the two cells at the face). \(\psi=\exp[-(t-t_{shock})/\tau]\) is a weight that decays with the time since the shock passed: \(t_{shock}\) is the last time at which the artificial-viscosity pressure of the cell exceeded 1% of its maximum over all cells, and \(\tau=C_{decay}\Delta r/c_s\) is the decay time (\(C_{decay}\) is post_shock_heat_decay, default 3.0). Because this heat flux is solved explicitly, the stability condition \(\Delta t_{ps}=0.5\,m_i/\sum_fG_f\) (\(G_f=A_fC_{ps}\rho_fc_{s,f}\psi_f\), with \(\rho_f\), \(c_{s,f}\), \(\psi_f\) the means over the two adjoining cells) is evaluated and included in \(\Delta t_{hydro}\).
  • Axis-margin cap (2D, off by default): with axis_margin_dt_floor_fraction (\(f_{axis}\), default 0.0) positive, the largest \(\Delta t\) for which the axis margin \(M_j\) of each cell row that touches the axis (the geometric room that keeps the row from inverting when the nodes move; unit cm²) stays at least \(f_{axis}\) times its value \(M_j^n\) at the start of the step is found by bisection and included as a cap on \(\Delta t_{hydro}\). The default 0.0 disables it and leaves the hydro \(\Delta t\) bitwise unchanged.
  • Volume-rate CFL (2D, off by default): with volume_rate_cfl_enabled=True (default False), the relative volume change \(f_{V,c}=|V_c^{new}-V_c^{old}|/V_c^{old}\) of a cell measured over the previous hydro step, divided by the length \(\Delta t_{used}\) of that step, gives the relative rate \(r_{V,c}=f_{V,c}/\Delta t_{used}\); the next step is capped at \(\min_c(f_{V,max}/r_{V,c})\), where \(f_{V,max}\) is the relative change allowed per step (volume_rate_cfl_threshold, default 0.5). It is a one-step-late safety net for source-driven volume changes that the acoustic CFL can underestimate; after an accepted ALE the baseline volume is reset so ALE displacement is not mistaken for flow.
  • Trial-volume floor (2D, off by default): with trial_volume_cfl_enabled=True (default False), just before the hydro corrector commits the node positions, the trial volume \(V_c^{trial}\) of every cell (the cell volume computed from the node positions about to be committed) is checked against \(V_c^{trial}\ge f_{trial}V_c^n\) (\(V_c^n\) is the volume at the start of the step; \(f_{trial}\) is trial_volume_cfl_floor_fraction, default 0.05). A violation logs a warning with a suggested retry step \(f_{shrink}\Delta t\) (\(f_{shrink}\) is trial_volume_cfl_shrink_fraction, default 0.5). With full-step retry disabled the step is not rerun; with it enabled, the violation requests a full-step retry.
  • Corner-Jacobian ALE trigger (2D, off by default): with corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_enabled=True (default False), consider for the candidate step \(\Delta t_*\) the trial trajectory \(\mathbf x^n+\tau\mathbf v^n\) (\(0\le\tau\le\Delta t_*\)) of the nodes moving at their current velocities. Bisection finds the first \(\tau\) at which the corner Jacobian \(J_{c,k}\) of corner \(k\) of cell \(c\) (signed; the two-dimensional cross product of the two edge vectors at the corner) falls below \(\epsilon_J\) times its value \(J^n_{c,k}\) at the start of the step (\(\epsilon_J\) is corner_jacobian_floor_eps, default \(10^{-6}\)). If the fraction \(\tau/\Delta t_*\) of the candidate step that can be taken without crossing (the minimum over all corners) is below corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_scale (default 0.5), an ALE rezone is forced before the split operators run (ALE triggers). It does not clamp \(\Delta t\) directly.
  • Static mesh repair at retry (2D, off by default): with driver_retry_active_mesh_repair_enabled=True (default False; it acts when full-step retry and ALE are enabled), a check that uses only the corner Jacobians at the entry of the step (\(\tau=0\)) is made at every retry attempt; it is called static because it does not depend on \(\Delta t\). For each cell whose four corner Jacobians are all positive and finite, the corner balance \(q_{bal,c}=\min_kJ_{c,k}/\max_kJ_{c,k}\) is computed. The check fails when some cell has \(q_{bal,c}\) below driver_retry_corner_balance_threshold (default 0.01), or a corner Jacobian that is not positive or not finite. On failure, an ALE rezone is forced before the retry. At the first attempt the check is only recorded and no rezone is forced.

In addition, the admissibility check of the mesh quality (minimum altitude, corner Jacobians) can predictively limit the step, as described on Meshes & geometry. Observationally, the hydro components (acoustic, axis margin, volume rate, post-shock) can be recorded separately by the dt-breakdown diagnostics below.

Conduction: STS relaxation and the implicit solvers

The raw explicit-diffusion stability limit is, in 1D,

$$ \Delta t_{exp}=C_{cond}\cdot\min_c\!\left(\frac{(\Delta l_c)^2}{D_{eff,c}}\right), \qquad C_{cond}=0.25\;(\text{default}) $$

(\(\Delta l_c\) is the cell width and \(D_{eff}\) is the effective thermal diffusivity — see conduction). In 2D it follows from an upper bound \(\lambda_G\) of the eigenvalues of the assembled conduction operator (by the Gershgorin circle theorem; unit 1/s):

$$ \Delta t_{exp}=\frac{4\,C_{cond}}{\lambda_G} $$

The default STS solver (conduction.solver="sts") does not impose this value on the global \(\Delta t\). STS widens the stability region to \(O(s^2)\) with an \(s\)-stage Chebyshev stage sequence, so the cap proposed to the driver is relaxed up to the stage ceiling \(s_{max}\) (sts_max_stages, default 40):

$$ \Delta t_{cond}=\frac{s_{max}(s_{max}+1)}{2}\,\Delta t_{exp} $$

— a factor \(40\times41/2=820\) at the defaults, which removes conduction from the limiting set in most configurations. This cap does not contain the safety factor \(\eta\) (sts_subcycle_eta, default 0.9) with which STS splits a step. In a step that conduction sets, STS therefore splits the step into \(n_{sup}=2\) supersteps and, at the defaults, takes 29 stages in each, 58 in all, to cover \(820\,\Delta t_{exp}\) (supersteps and stages are defined in the terms section of the conduction page). In 2D, sts_scheme="rkl2" replaces the Chebyshev stage sequence by the stage sequence of the second-order Runge–Kutta–Legendre method (RKL2) and changes the factor of the cap (779 at the defaults sts_max_stages = 40 and cfl_cond = 0.25); "auto" uses the Chebyshev factor for the cap.

The implicit electron solvers (1D "implicit", 2D RZ "dmp_implicit") are backward Euler and unconditionally stable, so \(\Delta t_{cond}=\infty\): electron conduction never limits the global step. "dmp_implicit" is the backward Euler method for the 2D RZ electron conduction with nonlinear two-point fluxes that satisfy the discrete maximum principle (the key table of the conduction page). "hypre" does not run as an implicit solver (input validation rejects it in 2D, and in 1D it falls back to STS with a warning), so the STS cap applies. Ion conduction (conduction.ion_conduction) is an implicit solve without a cap in 1D. In 2D it adds a cap of the same STS form besides the electron one (with "dmp_implicit" the electron cap is \(\infty\), so only the ion cap remains).

Radiation: no cap on the global step

FLD solves the radiation–matter coupling implicitly by the Fleck linearization (see the FLD page). The Fleck factor is

$$ f_c=\frac{1}{1+\alpha\,c\,\beta_c\,\sigma_{P,c}\,\Delta t},\qquad \beta_c=\frac{4a_{\rm eV}T_{e,c}^3}{C_{v,e,c}},\qquad \sigma_{P,c}=\rho_c\kappa_{P,c} $$

where \(\alpha\) is the time-centering parameter of the Fleck linearization, 1 (fully implicit) in FLD. The larger \(\Delta t\), the smaller \(f_c\) and the larger the fraction of within-step emission/reabsorption represented as effective scattering. FLD keeps \(f_c\) unbounded below (to preserve the stiff-cell limit), so radiation proposes no candidate for the global step. SN does not use the Fleck linearization (\(f=1\)) and solves the coupling to the matter implicitly, by Newton iteration among others; radiation proposes no candidate there either.

Numerics.dt.f_min_fleck is the key that the former Monte Carlo radiation used as a floor of the Fleck factor. The current version accepts it, has no effect from it, and prints a warning.

Explicit-source trailing limits: burn, hot electrons, viscosity

Burn and hot electrons cap the next step based on the power they deposited in the current one. These are limits on explicit sources that never re-update the temperature mid-stage, keeping one step's self-heating small relative to the stored internal energy; both use explicit_source_limit (default 0.2, a same-named key in each block).

  • Burn: the minimum over all cells of \(f_E\,e_{cell}/P_{dep}\) caps the next step (\(f_E\) is Burn.explicit_source_limit, \(e_{cell}=\rho_cV_c(e_e+e_i)\) is the total cell internal energy, and \(P_{dep}\) is the power that burn deposits in the cell [erg/s]). A cap on the subcycle count comes in addition. The reaction network needs \(M_{req}\) subcycles for one step; when \(M_{req}\) exceeds the cap \(M_{max}\) (Burn.subcycle_max), 1D advances the step with the cap and puts \(0.9\,\Delta t\,M_{max}/M_{req}\) into the next step's cap. 2D does not accept the step: it restores the entry state and reruns the step with \(\Delta t\) set to the smallest \(0.9\,\Delta t\,M_{max}/M_{req}\) over all cells (the run stops if full-step retry is disabled). In 2D the next step's cap also contains \(0.9\,M_{max}\,\epsilon_{dep}/r\), where \(r\) is the relative reaction rate of the network at the end of the step (Burn.eps_deplete gives \(\epsilon_{dep}\), default 0.1; \(r\) is the largest, over the reactants, of the larger of the gross production and consumption rates divided by the abundance [1/s]; burn page).
  • Hot electrons: \(\Delta t\le f_E\min_{P_i>0}(m_ie_{e,i}/P_i)\); the reservoir is the electron energy only, unlike burn's electron+ion (hot-electron page).
  • Braginskii viscosity: not trailing — evaluated in place at the head of the step as the explicit diffusion limit \(\Delta t_{visc}\le\texttt{dt_safety}\cdot\rho\Delta l^2/[(8/3)\eta_{\rm eff}]\) (viscosity page).

Trailing limit of the mixed-cell pressure relaxation (2D)

In 2D with Numerics.materials.per_material_conservation_enabled=True and Numerics.materials.mixed_cell_closure="pressure_relaxation", a mixed cell — a cell in which several materials coexist — carries a volume and a pressure per material and distributes the volume change while the pressures relax (docs/NUMERICS.md §3.2.10b in the repository). In a cell that holds a stiff and a soft material, the volume of the soft material can change much faster than that of the cell, so the following cap applies.

$$ \Delta t_{mixed}=\Delta t_{h}\,\frac{f_{mix}}{\delta_{max}} $$

Here \(\Delta t_{h}\) is the length of the preceding hydro step (hydro takes two half steps per step, so half of the previous \(\Delta t\); see operator splitting), \(\delta_{max}\) is the largest fraction (relative change) by which the volume of a material in any mixed cell changed in that step, and \(f_{mix}\) is Numerics.materials.mixed_cell_volume_change_limit (default 0.2; greater than 0 and less than 1). Without mixed cells, or with the other closure (the default "equal_compressibility"), the cap is \(\infty\). It is a trailing limit set by the preceding step, and the constraint that set \(\Delta t\) is recorded as mixed_cell.

Landing on output times

With any seconds-based cadence active (plot_every_s, history_every_s, checkpoint_every_s > 0), the step is kept from crossing the next scheduled output time by including

$$ \Delta t_{output}=\min\bigl(t_{next,plot}-t,\;t_{next,history}-t,\;t_{next,ckpt}-t\bigr) $$

in the candidates (only active series enter the min; all-inactive gives \(\infty\)). Output firing uses the floating-point tolerance \(\varepsilon=10^{-14}\times\max(|t|,X\_every\_s)\), firing at \(t\ge t_{next}-\varepsilon\). Each term of \(\Delta t_{output}\) is replaced by the larger of \(2\varepsilon\) and \(2\,\texttt{dt.min_s}\) when the time \(t_{next}-t\) left to the scheduled time is smaller than that (so that \(\Delta t\) stays above the floor and the step surely crosses the scheduled time). After firing, a while loop advances \(t_{next}\) in \(X\_every\_s\) increments, so even extreme settings (\(\Delta t\) larger than the cadence) never lose a scheduled time. Initialization is \(t_{start}+X\_every\_s\); a restart restores the schedule from the checkpoint's output_state/. Because \(\Delta t_{output}\) only ever shrinks the step, it cannot harm stability.

Floors and stall detection

A step with \(\Delta t<\texttt{dt.min_s}\) (default \(10^{-20}\) s when the deck does not set it) stops the run FATAL once Numerics.dt.min_consecutive_steps (default 1) such steps occur in a row; by default the first one stops it. The run must not keep taking vanishing steps that look alive but make no progress. While the count is below that number, the driver prints a warning and raises \(\Delta t\) to the floor (or to a smaller physical cap, if there is one) and continues. The two main cases that do not stop the run are these. (a) The constraint that set \(\Delta t\) is growth and the hydro \(\Delta t\) is at least the floor (recovery after a retry shortened the step): \(\Delta t\) is raised to the floor and the run continues. (b) The last landing, where the adopted \(\Delta t\) equals the remaining time \(t_{end}-t\), is exempt from the floor check. The stall detector, which is off by default, Numerics.dt.floor_stall_max_consecutive_steps=N (default 0 = disabled) additionally counts accepted steps that satisfy all three conditions: (i) \(\Delta t\) within a factor \(1+10^{-6}\) of the floor, (ii) remaining time exceeding \(10^3\) floors, (iii) the constraint that set \(\Delta t\) is not output or t_end; exceeding \(N\) consecutive such steps stops FATAL with diagnostics (floor, current \(\Delta t\), remaining time, the constraint that set \(\Delta t\), and how to disable).

Observing the constraint that set \(\Delta t\)

  • History /dt: the adopted \(\Delta t\) series is always recorded in <case>_history.h5 (Outputs & analysis).
  • Constraint names in the log: with Main.verbosity="verbose", each step's \(\Delta t\) and the name of the constraint that set it (dt_winner=) appear in the run log's [dt_breakdown] line. Look here first.
  • dt-breakdown diagnostics (on by default): with Numerics.diagnostics.dt_breakdown_history_enabled (default True), diagnostics/dt_breakdown_history/ is written to the history every step (in 1D, only this group and diagnostics/cfl_winner/ below are written every step; the other history rows follow Output.history_every), recording dt_chosen, dt_hydro, dt_cond, dt_post_shock, dt_growth, dt_max, dt_output, dt_remaining, the hydro components (acoustic, axis margin, volume rate), and the name dt_winner of the constraint that set \(\Delta t\) (the dt_rad column in the history of an earlier version was the cap of the former Monte Carlo radiation; the current version does not write it); diagnostics/cfl_winner/ additionally records the cell that sets the acoustic-CFL minimum (position, \(\Delta l\), \(c_s\), \(\rho\)).

Typical readings: the constraint that set \(\Delta t\) is hydro with cfl_winner pinned to one cell points at that cell's zoning (collapsed or tiny cells) or its sound speed (overheating, floors); conduction points at the coronal \(D_{eff}\) blow-up (the κ∝\(T^{5/2}\) of the conduction page); a run that only ever shows growth is simply still ramping up.
