Time-step control & CFL reference

All TENRYU kernels share one global time step \(\Delta t\). Every step, each kernel proposes a cap — "no further than this" — from its own stability or accuracy condition, and the driver takes the minimum. CFL-type constraints therefore differ kernel by kernel, and which kernel is limiting changes from step to step. This page collects the full decision rule and every per-kernel \(\Delta t\) formula in one place (the canonical source for the equations is docs/NUMERICS.md §2.2 in the repository).

At a glance

  • The global rule is \(\Delta t=\min(\Delta t_{hydro},\Delta t_{cond},\Delta t_{rad},\Delta t_{burn},\Delta t_{hot\,e},\Delta t_{visc},\ldots)\), further capped by the growth limit, dt.max_s, landing on output times, and the remaining time. The name of the winning constraint (hydro/conduction/radiation…) is recorded as the limiter in the log and diagnostics.
  • Hydro is the acoustic CFL (\(C_{CFL}=0.3\)). Conduction proposes the STS-relaxed value — \(s_{max}(s_{max}+1)/2\) times the explicit limit, a factor 820 at the default \(s_{max}=40\) — and drops out entirely (\(\infty\)) with the implicit solvers. Radiation derives its cap from the Fleck-factor floor \(f_{min}=0.01\).
  • Laser and CBET have no independent \(\Delta t\). Burn and hot electrons are trailing limits: the power deposited in one step caps the next step.
  • Under MPI, rank-local minima are combined into the shared \(\Delta t\) by a single MPI_Allreduce(MPI_MIN).

The decision rule

Choosing the step is a single gather-and-minimize. First the kernels' caps are combined,

$$ \Delta t_{phys}=\min\bigl(\Delta t_{hydro},\;\Delta t_{cond},\;\Delta t_{rad},\; \Delta t_{burn},\;\Delta t_{hot\,e},\;\Delta t_{visc}\bigr) $$

and then the physics-independent operational caps are applied on top:

$$ \Delta t=\min\bigl(\Delta t_{phys},\; g_{dt}\,\Delta t^{n},\; \texttt{dt.max\_s},\; \Delta t_{output},\; t_{end}-t\bigr),\qquad g_{dt}=1.2\;(\text{default}) $$

The pieces mean the following.

  • Growth limit \(g_{dt}\): caps sudden expansion relative to the previous step (Numerics.dt.growth_factor, default 1.2). Even when the physics caps loosen, \(\Delta t\) grows by at most 20% per step. This is also what absorbs, in practice, the one-step coefficient lag described under radiation below.
  • Ceiling and floor: dt.max_s is the user ceiling. If \(\Delta t\) falls below the floor dt.min_s (default \(10^{-20}\) s), the run stops FATAL to prevent silent stalling.
  • Initial value: dt.initial_s is used only on step 0 (there is no previous step, so the growth limit cannot apply). From step 1 it is ignored; a restart inherits the \(\Delta t\) stored in the checkpoint.
  • Landing on outputs and the end time: with seconds-based output cadences active, \(\Delta t_{output}\) keeps the step from jumping past the next scheduled output time (below); the remaining time \(t_{end}-t\) works the same way.
  • Retry: in configurations with full-step retry, a rejected hydro corrector restores the entry snapshot and reruns the whole split sequence at half the step (see Numerical foundations); the limiter is then recorded as driver_retry.

Which candidate the adopted \(\Delta t\) matched — hydro, conduction, burn, hot_electron, braginskii, radiation, growth, output, t_end, max, init, driver_retry — appears in the log as the limiter name. When "\(\Delta t\) keeps shrinking", the first move is always to read this name and identify the limiting kernel (see below).

Under MPI, each CFL's \(\min_c\) runs over rank-local cells and one MPI_Allreduce(MPI_MIN) makes the global step. Time and the scheduled output times are identical on every rank, so \(\Delta t_{output}\) needs no extra communication.

The hydro CFL and its guard family

The base form is the acoustic CFL: information — sound waves and material motion — must not skip a cell in one step.

$$ \Delta t_{hydro}=C_{CFL}\cdot\min_c\!\left(\frac{\Delta l_c}{|\mathbf u_c|+c_{s,c}}\right), \qquad C_{CFL}=0.3\;(\text{default}) $$

\(\Delta l_c\) is the cell length scale: the cell width \(\Delta r_i\) in 1D, the square root of the meridian-plane cell area \(\sqrt{A_c}\) in 2D. The minimum runs over hydro-active cells only; \(c_s=0\) cells (unheated fill regions) count as \(\Delta t_{hydro}\to\infty\) and leave the limiting to other cells. If every cell is inactive, hydro drops out of the ladder.

Depending on the configuration, the following guards join the base form. All of them act only to shrink \(\Delta t\) (or to force a rezone).

  • Post-shock heat (1D, opt-in): with post_shock_heat=True, an explicit constraint \(\Delta t_{ps}\) on the post-shock heat flux is evaluated and \(\Delta t_{hydro}=\min(\Delta t_{acoustic+AV},\Delta t_{ps})\).
  • Axis margin (2D, opt-in): with axis_margin_dt_floor_fraction>0, a shrink-side constraint derived from the near-axis margin joins \(\Delta t_{hydro}\). The default 0.0 disables it, bitwise identical to the ordinary hydro \(\Delta t\).
  • Volume-rate CFL (2D, opt-in): with volume_rate_cfl_enabled=True, the relative volume-change rate \(r_{V,c}=f_{V,c}/\Delta t_{used}\) measured over the previous hydro step caps the next step at \(\min_c(f_{V,max}/r_{V,c})\). It is a one-step-late safety net for source-driven volume changes that the standard \(|v|/\Delta x\) CFL can underestimate; after an accepted ALE the baseline volume is reset so ALE displacement is not mistaken for flow.
  • Trial-volume floor (2D, opt-in, diagnostic-only): with trial_volume_cfl_enabled=True, the trial volumes the corrector is about to commit are checked against \(V_c^{trial}\ge f_{trial}V_c^n\); a violation warns with a suggested retry step \(f_{shrink}\Delta t\) but does not rerun the step.
  • Corner-Jacobian ALE trigger (2D, opt-in): with corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_enabled=True, bisection along the trial trajectory \(\mathbf x^n+\tau\mathbf v^n\) finds when a signed corner Jacobian would cross its floor \(\epsilon_JJ^n_{c,k}\); if the admissible fraction of \(\Delta t_*\) is below a threshold, an ALE rezone is forced before the split operators run. It does not clamp \(\Delta t\) directly.
  • Retry-time static mesh repair (2D, opt-in): with driver_retry_active_mesh_repair_enabled=True, each retry attempt checks the corner-Jacobian balance \(q_{bal,c}=\min_kJ_{c,k}/\max_kJ_{c,k}\) and forces a rezone before retrying when inadmissible.

In addition, the mesh-quality guards (minimum altitude, corner Jacobians) can predictively limit the step, as described on Meshes & geometry. Observationally, the hydro components (acoustic, axis margin, volume rate, post-shock) can be recorded separately by the dt-breakdown diagnostics below.

Conduction: STS relaxation and the implicit solvers

The raw explicit-diffusion stability limit is

$$ \Delta t_{exp}=C_{cond}\cdot\min_c\!\left(\frac{(\Delta l_c)^2}{D_{eff,c}}\right), \qquad C_{cond}=0.25\;(\text{default}) $$

but the default STS solver (conduction.solver="sts") does not impose it on the global \(\Delta t\). STS widens the stability region to \(O(s^2)\) with an \(s\)-stage Chebyshev ladder, so the cap proposed to the driver is relaxed up to the stage ceiling \(s_{max}\) (sts_max_stages, default 40):

$$ \Delta t_{cond}=\frac{s_{max}(s_{max}+1)}{2}\,\Delta t_{exp} $$

— a factor \(40\times41/2=820\) at the defaults, which removes conduction from the limiting set in most configurations. The shape avoids a design contradiction: if \(\Delta t_{exp}\) itself entered the global min, then \(N_{sub}=\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\rceil=1\) would always hold and subcycling would be effectively disabled. The implicit solvers (1D "implicit", 2D "hypre") are backward Euler and unconditionally stable, so \(\Delta t_{cond}=\infty\): conduction never limits the global step.

Radiation: a cap derived from the Fleck-factor floor

Radiation–matter coupling is characterized by the implicitness of the Fleck linearization (see the FLD page):

$$ f_c=\frac{1}{1+\alpha\,c\,\beta_c\,\sigma_{P,c}\,\Delta t},\qquad \beta_c=\frac{4a_{\rm eV}T_{e,c}^3}{C_{v,e,c}},\qquad \sigma_{P,c}=\rho_c\kappa_{P,c} $$

The larger \(\Delta t\), the smaller \(f_c\) and the larger the fraction of within-step emission/reabsorption represented as effective scattering. To avoid extreme \(f_c\), the driver proposes the cap equivalent to enforcing a floor \(f_{min}\) (Numerics.dt.f_min_fleck, default 0.01):

$$ \Delta t_{rad}=\min_c\!\left(\frac{1-f_{min}}{f_{min}}\cdot \frac{1}{\alpha\,c\,\beta_c\,\sigma_{P,c}}\right) $$

It is evaluated whenever radiation is enabled with absorbing material (constant \(\kappa_a>0\) or a table opacity). Note that \(\sigma_{P,c}\) was computed at the head of the radiation operator; the hydro half-step afterwards changes \(T_e\), so the value is one step stale by the time \(\Delta t_{rad}\) is evaluated. The growth limit (\(g_{dt}=1.2\)) absorbs this in practice, and a large \(T_e\) change automatically shrinks the next step's \(\Delta t_{rad}\), making the lag self-correcting.

Explicit-source trailing limits: burn, hot electrons, viscosity

Burn and hot electrons cap the next step based on the power they deposited in the current one. These are limits on explicit sources that never re-update the temperature mid-stage, keeping one step's self-heating small relative to the stored internal energy; both use explicit_source_limit (default 0.2, a same-named key in each block).

  • Burn: \(\Delta t\le f_E\,e_{cell}/P_{dep}\) with \(e_{cell}=\rho_cV_c(e_e+e_i)\) the total cell internal energy, plus \(0.9\,\Delta t\,M_{max}/M_{req}\) when the subcycle count pinned at its cap (burn page).
  • Hot electrons: \(\Delta t\le f_E\min_{P_i>0}(m_ie_{e,i}/P_i)\); the reservoir is the electron energy only, unlike burn's electron+ion (hot-electron page).
  • Braginskii viscosity: not trailing — evaluated in place at the head of the step as the explicit diffusion limit \(\Delta t_{visc}\le\texttt{dt\_safety}\cdot\rho\Delta l^2/[(8/3)\eta_{\rm eff}]\) (viscosity page).

Landing on output times

With any seconds-based cadence active (plot_every_s, history_every_s, checkpoint_every_s > 0), the step is kept from crossing the next scheduled output time by including

$$ \Delta t_{output}=\min\bigl(t_{next,plot}-t,\;t_{next,history}-t,\;t_{next,ckpt}-t\bigr) $$

in the candidates (only active series enter the min; all-inactive gives \(\infty\)). Output firing uses the floating-point tolerance \(\varepsilon=10^{-14}\times\max(|t|,X\_every\_s)\), firing at \(t\ge t_{next}-\varepsilon\). After firing, a while loop advances \(t_{next}\) in \(X\_every\_s\) increments, so even extreme settings (\(\Delta t\) larger than the cadence) never lose a scheduled time. Initialization is \(t_{start}+X\_every\_s\); a restart restores the schedule from the checkpoint's output_state/. Because \(\Delta t_{output}\) only ever shrinks the step, it cannot harm stability.

Floors and stall detection

\(\Delta t<\texttt{dt.min\_s}\) (default \(10^{-20}\) s) is a FATAL stop — the run must not keep taking vanishing steps that look alive but make no progress. The opt-in stall detector Numerics.dt.floor_stall_max_consecutive_steps=N (default 0 = disabled) additionally counts committed steps that satisfy all of: (i) \(\Delta t\) within a factor \(1+10^{-6}\) of the floor, (ii) remaining time exceeding \(10^3\) floors, (iii) limiter not output/t_end; exceeding \(N\) consecutive such steps stops FATAL with diagnostics (floor, current \(\Delta t\), remaining time, limiter, and how to disable).

Observing which constraint won

  • History /dt: the adopted \(\Delta t\) series is always recorded in <case>_history.h5 (Outputs & analysis).
  • Limiter names in the log: each step's \(\Delta t\) and the winning constraint's name appear in the run log. Look here first.
  • dt-breakdown diagnostics (opt-in): Numerics.diagnostics.dt_breakdown_history_enabled=True creates diagnostics/dt_breakdown_history/ in the history every step, recording dt_chosen, dt_hydro, dt_rad, dt_cond, dt_post_shock, dt_growth, dt_max, dt_output, dt_remaining, the hydro components (acoustic, axis margin, volume rate), and the winner name dt_winner; diagnostics/cfl_winner/ additionally records the acoustic-CFL winner cell (position, \(\Delta l\), \(c_s\), \(\rho\)).

Typical readings: limiter hydro with cfl_winner pinned to one cell points at that cell's zoning (collapsed or tiny cells) or its sound speed (overheating, floors); conduction points at the coronal \(D_{eff}\) blow-up (the κ∝\(T^{5/2}\) of the conduction page); radiation at stiff high-β coupling cells; a run that only ever shows growth is simply still ramping up.
