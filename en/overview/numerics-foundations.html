Numerical foundations reference

This page collects what every kernel shares and no single physics page owns: the fixed cgs + eV unit system, the governing equations and the closures (including electron–ion relaxation), variable placement and the conservation-by-construction design, the activity mask for low-density fill regions, operator splitting and its re-closure discipline, floors, clamps, and numerical safeguards, and the energy-ledger identity. The time-step decision rule and per-kernel CFL (Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy) formulas live on the dedicated Time-step control & CFL page. The terms specific to this page are listed in the terms section. The canonical equations are in docs/NUMERICS.md (§0–§2, §10–§11) in the repository.

At a glance

  • Internal units are fixed: length cm, time s, density g/cm³, temperatures and radiation group edges eV, energy erg, pressure dyne/cm². The \(k_B\) in every formula is the eV→erg conversion constant.
  • Matter carries single-fluid momentum and separate ion/electron specific internal energies, coupled by PdV work, conduction, laser, radiation, and the Spitzer-type electron–ion relaxation \(Q_{ei}\).
  • Conservation is enforced by construction, not by after-the-fact fixes: invariant cell masses, compatible corner forces (corner force) for total energy, conservative remap transfers, and boundary traffic closed in the energy ledger — with the residual violation quantified every step as the conservation error \(\varepsilon_{budget}\).
  • Only hydro is split into bracketing half-steps; the full sequence of the five operators (laser, nuclear burn, hydro, conduction, and radiation) is not second order in general. With full-step retry enabled (off by default), the driver restores the entry state of a step on which an operator requested a retry and reruns the whole sequence at a reduced step.
  • Floors and clamps are numerical safeguards, not physics: the energy they raise is booked as artificial terms and is separated out of the conservation-error numerator.

Terms used in this calculation

Of the terms specific to this page, those that have a section of their own are defined at the start of that section: the energy ledger, the artificial terms, and the conservation error in The energy ledger and the conservation error; re-closure in Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline; the activity mask and the low-density fill region in The activity mask for low-density fill; and floors, clamps, and numerical safeguards in Floors, clamps, and numerical safeguards. The two terms without a section of their own, full-step retry and acceptance, are defined next.

Full-step retry

Full-step retry is the mechanism by which, when any operator requests a retry, the driver restores the state at the entry of the outer step, reduces the time step, and reruns the whole operator sequence. It is needed because choosing a smaller step in advance cannot predict every nonlinear mesh-admissibility failure. The key is Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled (default False). Setting it to True makes the driver save the state as a snapshot before entering the operator sequence and, if a retry is requested, discard the partially advanced laser, energy, radiation, and mesh states and restore the snapshot. The decks that Studio writes set True in 1D and do not write the key in 2D (the default False applies). The profile Numerics.profile.icf_standard_ale, which checks that the ALE settings stay within the permitted range (enabled defaults to False), by default requires this key to be True when it is enabled and enforced (enforce, default True); a deck that does not comply is an input error.

The cap on retries is Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_max_attempts (default 3), the number of retries allowed after the first failure. For most requests the step is halved. When the 2D RZ (axisymmetric (R, Z) meridian plane) hydro rejects a trial on a cell-shape test, the retry uses the step estimated to satisfy that test (floored at Numerics.dt.min_s). The 2D nuclear burn uses \(0.9\Delta t\) times the ratio of Burn.subcycle_max (default 64) to the number of subcycles required by the cell that needs the most. The operators that request a retry are the following (the overall flow is in Figure A).

  • 1D hydro: a cell volume reaching zero or below (nodes crossing under compression), and a failed explicit-stability audit of the Braginskii viscosity.
  • 2D RZ hydro: the corrector reporting a trial with an inadmissible cell shape (corner Jacobian, Gauss-point Jacobian, RZ volume, axis margin, the trial-volume floor), a failed solve of the implicit artificial viscosity, or a failed pressure relaxation of a mixed cell (a cell that contains several materials).
  • Conduction: the total number of STS (super time-stepping) stages exceeding Numerics.conduction.sts_total_stages_max, or a failed STS stability-limit check; in the 2D implicit solver (solver="dmp_implicit"), a failed linear solve, a Picard iteration that does not converge, or result temperatures outside the maximum-principle range.
  • Radiation: the SN (discrete ordinates) material Newton solver finding no root above the temperature floor; a 2D FLD (flux-limited diffusion) step rejected for stagnation, reaching the iteration cap, an invalid value, or an inadmissible negative energy.
  • Nuclear burn (2D): the reaction network of some cell needing more subcycles than Burn.subcycle_max.
  • Mixed-cell closure (2D with per-material conservation): a failed mixed-cell pressure relaxation after conduction, radiation, electron–ion exchange, or the ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian) remap.

When retry is unavailable (disabled, or the attempt cap is used up), a request from hydro, conduction, burn, or the mixed-cell closure stops the run with an error. A request from the SN material Newton solver warns and continues with the locally corrected state. A 2D FLD rejection stops the run if Radiation.multigroup_diffusion.cap_exit_policy is "fail", and warns and keeps the rejected candidate if it is "warn" (default).

Acceptance

Acceptance is the admission of a step as final: a step in which every operator finished and none requested a retry is accepted. Time, outputs, and the cumulative energy ledger commit only after acceptance, so failed attempts are never double-counted in histories or energies.

Units and symbols

Never converting units at module boundaries is the starting point for the numbers that every kernel exchanges. In particular, temperature \(T\) is in eV, and the \(k_B\) in \(P=nk_BT\) means eV_to_erg = \(1.6022\times10^{-12}\) erg/eV — do not confuse it with the kelvin-based Boltzmann constant \(1.3807\times10^{-16}\) erg/K. The radiation constant follows the same convention, \(a_{\rm eV}=1.3720\times10^{2}\) erg cm⁻³ eV⁻⁴, so the equilibrium radiation energy density is \(a_{\rm eV}T^4\) with \(T\) in eV. Specific heat per mass \(c_v\) [erg/(g·eV)] and per volume \(C_v=\rho c_v\) [erg/(cm³·eV)] are also kept distinct. The shared constants and overloaded symbols are collected in the nomenclature.

Governing equations and closures

The hydro operator solves Lagrangian single-fluid motion with two-temperature internal energies (Main.temperature_model="2T"; with "1T" ions and electrons share one temperature, and the default "auto" selects 2T if any cell starts with \(T_e\ne T_i\) and 1T otherwise). With the material derivative \(D/Dt\), the mass and momentum equations are

$$ \frac{D\rho}{Dt}=-\rho\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u, \qquad \rho\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt} =-\nabla(P_i+P_e+Q)+\mathbf f_{\mathrm{geom}}. $$

Here \(Q\) is the artificial-viscosity pressure and \(\mathbf f_{\mathrm{geom}}\) the coordinate-geometry term. The radiation-pressure force, with \(P_r=\tfrac13\sum_gE_g\) (\(E_g\) the group radiation energy density), enters the momentum equation as \(-\nabla P_r\) only in 1D FLD with Radiation.multigroup_diffusion.hydro_coupling="gamma_r_43" (the default), and its work is paid from the radiation-field energy. In 2D RZ and in SN, the momentum equation contains no radiation-pressure force. The ion and electron specific internal energies obey

$$ \rho\frac{De_i}{Dt}=-(P_i+Q)\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u+Q_{ei}+S_i, $$ $$ \rho\frac{De_e}{Dt}=-P_e\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u -\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf q_e-Q_{ei}+S_e+S_L+S_r. $$

\(\mathbf q_e\) is the electron heat flux, \(S_L\) the laser deposition, and \(S_r\) the radiation exchange. \(S_i\) and \(S_e\) are the remaining additional sources, placeholders fixed at zero in v1.0 (nuclear burn deposits directly as an independent operator stage, and hot electrons deposit directly through the laser source path). Sign convention: \(Q_{ei}>0\) means energy flowing from electrons to ions, i.e. ion heating. The same \(Q_{ei}\) appears in both equations with opposite signs, so in-cell exchange alone never changes the total matter energy.

Electron–ion relaxation \(Q_{ei}\)

The exchange term at the heart of the two-temperature model closes with Spitzer–Braginskii collisional relaxation:

$$ Q_{ei}=\frac{C_{v,e}\,(T_e-T_i)}{\tau_{eq}}\ \ [\mathrm{erg/cm^3/s}], \qquad \tau_{eq}=\frac{A\,m_p}{2\,m_e}\,\tau_e, $$ $$ \tau_e=\frac{3.44\times10^{5}\;T_e[\mathrm{eV}]^{3/2}} {n_e[\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]\;\bar Z\;\ln\Lambda_{ei}}\ \ [\mathrm s], \qquad \tau_{eq}=\frac{3.16\times10^{8}\;A\;T_e[\mathrm{eV}]^{3/2}} {\bar Z^{2}\;n_i[\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]\;\ln\Lambda_{ei}}\ \ [\mathrm s]. $$

Here \(C_{v,e}\) is the electron heat capacity per volume, \(A\) the ion mass number, \(\bar Z\) the mean ionization, \(n_e\) and \(n_i\) the electron and ion number densities, \(\ln\Lambda_{ei}\) the electron–ion Coulomb logarithm (floored at 2), and \(m_p\) and \(m_e\) the proton and electron masses. The term relaxes the temperature difference \(T_e-T_i\), weighted by the electron heat capacity, on the equilibration time \(\tau_{eq}\). Because a single collision transfers only a fraction of order \(m_e/m_i\) of the energy, \(\tau_{eq}\) is the electron collision time \(\tau_e\) multiplied by \(Am_p/(2m_e)\); the right-hand expression writes this relation with numerical coefficients in cgs + eV units. The \(T_e^{3/2}\) dependence makes relaxation slow in hot plasma, so the corona keeps its electron and ion temperatures separated for a long time. The formula is the ICF-regime approximation, valid for \(T_e\gg(m_e/m_i)T_i\), and it is consistent with the sign convention: \(T_e>T_i\) gives \(Q_{ei}>0\) (ion heating). The exchange is applied inside each hydro half-step's corrector, and the subsequent conduction operator receives the \(Q_{ei}\)-updated \(T_e\) as its initial value.

Discretizing the divergence; cell mass

The Lagrangian discretization never differences \(\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u\) independently: it evaluates it as the cell volume rate, \((\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf u)_c=\dot V_c/V_c\). In 1D spherical symmetry, \(\dot V_i=A_{j+1}u_{j+1}-A_ju_j\) with \(A_j\) the area of the shell boundary at node \(j\), where cell \(i\) lies between nodes \(j=i\) and \(j+1\). The continuity equation is exactly the cell version of this identity, and the cell mass \(\Delta M_c=\rho_cV_c\) stays constant by construction.

The radiation transport equation

For group \(g\), the continuous transport equation for the specific intensity \(I_g(\mathbf r,\mathbf\Omega,t)\) is

$$ \frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial I_g}{\partial t} +\mathbf\Omega\!\cdot\nabla I_g =\sigma_{a,g}B_g(T_e)-\sigma_{t,g}I_g+\mathcal S_{\mathrm{sca}}, $$

where \(B_g(T_e)\) is the group Planck function, \(\sigma_{t,g}=\sigma_{a,g}+\sigma_{s,g}\) the total extinction, and \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{sca}}\) the scattering source (below). The exchange term entering the electron energy equation is

$$ S_r=\sum_g c\,\sigma_{a,g} \left[E_g-a_{\rm eV}T_e^4 b_g(T_e)\right]. $$

\(b_g(T_e)\) is the per-group Planck fraction, normalized to sum to one. \(S_r>0\) is net heating of electrons by the radiation field. Physical scattering \(\sigma_{s,g}=\rho\kappa_s\) (opacity.kappa_s is a constant shared by all groups) exists only in 1D SN. FLD has no scattering, and a material with a positive kappa_s is an input error. 2D SN takes \(\sigma_{s,g}=0\). The Fleck effective scattering \((1-f)\sigma_a\) on the FLD page is a term that appears when the radiation–matter coupling is treated implicitly, not physical scattering.

Variable placement and conservation by construction

Variable placement is shared by every kernel. Thermodynamic state — density, ion/electron specific internal energies, temperatures, pressures, artificial-viscosity pressure, material volume fractions — lives at cell centers; kinematic state — positions and velocities — lives at nodes. 1D nodes are cell boundaries (shells in spherical and cylindrical geometry, planes in planar geometry); 2D RZ nodes are quadrilateral corners. The primary evolved variables are the conserved quantities (mass, momentum, energy); temperatures and pressures are derived by the equation of state (EOS). The HDF5 mesh/ and hydro/ dataset names map 1:1 to these internal State fields (Outputs & analysis).

The conservation policy is "write it in a form that cannot break", not "fix it afterwards". What guarantees what, and where, is summarized below; details live on the kernel pages.

What still leaks — floor corrections, rounding, algorithmic limits — is not hidden: it is quantified every step by the energy ledger below.

The activity mask for low-density fill (hydro_active)

The activity mask is the set of per-cell hydro activity flags hydro_active (1 is active, 0 is inactive) (docs/NUMERICS.md §2.1.1, "hydro start temperature condition", in the repository). An inactive cell contributes no pressure or artificial-viscosity force and receives no PdV work. Treating a low-density fill region (the stand-in for vacuum) as ordinary matter creates meaningless sound waves and extreme CFL limits, so the activity mask keeps that region quiet. An inactive cell becomes active when its electron temperature \(T_e\) reaches the activation threshold Numerics.hydro.T_start_eV (default 0.0 eV). With a threshold of 0 the temperature rule never deactivates a cell, and every cell is active except those that another rule excludes (void cells, for example). Studio's default is also 0.0 eV. A cell that has become active never returns to inactive when its temperature falls (except for the cells that another rule excludes).

Numerics.hydro.T_start_inactive_cells decides how inactive cells are treated. Under the default "passive_fill", a node moves if any adjacent cell is active (OR propagation). Nodes that touch no active cell are treated differently by dimension. In 2D RZ their positions and velocities stay fixed. In 1D, the inactive nodes that continue outward from an active node copy the displacement and velocity of the nearest active node on the inner side, so the fill region follows the plasma edge (with no active node on the inner side they stay fixed). The density of an inactive cell is also recomputed every step from the current volume as \(\rho=\Delta M/V\), and its specific internal energy is not changed by hydro work. The state at the moment a cell activates is therefore consistent with the current volume, and the first post-activation PdV work is continuous. With "rigid_wall" (1D only), a node moves only when the cells on both sides are active, inactive cells hold their volume as rigid walls, and the electron–ion relaxation is not masked. With "cold_equilibrium" (1D, two temperatures, tabulated EOS only), hydro is not masked at all. Studio writes this key only when it is not "passive_fill". The CFL minimum runs over active cells only, and with every cell inactive, hydro drops out of the time-step constraints entirely (Time-step control).

Usage constraint (important). Under the default passive_fill, this flag is a numerical mask for statically holding low-density fill (\(\rho\ll\rho_{shell}\)); it must not be used to suppress the motion of dense matter by temperature conditions. An inactive cell is in a one-sided mechanical state — it can be moved but pushes nothing back — which is acceptable for fill regions but injects or destroys unphysical energy and momentum when applied to dense material. Excluding void cells from the coupling to conduction, radiation, and the laser is a separate mechanism (cells of a material with Material.is_void are excluded through the cell_is_void mask; EOS, opacity & ionization).

Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline

Operator splitting is a time-integration method that divides one outer step into operators, one per kind of physics, and applies them in sequence. The most accident-prone part of splitting is the next operator reading, through a stale closure, the state that the previous one changed. A closure is the relation that determines temperature, pressure, and specific heat from the conserved quantities; in TENRYU the EOS provides it. Re-closure is recomputing, after an operator has changed the state, the temperature \(T\), pressure \(P\), and specific heat \(c_v\) (the heat capacity per volume is \(C_v=\rho c_v\)) from \((\rho,e)\) with the EOS. After an operator that updates \(T_e\) directly, such as conduction, \(e_e\), \(P_e\), and \(C_v\) are recomputed from \(T_e\).

One outer step is the map below; composition acts right to left, so the execution order is \(\mathcal L\), \(\mathcal B\), \(\mathcal H/2\), \(\mathcal C\), \(\mathcal R\), \(\mathcal H/2\):

$$ \mathcal U^{n+1}= \mathcal H_{\Delta t/2}\circ \mathcal R_{\Delta t}\circ \mathcal C_{\Delta t}\circ \mathcal H_{\Delta t/2}\circ \mathcal B_{\Delta t}\circ \mathcal L_{\Delta t}(\mathcal U^n). $$

\(\mathcal H\) is Lagrangian hydro with boundary conditions (each half-step's corrector includes \(Q_{ei}\)), \(\mathcal C\) electron conduction, \(\mathcal L\) ray tracing and deposition, \(\mathcal B\) nuclear burn (which does nothing under the default Burn.enabled = False), and \(\mathcal R\) radiation transport with matter exchange. In 2D RZ, ALE rezone and remap normally run conditionally after the second hydro half-step. When the corner-Jacobian ALE trigger (Numerics.hydro.corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_enabled, default False) fires, and when the mesh is repaired after a retry, ALE may also run before the operator sequence. How the candidate \(\Delta t\) is chosen before the sequence starts is on Time-step control & CFL.

TENRYU re-closes after every operator that changes \(T_e\) or \(e_e\), so that no later operator reads stale \(T\), \(e\), \(P\), or \(C_v\). Under MPI (Message Passing Interface), the ghost regions (the regions that hold copies of the neighboring ranks' boundary cells) are exchanged right after the owned regions are written and before the first ghost read (MPI decomposition); dynamic mean ionization is likewise re-evaluated at each operator's entry (EOS page).

Calling the splitting "Strang-type" needs a qualification: hydro brackets the conduction–radiation block with half-steps, so that combination is symmetric. The laser and nuclear burn each run once as a full step at the head of the step, and conduction and radiation are ordered full steps relative to each other. The couplings of the laser and burn with the rest, and of conduction with radiation, are first-order Lie splittings — operators simply applied in sequence — so the complete sequence of five operators is not generally second order.

This first-order coupling is acceptable because the splitting errors between the laser and the rest, and between conduction and radiation, are not the dominant error sources in ICF runs — each operator advances different physical quantities, and practical accuracy is secured by the time-step control. The fully second-order multi-stage splitting \(\mathcal H_{/2}\circ\mathcal C_{/2}\circ\mathcal L_{/2}\circ\mathcal R\circ\mathcal L_{/2}\circ\mathcal C_{/2}\circ\mathcal H_{/2}\) is recorded as a future option in docs/NUMERICS.md §2.1 in the repository.

A step in which every operator finished but at least one requested a retry is not accepted and reruns from the entry state. The conditions for a retry, how the step is reduced, and the operators that request one are defined in the terms section. The overall flow is shown in Figure A.

Floors, clamps, and numerical safeguards

A floor is a lower limit that a state quantity must not fall below, and a clamp is the operation that returns a quantity beyond a lower or upper limit to the limit. Neither is a physics model: they are numerical safeguards against NaNs and negative temperatures destroying the whole calculation. The policy is to count every state that is corrected and to book in the energy ledger every energy that is raised. The exception is an out-of-range opacity-table lookup, which the device handles silently without counting (below).

  • Temperature floors: \(T_e\ge T_{e,floor}\), \(T_i\ge T_{i,floor}\) (Te_floor_eV and Ti_floor_eV in Numerics.floors; both default to \(10^{-3}\) eV, and Studio writes the same values). In a deck with a tabulated EOS that does not set the floor, a floor is raised to the table's lowest temperature when that exceeds \(10^{-3}\) eV. A floor that the deck sets below the table's lowest temperature is an input error (except with the cold-equilibrium branch, T_start_inactive_cells="cold_equilibrium"). The default 1D closure (Numerics.hydro.eos_closure_mode="energy_authoritative") applies the floor to the temperature only. It leaves the specific internal energy unchanged except for the lower-bound handling in the next item, so the temperature floor by itself injects no energy.
  • Density floor: when the cell density \(\rho=\Delta M/V\) falls below Numerics.floors.rho_floor_gcc (default \(10^{-10}\) g/cm³, the same in Studio), the density value is raised to the floor. The cell mass is unchanged, so the raised \(\rho\) differs from \(\Delta M/V\) and no energy is injected. Each raise counts toward the clamp count, except in void cells.
  • Specific-energy lower bound: the hydro update raises a negative specific internal energy of an ideal-gas cell to 0 and books the raised amount in the floor correction \(E_{floor}\). Negative energies of a tabulated EOS are valid values and are not raised. The conduction floor limiter, the lower-bound handling after laser, radiation, and burn injections, and the ALE remap floor are booked in \(E_{floor}\) in the same way.
  • Clamp-count monitoring: clamp_count resets to zero at step start and increments on every operator clamp. More than Numerics.safety.clamp_warn_threshold (default 100) clamps in a step warns, and more than clamp_fatal_threshold (default 10000) stops the run with a fatal error. The series persists as safety/clamp_count in the history.
  • Opacity-table out-of-range: the treatment depends on the lookup path. A multi-material LTE (local thermodynamic equilibrium) table lookup clamps \((\rho,T)\) into the table domain before interpolation. The non-LTE coefficient evaluation for a single-material tabulated opacity extrapolates \(\log\kappa\) with the slope at the table edge above the table's highest temperature and below its lowest ion density. Both run silently on the device, and neither counts occurrences. The only warning comes from the host-side IONMIX reader when it clamps, capped at 10 per process.
  • Energy-budget and non-finite monitoring: \(\varepsilon_{budget}\) above Diagnostics.energy_budget.warn_threshold (default \(10^{-3}\)) warns (the first step is not checked). Setting Numerics.safety.energy_fatal (default False) to True makes \(\varepsilon_{budget}\) above Numerics.safety.energy_budget_tol (default \(10^{-3}\)) a fatal error. The relative contributions of the artificial terms, \(E_{floor}/E_{denom}\), \(E_{safety}/E_{denom}\), and \(E_{redist}/E_{denom}\) (each term and \(E_{denom}\) are defined in the energy ledger section), also warn above \(10^{-3}\). A NaN or infinity in temperature, density, or specific internal energy stops the run with a fatal error from the per-operator check (Numerics.safety.nan_fatal, default True).
  • Kernel-specific safeguards: the laser's refractive-index floor \(\varepsilon_n\) and Coulomb-logarithm floor of 2, conduction's mask that excludes void cells and its floor-protection throttle (the treatment that throttles the outflow flux of a cell whose temperature would fall below the floor, by one factor common to both sides of the face), the EOS's non-positive-\(c_v\) clamp, and others are described on their kernel pages. The shared policy: warn on firing and, where applicable, book the injection.

The energy ledger and the conservation error \(\varepsilon_{budget}\)

The energy ledger is the record, accumulated step by step, of the change in the total energy of the system, split into the terms of the flows and corrections that cause it. Conservation is enforced by construction (Variable placement and conservation by construction), but violations from rounding, floors, and algorithmic limits remain. The ledger is needed to check every step that the change in total energy equals the sum of its terms, and to measure the part that does not as the conservation error \(\varepsilon_{budget}\). The terms divide into physical traffic, such as laser energy incident or radiation leaving through a boundary, and artificial terms, energy that a numerical operation added to the state, such as the floor correction. Because the artificial terms are accumulated separately from the physical traffic, one can check separately whether the numerical safeguards dominate a result.

The terms of the energy ledger are tied together by the following identity:

$$ \Delta E_{total}=\Delta E_{int,e}+\Delta E_{int,i}+\Delta E_{kin}+\Delta E_{rad} $$ $$ =E_{source}-E_{sink}+E_{supply}+E_{rad,mesh\_adv}+E_{artificial}. $$

The left side sums the electron-internal, ion-internal, kinetic, and radiation-field energy changes. The terms on the right are the following.

  • Inflow: \(E_{source}=E_{laser,in}+E_{Marshak,in}+E_{volume,in}+E_{burn,in}+\max(E_{solver},0)\). \(E_{laser,in}\) is the laser energy incident, \(E_{Marshak,in}\) the radiation energy entering through the Marshak boundary, \(E_{volume,in}\) the energy injected by the radiation volume source Radiation.volume_source_rate, and \(E_{burn,in}\) the energy that nuclear burn deposited in matter (charged-particle deposition, plus the heating by the first collisions of neutrons when Burn.neutron_heating is on).
  • Outflow: \(E_{sink}=E_{laser,esc}+E_{CBET,IAW}+E_{rad,esc}+E_{num.loss}+E_{pdV}^{bdry}+\max(-E_{solver},0)\). \(E_{laser,esc}\) is the laser energy that was not deposited in matter (the unabsorbed energy of rays that ended at the critical surface or outside the ray-tracing mesh, and the energy of hot electrons that escaped the domain), \(E_{CBET,IAW}\) the energy that CBET (cross-beam energy transfer) removed from the light and handed to the ion-acoustic wave (nonzero only with CBET on), \(E_{rad,esc}\) the radiation energy that left through the boundary, and \(E_{pdV}^{bdry}\) the PdV work that the boundary pressure did outward (positive in expansion).
  • Numerical loss: \(E_{num.loss}\) is energy that, because of an algorithmic limit, could not be put into matter or left the system; it is counted as a known outflow on the physical-traffic side. Examples are the energy removed when SNB (Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet) caps the electron temperature at its global maximum before the conduction step, and laser or burn energy that could not be injected because no target cell was found or because the target mass \(\rho V\) was below \(10^{-30}\) g.
  • State supply: \(E_{supply}\) is the energy that the state-supply faces of 2D RZ exchange with the domain. A state-supply face is a z face given by type="state_supply" in z_bottom or z_top of Numerics.hydro.boundary_2d; it holds the density, temperature, and z velocity of the cells that touch it at the specified values. \(E_{supply}\) is the sum of the changes booked for its overrides, velocity restores, and remap boundary fluxes and the flow work through the face, positive into the domain. Without a state-supply face it is 0.
  • Radiation-field energy change from mesh motion: \(E_{rad,mesh\_adv}\) is the sum, over the hydro half-steps, of the change of the total radiation-field energy \(\sum_{c,g}E_{g,c}V_c\) (\(E_{g,c}\) the radiation energy density of group \(g\) in cell \(c\), \(V_c\) the cell volume) during the half-step. When the radiation energy density does not follow volume changes (Radiation.multigroup_diffusion.hydro_coupling="none"), a volume change is directly a change of radiation energy. The term is not a physical boundary flux, so the conservation error subtracts it from the total-energy change.
  • Artificial terms: \(E_{artificial}=E_{floor}+\max(E_{safety}-E_{floor},0)+E_{redist}\). The artificial terms are energy that numerical operations added to the state, not physical traffic. \(E_{floor}\) (the floor correction) is the sum of the energy that each operator raised directly (Floors, clamps, and numerical safeguards). \(E_{safety}\) (the safety correction) is the book entry for the conduction safety correction, but the current implementation books conduction corrections in \(E_{floor}\) and gives \(E_{safety}\) the same value as \(E_{floor}\), so \(\max(E_{safety}-E_{floor},0)\) is 0. \(E_{redist}\) (unresolved ALE redistribution) is the sum of the energy difference when the 2D RZ ALE remap puts a cell whose mass fell to the mass floor into its floor state and the deficit that the redistribution of the kinetic-energy closure (Numerics.ale.ke_closure_redistribute_floor, default False) could not absorb; it is 0 without ALE.
  • Implicit-solver residual: \(E_{solver}\) exists only as a book entry; no operator adds to it, so it is always 0. It would count as inflow or outflow according to its sign.

The conservation error is the difference between the total-energy change without the artificial terms and the radiation-energy change from mesh motion and the physical traffic, normalized by the larger of the size of the energy at the start of the step and the size of the inflow (with a lower floor):

$$ \begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{budget}&=\frac{\bigl|(\Delta E_{total}-E_{artificial}-E_{rad,mesh\_adv})-(E_{source}-E_{sink}+E_{supply})\bigr|}{E_{denom}},\\ E_{denom}&=\max\bigl(|E_{int,e}^{\,n}|+|E_{int,i}^{\,n}|+|E_{kin}^{\,n}|+|E_{rad}^{\,n}|,\,E_{source},\,10^{-20}\ \mathrm{erg}\bigr) \end{aligned} $$

Here \(E^{\,n}\) is the value at the start of the step. \(\varepsilon_{budget}\) measures physical conservation violation, while the artificial fractions (\(E_{floor}/E_{denom}\) etc.) are monitored independently to detect numerical safeguards dominating a result. The \(10^{-20}\) erg floor of the denominator prevents division by zero and false fatal errors on cold starts (total energy ≈ 0).

Most of the terms appear in the history's energy/ group — laser_incident, laser_escaped, marshak_in, E_volume_in, E_cbet_iaw, radiation_escaped, pdv_boundary, numerical_loss, floor_injected, safety_injected, redistribution_unresolved, and solver_residual, with state_supply and supply_flow_work when a state-supply face exists, and conservation_error for \(\varepsilon_{budget}\) (full enumeration on Outputs & analysis). The cumulative \(E_{burn,in}\) equals the sum of E_dep_e_cum and E_dep_i_cum in the burn/ group, and \(E_{rad,mesh\_adv}\) is not written. Reading guide: when \(\varepsilon_{budget}\) jumps, look at the artificial fractions first. If they dominate, chase the floor/clamp source (which operator; the clamp_count trend); otherwise question the boundary and source attribution (absorption, escapes).
