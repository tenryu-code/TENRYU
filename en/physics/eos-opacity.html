EOS, opacity & ionization reference

TENRYU's material closure connects composition \(A,Z\), the equation of state (EOS), mean ionization, and opacity to one cell state. It offers an analytic two-temperature ideal gas or a tabulated TMAT-H5 EOS, fixed or state-dependent \(\bar Z\), and constant or tabulated mass opacity. The resulting electron density feeds conduction and laser physics; group coefficients and Planck fractions feed radiation transport. Terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • ideal_gas is an analytic two-temperature (2T) EOS; tmat interpolates \(P,e,c_v\) tables and provides a \(T(\rho,e)\) inverse map.
  • \(n_e=\bar Z\rho/(A m_p)\) is the central link from EOS and ionization to conduction and laser coefficients.
  • Opacity \(\kappa\) is a mass opacity in cm²/g. Transport uses the distinct length opacity \(\sigma=\rho\kappa\) in cm⁻¹.
  • PlanckTable builds the Planck fractions \(b_g(T)\) on finite group bounds and diagnoses the spectral tails that fall outside the groups (the tail deficit). A temperature outside the table range is clamped to the value at the nearer end.

Physical mechanism

The evolution operators advance conserved quantities; everything else needed by the physics — \(P\), \(T\), \(c_v\), \(\bar Z\), and \(\kappa\) — must be reconstructed from material closures at the cell state. High-energy-density matter can span an enormous range within one shot, from cold solid to multi-keV plasma, while opacity changes by orders of magnitude. The analytic-versus-tabulated choice is therefore a per-material choice between clarity and fidelity.

The analytic baseline is a two-temperature ideal gas with independent electron and ion branches, whereas real target materials use TMAT-H5 tables of \(P\), \(e\), and \(c_v\) (TMAT-H5 is TENRYU's tabulated-material format and its HDF5 container, specified in the repository's docs/TMAT_H5_SPEC.md; see the terms section). The inverse map \(T(\rho,e)\) is the workhorse because hydro closes energy, not temperature: every re-closure performs a monotone binary search of the energy row at a fixed density bracket, then interpolates in \(\log T\). The GPU path is deliberately Newton-free — robust, deterministic, and free of a convergence branch. An energy below the table's lowest temperature clamps to the end temperature; an energy above the highest temperature is closed by an ideal-gas extension rather than clamped in the default 1D closure (see Ideal-gas and TMAT equations of state).

Mean ionization is the key coupling quantity. The mean ionization \(\bar Z\) sets \(n_e=\bar Z\rho/(A m_p)\), and \(n_e\) enters Spitzer conduction, inverse-bremsstrahlung (IB) collision rates, the electron EOS branch, and radiation–matter coupling. TENRYU can use fixed \(\bar Z=Z\), the Thomas–Fermi More fit, or tabular \(\bar Z(\rho,T_e)\); dynamic models are reevaluated at the conduction and radiation entrances so each operator sees ionization consistent with its starting state. Collision rates weight \(Z^2\), not \(Z\): when charge-state fractions are available, \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\) upgrades the Spitzer and SNB (Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet nonlocal electron conduction — see Conduction) charge factors to the collisional effective charge \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\).

Transport needs the Rosseland mean, a flux-weighted mean of \(1/\kappa_\nu\) (a harmonic mean of the opacity). Radiation flows through all frequency channels simultaneously — in parallel — so, exactly as with parallel resistors, the easiest path dominates: transparent spectral windows control the Rosseland mean. Emission and absorption instead need Planck means, linear emission-weighted means of \(\kappa_\nu\) in which strong spectral lines dominate. The mixed-cell rule "harmonic_mass_R" follows the same physics: Planck opacities add mass-linearly as absorbers, while Rosseland opacities combine harmonically through transparent paths. Planck fractions \(b_g(T)\) close the finite group structure by summing to one, and the tail that falls outside the groups is diagnosed as a deficit (Groups, Planck fraction, and tail deficit). An IONMIX table (the .cn4 material-table format of the collisional-radiative code Ionmix) without an emission-opacity block is taken as an LTE (local-thermodynamic-equilibrium) table, with a warning and \(\kappa^{PE}=\kappa^{PA}\).

From material data to transport coefficients

Material.A is the mean mass per ion in amu, while Material.Z is the mean atomic number or representative charge. For one material, \(n_i=\rho/(A m_p)\) and \(n_e=\bar Z n_i\), where \(m_p=1.6726\times10^{-24}\) g is the proton mass. TENRYU deliberately treats the atomic mass unit as \(m_p\), a convention that fixes its set of physical constants; the ≈0.73% offset from the true amu is absorbed into the material's effective \(A\). For a compound, \(A\) is the number-fraction mean ion mass, not the molecular mass: equiatomic CH uses 6.5 and CD uses 7. This convention aligns the EOS and opacity density axes with downstream particle-density calculations.

The EOS closes pressure, temperature, and heat capacity; ionization supplies \(\bar Z\) (Figure A). The resulting \(n_e\) drives electron conduction and inverse-bremsstrahlung laser absorption. On a separate branch, opacity combines with temperature-dependent Planck fractions to produce group coefficients for flux-limited diffusion (FLD) or discrete ordinates method (\(S_N\)) radiation transport. All branches share the material state but remain independently testable.

  • \(A\) — the number-fraction mean ion mass: CH uses 6.5 and CD uses 7. This density-axis convention keeps EOS, opacity, and particle densities consistent.
  • \(n_e=\bar Z\rho/(A m_p)\) — the electron density passed from composition and ionization into collisional transport and coupling coefficients.
  • \(\kappa\) versus \(\sigma=\rho\kappa\) — mass opacity is stored in cm²/g; transport consumes the distinct length opacity in cm⁻¹.
  • cv_e_override — a volumetric heat capacity in erg/(cm³·eV). It feeds the same constant to the Fleck factor (the linearization coefficient that couples radiation emission and absorption implicitly — see FLD) as a verification convention, not as a material-fitting knob.
  • \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\) — clamped to 1–10 before forming the collisional \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\) (terms). The clamp is a numerical safeguard, not physics: \(r_2\ge1\) holds by construction (it is a variance ratio), and the ceiling of 10 bounds the correction against degenerate or corrupt fraction tables.
  • \(10^{-20}\) cm²/g — the floor (see Floors, clamps, and safety guards) applied to each component's Rosseland opacity before the harmonic mean of "harmonic_mass_R". It sits many orders below any physical opacity and exists solely to keep the harmonic combination finite when a component is exactly zero.

Terms used in this calculation

The material closure rebuilds the pressure, temperature, ionization, and opacity of each cell from the conserved quantities. In that process the re-closure procedure, the format of the material tables, the grids of groups and temperatures, the treatment of mixed cells, and the charge that enters collisions all carry meanings specific to TENRYU's implementation, so they are defined together here.

Re-closure and the inverse map

Re-closure is recomputing the temperature \(T\), pressure \(P\), and specific heat \(c_v\) from \((\rho,e)\) with the EOS after an operator has changed the state. A closure is the relation that fixes these quantities from the conserved ones; on this page the EOS, mean ionization, and opacity together are called the material closure (Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline). Hydro advances energy, not temperature, so the \(T\), \(P\), and \(c_v\) read by conduction, radiation, and the laser must be rebuilt from the energy each time it changes. In this page's procedure, after each hydro half-step the inverse map recovers \(T\) and forward lookups evaluate \(P\) and \(c_v\) (Where the closures are called, step by step).

The inverse map computes the temperature \(T(\rho,e)\) from the density \(\rho\) and the energy \(e\), and is the first stage of a re-closure. For a tabulated EOS it looks the \(e(\rho,T)\) table up backwards in temperature. A density bracket (RhoBracket in the code) is the pair of adjacent indices of the table's density grid that enclose the queried density, together with the interpolation weight. The GPU path binary-searches the energy row at this bracket, relying on the energy increasing with temperature, to fix the temperature index, and then interpolates between adjacent indices in \(\log T\). It does not use Newton iteration, so the result is deterministic and has no convergence branch. The subsequent forward lookups of \(P\), \(e\), and \(c_v\) share the same density bracket.

The treatment of energies beyond the table edges depends on the closure. The default of Numerics.hydro.eos_closure_mode is "energy_authoritative" (1D; the evolved energy is authoritative and is never rewritten), while "legacy" and the 2D RZ closure clamp to the table edge. Studio does not write this key, so Studio decks also use the parser default (details in Ideal-gas and TMAT equations of state). The inverse map keeps the conserved energy consistent with the temperature that conduction and radiation read.

TMAT-H5

TMAT-H5 is TENRYU's HDF5 format for material tables (file extension .tmat.h5). Its specification is the repository file docs/TMAT_H5_SPEC.md. One file describes one material; the units are fixed to cgs + eV, and no unit conversion is done at run time. The format was created to replace IONMIX (Fortran unformatted binary) and SESAME (ASCII) tables with a single self-describing format for units, composition, and provenance; it lets the EOS table and the opacity table sit on separate grids, and a file with only one of them can be read.

A file consists of the following blocks. The material composition (/material: the atomic number \(Z\), mass \(A\), and mass fraction of each element). The EOS table (/eos) gives, on a grid of ion number density \(n_i\) [cm⁻³] and temperature \(T\) [eV], the mean ionization \(\bar Z\), the ion and electron pressures \(P_i\), \(P_e\) [dyne/cm²], and the specific energies \(e_i\), \(e_e\) [erg/g]; the specific heats \(c_{v,i}\), \(c_{v,e}\) [erg/(g·eV)] are optional. The opacity table (/opacity) gives, on three axes of group, ion number density, and temperature, the Rosseland mean \(\kappa_R\), the Planck absorption \(\kappa^{PA}\), and the Planck emission \(\kappa^{PE}\) [cm²/g]; the table is an LTE table when @is_lte is 1. The charge-state fractions (/ionization) are optional.

Because the density axis is the ion number density, it is converted at run time to the mass density with \(\rho=n_iAm_p\). A material selects TMAT-H5 with eos.model="tmat" and opacity.model="tmat" (Namelist controls). From the EOS table a total table is also built as the sum of the ion and electron tables. The charge-state fractions are used to build the table of the collisional effective charge (terms).

Groups, Planck fraction, and tail deficit

A group is an interval \([E_{g-1},E_g)\) into which radiation transport divides the photon energy, numbered \(g=1,\dots,G\). The bounds \(E_0<E_1<\dots<E_G\) [eV] are given by Radiation.group_bounds_eV (a list of numbers, or "log_uniform") and the number of groups \(G\) by Radiation.groups (default 1). With one group the calculation is gray. Both the opacity coefficients and the radiation energy are defined, and transported, group by group.

The Planck fraction \(b_g(T)\) is the fraction of the blackbody spectrum at temperature \(T\) that falls in group \(g\) (the formula is in PlanckTable and group-coverage diagnostics). It serves as the weight that distributes the radiative emission source among the groups, and it is normalized so that its sum over the groups is 1. At initialization it is computed and tabulated on a temperature grid (planck_fraction.compute_N_T points, default 200, spaced log-uniformly over the temperature range) as the PlanckTable. The temperature range is given by Radiation.compute_T_range_eV. If it is omitted, it is derived from the active electron-EOS tables, then the opacity tables, then the fallback [0.01, 1000] eV. A Studio deck with radiation enabled always writes compute_T_range_eV (for the default group bounds [0.1, \(10^5\)] eV it is [0.1, \(10^5\)] eV).

The tail deficit \(\delta(T)=1-\sum_g b_g^{raw}(T)\) is the fraction of the blackbody spectrum that falls outside the full range \([E_0,E_G]\) of the groups. Here \(b_g^{raw}\) is \(b_g\) before normalization. Because the photon-energy range is finite, the sum of \(b_g^{raw}\) can be smaller than one. Normalization does not recover the missing spectrum; it only redistributes the part that fell outside into the groups, so a narrow group range makes the radiative emission more concentrated in the group range than it really is. At initialization, therefore, the maximum of \(\delta(T)\) over the whole temperature grid is found and recorded. A warning is issued when the temperatures at which \(\delta\) exceeds \(10^{-3}\) cover every temperature of the table; otherwise the temperature interval where \(\delta\le10^{-3}\) and the maximum are logged as information. With one group, \(b_1=1\) and the deficit is not checked.

Mixed cell, dominant material, and partial density

A mixed cell is a cell whose volume is shared by two or more materials. The volume fraction \(f_m\) of material \(m\) (the normalized Geometry.volfrac, summing to 1) is the share of the cell volume that material \(m\) occupies. A void (defined here) is a vacuum without ions, so it is left out of the material averages, which are renormalized by the sum of the volume fractions of the non-void materials. A cell in which only one non-void material has a volume fraction is called a pure cell.

The dominant material is the non-void material with the largest volume fraction in the cell (the lower-numbered material on a tie, and the first non-void material in a cell where no non-void volume fraction is positive). In a pure cell the dominant material selects the opacity table. A cell whose dominant material uses an NLTE (non-LTE) table, even a mixed cell, is filled by that material's NLTE computation from the dominant material's table alone (the dominant-material approximation).

The partial density is the density of material \(m\) itself, the mass of material \(m\) divided by the volume it occupies, \(\rho_m=\rho\,w_m/f_m\). Here \(w_m\) is the mass fraction (the volume fraction when the material masses are not tracked). Because a material's table is a function of density, in a mixed cell each table is read at the material's partial density rather than the cell density. A material with \(f_m\le10^{-12}\) takes the cell density as its partial density (to avoid division by zero). Under the default linear rule Materials.opacity_mix_rule="linear_mass" (Studio does not write this key) the length opacity of a mixed cell is \(\sigma=\rho\sum_mw_m\kappa_m(\rho_m,T_e)\) (Opacity and material mixing).

Collisional effective charge and the mixed-cell averages

The collisional effective charge \(Z_{\rm eff}\) is the mean square of the ion charge divided by the mean, \(Z_{\rm eff}=\langle Z^2\rangle/\langle Z\rangle\). With \(\langle Z\rangle=\bar Z\) and \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\), this is \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\). Electron–ion collision rates scale with \(Z^2\), not \(Z\), so for the same \(\bar Z\) the collisions are stronger the more the ion charges differ. Since \(r_2\) is a variance ratio, \(r_2\ge1\) holds by construction, and TENRYU clamps it to 1–10. For example, for fully ionized CD (carbon and hydrogen 1:1), \(\bar Z=3.5\) gives \(Z_{\rm eff}=(36+1)/(6+1)\approx5.29\) and \(r_2\approx1.51\).

\(Z_{\rm eff}\) is used in two places. In conduction, when the TMAT file has charge-state fractions, the Spitzer conduction and SNB charge factors are replaced by \(Z_{\rm eff}\) (1D_SPH only). In laser inverse bremsstrahlung, the charge dependence of the absorption coefficient is given by \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\) instead of \(\bar Z\), and Laser.ib.zeff_model selects how \(r_2\) is obtained. The values are auto (the default; Studio does not write this key), off (\(r_2=1\)), table (a table built from the TMAT charge-state fractions), and sequential_strip (described below).

The mixed-cell averages \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}\) and \(A_{\rm eff}\) are the averages that combine the per-material values into the cell's representative values in a mixed cell. The names are similar, but they are different quantities from the \(Z_{\rm eff}\) above. \(A_{\rm eff}\) is the volume-fraction-weighted harmonic mean, \(1/A_{\rm eff}=\sum_mf_m/A_m\). \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}\) is the mean of the per-material mean ionizations \(\bar Z_m\) weighted by the ion number \(f_m/A_m\), \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}=\sum_m(f_m/A_m)\bar Z_m\big/\sum_m(f_m/A_m)\). The sums run over the non-void materials only and are renormalized by the sum of their volume fractions. With these weights, \(n_e=\rho\bar Z_{\rm eff}/(A_{\rm eff}m_p)\) equals the sum of \(f_m\rho\bar Z_m/(A_mm_p)\) over the materials. The conduction coefficients and the electron–ion energy exchange read this \(n_e\) and \(A_{\rm eff}\) as the cell values.

Sequential stripping

Sequential stripping is an approximation that distributes the mean ionization \(\bar Z\) (the mean number of free electrons per atom) of a plasma of several elements over the charges \(z_s\) of the individual elements. Starting from the element of the lowest atomic number, it ionizes each element fully until the electrons run out and passes the remaining electrons on to the next element. With \(x_s\) the number fraction and \(Z_s\) the atomic number of element \(s\) (in ascending order) and \(R\) the remaining number of electrons (initially \(\bar Z\)), it applies \(z_s=\min(Z_s,\,R/x_s)\) and \(R\leftarrow R-x_sz_s\) in turn. It is exact in the fully ionized limit.

From the resulting \(z_s\) it forms \(Z_{\rm eff}=\sum_sx_sz_s^2\big/\sum_sx_sz_s\), clamps the ratio \(r_2=Z_{\rm eff}/\bar Z\) to 1–10, and uses \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\) in the absorption coefficient of laser inverse bremsstrahlung. The elements are given by Laser.ib.species=[[Z, x], …]. There are at most four elements, and with only one, \(r_2=1\). The atomic numbers must be strictly increasing, each \(x_s>0\), and the sum must lie in [0.99, 1.01]. Laser.ib.zeff_model="sequential_strip" requires species. Under auto this approximation is chosen when species is given, or in 1D spherical geometry when all non-void materials share one multi-species composition (Mean ionization and collisional effective charge).

Ideal-gas and TMAT equations of state

eos.model="ideal_gas" independently closes the electron and ion branches. For the default monatomic gas, \(\gamma=5/3\), \(P_i=\rho k_BT_i/(A m_p)\), and \(P_e=\bar Z\rho k_BT_e/(A m_p)\), with \(k_B\) the eV_to_erg conversion as everywhere in the cgs + eV system. These temperature branches are the primary closure. The pressure–energy form \(P=(\gamma-1)\rho e\) with the configured ideal_gas.gamma, used when closing from evolved energy, is an equivalent statement of the same ideal gas: since \(e_i=k_BT_i/[(\gamma-1)Am_p]\) for the ion branch and \(e_e=\bar Z\,k_BT_e/[(\gamma-1)Am_p]\) for the electron branch, the two forms are consistent by construction when \(\bar Z\) is constant. When \(\bar Z\) depends on temperature, the electron heat capacity is still \(\bar Z k_B/[(\gamma-1)Am_p]\) with the current \(\bar Z\); the \(T_e\,d\bar Z/dT_e\) contribution is not included (a known limitation).

cv_e_override supports verification problems, such as Marshak-wave or Su–Olson setups, that prescribe a constant electron volumetric heat capacity. Its unit is erg/(cm³·eV), unlike the mass-specific \(c_v\) in erg/(g·eV) returned by an EOS table. The override supplies the same constant \(C_{v,e}\) to the radiation Fleck factor. It therefore expresses the constant-\(C_v\) verification condition, not a general material-fitting parameter.

eos.model="tmat" builds ion, electron, and total (the sum of ion and electron) \(P(\rho,T)\), \(e(\rho,T)\), and \(c_v(\rho,T)\) tables from TMAT-H5. The native ion-number-density axis is converted with \(\rho=n_i A m_p\). Runtime evaluation is bilinear in \((\log\rho,\log T)\) and clamps to the table edge outside the tabulated domain (the treatment of the top of the temperature range is given in the next paragraph). A non-positive \(c_v\) is raised to a floor, with a single warning when the table is built.

Hydrodynamic re-closure from evolved energy does not run Newton iterations. At a fixed density bracket, the GPU path performs a monotone binary search of the energy row, then interpolates between adjacent temperature indices in \(\log T\). The inverse map \(T(\rho,e)\) and the subsequent forward \(P,e,c_v\) evaluations reuse the same density bracket. A target energy below the table's value at its lowest temperature returns the lowest temperature. A target energy above the value \(e_{top}\) at the highest temperature \(T_{top}\) is treated according to the closure. The default 1D closure (Numerics.hydro.eos_closure_mode="energy_authoritative", which treats the evolved energy as authoritative) does not clamp; it closes with an ideal-gas extension attached to the \(T_{top}\) row, \(e=e_{top}+c_{v,top}(T-T_{top})\), \(P=P_{top}T/T_{top}\), \(c_v=c_{v,top}\) (the subscript top denotes the table value at \(T_{top}\)), and leaves the evolved energy unchanged. The "legacy" closure and the 2D RZ closure clamp at \(T_{top}\), and under "legacy" the excess energy is replaced by the table value and lost. The one exception to this Newton-free inverse is the cold-equilibrium constitutive model (Numerics.hydro.T_start_inactive_cells="cold_equilibrium"), whose inverse takes a bracketed Newton step in the temperature range where its correction fades out.

Mean ionization and collisional effective charge

The default Materials.zbar.model="fixed" uses \(\bar Z=Z\), implying full ionization, unless Materials.zbar.fixed_value (unset by default) is given; a fixed_value of 0 or more is used as \(\bar Z\) in every non-void cell. State-dependent alternatives are thomas_fermi, which uses More's fit to the Thomas–Fermi ionization (Table IV of reference [2], in the similarity variables \(\rho/(ZA)\) and \(T_e/Z^{4/3}\), the same variables as the Thomas–Fermi scaling of QEOS [1]), and tabular, which interpolates \(\bar Z(\rho,T_e)\). Dynamic models are reevaluated at the entrances to the hydro half-steps and to the conduction and radiation operators, and in 1D also before the laser operator. Thus ionization after hydro compression reaches conduction, and the temperature change after conduction reaches radiation closure.

If a TMAT file contains optional per-element charge-state fractions under /ionization, TENRYU can retain a collisional moment that a single \(\bar Z\) loses. It reduces the fractions to \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\), clamps that ratio to 1–10, and uses \(\bar Zr_2=Z_{\rm eff}\) (terms) for Spitzer conduction and the SNB charge factor. With no fraction table, no correction is applied. This table-moment path is restricted to 1D_SPH; in a deck with several materials, each cell uses the fraction table of its own material, and a material without one gets no correction.

The material zbar.model and laser-IB Laser.ib.zeff_model are separate controls. The latter defaults to auto, which is resolved at build time. It becomes table when TMAT ionization fractions exist. Otherwise, in 1D spherical geometry, it becomes sequential_strip (sequential stripping) when Laser.ib.species is given or when every non-void material shares one multi-species TMAT composition (which then becomes the species list, such as C and H for CD). In all other cases it becomes off, with a warning for a mixture in 1D spherical geometry. In a 1D spherical deck with two or more non-void materials, auto is resolved per material: a material's own ionization fractions give table, given species or a TMAT composition of two to four species give sequential_strip, and any other material gets off. Fixed \(\bar Z\) and automatic \(Z_{\rm eff}\) therefore govern electron density and multispecies collision weighting, respectively.

Opacity and material mixing

opacity.model="constant" supplies absorption kappa_a and scattering kappa_s in cm²/g and assigns them to every group. The 1D \(S_N\) solver uses kappa_s as the physical scattering opacity. FLD has no physical scattering, so it rejects a kappa_s greater than zero as an input error. The 2D \(S_N\) solver sets physical scattering to zero and does not consume it. A table model supplies density-, temperature-, and group-dependent Rosseland \(\kappa_R\), Planck absorption \(\kappa^{PA}\), and, when present, Planck emission \(\kappa^{PE}\). Transport converts each mass opacity to \(\sigma=\rho\kappa\). An IONMIX table without an emission-opacity block is taken as an LTE table, with a warning and \(\kappa^{PE}=\kappa^{PA}\) (Kirchhoff's law).

In a mixed cell, Materials.opacity_mix_rule selects how the material opacities combine, with mass-fraction weights (volume fractions when the material masses are not tracked). The default "linear_mass" mixes both the Planck and the Rosseland opacity linearly, as in \(\kappa_{P,mix}=\sum_\alpha f_{m,\alpha}\kappa_{P,\alpha}\). "harmonic_mass_R" keeps the Planck mixing linear and takes the Rosseland harmonic mean, \(1/\kappa_{R,mix}=\sum_\alpha f_{m,\alpha}/\kappa_{R,\alpha}\), and "max" takes the largest material opacity for both. The two non-linear rules exist in 1D only; 2D accepts only "linear_mass". The diagnostic environment switch TENRYU_MM_OPACITY_MIX=harmonic mixes constant opacities harmonically for both means.

Per-material table opacities (multi-material 1D)

In a 1D_SPH multigroup-diffusion deck, each non-void material may carry its own tmat opacity table — an LTE table, or a non-LTE (NLTE) table with an emission opacity independent of the absorption — or a constant. A material may also use table_nlte (an IONMIX table), power_law, or freq_dep_marshak, and sn_transport decks accept the same per-material models. In a pure cell the dominant material, meaning the one with the largest volume fraction, selects the table, whose group opacities are interpolated at the cell's \((\rho,T_e)\). Mixed interface cells combine every contributing material exactly — under the default linear rule, \(\sigma = \rho \sum_m w_m \kappa_m(\rho_m, T_e)\) with mass-fraction weights \(w_m\) (volume fractions as fallback), each material's table evaluated at its partial density \(\rho_m = \rho\,w_m/f_m\). Only cells whose dominant material is NLTE are filled by the NLTE kernel under the dominant-material approximation. In 2D, a multi-material \(S_N\) deck requires constant opacities for every non-void material, whereas the 2D FLD evaluates each material's opacity (the model may differ per material) at its partial density and mixes them; only the "linear_mass" rule is available.

PlanckTable and group-coverage diagnostics

For group \(g=[E_{g-1},E_g)\), the Planck fraction is the in-group blackbody integral divided by the integral over the full spectrum:

$$ b_g(T)=\frac{15}{\pi^4}\int_{E_{g-1}/T}^{E_g/T}\frac{x^3}{e^x-1}\,dx. $$

With planck_fraction.method="compute", initialization evaluates this integral on a temperature grid; at run time PlanckTable interpolates the logarithms of the cumulative fractions (or of their complementary tails) with cubic Hermite polynomials in \(\ln T\) through their exact slopes and takes \(b_g\) as differences, so the fractions sum to one by construction. Finite photon bounds can make the raw sum smaller than one, so production weights are renormalized to \(\sum_gb_g=1\). Renormalization does not recover missing spectrum: TENRYU also records the tail deficit \(\delta(T)=1-\sum_gb_g^{raw}(T)\) and reports its maximum over the table grid. It warns when the deficit exceeds \(10^{-3}\) at every table temperature; otherwise it logs the temperature interval in which the deficit is at most \(10^{-3}\) together with the maximum deficit.

compute_T_range_eV defines the Planck table's temperature axis. If omitted, it is derived from active electron-EOS tables, then opacity tables, then the fallback [0.01, 1000] eV. A Studio deck with radiation enabled always writes it, from the smaller of the lowest group bound and 0.1 eV to the larger of the highest group bound and \(10^5\) eV. A runtime temperature outside this interval is clamped to the value at the nearer end (no extrapolation). The number of such accesses and the amount by which they exceed the range are not recorded. A warning appears only on the host-side path that evaluates \(b_g\) at the drive temperature \(T_r\) of the radiation Marshak boundary, and only once. This temperature axis is distinct from the photon group bounds \([E_0,E_G]\). When the group bounds are generated with "log_uniform", or when neither group bounds nor a table group structure is given, the group bounds are built log-uniformly from this temperature range (with a lower end of at least \(10^{-3}\) eV), so the two ranges coincide. When the group structure of an opacity table or an explicit group_bounds_eV is used, the two ranges are independent. Design the group bounds to cover the expected plasma spectrum and the temperature axis to cover the temperatures needed for interpolation.

Where the closures are called, step by step

  1. Initialize. Load the material tables, tabulate the configured Python callables and freeze the configuration, and build PlanckTable on the radiation-group bounds.

  2. Re-close after each hydro half-step. Recover \(T\) from the evolved \((\rho,e)\) with the inverse map, then evaluate \(P\) and \(c_v\) forward while reusing the same density bracket.

  3. Enter conduction. Refresh dynamic \(\bar Z\), form \(n_e=\bar Z\rho/(A m_p)\), and evaluate the conductivity coefficients from that entrance state.

  4. Enter radiation. Refresh \(\bar Z\) and \(Z_{\rm eff}\), evaluate \(\kappa_R\), \(\kappa^{PA}\), and \(\kappa^{PE}\) at \((\rho,T_e)\), convert each mass opacity to \(\sigma=\rho\kappa\), and evaluate \(b_g(T_e)\).

  5. Mix material contributions. In mixed cells, combine the material opacities by Materials.opacity_mix_rule, evaluating each material's table at its partial density; a cell whose dominant material is NLTE takes that material's NLTE coefficients.

  6. Apply the safeguards. Raise a non-positive \(c_v\) to a floor (with a single warning when the table is built), clamp table-domain accesses to the end values, and apply the Rosseland opacity floor. In the default 1D closure, an energy above the table's value at its highest temperature is closed by an ideal-gas extension rather than clamped (see Ideal-gas and TMAT equations of state).

Void materials

A void is a material that represents a region without matter, such as the vacuum outside the target, on the grid. A material with is_void=True (a void material) skips EOS and opacity table loading. Its EOS is fixed to an ideal gas, its opacity to kappa_a=kappa_s=0, and its \(Z\) to 0; \(A\) is 1 unless given. A cell whose non-void volume fractions sum to at most \(10^{-12}\) is a void cell, and its cell_is_void mask is set to 1 at initialization. A void material is excluded from the mixed-cell averages \(\bar Z_{\rm eff}\) and \(A_{\rm eff}\) (terms; these are mixture averages — distinct from the collisional \(Z_{\rm eff}=\bar Zr_2\) above), from the EOS averages, and from mass-fraction mixing; its volume fraction only derives the cell_is_void mask. A void cell forces \(\bar Z=0\) and skips the Thomas–Fermi evaluation. In a void cell the thermal conductivity is set to zero, so the cell is adiabatic; the absorption and emission coefficients are set to zero, so radiation and matter exchange no energy; and the electron density is zero, so the laser is neither absorbed nor refracted. The initial \(\rho,T_e,T_i\) of a void cell come from Materials.void_config, not from the deck's Geometry.rho, Te, and Ti.

Verification

  • The paired TMAT lookup tests combine forward \((\log\rho,\log T)\) interpolation against manufactured tables with a GPU smoke test of the inverse map on the GXII (GEKKO XII) CD table. At \(\rho=1.05\) g/cm³ and \(e_e=10^6\ldots10^{12}\) erg/g, the smoke test requires zero bracket failures and zero fallbacks.
  • The Planck sum gate covers 1, 4, 16, and 64 groups over \(T=1\ldots10^5\) eV, checking normalized weights, the terminal cumulative distribution function (CDF), and the group-summed \(aT^4\). Separately, initialization reports the maximum tail deficit; accesses outside the Planck table's temperature range are not recorded.
  • Opacity-parity benchmarks must align physical assumptions. The LTE parity A/B against the radiation-hydrodynamics code HELIOS uses a TENRYU Kirchhoff's-law branch with kappa_PE := kappa_PA, separating that matched LTE treatment from the raw CRE (collisional-radiative-equilibrium) non-LTE emission table.
