Electron & ion conduction reference

TENRYU's local electron transport combines Spitzer–Härm (SH) conductivity with the Z-dependent γ0 correction (defined in the local model section), which is always included, a harmonic flux limiter, and super-time-stepping (STS). In 1D and 2D RZ, the opt-in SNB (Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet) multigroup model adds a nonlocal correction and couples it in time with the implicit-SNB (iSNB) Picard iteration of Cao 2015. Ion conduction is a separate option. Terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • The Z-dependent γ0 correction applies on every path; no setting turns it off.
  • The local SH model and SNB share the same harmonic flux limiter. The default f_lim is 0.06; with SNB, a deck sets 0.5 (the GUI switches to 0.5 when SNB is selected).
  • 1D uses a conservative finite-volume operator. 2D RZ uses a node-local operator with symmetric conductances. STS accelerates both explicit diffusion paths. The per-material conservation mode (per_material_conservation_enabled) is available only in 2D RZ. These terms are defined in the terms section.
  • Void cells are insulating (adiabatic) with κeff=0. The SNB multigroup correction cannot cross a void either.

Physical mechanism

In a laser target, the place where laser energy is absorbed (the dilute corona outside the critical-density surface) and the place where that energy does work as ablation pressure (the dense ablation front) are spatially separate. Electron thermal conduction is the main energy transport that connects them. Its strength sets the ablation pressure and the mass ablation rate, and it also sets how much heat runs ahead of the front into cold material — the preheat.

The starting model makes the heat flux proportional to the local temperature gradient: \(\mathbf q_{SH}=-\kappa_{SH}\nabla T_e\), i.e. a Fourier law, with the conductivity \(\kappa_{SH}\) taken from Spitzer–Härm (SH) theory of electron–ion Coulomb collisions. The temperature scaling follows from a short chain. Coulomb scattering deflects fast electrons poorly, so the collision frequency drops steeply with speed, \(\nu_{ei}\propto v^{-3}\). Evaluated for thermal electrons (\(v\sim v_{th,e}\propto T_e^{1/2}\)), this gives \(T_e^{-3/2}\), and the mean free path (mfp) \(\lambda_{ei}=v/\nu_{ei}\) grows as \(\lambda_{ei}\propto T_e^{2}/(n_e Z\ln\Lambda)\) — quadratically in temperature. Since conductivity is roughly "density × thermal speed × mean free path," the density dependence cancels and \(\kappa_{SH}\propto T_e^{5/2}/(Z\ln\Lambda)\) remains. Doubling the temperature multiplies the conductivity by about 5.7 — a strongly nonlinear response. A hotter corona therefore conducts much more strongly, and, as discussed below, becomes numerically "stiff" to integrate explicitly, with a vanishing stable time step. The Fourier law itself is a local approximation — the electron is assumed to feel only the gradient at its own position — and it is justified only while the electron mean free path stays well below the temperature-gradient scale length \(L_T=T_e/|\nabla T_e|\).

Most of the heat, however, is not carried by average thermal electrons. When the flux is decomposed by electron energy (the SNB spectral weight \(\beta^4e^{-\beta}/24\) introduced below is this distribution), the contribution peaks for suprathermal electrons moving at roughly \(3–4\,v_{th,e}\). Because the mean free path scales as \(v^4\) (a consequence of \(\lambda=v/\nu\) with \(\nu\propto v^{-3}\)), these electrons have mean free paths 34–44, roughly 80–260 times longer than those of thermal electrons. The local condition \(\lambda\ll L_T\) can therefore hold when evaluated for thermal electrons while already being violated for the electrons that actually carry the heat. The violation sets in first near the ablation front, where the gradient is steepest.

Even in the strong-gradient limit where the local law fails, the flux has a firm physical ceiling: the free-streaming flux \(n_e k_BT_ev_{th,e}\), obtained if the entire thermal energy content streamed one way at the thermal speed. Kinetic calculations and experiments both show that only a fraction of it can actually be sustained, and TENRYU carries that fraction in the coefficient \(f_{lim}\), imposing the cap \(q_{max}=f_{lim}n_ek_BT_ev_{th,e}\). The cap is applied through the factor \(\theta=1/(1+|q_{raw}|/q_{max})\); the resulting flux \(q=\theta q_{raw}\) satisfies, in magnitude, \(1/|q|=1/|q_{raw}|+1/q_{max}\) — the same harmonic composition as resistors in parallel, hence the name harmonic flux limiter. Where gradients are gentle, \(q\approx q_{raw}\); at strong gradients it saturates smoothly toward \(q_{max}\) with no kink. The local-SH default \(f_{lim}=0.06\) lies in the calibration range long used against direct-drive experiments. The value 0.5 used with SNB is much looser because the flux limiter plays a different role there: SNB computes the flux suppression itself, and the flux limiter acts only when the composite flux, with the nonlocal correction added, becomes anomalously large (see the SNB section).

A flux limiter alone cannot represent nonlocal transport. The \(v^4\) mean-free-path scaling produces two consequences at once. First, at the steepest point, the electrons cross the gradient without colliding, so the flux never builds up to the local-gradient prediction: the real flux falls below the Spitzer–Härm value (flux inhibition). Second, the same electrons keep carrying heat forward for several mean free paths before stopping, heating material beyond the ablation front (preheat). Because a flux limiter can only reduce the local flux, it can approximate the first effect but cannot, in principle, represent the second — moving heat ahead of the gradient. Capturing both requires a nonlocal model that follows electrons by energy and resolves how far they travel and where they deposit their heat.

The Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet (SNB) model is the standard reduced-kinetic model for this purpose. Instead of solving the full kinetic equation, it splits the SH flux into electron-energy groups and, for each group, solves a diffusion equation for a transport field \(H_g\) (dimensionally a heat flux) whose spatial scale is the group's mean free path \(\lambda_g\). \(H_g\) spreads away from its source, the group's share of the local SH flux. This represents the electrons of the group carrying heat to positions away from the local gradient, and the reconstructed correction \(\delta q=-\sum_g(\lambda_g^{tr}/3)\nabla H_g\) both reduces the flux at the steep point and produces preheat ahead of it. In TENRYU, SNB is an additive correction to the same limited SH pipeline, not an alternative solver, and the corrected composite flux passes through the single shared flux limiter. The quantities of SNB, such as the groups and the transport field, are defined in the terms section. The default mean-free-path calibration (the \(r=2\) form of Sherlock 2017) already folds in electric-field flux suppression, so the optional explicit E-field term defaults off to avoid double counting. The calibration factor \(r\) is the scaling parameter of the group mean free path in the notation of Brodrick et al.; the adopted mean free path (Sherlock 2017) is equivalent to the choice \(r=2\).

The main numerical challenge is the stability limit of explicit diffusion, \(\Delta t\lesssim\Delta x^2/D\). The thermal diffusivity \(D\propto T_e^{5/2}/\rho\) grows rapidly in a hot, dilute corona, so the stable substep becomes far smaller than the hydrodynamic global step. In a representative GXII (GEKKO XII) corona (coronal cells of the 500 µm benchmark capsule at \(T_e\approx2\) keV, \(\rho\approx10^{-3}\) g/cm³, \(\Delta l\approx50\,\mu\)m), one global driver step would require 130–340 ordinary substeps. STS replaces that chain of substeps with \(s\) stages of different lengths whose combined stability interval grows as \(O(s^2)\), so the required stage count is only about the square root of the substep count (\(s\approx\lceil\sqrt{2N_{sub}}\rceil\)) — 17–27 stages in this example. Coefficients are fixed at the entry temperature (only the 2D RZ Kirchhoff secant is evaluated at the stage temperature; see the super-time-stepping section), and one re-closure (recomputing temperature, pressure, and specific heat from \((\rho,e)\) with the equation of state, EOS) ends the step. Every update is conservative, and the per-material conservation mode uses the same STS. In 1D the optional ion conduction does not use STS but solves a separate backward-Euler tridiagonal system with the same face-κ policy. In 2D RZ it puts the ion coefficients into the same node-local operator as the electrons and solves it with the same STS.

Local Spitzer–Härm model and flux limiter

Electron conductivity is evaluated in the cgs + eV unit system. The mean ionization \(\bar Z\) comes from the material model. Because Coulomb collision rates are governed by the mean of the charge squared, materials that provide the charge-moment ratio \(r_2=\langle Z^2\rangle/\bar Z^2\) replace \(\bar Z\) with the collisional effective charge \(\bar Z r_2\). The tables that give \(r_2\) are read in 1D_SPH only, so 2D RZ electron conduction keeps \(\bar Z\) (see EOS, opacity & ionization). The conductivity is:

$$ \kappa_{SH}=\kappa_0\,\xi(\bar Z)\, \frac{T_e^{5/2}}{\bar Z\ln\Lambda},\qquad \xi(Z)=\frac{(Z+0.24)/(Z+4.2)}{(1+0.24)/(1+4.2)}, $$ $$ \kappa_0=3.06\times10^9 \;\mathrm{erg/(cm\,s\,eV^{7/2})},\qquad \mathbf q_{SH}=-\kappa_{SH}\nabla T_e. $$

Here \(\kappa_0\) is the Spitzer prefactor in cgs + eV units, \(\xi(\bar Z)\) is the Z-dependent correction detailed in the list below, \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm, and \(\mathbf q_{SH}\) is the resulting unlimited heat flux.

In 1D the flux limit is applied per face. The face electron density and temperature — each the arithmetic average of the adjacent cells — and the thermal speed at that temperature build \(q_{max}\), and the harmonic composition described in the mechanism section caps the flux (\(k_B\) is the eV→erg conversion constant eV_to_erg, as everywhere in the cgs + eV system):

$$ v_{th,e}=\sqrt{\frac{k_BT_e}{m_e}},\qquad q_{max}=f_{lim}n_ek_BT_ev_{th,e},\qquad \theta=\frac{1}{1+|q_{raw}|/q_{max}},\qquad q=\theta q_{raw}. $$

In 2D RZ the flux limit is applied per cell. The cell's electron density and temperature give \(q_{max}\), the cell's temperature-gradient magnitude \(|\nabla T_e|\) (the mean of the gradient magnitudes at the cell's nodes) gives \(q_{raw}=\kappa_{SH}|\nabla T_e|\), and the effective cell conductivity is \(\kappa_{eff}=\theta\kappa_{SH}\).

  • \(\xi(Z)\). γ0(Z) is the dimensionless electron heat-conduction coefficient of Braginskii transport theory (the prefactor of the Spitzer-type conductivity; 3.16 at Z=1), and ξ(Z) is the Epperlein–Short fit of its Z dependence, normalized so that ξ(1)=1, with ξ(∞)=4.19. The Z=1 component (3.16) is already folded into κ0. The correction applies in 1D, 2D, the per-material conservation mode, and the SH base flux used by SNB; no setting turns it off.
  • \(q_{max}\) and \(\theta\). \(q_{max}=f_{lim}n_ek_BT_ev_{th,e}\) sets the free-streaming fraction and θ provides harmonic interpolation. The local path uses \(q_{raw}=q_{SH}\), whereas SNB uses \(q_{raw}=q_{SH}+\delta q\); SH and SH+δq pass through one shared flux limiter, never a second one (Figure A).
  • Face-κ policy. In 1D, the rule that builds a face κ from the two adjacent cell values is set by face_kappa_policy (default: the Kirchhoff secant, kirchhoff_same_material). The terms section gives the definition and the formula, and the face-conductivity section the usage. The limiter's SH estimate and the applied flux always use the same face κ.
  • Voids. cell_is_void forces κeff=0, forming an adiabatic boundary and preventing floor-density exterior cells from collapsing the explicit stability limit Δtexp. SNB gives voids an identity row and zeroes adjacent group diffusivities.
  • SNB groups. In the temperature-normalized electron energy β̂=E/(kB max Te), the lowest group is [0, 0.1], and the group edges above it form a geometric progression from 0.1 to snb_E_max_over_Te (23 geometric intervals for the default 24 groups; Figure B). The weights ξg=P(βg)−P(βg−1) are formed as exact differences of the antiderivative P. The two mean-free-path variants are the default geometric_r2 and the original Schurtz form. The definitions of groups and weights are in the terms section.
  • iSNB Picard. The Cao 2015 coupling fixes (θ, δq), repeats the STS superstep from \(T_e^n\), and recomputes the correction. It performs at least two iterations and uses a flux max-norm relative tolerance; at convergence the amplification factor lies in (0,1) for every Δt, giving unconditional stability. The iteration is defined in the terms section.

Terms used in this calculation

The conduction calculation uses several words in the sense of TENRYU's implementation: the words for the time steps of STS, the policy that builds the face conductivity, the node-local operator of 2D RZ, the per-material conservation mode, and the quantities of SNB. They are defined together here.

Time steps of STS

The global step is the time step \(\Delta t\) that the driver advances in one outer step; how it is chosen is described on the time-step control page. Conduction proposes as the upper limit of the global step the largest interval that one STS cycle of Chebyshev stages can cover, \(s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\) (820 times \(\Delta t_{exp}\) by default; see the conduction section of that page).

The explicit stability limit \(\Delta t_{exp}\) is the time step at which an explicit update of heat diffusion with the forward Euler method is stable, to within a safety factor. Because κ is proportional to \(T_e^{5/2}\), \(\Delta t_{exp}\) is several hundred times smaller than \(\Delta t\) in the corona. It is obtained from the cell width and the effective thermal diffusivity in 1D, and from the Gershgorin bound of the operator used in the update in 2D RZ (step 3). The safety factor \(C_{cond}\) is the key Numerics.dt.cfl_cond (default 0.25, range (0, 1]). The ratio \(\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\) sets the number of STS stages.

A substep is one explicit update of size \(\Delta t_{exp}\) in the hypothetical case that STS is not used and \(\Delta t\) is divided into explicit updates. The number needed is \(N_{sub}=\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\rceil\), which is 130–340 in the GXII corona. TENRYU does not run substeps; \(N_{sub}\) is the reference quantity that measures the benefit of STS.

A superstep is the interval \(\Delta t_{sup}\) that STS advances in one sequence of stages. When \(\Delta t\) is at most \(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\), the global step is one superstep. Above that, \(\Delta t\) is divided into \(n_{sup}\) equal supersteps. Here \(s_{max}\) is the ceiling on the number of stages in one superstep (sts_max_stages, default 40, range 1–200) and \(\eta\) is the coefficient that decides the division (sts_subcycle_eta, default 0.9, range (0, 1]). By default one superstep covers up to about 738 times \(\Delta t_{exp}\) (\(0.9\times820\)).

A stage is one forward Euler update inside a superstep. A superstep consists of \(s\) stages, and the time step \(\tau_j\) of the \(j\)-th stage differs from stage to stage (formula in step 5); the \(\tau_j\) sum to \(\Delta t_{sup}\). Combining long and short stages makes the interval over which the whole sequence is stable grow in proportion to \(s^2\), so that about \(\sqrt{2\Delta t_{sup}/\Delta t_{exp}}\) stages suffice. The distribution of the stage lengths is set by the number of stages and the damping \(\nu\) (sts_damping, default 0.01, range (0, 1)).

Face-κ policy

The face-κ policy is the rule that decides, in 1D, the thermal conductivity \(\kappa_f\) of the face between two adjacent cells from the κ of the two cells. Its key is Numerics.conduction.face_kappa_policy, and its values are "kirchhoff_same_material" (default) and "harmonic".

Because \(\kappa\propto T^{5/2}\) changes by orders of magnitude across a steep heat front, the way \(\kappa_f\) is built governs how the front advances. "harmonic" takes the harmonic mean \(2\kappa_L\kappa_R/(\kappa_L+\kappa_R)\) of the κ of the left and right cells as \(\kappa_f\). The harmonic mean is pulled toward the smaller κ, so \(\kappa_f\) is too small ahead of the front and the front tends to pin to the grid. If κ is zero on either side, \(\kappa_f\) is zero and no heat passes.

"kirchhoff_same_material" uses the secant of the exact integral of \(T^{5/2}\) over the interval between the left and right temperatures \(T_L\) and \(T_R\), a face average based on the Kirchhoff transformation.

$$ \kappa_f=\tilde\kappa_f\,\frac27\,\frac{T_R^{7/2}-T_L^{7/2}}{T_R-T_L},\qquad \tilde\kappa_f=\frac{2\tilde\kappa_L\tilde\kappa_R}{\tilde\kappa_L+\tilde\kappa_R},\qquad \tilde\kappa=\frac{\kappa}{T^{5/2}}. $$

Here \(\tilde\kappa\) is the Spitzer prefactor taken from the cell κ. The secant is used only when the ratio of the left and right \(\tilde\kappa\) lies between 0.1 and 10. When the ratio is outside this range (a material interface, or a strong table-driven jump of \(\tilde\kappa\)), or when κ or \(T\) is not positive (a void), the harmonic mean is used. The same \(\kappa_f\) is used for the SH estimate of the flux limiter, for the applied flux, and, by default, for \(\Delta t_{exp}\) (step 3).

2D RZ uses the node-local operator (next item) and builds no face \(\kappa_f\). The key only chooses whether each pair of the operator is multiplied by the secant over the two cell temperatures (\(\tfrac27(T_R^{7/2}-T_L^{7/2})/(T_R-T_L)\) in the formula above): "kirchhoff_same_material" multiplies and "harmonic" does not. SNB in 2D RZ uses no secant.

Node-local operator

The node-local operator is the symmetric linear operator that couples cell-centered temperatures in the 2D RZ conduction (electrons, ions, and the group equations of SNB). The 2D RZ mesh consists of polygonal cells in the meridian (r–z) plane. The operator is a sum of node contributions, and the contribution of a node is built as follows.

A corner is, for each cell that meets at a node \(p\), the part of the cell that touches \(p\). It is the quadrilateral through the cell's reference point (the centroid for a convex cell), the midpoint of the edge before \(p\), \(p\) itself, and the midpoint of the edge after \(p\), the same division as the corners of the hydrodynamics page.

A half-edge is the part on the side of \(p\) of an edge that leaves \(p\) and is split at its midpoint. The temperature \(\Theta\) of a half-edge is an unknown shared by the two corners on either side of it (one corner at a boundary). The temperature gradient in each corner is expressed with the Green–Gauss formula from the cell temperature and the half-edge temperatures, which gives the heat flow through the half-edges. The condition that the heat flows on the two sides of an interior half-edge are equal, and that the heat flow through a boundary half-edge is zero, lets one eliminate \(\Theta\) in favor of the temperatures of the cells that share the node. What remains is the matrix \(a\) that expresses the heat leaving cell \(c\) as a linear combination of the temperatures of the cells that share the node.

The rows and the columns of \(a\) both sum to zero (a uniform temperature carries no heat flow, and the heat flow is continuous), and \(a\) is symmetric positive semidefinite. With the pair coefficient \(G_{cd}=-(a_{cd}+a_{dc})/2\) of a cell \(c\) and a cell \(d\) that shares a node with it, the heat capacity \((\rho c_vV)_c\) of cell \(c\) times the rate of change of its temperature equals the sum of the heat flowing in from the neighboring cells (\(V\) is the volume of the solid of revolution).

$$ (\rho c_vV)_c\,\frac{dT_{e,c}}{dt}=\sum_dG_{cd}\,(T_{e,d}-T_{e,c}). $$

When the secant is used, each term of the right-hand side is multiplied by \(S_{cd}\) (step 6). Because \(G_{cd}=G_{dc}\), the pair power \(G_{cd}(T_d-T_c)\) differs between \(c\) and \(d\) only in sign, and thermal energy is conserved.

The operator does not use the mesh indices \((i,j)\), so the same procedure applies to cells that are not quadrilaterals and to meshes made of several blocks. The heat flow between cells of different conductivity follows from the continuity of the heat flow across the half-edge, and no harmonic mean of the face is used. The heat flow through a boundary half-edge is zero, except that the half-edges on the z_bottom and z_top sides where a state supply (state_supply) is active are held at the supply temperature (single-block meshes). There is no key that chooses the operator, and all 2D RZ conduction is solved with it. The pair coefficients \(G_{cd}\) determine the STS update and the bound that gives the explicit stability limit \(\Delta t_{exp}\).

Per-material conservation mode

The per-material conservation mode is the setting in which, when several materials are mixed in one cell, the state keeps the mass, volume fraction, and electron energy of each material and conserves the energy of each material. It is used so that the EOS of each material handles the temperature and pressure of that material. Its key is Numerics.materials.per_material_conservation_enabled (default False, 2D RZ only). The treatment of mixed cells is chosen with Numerics.materials.mixed_cell_closure: the default "equal_compressibility" keeps the volume fractions fixed and takes the cell pressure as the volume-fraction-weighted sum of the material pressures, and "pressure_relaxation" relaxes the material pressures at a limited rate.

In conduction, one electron temperature per cell is advanced with STS, and the resulting energy change is distributed among the materials; the rule is given in the face-conductivity section. The mode cannot be combined with SNB (input validation rejects it). In 2D RZ it needs mixed_cell_closure="pressure_relaxation" when it is combined with ion conduction.

Quantities of SNB

A group is an interval of electron energy \(E\). SNB solves the nonlocal transport for each energy group. The group edges are laid out in the energy \(\hat\beta=E/(k_B\max T_e)\), normalized by the largest electron temperature \(\max T_e\) of the step (the maximum over all cells). The lowest group is \([0,\,0.1]\), and the edges above it form a geometric progression from 0.1 to \(\hat\beta_{max}\). The number of groups is snb_n_groups (default 24, at least 2), and the upper end \(\hat\beta_{max}\) is snb_E_max_over_Te (default 20, larger than 1). The group edges of \(\hat\beta\) multiplied by \(k_B\max T_e\) give physical energies \(E_g\), which are held for the step. The representative energy of a group is \(\varepsilon_g=(E_{g-1}+E_g)/2\).

The group weight \(\xi_g\) is the fraction of the local SH flux that the electrons of group \(g\) carry (the group edges and weights are plotted in Figure B). The SH flux has the weight \(\beta^4e^{-\beta}/24\) (\(\beta=E/(k_BT_e)\)) in energy, so \(\xi_g=P(\beta_g)-P(\beta_{g-1})\), with \(P(\beta)=1-e^{-\beta}(\beta^4+4\beta^3+12\beta^2+24\beta+24)/24\), a difference of an analytic cumulative distribution (no numerical quadrature is used). Here \(\beta_g=E_g/(k_BT_e)\) is the group edge \(E_g\) normalized by the local temperature of the cell. For the same \(E_g\), the colder the cell, the larger \(\beta_g\), and the weight concentrates in groups of lower energy. It is a different quantity from the Z-dependent correction \(\xi(\bar Z)\).

The transport field \(H_g\) is the field of the heat flux that the electrons of group \(g\) carry nonlocally (dimensionally a heat flux). It is obtained, for each group, by solving a diffusion-type equation (SNB section) whose source is the group's share of the local SH flux, \(U_g=\xi_gq_{SH}\). \(H_g\) is the distribution that results when the source spreads to its surroundings over the scale of the group's mean free path.

The group mean free paths \(\lambda_g^{abs}\) and \(\lambda_g^{tr}\) are the two lengths in the equation for \(H_g\). \(\lambda_g^{abs}\) (the superscript abs stands for absorption) sets, through the term \(H_g/\lambda_g^{abs}\), the distance over which \(H_g\) decays away from the source. \(\lambda_g^{tr}\) (tr stands for transport) sets, through the coefficient \(\lambda_g^{tr}/3\) of the diffusion term, how fast \(H_g\) spreads. Both equal the mean free path \(\lambda_g\) of electrons of the representative energy \(\varepsilon_g\) (formula in the SNB section, key snb_mfp). With snb_efield="local", however, only the transport side is shortened, by adding a local electrostatic-field term to \(1/\lambda_g^{tr}\).

The nonlocal correction \(\delta q\) is the correction of the heat flux built from the gradients of \(H_g\), \(\delta q=-\sum_g(\lambda_g^{tr}/3)\nabla H_g\). It is added to the local SH flux to give the flux before limiting, \(q_{raw}=q_{SH}+\delta q\). At points of steep gradient \(\delta q\) reduces the SH flux (flux inhibition), and ahead of them it carries heat (preheat).

The iSNB (implicit-SNB) Picard iteration is the iteration that couples the nonlocal correction in time (the method of Cao et al.). It fixes the correction \(\delta q\) and the limiting factor \(\theta\), redoes the whole STS superstep from \(T_e^n\), and recomputes the correction with the temperatures at the end of the interval. It repeats until the largest change of the correction is at most snb_picard_rtol (default 0.01) times the sum of the largest SH flux and the largest correction. The iteration runs at least 2 and at most snb_picard_max_iters (default 8, at least 2) times. At convergence the amplification factor lies in (0, 1) for every \(\Delta t\).

Face conductivity, per-material conservation, and voids

The face-κ policy for 1D (definition and formula in the terms section) uses, with the default kirchhoff_same_material, the Kirchhoff secant on smooth same-material Spitzer faces and only there. A naive two-point evaluation underestimates the face κ just ahead of a steep heat front, and this underestimate tends to pin the front to the grid; the secant reduces that tendency. Material interfaces, voids, and places where the Spitzer prefactor extracted from the cell conductivity (\(\kappa_c/T_c^{5/2}\)) takes a strong table-driven jump use the harmonic mean. harmonic remains selectable; it handles material interfaces and large conductivity jumps conservatively — because it is a harmonic mean, a zero conductivity on either side blocks the face. The SH estimate used by the flux limiter and the applied flux always use the same face κ. In 2D RZ there is no face κ: the heat flow between cells of different conductivity follows from the continuity of the heat flow across each half-edge, and no harmonic mean is used. face_kappa_policy only chooses whether each pair of the node-local operator is multiplied by the secant over its two cell temperatures.

The 1D planar, cylindrical, and spherical operators use a conservative finite-volume form; geometry appears only through face areas \(A_f\) and cell volumes \(V_i\). The per-material conservation mode (defined in the terms section; 2D RZ only) advances one electron temperature per cell with STS and then distributes the energy change among the materials. The cell conductivity is the sum Σmαmκeff,m of the limited conductivities κeff,m of the materials \(m\), each built from the material's own temperature, density, \(\bar Z\), and lnΛ and weighted by its volume fraction αm. After all stages, the electron-energy change of cell \(c\), \(\Delta E_c=(\rho c_vV)_c(T_c^{n+1}-T_c^n)\), is distributed by the following rule. For \(\Delta E_c>0\), the energy is shared in proportion to the materials' electron heat capacities at the step entry, so that every material's temperature rises by the same amount. For \(\Delta E_c<0\), it is shared in proportion to each material's electron energy above its state at the temperature floor (Numerics.floors.Te; definition). If the total excess is smaller than \(-\Delta E_c\), all materials go to their floor states and the shortfall is booked in the energy ledger as floor-injected energy. When the excess suffices, the change in the sum of the materials' electron energies equals the cell's change to round-off. With mixed_cell_closure="pressure_relaxation", conduction advances with cell coefficients that do not distinguish the materials, and the driver distributes the energy among the materials by the rule of the mixed-cell pressure relaxation.

A cell_is_void cell is forced to κeff=0, making its boundary an insulating, adiabatic surface. The mask is also needed for performance: if a floor-density void cell were treated as ordinary matter, the tiny ρcv denominator of the diffusivity κ/(ρcv) would produce an enormous diffusion coefficient, collapse the stability limit Δtexp, and effectively stall STS. SNB applies the same discipline: a void cell becomes an identity row with Hg=0 and the adjacent group diffusivities are zeroed, leaving no path for the group diffusion to carry preheat across a void.

ion_conduction=True (two-temperature runs, in 1D and 2D RZ) solves the ion temperature after the electron conduction with the Braginskii ion conductivity κi=3.906 niTiτi/mi, where τi is the ion collision time. In 1D it solves one backward-Euler tridiagonal system. Face κ is built with the same face_kappa_policy as for the electrons, and a harmonic flux limiter of the same form limits the face flux. In 2D RZ it puts the ion coefficients into the same node-local operator as the electrons and solves it with the same STS. The cell conductivity is limited by the same harmonic flux limiter with the cell's gradient magnitude of \(T_i\), and the Kirchhoff-secant setting (face_kappa_policy) is the same as for the electrons. In both cases the flux cap is ion_f_lim (default 1) times the ion free-streaming flux niTi(Ti/mi)1/2. The moved heat is booked in the ion internal energy and Ti is recomputed by the same re-closure as in the hydrodynamics, so energy is conserved to round-off. For Z=1, κi is about 1/(25√A) of the electron κSH, so ion conduction is off by default, but ions carry the heat where Ti≫Te, such as the center right after a shock converges. In 2D RZ, the time-step limit from the ions' explicit stability bound limits the global step alongside the electrons' limit.

Super-time-stepping

Because Spitzer conductivity scales as \(T_e^{5/2}\), marching the global step \(\Delta t\) in explicit substeps of size \(\Delta t_{exp}\) would require \(N_{sub}=\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\rceil\) CUDA kernel launches. In a representative GXII corona — the same \(T_e\approx2\) keV, \(\rho\approx10^{-3}\) g/cm³, \(\Delta l\approx50\,\mu\)m conditions quoted in the mechanism section — \(N_{sub}\) reaches 130–340. STS combines stages of different lengths to widen the stability interval and covers the same interval in 17–27 stages (\(s\approx\lceil\sqrt{2N_{sub}}\rceil\)). These terms are defined in the terms section. The procedure is as follows.

One conduction step, step by step

  1. Build every face κ. In 1D, evaluate face κ with kirchhoff_same_material on eligible smooth same-material Spitzer faces and the harmonic mean on interfaces, voids, and strong table jumps. In 2D RZ there is no face κ, and the node-local operator is assembled from the cell κeff. The conductivity that goes into the operator for each cell is κeff/Te5/2 at the entry temperature when the secant is used, and κeff otherwise.

  2. Apply the flux limiter to every face. In 1D, form the SH face estimate with the same face κ, evaluate \(q_{max}\), and fix \(\theta=1/(1+|q_{raw}|/q_{max})\). The product of θ and the face κ is the limited κeff used by the step. In 2D RZ, \(\kappa_{eff}=\theta\kappa_{SH}\) is formed per cell (previous section), and this is the limited conductivity.

  3. Find the explicit stability limit. In 1D, form \(D_{eff}=\kappa_{eff}/(\rho c_v)\) and take the most restrictive cell value (\(\Delta l_c\): the cell width):

    $$ \Delta t_{exp}=C_{cond}\min_c\frac{(\Delta l_c)^2}{D_{eff,c}},\qquad C_{cond}=0.25\ \text{(default)}. $$

    In 1D with the default Kirchhoff-secant face κ (face_kappa_policy), each cell also gives a second value, \(2(\rho c_vV)_c/\sum_fG_f\) (\(G_f=A_f\kappa_f/\Delta r_f\) is the conductance of one of the cell's two faces, \(\kappa_f\) the face κ before flux limiting, and \(\Delta r_f\) the distance between cell centers), and the smaller of the two values enters the minimum.

    In 2D RZ, \(\Delta t_{exp}=4C_{cond}/\lambda_G\), where \(\lambda_G\) is the Gershgorin bound of the operator that the stages apply.

    $$ \lambda_G=\max_c\frac{w_c\left|\sum_dG_{cd}+b_c\right|+\sum_dw_d\left|G_{cd}\right|}{(\rho c_vV)_c}. $$

    Here \(G_{cd}\) is the pair coefficient of cell \(c\) and its neighbor \(d\), and \(b_c\) is the sum of the conductances of the cell to the half-edges held at a fixed temperature (0 when no boundary is held). The sum over \(d\) leaves out void cells and cells with \(\rho c_v\le0\). The temperature weight \(w\) is \(w_c=T_{e,c}^{5/2}\) when the Kirchhoff secant is used (then \(G_{cd}\) is assembled from \(\kappa_{eff}/T_e^{5/2}\)) and \(w_c=1\) otherwise. The operator of the stages is the matrix \(M^{-1}K\) with \(M=\mathrm{diag}(\rho c_vV)\), \(K_{cd}=-G_{cd}\), and \(K_{cc}=\sum_dG_{cd}+b_c\). By the Gershgorin circle theorem, \(\lambda_G\) bounds every eigenvalue of \(M^{-1}K\), so \(\Delta t_{exp}\) guarantees by construction that \(\lambda\Delta t_{exp}\le4C_{cond}\) for every eigenvalue \(\lambda\). When the secant is used, the bound holds at the temperatures where it was computed, and every stage checks it again (step 6).

  4. Choose the stage count. The Chebyshev interval grows as \(O(s^2)\). Let \(\Delta t_{sup}\) be the interval that one superstep advances; the stage count is

    $$ s=\min\!\left(s_{max},\ \max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\sqrt{2\Delta t_{sup}/\Delta t_{exp}}\right\rceil\right)\right). $$

    Here \(s_{max}\) is the stage ceiling (sts_max_stages, default 40). When \(\Delta t\) exceeds \(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\), it is divided into \(n_{sup}=\lceil\Delta t/(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2)\rceil\) equal supersteps with \(\Delta t_{sup}=\Delta t/n_{sup}\), where \(\eta\) is sts_subcycle_eta (default 0.9). Otherwise \(n_{sup}=1\) and \(\Delta t_{sup}=\Delta t\). The stage count and the division for the RKL2 option of sts_scheme in 2D RZ are given in the sts_scheme row of the key table.

  5. Build the stage lengths. With the default damping ν=0.01 (the Chebyshev damping parameter, sts_damping — distinct from the collision frequency \(\nu_{ei}\) of the mechanism section and the ion-acoustic damping rate \(\nu_a\) of the CBET page), construct

    $$ \tau_j=\frac{\Delta t_{exp}} {\nu^2+(1-\nu^2)\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(2j-1)}{4s+2}\right)}, \quad j=1,\ldots,s, $$

    then rescale all τj uniformly so the stage lengths sum to the superstep, \(\sum_j\tau_j=\Delta t_{sup}\). With the shipped defaults the sum before rescaling exceeds \(\Delta t_{sup}\), so this rescale shrinks the τj.

  6. Apply all stages with fixed coefficients. \(D_{eff}\), κeff (the face κ in 1D), and ρcv stay at their entry-\(T_e^n\) values, and only the temperature changes between stages. The 2D RZ Kirchhoff secant alone is evaluated at the stage temperature. Here \(\mathcal M_{1D}\) denotes the conservative 1D face-flux divergence operator at the fixed coefficients:

    $$ T_{e,j}=T_{e,j-1}+\frac{\tau_j}{\rho c_v}\mathcal M_{1D}(T_{e,j-1}),\qquad T_{e,c,j}=T_{e,c,j-1}+\frac{\tau_j}{(\rho c_vV)_c}\sum_dG_{cd}S_{cd}\,(T_{e,d,j-1}-T_{e,c,j-1}). $$

    \(G_{cd}=-(a_{cd}+a_{dc})/2\) is the pair coefficient built from the matrix \(a\) of the node-local operator (\(G_{cd}=G_{dc}\)), and \(d\) runs over the neighbors that share a node with \(c\) (void cells and cells with \(\rho c_v\le0\) are left out). \(S_{cd}\) is the \(T^{5/2}\) secant over the two cell temperatures, \(\tfrac27(T_d^{7/2}-T_c^{7/2})/(T_d-T_c)\), and \(S_{cd}=1\) for face_kappa_policy="harmonic". In 1D, each face heat flow enters the two adjacent cells with opposite signs. In 2D RZ, the pair power \(G_{cd}S_{cd}(T_d-T_c)\) likewise differs between \(c\) and \(d\) only in sign. Both updates are therefore conservative (unless a temperature-floor clamp intervenes). The per-material conservation mode applies the same update to the fixed cell coefficients.

    When the secant is used in 2D RZ, the spectrum of the operator changes between stages. Each stage therefore recomputes the row bound \(\lambda_G\) at its input temperatures and checks that it does not exceed \(1+\)sts_bound_margin times (default 1.1 times) the value the plan used. If a stage exceeds it, the step restarts from \(T_e^n\) on a replanned set of stages. The first attempt runs all stages to the end to measure the largest bound and replans for \(1+\)sts_bound_margin times that value. Later attempts stop at the stage that exceeds the plan and double the plan. At most sts_bound_retries_max restarts are made (default 4). If the plan is still exceeded, the step asks the driver for a full-step retry (the outer step is redone with the time step halved). When the retry is disabled (the default of Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled), or the retry attempt limit (driver_full_step_retry_max_attempts, default 3) or the time-step floor is reached, the run stops. The SNB path uses no secant and has no such check.

  7. Re-close once. After all stages, the electron-energy change that corresponds to the temperature change, \(c_{v,e}(T_e^{n+1}-T_e^n)\) (with \(c_{v,e}\) at the step entry), is booked in ee, and for materials with an EOS table Te, Pe, and Cv,e are recomputed from \((\rho,e_e)\). For ideal-gas materials without a table, ee and Pe are computed from the updated temperature. This is the default Numerics.hydro.eos_closure_mode="energy_authoritative"; with "legacy", materials with a table also get ee and Pe from the updated temperature.

SNB multigroup nonlocal model

nonlocal_model="snb" adds the opt-in Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet correction to electron conduction in 1D planar, cylindrical, or spherical geometry and in 2D RZ. The tridiagonal discretization below is the 1D one. In 2D RZ, each group's equation is assembled with the node-local operator (with \(\lambda_g^{tr}/3\) as the conductivity of the diffusion term and \(V/\lambda_g^{abs}\) added to the diagonal), and all groups are solved together with a Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate-gradient method; snb_efield="local" is rejected in 2D RZ. Each conduction step rebuilds the group edges in the normalized electron energy β̂=E/(kB max Te). The lowest group is [0, 0.1], and the edges above it divide the range from 0.1 to snb_E_max_over_Te geometrically (24 groups by default, 23 of them geometric intervals). The group edges are laid out in the global \(\hat\beta\), then mapped to physical energies \(E_g\) and held there; when the weights \(\xi_g\) are evaluated, the edges are reread in each cell's local-temperature \(\beta_g=E_g/(k_BT_e)\) to evaluate \(P\) (1D faces use the face-averaged \(T_e\), and in 2D RZ each pair of cells uses the pair-averaged \(T_e\)). Each group then solves the following diffusion-type equation for its transport field \(H_g\) (dimensionally a heat flux). The source on the right-hand side is the divergence of \(U_g\), the group's share of the local SH flux; \(H_g\) is that injected source spread out over the scale of the mean free path, and its gradient rebuilds the nonlocal correction defined below:

$$ \frac{H_g}{\lambda_g^{abs}}- \nabla\!\cdot\!\left(\frac{\lambda_g^{tr}}{3}\nabla H_g\right) =-\nabla\!\cdot U_g,\qquad U_g=\xi_g q_{SH}, $$ $$ \xi_g=P(\beta_g)-P(\beta_{g-1}),\qquad P(\beta)=1-\frac{e^{-\beta}}{24} (\beta^4+4\beta^3+12\beta^2+24\beta+24). $$

Here \(P(\beta)\) is the cumulative distribution of the Spitzer–Härm heat flux over normalized electron energy — the flux carries a \(\beta^4e^{-\beta}/24\) spectral weight, and \(P\) is its running integral — so the weight ξg is an exact difference of the antiderivative, not a numerical quadrature. The total correction is \(\delta q=-\sum_g(\lambda_g^{tr}/3)\nabla H_g\). In 1D, the cell-centered finite-volume equation for Hg, integrated over the cell volume, is a strictly diagonally dominant tridiagonal system, and all groups are solved as a strided batch. The face diffusivity is the harmonic mean of the neighboring λtr values divided by three, and closed boundaries impose a reflecting condition.

The representative energy \(\varepsilon_g=(E_{g-1}+E_g)/2\) is evaluated in erg. The group mean free path \(\lambda_g\) has two variants. Both \(\lambda_g^{abs}\) and \(\lambda_g^{tr}\) equal \(\lambda_g\) unless the E-field option below shortens the transport one:

$$ \lambda_g= \begin{cases} \dfrac{2\sqrt2\,\varepsilon_g^2} {4\pi n_e e^4\ln\Lambda\,\sqrt{\bar Z\varphi}}, & \texttt{geometric_r2}\quad(\text{Sherlock 2017; default}),\\[6pt] \dfrac{2\,\varepsilon_g^2} {4\pi n_e e^4\ln\Lambda\,\sqrt{\bar Z+1}}, & \texttt{original}\quad(\text{Schurtz 2000}), \end{cases} \qquad \varphi=\frac{\bar Z+4.2}{\bar Z+0.24}. $$

Normally λabs=λtr. snb_efield="local" shortens only the transport-side mean free path through \(1/\lambda_g^{tr}\mathrel{+}=|e\mathcal E|/\varepsilon_g\), where \(\mathcal E\) (script, to avoid clashing with the energies \(E\)) is the local electrostatic field magnitude evaluated from the plasma state; the option defaults to none because the r=2 calibration already incorporates electric-field suppression, so adding it can double count the effect.

The SNB correction, step by step

  1. Rebuild the groups. At every conduction step, use max \(T_e\) to reconstruct the global β̂ group edges (the lowest group is [0, 0.1], and the edges above it form a geometric progression from 0.1 to snb_E_max_over_Te) and form the weights ξg as exact differences of the antiderivative P.

  2. Evaluate mean free paths. Compute each λg from the local electron density, Coulomb logarithm, charge state, and group-center energy, using geometric_r2 by default or the original Schurtz form.

  3. Assemble. In 1D, for every group, volume-integrate the cell-centered finite-volume equation into a strictly diagonally dominant tridiagonal system. Reflecting boundaries close the domain; voids receive identity rows and zero adjacent diffusivity. In 2D RZ, each group's operator is assembled with the node-local operator, and \(V/\lambda_g^{abs}\) is added to the diagonal. The boundaries are reflecting, and the rows of void cells, of cells with \(\rho c_v\le0\), and of cells without a mean free path are identity rows (\(H_g=0\)).

  4. Solve all groups. In 1D, run the groups together as a strided-batch tridiagonal solve for \(H_g\). In 2D RZ, solve all groups together with a Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate-gradient method. The relative-residual tolerance is \(10^{-10}\) and the iteration limit is 1000; both are fixed values without keys. If the limit is reached without convergence, a warning is issued and the last iterate is accepted.

  5. Reconstruct the correction. Accumulate groups in fixed order:

    $$ \delta q=-\sum_g\frac{\lambda_g^{tr}}{3}\nabla H_g. $$

  6. Compose the raw flux. In 1D, form \(q_{raw}=q_{SH}+\delta q\) using the same face κ as the local SH path. In 2D RZ, the node-local operator that builds the base SH flux uses neither a flux limit nor a secant (it uses \(\kappa_{SH}\)), and the pair power of the nonlocal correction is added to its pair power.

  7. Apply the shared flux limiter. Evaluate \(\theta=1/(1+|q_{raw}|/q_{max})\) once and apply \(q=\theta q_{raw}\); SNB adds no second flux limiter. In 2D RZ, \(\theta_c\) is formed per cell (\(q_{raw}\) is \(-\kappa_{SH}\nabla T_e-\sum_g(\lambda_g^{tr}/3)\nabla H_g\), evaluated from the mean of the cell's node gradients), and the pair power of cells \(c\) and \(d\) is multiplied by \(\min(\theta_c,\theta_d)\).

  8. Close the iSNB Picard loop. Fix (θ, δq), repeat the full STS superstep from \(T_e^n\), and recompute the correction at the endpoint. Run at least two iterations and test the relative flux max norm against snb_picard_rtol, up to snb_picard_max_iters. Nonconvergence is reported and the final iterate is accepted; at convergence the amplification factor is in (0,1) for every Δt.

With SNB, f_lim is set to a loose 0.5. SNB itself computes how strongly the flux is suppressed, so this value does not tune the nonlocal strength; the loose flux limiter acts only when the composite flux, with the nonlocal correction added, becomes anomalously large. The GUI (Studio) switches to this value when SNB is selected, but the solver default stays 0.06 with SNB, so a hand-written deck sets 0.5 explicitly.

Verification

  • Agreement with SNB switched off: with nonlocal_model="none", the six FLD metrics of the GXII run stay bit-identical (relative difference 0) to the run of the local model before SNB was added.
  • SNB local limit (snb_local_limit_1d): SNB reduces to local SH as the mean free path λ→0. This is tested with the flux-reduction ratio for a sinusoidal temperature perturbation (the dispersion response) \(R(k)=\sum_g\xi_g/(1+\lambda_g^{abs}\lambda_g^{tr}k^2/3)\), and the discrete operator's \(R_{disc}\) is held to ∣R−Rdisc∣≤5.5×10−8 at tolerance 10−3. The small-k asymptote \((1-R)\propto k^2\) is also checked, with a log–log slope of 1.986 (≈2). For two tanh temperature ramps whose widths differ by a factor of 10, the relative departures of the SNB flux from the SH flux each stay within the derived face-local curvature bound, and the ratio of the two departures, 100.7, matches the quadratic-law expectation of the local limit (≈100).
  • SNB comparison with the analytic solution (snb_dispersion_1d): SNB matches the discrete Epperlein–Short solution within 7.2×10−7 (tolerance 3×10−3; the dispersion check covers the band \(k\lambda_{ei}<0.5\)).
  • SNB conservation (snb_conservation_1d_planar, snb_conservation_1d_spherical, snb_conservation_1d_cylindrical): the energy budgets of the planar, cylindrical, and spherical cases close to 7.4×10−17 against a tolerance of 10−14.
  • SNB maximum principle (snb_max_principle_1d): on a spherical hot spot with a dilute receiving band, every step keeps the largest electron temperature at or below its value on entry; the energy closes to 10−13 over the run with the energy removed by the clamp to the entry maximum temperature included; and a control run without the dilute band shows that this clamp never fires.
  • STS comparisons: 1D zero-flux eigenmodes with analytic solutions test the spherical, planar, and cylindrical STS computations against the corresponding implicit and per-material computations, checking the spatial order of accuracy and closed-domain electron-energy conservation. In addition, test_conduction_pattle_1d checks the propagation of the \(T^{5/2}\) heat front against the Pattle nonlinear-diffusion similarity solution [4].
  • GXII reference solution: with conduction active, an integrated regression test checks absorbed energy, bang time, minimum shell radius, peak density, areal density, and central temperature.
  • Kirchhoff-secant checks: on steep same-material faces, four checks run: the ratio of heat flow and time step between the Kirchhoff secant and the harmonic mean, the bound \(|q|\le q_{max}\) while the flux limiter is active, the time-step protection given by a Gershgorin-disc diagonal-dominance check of the operator with fixed coefficients (zero-volume cells excluded), and the correct fallback to the harmonic mean at material interfaces.
