Radiation: multigroup FLD reference

Multigroup flux-limited diffusion (FLD) is one of TENRYU's production deterministic radiation solvers. It partitions photon energy into groups, couples the "stiff" emission and absorption implicitly to electron matter through Fleck linearization, and solves conservative finite-volume systems in 1D (spherical, cylindrical, or planar measures) or 2D axisymmetric RZ geometry. It recovers diffusion in optically thick regions while a flux limiter bounds transport in thin regions. Terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • Normalized Planck fractions \(b_g(T_e)\) and the Fleck factor \(f\) close group emission and the matter-energy update with one linearization.
  • The default Levermore–Pomraning limiter [2] controls \(D_g=c\lambda_g/\sigma_{R,g}\) from the local radiation gradient.
  • 1D uses a batched tridiagonal solve. 2D RZ assembles, for every group, a node-local operator in which each cell couples to the cells that share a node with it, stores it as a matrix in CSR (compressed sparse row) format, and solves it with preconditioned conjugate gradients (CG). The outer iteration uses multigroup–gray synthetic acceleration (gray acceleration) by default in 1D. In both 1D and 2D RZ the flux limiter is by default evaluated from the first iterate's radiation field and then held fixed within the step.
  • Marshak incident flux, vacuum escape, and matter deposition are tallied from the same face and source terms, so the balance of the energy ledger (its items are defined in the terms section) closes. In a closed balance the group sum telescopes to machine precision.

The physics: why flux-limited diffusion

In an ICF (inertial-confinement-fusion) or HED (high-energy-density) plasma, photons emitted by hot matter carry energy across the target. TENRYU evolves the group-integrated radiation energy density \(E_g\) in every cell and couples it to electron internal energy. That exchange is often the stiffest process in the calculation — "stiff" meaning its intrinsic timescale is far shorter than the hydrodynamic time step: thermal emission scales as \(T_e^4\) and soars with temperature, while absorption can act over a photon mean free path much shorter than a mesh cell.

When the photon mean free path \(1/\sigma_R\) is short compared with the radiation-gradient scale, repeated scattering and reabsorption erase directional memory: the angular intensity is nearly isotropic and the first angular moment (the flux) closes as Fick's law, \(\mathbf F_g=-D_g\nabla E_g\), with \(D_g=c\lambda(R)/\sigma_{R,g}\). The Levermore–Pomraning flux limiter [2] guards the regions where that ordering fails. Physically, radiation can never carry more flux than its energy density moving at the speed of light, \(cE_g\); the limiter returns \(\lambda\to1/3\) in the diffusion limit, recovering ordinary diffusion, and lowers \(\lambda\) in a steep gradient so that \(|\mathbf F_g|\le cE_g\) always holds. FLD therefore transitions toward free streaming without violating the causal bound — but it does not retain angular information; beams, shadows, and directionally illuminated gaps belong to the companion \(S_N\) solver (discrete ordinates).

During one hydro step a cell may emit and reabsorb its thermal content many times. The stiffness ratio \(\beta=4a_{\rm eV}T_e^3/C_{v,e}\) is the temperature derivative of the equilibrium radiation energy density divided by the electron heat capacity. Treating the \(T_e^4\) source explicitly would make the step prohibitively small, so TENRYU uses the Fleck linearization [1] \(f=(1+\alpha\beta c\Delta t\,\sigma_a)^{-1}\), where \(\alpha\) is the standard Fleck implicitness parameter (fixed at its fully implicit value 1.0 in production): the fraction \(f\) is new within-step thermal emission, while \(1-f\) is represented as within-step effective scattering, a scattering-like re-emission. Small \(f\) marks the stiff regime, and the linearization removes the need for a nonlinear matter iteration inside each group solve. The stiffness ratio, the Fleck factor, and effective scattering are defined in the terms section.

The model is multigroup because opacity and emission can vary by orders of magnitude across photon energy; a gray average can misrepresent both Marshak-wave [7] penetration and the emitted spectrum. Coefficients may come from LTE (local-thermodynamic-equilibrium) or table/NLTE (non-LTE) paths. Like TENRYU's other production radiation solver, FLD is a deterministic static-medium transport approximation. Of the material-motion \(O(v/c)\) terms it omits the Doppler shift between groups, aberration, and velocity-dependent opacity (the repository's docs/NUMERICS.md §0.5). Radiation pressure and compression work enter separately, as the coupling to the fluid. In 1D the default Radiation.multigroup_diffusion.hydro_coupling="gamma_r_43" adds the isotropic radiation pressure \(p_r=\sum_gE_g/3\) to the fluid force and subtracts from the radiation energy the work that this force does on the nodes in each hydro half-step (the ratios of the groups are kept). "none" includes neither force nor work and holds the radiation energy density fixed while the mesh moves. In 2D RZ hydro_coupling resolves to "none" (validation rejects an explicit "gamma_r_43"), so neither a radiation force nor compression work enters.

Group structure and Fleck linearization

For bounds \(\epsilon_0<\epsilon_1<\cdots<\epsilon_G\), \(E_{c,g}\) is the radiation energy density in cell c integrated over \([\epsilon_g,\epsilon_{g+1})\). The normalized Planck fraction at temperature \(T\) is \(b_g(T)\), with \(\sum_g b_g=1\). Equilibrium initialization is therefore \(E_{c,g}=a_{\rm eV}T^4b_g(T)\), whose group sum is \(a_{\rm eV}T^4\); here \(a_{\rm eV}=1.3720\times10^{2}\) erg cm⁻³ eV⁻⁴ is the radiation constant expressed in eV units. Bounds are specified in eV; TENRYU's internal units remain cgs + eV.

When the bounds are generated with Radiation.group_bounds_eV="log_uniform", TENRYU spans \(G\) logarithmically equal bins over the photon-energy window compute_T_range_eV \(=[T_{min},T_{max}]\). Logarithmic spacing is used because the Planck peak moves in proportion to temperature (\(h\nu\approx2.82\,k_BT\)) and opacities vary by orders of magnitude across photon energy, so geometric bounds give a roughly constant relative resolution over the whole expected temperature range. The blackbody tail above the window's upper edge falls outside the group structure (the normalized \(b_g\) still sum to one, so conservation is not broken); give the range generous margins below the coolest and above the hottest temperatures the run will reach.

The material response is built from the electron constant-volume heat capacity and the stiffness ratio,

$$ C_{v,e}=\rho\left(\frac{\partial e_e}{\partial T_e}\right)_\rho, \qquad \beta=\frac{4a_{\rm eV}T_e^3}{C_{v,e}}. $$

FLD and SN share the primitives of this material linearization. The standard Fleck factor is \(f=(1+\alpha\beta c\Delta t\,\sigma_{a,P})^{-1}\), with \(\alpha\) the Fleck implicitness parameter (1.0 in production). The current constant-opacity FLD path resolves it by group as \(f_{c,g}=(1+\alpha\beta_c c\Delta t\,\sigma_{a,c,g})^{-1}\) (this \(\sigma_{a,c,g}\) is the same quantity as the absorption term \(\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}\) of the group diffusion equation below); the table/NLTE path uses an emission-mean coefficient. A smaller \(f\) linearizes more of the within-step emission and reabsorption as effective scattering, reducing the explicit temperature response. FLD and SN keep \(f\) unbounded below and do not limit the time step through it (Numerics.dt.f_min_fleck is accepted with a warning and ignored; see Time-step control & CFL).

Each group's backward-Euler finite-volume equation uses that same linearized source (the whole flow is in Figure A):

$$ \begin{aligned} V_cE^{n+1}_{c,g} &+\Delta t\,c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}V_cE^{n+1}_{c,g} -\Delta t\sum_{f\in\partial c} A_fD_{f,g}\frac{E^{n+1}_{n(f),g}-E^{n+1}_{c,g}}{d_f} \\ &=V_cE^n_{c,g} +\Delta t\,V_cf_{c,g}\eta_{c,g} +\Delta t\,V_c(1-f_{c,g})c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}E^n_{c,g}, \end{aligned} $$ $$ \eta_{c,g}=c\sigma^{PE}_{c,g}a_{\rm eV}(T_e^n)^4b_g(T_e^n). $$

Here \(\sigma^{PA}\) is the Planck absorption coefficient and \(\sigma^{PE}\) the Planck emission coefficient, the group coefficients used for absorption and emission. The right-hand terms \(f\eta_g\) and \((1-f)c\sigma^{PA}E_g^n\) are the linearized thermal emission and the re-emission by effective scattering within the step. The sum over faces in the equation is the 1D form. In 2D RZ it is replaced by the node-local operator (Flux limiter and linear solvers), and the coupling to matter takes the Newton form described in the outer-iteration section.

  • \(b_g(T)\) is the normalized Planck fraction: the share of blackbody energy and thermal emission in photon-energy group \(g\), with \(\sum_g b_g=1\).
  • \(\beta=4a_{\rm eV}T_e^3/C_{v,e}\) is the stiffness ratio: the temperature derivative of equilibrium radiation energy density divided by the electron volumetric heat capacity.
  • \(f_{c,g}\) is the Fleck factor. The term \((1-f_{c,g})c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}E^n_{c,g}\) returns within-step absorption as re-emission by effective scattering rather than as a new thermal source.
  • \(\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}\) is the absorption coefficient used for removal and deposition, whereas \(\sigma^{PE}_{c,g}\) sets the group emission through \(\eta_{c,g}\). They coincide on LTE paths but may differ for NLTE tables.
  • \(\sigma_{R,c,g}\) is the in-group Rosseland-mean length opacity [1/cm]. It enters the denominators of the diffusion coefficient and of the limiter argument, and its averaging differs from that of the absorption \(\sigma^{PA}\).
  • \(D_{f,g}\) is the diffusion coefficient on face \(f\) in 1D. It is evaluated from face-centered \(E\) and a face \(\sigma_R\) equal to the optical depth between the two cell centers divided by their distance (the series resistance of the two half cells). Placing the coefficient on the face is the same as in Turner and Stone [4]. One coefficient is shared by both cells. In the flux term, \(A_f\) is the face area, \(d_f\) the distance between the two adjacent cell centers, and \(n(f)\) the neighbor cell across face \(f\).
  • \(R=|\nabla E_g|/(\sigma_{R,g}E_g)\) compares the photon mean free path with the radiation-energy gradient length. \(R\ll1\) is diffusive; \(R\gg1\) signals the free-streaming limit where the limiter becomes active.

Terms used in this calculation

This section defines the words of the FLD description that carry a meaning specific to this calculation: the linearization of the exchange with matter (Fleck factor, stiffness ratio, effective scattering), the outer iteration and its acceleration (gray acceleration, Anderson mixing, limiter evaluation), the items of the energy ledger, and the node-local operator and aggregation multigrid of 2D RZ.

Fleck factor, stiffness ratio, and effective scattering

The stiffness ratio \(\beta\) is the temperature derivative \(4a_{\rm eV}T_e^3\) of the equilibrium radiation energy density \(a_{\rm eV}T_e^4\) (the radiation's volumetric heat capacity) divided by the electron volumetric heat capacity \(C_{v,e}\). The product \(\beta c\sigma\) is the rate at which the matter temperature relaxes toward equilibrium when the radiation field is held fixed.

The Fleck factor is \(f=1/(1+z)\) with \(z=\alpha\beta c\Delta t\,\sigma\), where \(\sigma\) is the absorption coefficient and \(\alpha\), the Fleck implicitness parameter, is fixed at 1.0. The quantity \(z\) is the time step in units of the relaxation time. The factor satisfies \(0<f\le1\), with \(f\to1\) for \(z\ll1\); in the stiff regime \(z\gg1\) it approaches 0 as \(f\simeq1/z\). The factor \(f\) is the fraction of the radiation absorbed during the time step that actually changes the matter energy. Linearizing the \(T_e^4\) emission lets the emission and absorption be treated implicitly without a nonlinear matter iteration inside the group solves.

Effective scattering is the remaining fraction \(1-f\) of the absorption. It exchanges no net energy with matter and returns, as in scattering, to the radiation field of the same cell and group. In the equations, the full absorption \(c\sigma^{PA}E^{n+1}\) enters the removal term on the left, and the re-emission source \((1-f)c\sigma^{PA}E^n\) is added on the right. In the local, diffusion-free limit with \(f\to0\) the two cancel and \(E^{n+1}=E^n\), so this source cannot amplify the radiation energy. Effective scattering is a device of the implicit time discretization, not physical scattering. FLD has no physical scattering, and validation rejects a material whose opacity.kappa_s is above zero.

The numbers and defaults are as follows. \(\alpha\) is fixed at 1.0 and has no key. The default of fleck_mode is "fleck_cummings"; "afi" (almost fully implicit [5]) does not use the Fleck factor, and the outer iteration converges the fully implicit emission instead (max_outer_iterations of 40 or more is recommended). The default "table" of fleck_cv_source takes \(C_{v,e}\) from the electron equation-of-state (EOS) table when one exists, which is the heat capacity that the matter update uses (without a table, the earlier chain applies); "legacy" always uses the earlier chain: the cell's heat-capacity override, the state's heat capacity, and the ideal-gas heat capacity, in this order. fleck_beta (default "tangent"; also "secant" and "guard") and fleck_form (default "be", \(f=1/(1+z)\); "exp_phi1" gives \(f=(1-e^{-z})/z\)) select how the stiffness ratio and the Fleck factor are formed. source_integrator="exp_rosenbrock" does not use the Fleck factor and splits the exchange between matter and radiation instead. All of these keys are read by 1D only, and in 2D RZ validation rejects any value other than the default.

The factor \(f\) enters the diagonal and right-hand side of each group solve and the matter-energy update. Even where \(f\) is small and the regime stiff, the small time step that an explicit treatment would need is not required. In 2D RZ, \(f\) is formed from the coefficients of the Newton iteration that linearizes the coupling to matter (outer-iteration section).

Outer iteration, gray acceleration, and Anderson mixing

The outer iteration solves one step repeatedly, re-evaluating the coefficients that depend on the electron temperature \(T_e\) (\(b_g\), the opacities, \(f\), and the emission \(\eta_{c,g}\)) from the \(T_e\) obtained in the group solves and the matter update. The group system needs coefficients that depend on \(T_e\), and \(T_e\) in turn follows from the group solution, so the iteration seeks a self-consistent \(T_e\). It converges when the largest relative change of the electron temperature between successive iterates falls below outer_tol (default 1e-5), and max_outer_iterations (default 20) caps it (step 5; see the long arrow of Figure A).

Gray acceleration is the 1D default acceleration. "Gray" refers to a single-group quantity obtained by summing over all groups. The groups couple through matter, so the convergence of the plain outer iteration is set by the slowest-decaying mode of the group errors, the slowest mode of an infinite medium. The error of group \(g\) is therefore taken proportional to the spectrum of that mode:

$$ e_g=\xi_g\,\varepsilon, \qquad \xi_g\propto\frac{w_g}{1+\Delta t\,c\sigma^{PA}_g}, \qquad \sum_gc\sigma^{PA}_g\,\xi_g=1. $$

Here \(e_g\) is the error of the radiation energy in group \(g\), \(\varepsilon\) is its amplitude, and \(w_g\) is the change of the source of group \(g\) per change of temperature, divided by the total response including the matter heat capacity. Summing over the groups gives one tridiagonal equation for \(\varepsilon\), the gray diffusion equation. Its solution corrects the temperature used in the next iteration (the multigroup–gray synthetic acceleration of Morel, Larsen, and Matzen [8]). The correction vanishes with the change between iterates, so the converged solution is that of the unaccelerated iteration. The key is outer_accel; its default "auto" resolves to "grey" in 1D and to "none" in 2D RZ. The value "grey" exists in 1D only, and validation rejects it in 2D RZ.

Anderson mixing chooses the linearization temperature of the next iteration from the history of the last iterations. Let \(\mathbf u_k\) be the vector of the cell linearization temperatures and \(\mathbf q_k=\Phi(\mathbf u_k)-\mathbf u_k\) the residual of one outer iteration, where \(\Phi\) maps a linearization temperature to the temperature that the matter update returns. The mixing is (Walker–Ni form [10])

$$ \mathbf u_{k+1}=\mathbf u_k+\beta_{\rm A}\mathbf q_k-\sum_{j=1}^{m_k}\gamma_j\left(\Delta\mathbf u_j+\beta_{\rm A}\Delta\mathbf q_j\right). $$

Here \(\Delta\mathbf u_j\) and \(\Delta\mathbf q_j\) are differences between successive iterates, \(m_k\le m\) is the number of history entries used, and \(\gamma_j\) is the least-squares solution (with Tikhonov regularization) for the last \(m_k\) residual differences. Mixing is applied only when the iteration continues without having converged. The state of an iteration that exits converged is the unmixed raw output, so the accepted solution meets the same outer_tol as it does without acceleration. A degenerate least-squares problem is not mixed, mixed temperatures are held at or above the temperature floor (definition) Te_floor_eV, and a cell with a non-finite value falls back to the raw output. The depth \(m\) is anderson_m (default 2, range 1–4), and the damping \(\beta_{\rm A}\) is anderson_beta (default 1.0, greater than 0 and at most 1). The method serves cases in which the plain iteration can fail to converge by alternating between two values, as in a cold cell where the Planck-mean opacity depends steeply on temperature and \(f(T)\) changes abruptly. In 2D RZ the default is no acceleration, and outer_accel="anderson" can be chosen in 1D and 2D RZ alike.

Limiter evaluation is the choice of the radiation field from which the limiter argument \(R\) is formed. The default "predictor" of limiter_evaluation evaluates \(R\) from the radiation field of the first outer iteration (the groups solved with the limiter evaluated from the step-start field and with the emission at the step-start temperature) and holds it fixed for the step. The outer iteration then converges only the coupling between matter and radiation. "iterate" re-evaluates \(R\) from the radiation field of every iterate; that lagged fixed-point iteration tends to stall in optically thin, streaming-dominated regions. The option applies to 1D and 2D RZ alike.

The four items of the energy ledger

The energy ledger is the book that records, item by item, the energy exchanged between radiation and matter and the energy that crosses the boundaries. FLD forms the following four items at every step. All are in erg.

  • rad_dep (deposition) is the energy that matter absorbs from the radiation field. Per cell and group it is \(\Delta t\,V_c\,c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}E^{n+1}_{c,g}\) in 1D.
  • rad_emit (emission) is the energy that matter emits into the radiation field, as actually used for the source of the radiation equation. Per cell and group it is \(S^{\rm used}_{c,g}\) of step 3 in 1D.
  • marshak_in is the energy that enters the domain through a Marshak face. It is the product of the incident flux, the face area, and the time step, summed over the groups and collected face by face. History carries energy/marshak_in (cumulative) and energy/marshak_in_step.
  • escaped (escape) is the energy that leaves the domain through vacuum and Marshak faces. History carries it as energy/radiation_escaped.

These four items close the energy balance of radiation and matter together. The change of the radiation energy over a step equals

$$ \sum_{c,g}V_c\left(E^{n+1}_{c,g}-E^{n}_{c,g}\right) =\sum_{c,g}\left(\mathrm{rad\_emit}-\mathrm{rad\_dep}\right)_{c,g}+\mathrm{marshak\_in}-\mathrm{escaped}. $$

With a volume source (Radiation.volume_source_rate), the energy it injects (energy/E_volume_in in history) is added to the right-hand side. The change of the matter energy is \(\sum(\mathrm{rad\_dep}-\mathrm{rad\_emit})\), so the sum of radiation and matter changes only by what crosses the boundaries. Because the contribution of an interior face (1D) cancels exactly between its two cells and that of a node (2D RZ) among the cells around it, the equality holds up to the residual of the linear solve and floating-point rounding. rad_dep and rad_emit are state arrays and are written to the checkpoint as radiation/rad_dep and radiation/rad_emit. In 2D RZ, quantities of the same meaning are formed from the source that the last solve used.

Node-local operator and aggregation multigrid

The node-local operator is the discretization of the 2D RZ diffusion term \(-\nabla\cdot(D\nabla E)\) as a matrix that couples cell-centered values \(E_c\). It is built as in the 2D operator of electron conduction (Heat conduction: spatial discretization), with the diffusion coefficient \(D_{c,p,g}\) in place of the conductivity. For each mesh node \(p\), two objects are defined.

  • A corner (Figure B, left) is the part of a cell \(c\) around node \(p\): the quadrilateral formed by the cell point (the centroid, for a convex cell), the midpoint of the edge before \(p\), \(p\) itself, and the midpoint of the edge after \(p\). A corner of non-positive area does not contribute to the operator.
  • A half-edge is the half of an edge leaving \(p\) that lies on the \(p\) side, after the edge is split at its midpoint. In RZ it is a surface of revolution. An unknown radiation energy density \(\theta\) is placed on each half-edge.

Inside a corner the Green–Gauss formula gives the gradient, and the outward flux through the two half-edges of the corner is a linear function of the differences between \(\theta\) and the cell value \(E_c\):

$$ \nabla E=\frac{J\,(\boldsymbol\theta-E_c\mathbf 1)}{V_{\rm corner}}, \qquad \mathbf Q=-S\,(\boldsymbol\theta-E_c\mathbf 1), \qquad S=\frac{D\,J^{T}J}{V_{\rm corner}}. $$

Here \(\boldsymbol\theta\) is the vector of the \(\theta\) of the two half-edges of the corner, \(\mathbf 1\) is the vector whose entries are all 1, \(J=[w_-\mathbf N_-,\,w_+\mathbf N_+]\) with \(\mathbf N_\pm\) the outward normals of the half-edges multiplied by their lengths, \(w=2\pi r\) (\(r\) is the radius of the half-edge midpoint), and \(V_{\rm corner}=2\pi\int r\,dA\) is the volume of revolution of the corner. On an interior half-edge the outward fluxes of the two adjacent corners sum to zero (flux continuity). A boundary half-edge carries zero flux (reflecting) or a transfer coefficient of \(c/2\) (vacuum) or \(c/4\) (Marshak), with an outward flux equal to the coefficient times \(\theta\) (a Marshak edge also subtracts the incident flux \(F_{{\rm inc},g}\)); on a state-supply side \(\theta\) is held at the supply's radiation energy density. Solving the simultaneous equations of the half-edges of a node for \(\theta\) and eliminating it leaves the node's contribution \(a^{(p)}\) to the cell values, a \(k\times k\) matrix with \(k\) the number of cells around the node. It is a symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix whose rows and columns sum to zero. The full matrix is the sum of the node contributions, so row \(c\) couples \(c\) to the cells that share a node with it. Inside a logically rectangular lattice this gives nine entries: the cell itself and its eight neighbors.

Unlike a five-point stencil, which couples a cell only to the four cells across its faces (Figure B, right, compares the cells they couple), this operator forms consistent gradients and fluxes in the same way on cells whose edges do not follow the coordinate axes and on meshes assembled from several blocks. The operator is symmetric positive semidefinite, and the matrix with the absorption term added is symmetric positive definite, so CG applies. Because the rows and columns sum to zero, the interior contributions cancel and the energy is conserved. The group blocks share one nonzero pattern and differ in values.

The aggregation multigrid is the preconditioner of the CG that solves this matrix. With a diagonal preconditioner (the Jacobi preconditioner) alone, the number of CG iterations grows roughly in inverse proportion to the mesh spacing in diffusion-dominated systems. A multigrid reduces the long-wavelength errors that are hard to reduce on a fine mesh by working on a hierarchy of coarser meshes. The coarse meshes are built by aggregating cells.

  • The aggregates join 2×2 cells on the logical lattice of each block (an odd last row or column joins the neighboring aggregate), and the aggregation repeats until each group has at most 64 cells.
  • The prolongation is the aggregate indicator \(P_{\rm tent}\) smoothed as \(P=(I-\omega M^{-1}A)P_{\rm tent}\) (smoothed aggregation [11]). Here \(M=\mathrm{diag}(A)\) is the diagonal of the matrix \(A\) (the matrix of the Jacobi preconditioner), \(\omega=4/(3\Lambda_g)\), and \(\Lambda_g\) is the Gershgorin bound of \(M^{-1}A\) for group \(g\).
  • The coarse-level matrix is the Galerkin product \(A_{l+1}=P_l^{T}A_lP_l\), rebuilt at every solve.
  • The smoother is a Chebyshev polynomial of degree 2 in \(M^{-1}A\) on \([\Lambda_g/10,\Lambda_g]\), applied before and after the coarse-grid correction (a symmetric V-cycle).
  • The coarsest level is solved with the inverse built from the Cholesky factorization of its dense matrix, one per group.

All groups share the aggregates, and the coefficients differ by group. Every sparse product runs row by row in a fixed order and uses no atomic operations, so the preconditioner reproduces bit for bit. In the measurement of 2026-09-28 recorded in the repository's docs/NUMERICS.md §6.7.11 (two temperatures, four groups, 30 steps, constant opacity of 1 cm²/g), one solve took 29 iterations on 72×48 cells and 33 on 144×96 cells with the aggregation multigrid, against 561 and 1145 with the diagonal preconditioner. The diagonal preconditioner exceeds the default cap cg_max_iter (500). The preconditioner is selected by linear_solver_2d (Flux limiter and linear solvers).

Flux limiter and linear solvers

FLD constructs a local dimensionless gradient and diffusion coefficient for every group:

$$ R_{c,g}=\frac{|\nabla E_g|_c}{\sigma_{R,c,g}\max(E_{c,g},E_{\rm floor})}, \qquad D_{c,g}=\frac{c\lambda(R_{c,g})}{\sigma_{R,c,g}}, $$

where \(E_{\rm floor}\) is a small positivity floor that only guards the denominator of the dimensionless gradient.

$$ \lambda_{\rm LP}(R)=\frac{1}{R}\left(\coth R-\frac{1}{R}\right). $$

The Levermore–Pomraning form approaches \(\lambda=1/3\) as \(R\to0\), recovering diffusion, and limits flux at large gradients. The 1D path evaluates face coefficients from face-centered \(\sigma_R\) and \(E\). Both cells use the same \(A_fD_f/d_f\) on a shared face, so internal fluxes cancel with opposite signs.

The 2D RZ path forms no face coefficient. For every corner of a node \(p\), that is, for each cell \(c\) around \(p\), it forms \(D_{c,p,g}=c\lambda(R_{c,p,g})/\sigma_{R,c,g}\) with \(R_{c,p,g}=|\nabla E_g|/(\sigma_{R,c,g}\bar E_{p,g})\). Here \(\bar E_{p,g}\) is the arithmetic mean of \(E_g\) over the cells around \(p\), and \(|\nabla E_g|\) is the magnitude of the gradient of the least-squares plane through the centroids of those cells (at a node on the axis, the mirror images of the cells across the axis join the fit). At a node where the fit is not unique, such as a boundary node touched by only two cells, the one-dimensional slope along the line of the cell centers is used; at a node with a single cell, the cell's own gradient magnitude is used. A uniform field gives \(R=0\), and the coefficient at every corner equals \(c/(3\sigma_{R,c,g})\) of its cell.

In 1D, each group matrix is tridiagonal. On the GPU, \(G\) is the batch count and the number of cells is the system size for cusparseDgtsv2StridedBatch.

In 2D RZ, the node-local operator is assembled per group. All group blocks are stacked in one CSR matrix with row \(gN_c+c\) (\(N_c\) is the number of cells) and solved with preconditioned CG. The 2D RZ FLD runs on a single MPI (message passing interface) rank. On a mesh that the operator cannot take (a general polygonal mesh without multi-block connectivity information, or a single-block lattice whose edges carry no boundary tags), the 2D FLD stops the run.

The CG preconditioner is selected by linear_solver_2d. The accepted values are "auto" (default), "cusparse_cg_amg", "cusparse_cg_jacobi", and "jacobi". "cusparse_cg_amg" is the aggregation multigrid preconditioner; "cusparse_cg_jacobi" and "jacobi" are diagonal preconditioning. The values "cusparse_cg_zline", "cusparse_cg_rgmg", and "amgx_cg" name preconditioners of the earlier five-point operator; they are accepted, give one warning, and are treated as "cusparse_cg_amg". Resolution takes two steps. Validation resolves "auto" to "cusparse_cg_amg". At startup the driver then checks the mesh against the multigrid's condition: the cells must form a sequence of blocks, each a logical lattice of \(n_i\times n_j\) cells, and every coupling between two cells of one block must join lattice neighbors. On a mesh that fails the check, "auto" and the removed names are resolved again to "cusparse_cg_jacobi" with a warning, whereas an explicit "cusparse_cg_amg" stops the run. The requested and the resolved values are recorded in run_info.json and in the HDF5 metadata (solver_requested, solver_resolved).

A five-point stencil, which couples a cell only to the four cells across its faces (Figure B, right), gives inconsistent gradients and fluxes on cells whose edges do not follow the coordinate axes. The node-local operator couples all the cells that share a node and is built the same way on such lattices.

Outer iteration, matter update, and void cells

The equations of steps 1–5 describe the 1D implementation. In 2D RZ, every pass of the outer iteration applies Newton's method to the fully implicit backward-Euler system in \((E_g,T_e)\). It keeps the Jacobian of the coupling to matter and drops the temperature derivative of the diffusion coefficient [9]; TENRYU also drops that of the opacities. With one group the result is the Fleck–Cummings form with the Fleck factor \(f=C_{v,e}/(C_{v,e}+\Delta t\,\eta')\), where \(\eta'=c\sigma^{PE}\,d(a_{\rm eV}T_e^4b)/dT_e\). With several groups, the groups couple through matter only by the absorption rate per cell, \(\phi=\sum_gc\sigma^{PA}_g\Delta t\,E_g\), so the scalar equation for \(\phi\) is solved with GMRES (the generalized minimal residual method), preconditioned with the gray diffusion equation of Morel, Larsen, and Matzen [8]. The details are in the repository's docs/NUMERICS.md §6.7.

  1. Build coefficients. Resolve the group bounds, evaluate the normalized \(b_g(T_e)\), and fill \(\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}\), \(\sigma^{PE}_{c,g}\), and \(\sigma_{R,c,g}\). Form \(\beta_c\) and the Fleck factor from the same electron-EOS heat capacity, then the limiter argument and face diffusion coefficients for every active cell and group. A Marshak temperature callable has already been frozen (definition) into a table; its sampled value is fixed throughout this outer iteration.

  2. Assemble and solve every group. The 1D matrices are tridiagonal and all \(G\) systems are solved as one cusparseDgtsv2StridedBatch batch. The 2D RZ path assembles a node-local operator per group and solves it with preconditioned conjugate gradients (the preconditioner is chosen by linear_solver_2d in the section above). Its linear stopping test is \(\lVert r_k\rVert\le\texttt{cg_inner_tol}\,N_{\rm ref}\), with default cg_inner_tol=1e-10 and, according to cg_tol_norm, the reference norm \(N_{\rm ref}=\lVert r_0\rVert\) (the default "r0") or \(\max(\lVert b\rVert,\mathrm{tiny})\) ("rhs"); “tiny” is a minute positive constant that only guards a zero right-hand side. The iteration cap is cg_max_iter (default 500). This is a deterministic, non-particle transport path; alternative 2D preconditioners target the same \(A^{-1}b\), although bit-identical iteration sequences between preconditioners are not promised.

  3. Retain the source that was actually used. After all group systems are solved, retain

    $$ S^{\rm used}_{c,g}=\Delta t\,V_c \left[f_{c,g}\eta_{c,g}+(1-f_{c,g})c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}E^n_{c,g}\right]. $$

    This exact quantity, rather than a newly evaluated gross Planck source, is the emission side of the matter energy ledger (the 1D implementation re-evaluates the same form of emission at the final temperature inside the matter update, and the two agree on a converged outer iteration).

  4. Update the matter energy. A cell-local Newton solve updates electron internal energy from the absorption in the solved group fields and the same \(S^{\rm used}\):

    $$ \rho_cV_c\left[e_e(\rho_c,T_e^{n+1})-e_e(\rho_c,T_e^n)\right] =\sum_g\left[\Delta t\,V_c\,c\sigma^{PA}_{c,g}E^{n+1}_{c,g}-S^{\rm used}_{c,g}\right]. $$

    The left side is the cell's electron-energy change over the step, referenced to the step-start temperature \(T_e^n\); the right side is the absorbed group energy minus the emission actually applied, using exactly the retained \(S^{\rm used}\).

  5. Test the outer iteration. The quantity this loop drives to self-consistency — and the one whose convergence is tested — is the electron temperature \(T_e\), equivalently the coefficient set evaluated from it. The circularity is that the group systems need \(b_g\), the opacities, and \(f\) at \(T_e\) (and, except under the default limiter_evaluation="predictor", the limiter's diffusion coefficients at the current \(E_g\) iterate), while \(T_e\) itself comes from the matter energy update fed by the solved \(E_g\). In 1D, apart from the acceleration described below, each pass is plain, undamped Picard substitution that re-solves the whole backward-Euler step from the state at the start of the step — the right-hand sides always refer to \(E^n_g\) and the matter balance to \(T_e^n\), so a pass replaces the iterate rather than accumulating on it. Refresh \(b_g\), opacities, \(f\), and \(D_g\) from the new \(T_e\), then repeat the group solves until the change between successive iterates satisfies \(\max_c|T^{k+1}_{e,c}-T^{k}_{e,c}|/\max(T^{k+1}_{e,c},T_{\rm floor})<\texttt{outer_tol}\), where \(T_{\rm floor}\) is the configured temperature floor (Te_floor_eV). The production defaults are outer_tol=1e-5 and max_outer_iterations=20.

    If the cap is reached without meeting the tolerance, 1D accepts the final iterate and a rate-limited run-log warning records the unconverged residual. In 2D RZ the step is classified. A step that has converged, or has reached the accuracy attainable in double precision, is accepted. A step that has stagnated, reached a cap, become invalid, or produced a negative radiation energy beyond the error of its solve is rejected. A rejected candidate is redone with \(\Delta t/2\) when the full-step retry is possible (Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled is on, the number of attempts has not reached Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_max_attempts (default 3), and the halved time step still exceeds the lower bound Numerics.dt.min_s). Otherwise cap_exit_policy (default "warn") decides: "warn" keeps the candidate with a warning, and "fail" stops the run. The default of driver_full_step_retry_enabled is False; the deck that Studio writes sets it to True for 1D and does not write it for 2D.

    In 1D the default (outer_accel="auto") is gray acceleration: after each iterate the next iterate's temperature is corrected by the solution of one gray tridiagonal equation. The correction vanishes with the change between iterates, so the converged solution is that of the unaccelerated iteration. The flux limiter is evaluated by default (limiter_evaluation="predictor") from the first iterate's radiation field and held within the step ("iterate", the former method, re-evaluates it at every iterate; that lagged fixed-point iteration tends to stall in optically thin, free-streaming-dominated regions). In the GXII (GEKKO XII) 1D regression deck every step converges, in 2.6 outer iterations on average. The 2D RZ default is no acceleration; an opt-in Anderson mixing (outer_accel="anderson", also in 1D) is available.

  6. Close boundaries and the energy ledger. The terms used above define rad_dep \(=\Delta t\,V_c\,c\sigma^{PA}E^{n+1}\) and rad_emit \(=S^{\rm used}\); the same boundary rows define marshak_in and escaped (terms section). In 1D a shared face uses one \(A_fD_{f,g}/d_f\); in 2D RZ the rows and columns of each node contribution sum to zero. In both cases the interior contributions cancel and the group-summed radiation-plus-matter change telescopes to the boundary ledgers up to floating-point summation.

  7. Treat void cells. For void cells (cells of an is_void material; see Void materials) — in 1D with every opacity model, in 2D RZ on table/NLTE paths with an explicit void mask — set \(f=1\) and \(\sigma^{PA}=\sigma^{PE}=\sigma_R=\eta=0\), skip matter exchange and temperature updates, and apply the opacity floor only to keep diffusion-coefficient evaluation finite. This is a stable numerical void, not an angular free-streaming model.

Boundary conditions and energy accounting

A reflecting face has zero normal flux. A vacuum face uses the Milne outward-flux form \(F_{{\rm out},g}=cE_g/2\). A Marshak face pairs the incoming blackbody field and diffusion field as

$$ F_{{\rm out},g}=\frac{c}{4}E_g-F_{{\rm inc},g}, \qquad F_{{\rm inc},g}=\frac{c}{4}a_{\rm eV}T_r^4b_g(T_r). $$

Here \(E_g\) is the face value; a vacuum face has \(F_{{\rm inc},g}=0\) and \(F_{{\rm out},g}=cE_g/2\).

In 1D the face value is eliminated by balancing it against the diffusion flux \(D_0(E_{0,g}-E_g)/d\) across the half cell between the center of the outer cell and the face. The result is \(F_{{\rm out},g}=h_{\rm eff}(E_{0,g}-E_{{\rm inc},g})\) with \(h_{\rm eff}=h/(1+h\,d/D_0)\). Here \(E_{0,g}\) is the value in the outer cell, \(h\) is \(c/2\) at a vacuum face and \(c/4\) at a Marshak face, \(E_{{\rm inc},g}\) is 0 at a vacuum face and \(F_{{\rm inc},g}/h\) (the radiation energy density of the drive) at a Marshak face, \(D_0\) is the flux-limited diffusion coefficient of the outer cell itself, and \(d\) is half the width of the outer cell. The matrix diagonal receives \(\Delta t\,A_{\rm out}h_{\rm eff}\) and the right-hand side \(\Delta t\,A_{\rm out}(h_{\rm eff}/h)F_{{\rm inc},g}\), with \(A_{\rm out}\) the area of the outer face. For an optically thin outer cell, \(d/D_0\to0\), \(h_{\rm eff}=h\), and a Marshak face returns to adding \(\Delta t\,A_{\rm out}c/4\) to the diagonal. In an optically thick cell the face value is held at \(E_{{\rm inc},g}\).

The factor-of-two difference between the vacuum coefficient \(cE_g/2\) and the outgoing partial current \(cE_g/4\) of a Marshak face is a difference of boundary closures, not an inconsistency: the vacuum face imposes the Marshak/Milne zero-incoming condition under the P1 (diffusion) angular closure. In the P1 approximation the flux crossing the boundary decomposes into the outgoing and incoming partial currents \(J^\pm=cE/4\pm F/2\); the vacuum condition amounts to imposing \(J^-=0\) on the incoming side, and solving it gives a net outward flux of \(F=cE/2\) — twice the partial current \(cE/4\) of an isotropic field. By contrast, the Marshak drive face books the exchange of partial currents explicitly, \(cE_g/4\) outgoing against the prescribed incoming \(F_{{\rm inc},g}\), so that the net flux vanishes exactly at radiative equilibrium (\(E_g=a_{\rm eV}T_r^4b_g\)).

The 1D inner-r face is reflecting; the outer-r face may be vacuum, reflecting, or Marshak. In 2D RZ the outer-r and z edges carry an outward flux equal to the same coefficient (\(c/2\) for vacuum, \(c/4\) for Marshak) times the radiation energy density of the half-edge (a Marshak edge also subtracts the incident flux \(F_{{\rm inc},g}\)). The axis (\(r=0\)) and reflecting edges carry zero flux.

\(T_r\) may be a constant Radiation.boundary.marshak_Tr_eV or a deck callable Radiation.boundary.marshak_Tr. A callable is frozen into a time table at initialization — Python is never called during time integration. Its value is sampled once and remains invariant through the outer iteration. The sampling time is the midpoint of the interval that the radiation operator advances in 1D, and state.t at solve entry in 2D RZ.

The same discrete terms add positive Marshak input to marshak_in and vacuum or positive Marshak outward flux to escaped (written to history as energy/radiation_escaped). Matter-side rad_dep and rad_emit come directly from the absorption term and \(S^{\rm used}\). In 1D the shared-face terms cancel exactly between the two cells, and in 2D RZ the node contributions cancel exactly among the cells around each node, so the group-summed change in radiation plus matter telescopes to the boundary ledgers. A flat-opacity multigroup/gray collapse has the same balance apart from floating-point summation order.

Verification

  • The analytic multigroup Marshak-spectrum tests fld_1d_mg_planar_marshak_spectrum and fld_1d_mg_planar_freqdep_relaxation check a \(G=8\) plateau against independently computed Planck fractions and closed-form per-group relaxation for the same frequency-dependent opacity.
  • Linearized-coupling twin tests combine the closed-form 0D Fleck ODE, groupwise NLTE spectrum/Fleck probes, and the Su–Olson picket-fence problem [6] — a two-opacity-band model with a semi-analytic solution, whose superposition symmetry under swapping the band assignment the test exploits. fld_1d_volume_source_balance checks that the tallied volume-source injection equals \(\Delta t\,V\dot S\) (relative error below \(10^{-12}\)) and that, in a domain with a reflecting outer boundary, the increase of the summed radiation and matter energy equals the injection (relative error below \(10^{-8}\); \(2.1\times10^{-10}\) measured on 2026-07-03).
  • verify_gxii_1d_fld_regression is the GXII 1D golden regression. It gates absorbed radiation energy, bang time, shell radius, peak density, areal density, central temperature, and related radiative-implosion metrics. With the default outer iteration every step converges (2.6 iterations on average).
  • Energy-conservation tests close shared faces, marshak_in, escaped, material emission, and deposition in one energy ledger. The 2D multigroup/gray identity with the hydrodynamics held fixed requires \(\varepsilon_{L2}\le10^{-8}\) and \(\varepsilon_{L\infty}\le10^{-6}\) — the relative root-mean-square and maximum-magnitude field differences — and a ledger-drift difference \(\le10^{-10}\); measured closed-box drift is \(\le5.1\times10^{-16}\).
  • The 2D RZ layered slab-Marshak battery compares boundary ignition, front position, profile, material-interface flux, and each step's ledger residual with an independent implicit-Newton finite-volume reference. Its short-window ledger criterion is \(\le10^{-10}\); the acceptance test over the full time window requires \(\varepsilon_{L2}(E)\le0.02\) and an interface-flux discontinuity \(\le10^{-3}\).
