Radiation transport

TENRYU solves multigroup radiation transport coupled to matter (the electron internal energy) with two production deterministic solvers, selected by Radiation.mode: multigroup flux-limited diffusion (FLD, "multigroup_diffusion") and discrete-ordinates \(S_N\) transport ("sn_transport"). Both operate in the three coordinate geometries of 1D — spherical, cylindrical, and planar, set by Mesh.geometry_1d — and in 2D axisymmetric RZ geometry. They share the group structure and the material closure (the relations that give pressure, temperature, opacity, and similar quantities from the conserved quantities, explained on the EOS, opacity & ionization page), but differ in how angle is treated and how the coupling to matter is solved. This page explains each model, compares them, and points to the detailed model pages below.

Multigroup FLD

Flux-limited diffusion with Fleck-linearized matter coupling.

SN transport

Discrete-ordinates transport with sweeps and an iteratively solved matter coupling.

The two models

Multigroup FLD. Multigroup flux-limited diffusion partitions photon energy into photon-energy groups (each group is an interval of the photon-energy range) and couples the stiff emission and absorption of matter — whose timescales are far shorter than the time step — implicitly through the Fleck linearization. The Fleck factor \(f\) is the fraction of the absorption that actually changes the matter energy, and the remaining \(1-f\) is treated as a scattering-like process in which the absorbed radiation is re-emitted within the same step (effective scattering; see Group structure and Fleck linearization). In 1D, it solves a batched tridiagonal system over the groups. In 2D RZ, it builds the node-local operator (the symmetric operator on cell-centered values obtained by splitting each cell edge at its midpoint into two half-edges and eliminating, node by node, the values on that node's half-edges) into a conservative finite-volume system for each group, stored as a CSR (compressed sparse row) sparse matrix, and solves it with the conjugate-gradient (CG) method. The preconditioner is an aggregation multigrid by default, and a diagonal preconditioner on meshes that fail the aggregation conditions. It recovers diffusion in optically thick regions while the Levermore–Pomraning flux limiter bounds transport in thin regions.

\(S_N\) transport. Discrete-ordinates transport follows a finite set of directions (ordinates) in every photon-energy group and forms cell energies and face fluxes from the directional specific intensity. The \(N\) of \(S_N\) is an integer that sets the order of the angular quadrature; it is given by Radiation.sn_transport.n_angles (allowed values 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32; default 16). Only 1D cylindrical geometry takes \(n=2L^2\) in this key, where the level count \(L\) is an integer from 2 to 32 (8, 18, 32, …). It combines sweeps along wavefronts (a wavefront is a set of cells that can be processed together once the upstream work is done) with source iteration. The matter coupling is solved by Picard iteration in 2D RZ and with the 1D linear-characteristic scheme, and by a Newton iteration on the nodal electron temperatures with the 1D default linear-discontinuous scheme. Because the method retains angular information, it captures free streaming, shadowing, and anisotropy that a diffusion closure cannot represent.

Choose \(S_N\) when a transparent gap, a free-streaming radiation front, directional illumination, shadow, or ray effect makes the angular distribution part of the answer. FLD closes angle into scalar energy and limited diffusive flux and is generally cheaper in optically thick, nearly isotropic regions. Lower ordinate order costs less but increases angular truncation error and ray effects.

What the solvers share

  • Shared photon-energy group bounds, Planck fractions \(b_g(T)\) (the fraction of the blackbody energy at temperature \(T\) that falls in group \(g\)), and the primitives evaluated from the electron equation of state (EOS), such as internal energy and heat capacity.
  • Three boundary conditions — reflecting, vacuum, and Marshak (the Marshak boundary condition prescribes the flux incident from an external radiation field) — with face types nested under each mode's own block.
  • Void cells (cells filled with a material whose Material.is_void is true) are excluded from matter exchange.
  • The energy ledger (the record, by item, of the energy exchanged between radiation and matter and across the boundary) closes to machine precision.
  • Internal units cgs + eV.
