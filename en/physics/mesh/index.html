Meshes & geometry

TENRYU constructs its domain as a 1D radial grid or a 2D axisymmetric RZ (R–Z) mesh. The 1D path provides uniform and graded zoning with spherical, cylindrical, or planar measures (the way volumes and areas are taken); the deck assigns material regions (specified by volume fraction as a function of position through Geometry.volfrac; see regions and volume fractions), a preformed-plasma corona, and exterior void cells. The 2D path takes the structured rectangular, spherical-polar half-plane, polar-in-box, and multiblock mesh families (the multiblock families store connectivity in the compressed-sparse-row, CSR, format) and hands the physics kernels the same three things in the same form: the RZ volume of each cell, the geometry of the corners (defined on the hydrodynamics page), and the node–cell–face connectivity. The quantities that measure mesh quality (the admissibility check, the corner Jacobian, the Gauss-point Jacobian, and the axis margin) are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • 1D nodes are cell boundaries, cells hold thermodynamic state, and the coordinate measure is spherical, cylindrical, or planar.
  • uniform gives equal spacing; graded uses per-segment cell weights and boundary matching.
  • The deck-generating GUI TENRYU Studio can generate concentric volume-fraction regions, void cells excluded from conduction and radiation (void materials), and a monotone laser-target corona ramp.
  • The 2D default is a structured quadrilateral mesh. Polar, polar-in-box, cone-shell, and pentagon-belt forms are opt-in logical-mesh or multiblock families. Of these, polar-in-box (polar_in_box) and cone shell (cone_shell) currently build the mesh, initialize, and write the step-0 output only; starting the time evolution raises a ConfigError.

1D meshes & regions

Uniform and graded zoning, material regions (specified by volume fraction), void cells, and the corona ramp.

2D RZ mesh families

Structured rectangular, polar, polar-in-box, and multiblock RZ meshes.

ALE: rezoning & remap

Mesh optimization (rezoning) and conservative transfer of the coupled state (remap) in the arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian (ALE) method.

Mesh-quality guards

Builders fail loudly if the segments leave a gap or have a non-positive width, if the nodes are not strictly monotone, or, in polar coordinates, if the θ node list does not reach its end points θmin and π. The axis nodes of a polar mesh are placed exactly at R = 0.

In 2D RZ, at creation and again at the start of each Lagrangian step, after the predictor, and after the corrector, every cell must have a positive, finite orientation (the sign of the signed planar area) and RZ volume, and no two edges of a cell may cross. This check always runs. When it fails, the step is retried if Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled or Numerics.hydro.mesh_geometry_soft_fail_enabled (both False by default) is true, and the run stops with an error otherwise.

Beyond that, admissibility checks judge, with the corner Jacobian, the Gauss-point Jacobian, the RZ volume, and the axis margin, whether the shape of a cell may be passed to the volume, force, and remap operators. The ALE trigger and acceptance checks work by default once ALE is enabled. The checks that judge the candidate positions of a Lagrangian step with these quantities beforehand, including the time-step limit, are all off by default and are enabled by keys; a candidate that reaches a floor causes the step to be rejected and retried with a shorter time step. These are not physics models; they are conditions that keep the volume, force, and remap operators from being evaluated on invalid geometry. The terms section defines each quantity and lists the floor, key, and default of each check.

Terms used in this calculation

The quality of a 2D RZ mesh is measured by four quantities: the RZ volume \(V_c\) of a cell, the corner Jacobian \(J_{c,k}\), the Gauss-point Jacobian \(J_{c,q}\), and the axis margin \(M_j\). Here \(c\) is the cell, \(k=0,\dots,3\) its corner (vertex), \(q\) one of the 2×2 Gauss points, and \(j\) the index of a cell in the row that touches the axis (\(i=0\)). Coordinates are \((r,z)\) in cm, and the vertices are numbered \((r_0,z_0),\dots,(r_3,z_3)\).

Admissibility check

An admissibility check tests whether a node configuration may be passed to the volume, force, and remap operators. It examines the current mesh and the candidate mesh obtained after the nodes have moved. In an inverted or collapsed cell the RZ volume reaches zero or below. In a cell with an inverted corner, the local area factor \(J\) (defined under the corner Jacobian) is negative in part of the cell interior. In either case, the density \(\rho=M_c/V_c\) (with \(M_c\) the cell mass) and the corner forces lose their meaning. The check keeps such configurations away from the operators; it is not a physics model.

Four quantities are judged. The RZ volume \(V_c\) (cm³) is the volume of the solid obtained by revolving the cell's cross-section about the axis; for a quadrilateral it is

$$ V_c=\frac{\pi}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{3}(r_k+r_{k+1})(r_kz_{k+1}-r_{k+1}z_k) $$

(positive when the vertices run counterclockwise; \(k+1\) is taken modulo 4). The other three are defined in the next three subsections.

For a candidate mesh, some checks examine not only the positions but also the path along which the nodes move from their current positions to the candidate. Moving the nodes along the straight line \(\mathbf x(\sigma)=\mathbf x_{\rm old}+\sigma(\mathbf x_{\rm new}-\mathbf x_{\rm old})\) (\(0\le\sigma\le1\)) makes \(J_{c,k}\) and \(J_{c,q}\) quadratic in \(\sigma\) and \(V_c\) cubic. The minimum along the path therefore follows in closed form from the values at the end points and at the zeros of the derivative, so a cell that inverts temporarily during the motion and recovers at the end point is not missed. The smallest \(\sigma\) at which some quantity first reaches its floor, taken over all cells, is written \(\sigma_{\rm safe}\).

The floors, keys, and defaults of the checks follow; the list shows the main ones.

  • Geometry check (runs by default). At creation, at the start of each Lagrangian step, after the predictor, and after the corrector, it verifies in every cell that \(V_c>0\) and the signed planar area is positive, both finite, and that no cell edges cross. It has no key. What happens on failure is described in the section above.
  • ALE trigger, cell quality (runs by default when ALE is enabled). Every rezoning interval (Numerics.ale.every_n_steps, default 5) the minimum over all cells of the quality \(q_c\) (defined under the Gauss-point Jacobian) is evaluated, and the rezone runs if it falls below Numerics.ale.quality_threshold (default 0.2).
  • ALE trigger, axis (runs by default when ALE is enabled). When \(\min_jM_j<\alpha\,M_{\rm init}\), the rezone starts without waiting for the interval. \(M_{\rm init}\) is the value of \(\min_jM_j\) at the first evaluation, and \(\alpha\) is Numerics.ale.preventive_axis_guard_fraction (default 0.1; 0 disables it).
  • Acceptance of an ALE candidate (runs by default when ALE is enabled). A candidate moved by the rezone is rejected as inverted if any \(J_{c,q}<0\), \(J_{c,k}\le0\), or \(M_j\le0\). The blending fraction is then reduced and the candidate retried, and if none passes, the state before the rezone is restored (Admissibility and acceptance). Two keys widen what counts as inverted. With Numerics.ale.reject_zero_gauss_j true (default False), \(J_{c,q}\le\max(10^{-30},\,r_J\max_qJ_{c,q})\) also counts (\(r_J\) is Numerics.ale.zero_gauss_j_floor_rel, default \(10^{-8}\); the unit of \(10^{-30}\) is cm²). With Numerics.ale.corner_post_tangle_strict_floor_enabled true (default False), \(J_{c,k}\le\max(10^{-30},\,\epsilon_J\max_k|J_{c,k}|)\) also counts (\(\epsilon_J\) is Numerics.hydro.corner_jacobian_floor_eps, default \(10^{-6}\)). The corner check itself is switched by Numerics.ale.corner_jacobian_post_tangle_enabled (default True).
  • Path check of the Lagrangian step, time-step limit (off by default). Before the nodes move in the predictor and the corrector, it examines whether \(J_{c,k}\), \(J_{c,q}\), and \(V_c\) stay above \(10^{-8}\) times their values at the start of the path (cells that touch the axis also get an axis predicate). If some path falls below, the step is rejected and retried with the time step reduced to \(\alpha_{dt}\,\sigma_{\rm safe}\,\Delta t_s\) (\(\Delta t_s\) is \(\Delta t/2\) in the predictor and \(\Delta t\) in the corrector). It is enabled by Numerics.hydro.mesh_quality_dt_cfl_enabled (default False). The per-quantity switches are mesh_quality_dt_corner_j_enabled, mesh_quality_dt_gauss_j_enabled, and mesh_quality_dt_rz_volume_enabled (all True by default); the relative floors are mesh_quality_dt_corner_j_floor_rel, mesh_quality_dt_gauss_j_floor_rel, and mesh_quality_dt_rz_volume_floor_rel (all \(10^{-8}\) by default); the axis predicate is mesh_quality_dt_axis_margin_additive (default True); and \(\alpha_{dt}\) is mesh_quality_dt_safety_alpha (default 0.5).
  • Check before the positions of the Lagrangian step are committed (off by default). Just before the candidate positions are committed, the same path check runs, enabled per quantity by Numerics.hydro.in_hydro_corner_j_guard_enabled, in_hydro_gauss_j_guard_enabled, and in_hydro_rz_volume_guard_enabled (all False by default). The floors are \(\epsilon_J\) times the starting value for \(J_{c,k}\) (\(\epsilon_J\) is corner_jacobian_floor_eps, default \(10^{-6}\)), in_hydro_gauss_j_floor_rel times for \(J_{c,q}\) (default \(10^{-8}\)), and in_hydro_rz_volume_floor_rel times for \(V_c\) (default \(10^{-8}\)). A candidate that reaches a floor causes the step to be rejected and retried with a shorter time step.
  • ALE trigger before the hydro step (off by default). Before the operator splitting, along the predicted motion of the nodes in one step, \(\mathbf x(\tau)=\mathbf x^0+\tau\mathbf v^0\) (\(0\le\tau\le\Delta t\)), it finds for each corner the fraction of the step that can be taken before \(J_{c,k}\) reaches its floor \(\epsilon_JJ_{c,k}^0\), and starts ALE if the minimum of this fraction over all corners falls below Numerics.hydro.corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_scale (default 0.5). It is enabled by Numerics.hydro.corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_enabled (default False), and \(\epsilon_J\) is corner_jacobian_floor_eps (default \(10^{-6}\)).
  • Limits from the axis margin on the time step and on the velocity (off by default). They reduce either the time step (Numerics.hydro.axis_margin_dt_floor_fraction, \(f\)) or the radial velocity of the \(i=1\) nodes (Numerics.hydro.axis_motion_floor_fraction, \(f\)) so that \(M_j\) does not fall below \(f\) times its current value during the step. Both default to 0, which disables them.

When a path check or a pre-commit check finds a candidate that reaches a floor and rejects the step, the step is retried with a shorter time step if Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled is true, and the run stops with an error otherwise.

Corner Jacobian

The corner Jacobian \(J_{c,k}\) of corner \(k\) of cell \(c\) is the cross product of the two edge vectors that leave that vertex (Figure A(a)(b)).

$$ J_{c,k}=(\mathbf x_{k+1}-\mathbf x_k)\times(\mathbf x_{k-1}-\mathbf x_k) =(r_{k+1}-r_k)(z_{k-1}-z_k)-(z_{k+1}-z_k)(r_{k-1}-r_k) $$

Here \(\mathbf x_k=(r_k,z_k)\), indices are taken modulo 4, and the cross product is the two-dimensional one in the \((r,z)\) plane. The unit is cm². \(J_{c,k}\) equals the product of the two edge lengths and the sine of the interior angle at that corner. For a cell whose vertices run counterclockwise, it is positive at a convex corner, negative at a concave corner whose interior angle exceeds 180°, and zero when the corner collapses. For meshes whose vertices run clockwise (those that contain the tri-fan or pentagon belts of the spherical-polar families), the sign is reversed so that convex corners are positive.

The Jacobian of the bilinear map of the four nodes of the cell (next subsection) is bilinear in the coordinates of the reference square, so its minimum over the whole cell is attained at one of the four corners, and that value is the corner Jacobian. All four \(J_{c,k}\) being positive is therefore necessary and sufficient for the map not to invert anywhere in the cell, boundary included.

The corner Jacobian detects inversions that the Gauss-point Jacobian misses. For example, if the vertex \((1,1)\) of a 1 cm × 1 cm square is moved along the diagonal to \((0.4,0.4)\), the \(J_{c,k}\) of that corner becomes \(-0.2\) cm², and the interior angle of the corner exceeds 180°. The \(J_{c,q}\) at all four Gauss points nevertheless remain positive, with a minimum of 0.013 cm² (Figure A(b)).

The floor of the corner Jacobian differs from check to check (see the list under the admissibility check). For a candidate after a rezone, \(J_{c,k}\le0\) counts as inverted by default. The path check of the Lagrangian step and the ALE trigger before the hydro step use a floor relative to the starting value.

Gauss-point Jacobian

The Gauss-point Jacobian \(J_{c,q}\) is the Jacobian of the bilinear map that connects the four nodes of the cell to the reference square \([-1,1]^2\),

$$ r(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}N_k(\xi,\eta)\,r_k,\qquad z(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}N_k(\xi,\eta)\,z_k, $$ $$ N_0=\tfrac{(1-\xi)(1-\eta)}{4},\quad N_1=\tfrac{(1+\xi)(1-\eta)}{4},\quad N_2=\tfrac{(1+\xi)(1+\eta)}{4},\quad N_3=\tfrac{(1-\xi)(1+\eta)}{4}, $$

that is,

$$ J(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\partial r}{\partial\xi}\frac{\partial z}{\partial\eta}-\frac{\partial r}{\partial\eta}\frac{\partial z}{\partial\xi}, $$

evaluated at the four points \((\xi,\eta)=(\pm1/\sqrt3,\pm1/\sqrt3)\) of the 2×2 Gauss quadrature (Figure A(a)(b)). The unit is cm². \(J(\xi,\eta)\) is bilinear in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), so the two-point Gauss rule integrates it exactly, and the sum of the four values equals the signed planar area of the cell. Each value is one quarter of a weighted mean of the four corner Jacobians (\(4J_{c,q}=\sum_kw_{qk}J_{c,k}\)). The weights \(w_{qk}\) are positive and sum to one: 0.622 for the corner nearest the Gauss point, 1/6 for each of the two adjacent corners, and 0.045 for the opposite corner. If all \(J_{c,k}\) are positive, all \(J_{c,q}\) are therefore positive, but the converse does not hold (the example in the previous subsection).

The cell quality \(q_c\) is the ratio of the smallest to the largest of the four values (Figure A(a)):

$$ q_c=\frac{\min_qJ_{c,q}}{\max\left(\max_qJ_{c,q},\ 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{cm^2}\right)}. $$

\(q_c=1\) for a parallelogram, it decreases as the cell is distorted, and \(q_c\le0\) if any \(J_{c,q}\) is negative. For meshes with triangular or pentagonal cells, the 2×2 Gauss points are not available, so \(q_c\) is built from the corner Jacobians (\(\min_kJ_{c,k}/\max_kJ_{c,k}\) on a single block; on a multiblock mesh, the minimum over the corners of \(J_{c,k}\) divided by the product of the two edge lengths).

\(q_c\) is the indicator that starts an ALE rezone (\(\min_cq_c<\)Numerics.ale.quality_threshold, default 0.2), and \(J_{c,q}<0\) is one of the conditions for rejecting a candidate after a rezone. The path check of the Lagrangian step includes a check of \(J_{c,q}\) with a floor relative to the starting value (default \(10^{-8}\)). The keys and defaults are in the list under the admissibility check.

Axis margin

The axis margin \(M_j\) measures how much room a cell that touches the axis (\(r=0\)) has before the motion of the nodes inverts it. It is the minimum of the four corner Jacobians of that cell, written in closed form by using the fact that the axis nodes are fixed at \(r=0\). The vertices of cell \(j\) of the row that touches the axis (\(i=0\)) are \((0,z_{0,j})\), \((r_{1,j},z_{1,j})\), \((r_{1,j+1},z_{1,j+1})\), and \((0,z_{0,j+1})\), where the indices number the nodes by (radial, axial) position (Figure A(c)).

$$ s=z_{0,j+1}-z_{0,j},\qquad Q=r_{1,j}(z_{1,j+1}-z_{0,j+1})-r_{1,j+1}(z_{1,j}-z_{0,j}),\qquad M_j=s\,\min(r_{1,j},r_{1,j+1})+\min(Q,0) $$

\(s\) is the length of the edge on the axis, and \(Q\) is the difference of the crossed products of the radii and the axial offsets \(z_{1,j}-z_{0,j}\) and \(z_{1,j+1}-z_{0,j+1}\) of the two outer nodes; it reduces \(M_j\) when the cell leans in the direction that makes it negative. The unit is cm². If \(s\le0\), \(r_{1,j}\le0\), or \(r_{1,j+1}\le0\), \(M_j=-1\) is set and the cell counts as inverted. The four corner Jacobians of this cell are \(J_0=r_{1,j}s\), \(J_3=r_{1,j+1}s\), \(J_1=r_{1,j}s+Q\), and \(J_2=r_{1,j+1}s+Q\), so \(M_j\) equals their minimum. \(M_j>0\) is necessary and sufficient for the cell that touches the axis not to invert anywhere in the cell.

Because the axis nodes are fixed at \(r=0\), the corner Jacobians \(J_0\) and \(J_3\) of a cell that touches the axis are proportional to the radii \(r_{1,j}\) and \(r_{1,j+1}\) of the outer nodes, and when the outer nodes approach the axis the cell collapses into a thin sliver along it. \(M_j\) depends on six coordinates only and needs neither iteration nor search. It can be evaluated on every ALE call at a cost proportional to the number of rows along the axis, so it can be checked every time without waiting for the rezoning interval.

The axis margin is defined for the row that touches the axis in meshes that have a physical axis (Numerics.has_physical_rz_axis is true). The loader derives this setting from the deck: it is true in 2D RZ when the absolute value of Mesh.r_min is at most \(10^{-12}\) cm and the inner boundary is Numerics.hydro.boundary_2d.r_inner="axis". It is not defined for annular meshes (Mesh.r_min positive) or for a tri-fan center. It is used in four places. (1) The preventive axis trigger: \(\min_jM_j<\alpha M_{\rm init}\) starts a rezone (Numerics.ale.preventive_axis_guard_fraction, default 0.1). (2) Acceptance of a rezone candidate: \(M_j\le0\) counts as inverted. (3) The time-step limit: the time step is reduced so that \(M_j\) does not fall below \(f\) times its current value during the step (Numerics.hydro.axis_margin_dt_floor_fraction, default 0, which disables it). (4) The velocity limit: the radial velocity of the \(i=1\) nodes is reduced (Numerics.hydro.axis_motion_floor_fraction, default 0, which disables it).
