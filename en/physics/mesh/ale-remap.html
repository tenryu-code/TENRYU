ALE: rezoning & remap reference

TENRYU's 2D RZ ALE separates rezoning, which changes only mesh shape after Lagrangian hydrodynamics, from conservative remap, which transfers the material state to that mesh. The legacy Winslow family, reference/barrier targets, and the M1/TMOP objective solver connect to a common admissibility contract. Only an accepted target receives a conservative transfer of mass, momentum, internal energies, and radiation-group energies. In addition to these fixed-connectivity families, the opt-in second-generation ReALE (mesh_mode="reale_v2") rebuilds the mesh topology itself through generator advection and exact tessellation (described below).

At a glance

  • A cycle is Lagrangian step → trigger → target mesh → admissibility gates → conservative remap.
  • The default solver is bit-exact legacy Winslow; M1/TMOP and reference-barrier paths are opt-in.
  • Remap transports cell integrals, not intensive values, then recovers primitive state using the new volumes and masses.
  • A candidate that fails RZ-volume, corner-J, Gauss-J, axis-margin, or sweep-positivity checks is reduced or rolled back.
  • The opt-in second-generation ReALE (mesh_mode="reale_v2") is a separate machinery that rebuilds mesh topology through Voronoi re-partitioning rather than relaxing node coordinates; its trigger, convergence, welding, and projection decisions are driven by machine-epsilon-derived certificates.

The physical mechanism

A mesh that follows the material keeps interfaces sharp, but shear, vorticity, and converging asymmetries progressively strain that Lagrangian mesh: cells shear, flatten, and eventually invert, while the timestep collapses with the smallest cell altitude long before the material physics itself fails. ALE is the escape. TENRYU retains Lagrangian fidelity while the mesh is healthy; when it is not, it moves the mesh without moving the material. Rezoning proposes better node coordinates, and remap conservatively transfers the state onto them. The “arbitrary” in the name refers to exactly this freedom: the mesh velocity is pinned neither to the material velocity (Lagrangian) nor to zero (Eulerian) but may be chosen at will — TENRYU's choice being the material velocity while the mesh is healthy, and the rezoner's proposal once it is not.

Rezoning is geometry, not physics: it constructs only a target mesh through the Winslow-type smoothing and constrained variants in the solver family below, and must not silently change the answer. RZ-volume positivity, corner-J floors, Gauss-point Jacobians, and node-path checks enforce that separation through line search and rollback. An unacceptable target is rejected and the cycle simply remains Lagrangian. Cadence, mesh-quality metrics, and axis/corner-J guards decide when a rezone is attempted at all.

Remap is a conservation transaction between old and new meshes with the same topology. Exact bookkeeping uses the signed volumes swept between their faces. Each swept region takes one donor-cell state, with slope reconstruction and MS2 (Margolin–Shashkov second-order [4]) limiting for monotone second order, and transfers every extensive ledger—mass, species masses, momentum, internal and kinetic energy, and radiation-group energy—with one signed volume, one donor, and equal-and-opposite cell updates. Mesh motion therefore conserves those transported ledgers to round-off by construction. The documented energy closure deposits the kinetic-energy remap/projection discrepancy (projection: the operation that returns remapped cell momentum to nodal velocity — described below) into material internal energy, and subzonal corner masses (the masses of the subzones that split a cell around its nodes — used by the hourglass control and compatible energy bookkeeping of hydrodynamics) are rebuilt only after an accepted remap—the one point where their Lagrangian invariance may change.

The production managed-ALE shape is a banded controller rather than a global relaxation. Axis bands and Euler windows confine where respacing acts—the axis is where RZ Lagrangian meshes fail first—and band-only swept-volume remap keeps the conservation transaction local. Every accepted cycle also preserves the OFF bit-identity contract: if the controller is disabled or never triggered, the result is bitwise identical to the pure-Lagrangian path.

ALE cycle and triggers

The normal entry is the post-Lagrange point. TENRYU evaluates the every_n_steps cadence (default 5) and cell quality. It samples a bilinear map at four 2×2 Gauss points and defines

$$ q_c=\frac{J_{\min}}{J_{\max}}, \qquad \min_c q_c<q_{\mathrm{threshold}}. $$

The default is quality_threshold=0.2; \(q_c=1\) denotes a parallelogram, while zero indicates degeneracy. spacing_ratio_threshold is a parsed and validated reserved key with no runtime firing rule in v1 (the numerical contract, docs/NUMERICS.md §3.3 in the repository, defines none), so it is not counted as an active trigger here.

Protective paths can bypass cadence. The preventive axis guard forces rezoning when the axis margin falls below a configured fraction of its initial value. The axis margin is the geometric headroom against inversion of the axis-touching cell row under node motion, evaluated in closed form from the node coordinates of the axis row and the row outside it (units cm²). The default-off pre-hydro corner-J trigger finds where a signed corner Jacobian reaches its floor along the predicted trajectory and invokes ALE before operator splitting when the admissible fraction of the predicted one-step node displacement — how far it can proceed before a corner Jacobian reaches its floor, minimized over all corners — falls below the threshold. Reference-barrier ALE has additional near-axis margin, corner-J ratio, and diagonal-corner length-ratio triggers.

Rezone solver family

legacy_winslow preserves the simplified orthogonal Winslow update bit-exactly. Metric coefficients are lagged from the current logical-grid coordinates in a Jacobi iteration, and the cross derivative is omitted (here \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the Winslow metric coefficients — unrelated to the radiation pages' symbols):

$$ r_{i,j}^{new}=\frac{\alpha(r_{i+1,j}+r_{i-1,j})+\beta(r_{i,j+1}+r_{i,j-1})} {2(\alpha+\beta)}. $$

The same equation updates \(z\). An opt-in full R-Z metric variant adds the mixed derivative and an R-weighted candidate. Its node-local line search can test incident-cell Gauss-J, corner-J, signed RZ volume, and non-axis radii.

The reference-Winslow/barrier family constructs displacements toward targets such as the initial Eulerian mesh or an equal-angle spherical mesh, then uses a global line search with reference-relative floors. Near-axis reference barriers and protected patches may use a Winslow proposal as a seed and project it into the feasible volume/corner-J set. The path from the Lagrangian mesh to the endpoint is checked, not merely the endpoint.

m1_tmop is an opt-in damped Gauss–Newton solver. Its objective combines M1 quality relative to a reference metric, a tether to the Lagrangian position, polar-row spacing regularization, and a small-determinant activation barrier:

$$ \mathcal J(\mathbf x)= \frac{\sum_{c,q}\omega_{cq}\det(W_{cq})\,\mu(A_{cq}W_{cq}^{-1};\gamma_{\rm align})} {\sum_{c,q}\omega_{cq}\det(W_{cq})} +\frac{\lambda_{\rm tether}}{N_v}\sum_i\frac{\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_i^L\|^2}{h_i^2} +\frac{w_\theta}{N_v}\sum_{i\in\mathcal R} \left(\frac{d_{iL}-d_{iR}}{d_{iL}+d_{iR}}\right)^2 +\beta\sum_{c,q}\left[\det A_{cq}<\sigma_b\det W_{cq}\right] \log\frac{\sigma_b\det W_{cq}}{\det A_{cq}}. $$

Here \(A_{cq}\) and \(W_{cq}\) are the actual and reference corner Jacobians at Gauss point \(q\) of cell \(c\), with weights \(\omega_{cq}\) and the shape-quality measure \(\mu\) at alignment weight \(\gamma_{\rm align}\) — the M1 quality term; \(\lambda_{\rm tether}\) penalizes displacement from the Lagrangian positions \(\mathbf x^L_i\) on the local scale \(h_i\); \(N_v\) is the total node count; the \(w_\theta\) term regularizes polar-row spacing through the left/right neighbor distances \(d_{iL},d_{iR}\) over the row set \(\mathcal R\); and the \(\beta\) barrier activates where \(\det A_{cq}<\sigma_b\det W_{cq}\).

Each node proposal is capped by a local edge scale and receives up to 12 line-search halvings. A transaction is accepted only when the candidate-mesh oracle is admissible and

$$ \frac{\mathcal J_{before}-\mathcal J_{after}} {\max(|\mathcal J_{before}|,10^{-300})} >\texttt{m1\_min\_j\_dec\_rel}. $$

The 0.0 default requires a strict decrease while retaining small useful improvements. The first-generation M1 solver (“M1 v1”) targets polar-family meshes. The M1 reference mesh is not a fixed initial mesh but a moving reference, re-pinned to the accepted coordinates each time a rezone is accepted (this re-pinning is the re-anchoring). Accepted coordinates re-anchor the moving reference before conservative remap.

Admissibility and acceptance gates

Rezoner convergence is not mesh acceptance. Every active candidate cell must retain positive signed RZ volume and corner-J, satisfy the 2×2 Gauss-J floors, and, for axis cells, satisfy the analytic axis margin. The first directional-remap sweep must also keep intermediate volumes positive. Safe backtracking first validates the Lagrangian mesh at blend factor \(\lambda=0\) (\(\lambda\) blends the Lagrangian mesh toward the proposed target), samples \(\lambda=2^{-e}\), and bisects upward to the largest admissible blend. If no positive value passes, the mesh and state roll back to the pre-transaction snapshot.

Thus, a smaller objective or Winslow residual alone never commits a mesh. Reference targets, M1 targets, and managed repairs all end at the same geometry/path oracle and remap-positivity checks.

Swept-region conservative remap

The default legacy_split alternates the ordering of R and Z swept-region sweeps. Revolving the meridional polygon \(P_f\) between an old and new face gives the signed volume

$$ \Delta V_f=V(P_f)=\frac{\pi}{3}\sum_k(r_k+r_{k+1}) (r_kz_{k+1}-r_{k+1}z_k). $$

Here \((r_k,z_k)\) are the vertices of the meridional polygon \(P_f\) swept between the old and new face; the prefactor \(\pi/3\) comes from the exact revolution about the axis.

For each internal face, TENRYU chooses one donor and computes one flux, then applies equal-and-opposite updates. A conservative cell integral \(\bar q=qV\) changes as

$$ \bar q_i^{new}=\bar q_i^{old}-F_{i+1/2}+F_{i-1/2}. $$

(\(F_{i\pm1/2}\) are the conserved-quantity fluxes through the two faces of cell \(i\), evaluated from the donor-side reconstructed state and the swept volume.) The transferred quantities are mass, R/Z momentum, electron/ion internal energy, material volume, and every radiation-group energy. Density is recovered as \(m^{new}/V^{new}\), velocity as remapped momentum/mass, and specific energy as remapped extensive energy/mass. The central lesson is to transport conservative integrals first, rather than directly averaging intensives such as \(\rho\), \(\mathbf u\), or \(e\).

  • Signed swept region \(P_f\): its sign records the direction of face motion, while its magnitude is the volume exchanged across that face.
  • Donor selection: the sign of the swept volume selects the upwind donor cell; that same donor supplies the reconstructed state for the single face flux.
  • MS2 limiter: the limited linear reconstruction is monotone second order and prevents the remap from creating new local extrema.
  • Exact RZ geometry: polygon revolution evaluates both the swept volume and, for MS2, the swept polygon's exact first moment rather than a near-axis first-order approximation.
  • Energy closure: the documented closure adds the diagnostic-compatible kinetic-energy remap/projection discrepancy to material internal energy.
  • Corner-mass timing: corner masses remain invariant through Lagrangian motion and rejected proposals, and are rebuilt only after an accepted conservative remap.
  • Trigger metrics: cadence, cell quality, axis margin, and corner-J determine whether the geometry transaction is attempted.

MS2 limiters and energy closure

The opt-in ms2_moments path is a Margolin–Shashkov-style second-order moment remap. It reconstructs \(q(\mathbf x)=q_L+\nabla q_L\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_L)\) from a four-neighbor least-squares slope and limits it with van_leer or barth_jespersen. Using the exact first moment \(M_1(P_f)\), the donor integral is

$$ I_f=\rho_L|P_f|+\nabla\rho_L\cdot \left(M_1(P_f)-\mathbf x_L|P_f|\right). $$

(\(|P_f|\) is the revolved volume of the swept polygon \(P_f\), the same quantity as \(\Delta V_f\).) Legacy split and MS2 both use the same face flux with opposite signs on its two cells, so limiting preserves pairwise conservation. Per-sweep mass/internal/radiation positivity and final EOS reclosure remain separate gates.

Projecting cell momentum back to nodal velocity does not generally preserve kinetic energy exactly. The default-on ke_fixup is a control accepted for compatibility with legacy decks; what actually handles the post-projection kinetic-energy deficit is the following ke_conservation_closure. The explicit ke_conservation_closure (default off) remaps \(q_K=K_c^{diag}/V_c\) using the corner-mass/nodal-velocity measure (the result being \(K_c^{remap,*}\)), then deposits \(\Delta I_c=K_c^{remap,*}-K_c^{node,*}\) into material internal energy. An optional positivity redistribution draws a deficit from global positive capacity and records any unresolved remainder in a ledger.

Axis bands, Euler windows, and corner mass

axis_band_managed_remap_enabled is a default-off near-axis controller. When the row-K margin falls below its threshold, it remaps only \(i\in[0,K-1]\) to the equal-RZ-volume target

$$ r_i^{target}(j)=R_K(j)\sqrt{i/K},\qquad z_i^{target}(j)=z_K^{node}(j). $$

Here \(R_K(j)\) is the radius of the fixed row-\(K\) node at axial index \(j\), so the band nodes are respaced to equal revolved volumes while their axial coordinates follow row \(K\).

It widens K through the configured range while checking positivity and conservation ledgers. If every K fails, state is unchanged and control returns to legacy timestep reduction. Mass, momentum, electron/ion internal energy, and radiation groups are conserved; only the nodal-projection kinetic-energy delta is warning-level by construction.

euler_window.enabled is an opt-in multiblock CSR region target. A rectangle or annulus uses the frozen reference grid inside and Lagrangian coordinates outside, joined by a C1 transition weight. The blended target passes the candidate oracle, re-anchors the reference, and enters the same conservative-remap transaction as accepted M1 rezoning. It requires conservative remap and is mutually exclusive with M1/TMOP.

Hydrodynamic subzonal corner masses remain fixed during pure Lagrangian motion. A rejected geometric proposal does not change them; they are rebuilt from remapped cell mass and the post-remap mesh only after an accepted rezone/remap. This lifecycle preserves the Lagrangian invariance required by compatible work and anti-hourglass/subzonal-pressure terms.

One accepted ALE cycle, step by step

  1. Evaluate triggers. At the ALE entry point, evaluate scheduled cadence together with cell-quality, axis-margin, and corner-J guards. If nothing fires, retain the Lagrangian mesh and state unchanged.

  2. Construct a target. Run the selected Winslow, reference/barrier, or M1/TMOP solver family to propose node coordinates only. The managed controller confines this proposal to its selected axis band or Euler window.

  3. Gate admissibility. Test positive signed RZ volume, corner-J, 2×2 Gauss-J floors, the applicable axis margin, and the node path from the Lagrangian mesh to the target. Line search reduces the displacement when needed; safe backtracking validates \(\lambda=0\), searches \(\lambda=2^{-e}\), and refines the largest admissible blend. If no positive blend passes, restore the pre-transaction snapshot and stay Lagrangian.

  4. Build swept regions. Between every old and new face, construct the oriented polygon and evaluate its exact axisymmetric signed volume and, for the second-order path, exact first moments. The first directional sweep's intermediate volumes must remain positive.

  5. Transfer conservative ledgers. Select the donor from the swept-volume sign, reconstruct its state with the selected Van Leer or MS2 limiter, and apply each face flux once with opposite signs to its two cells. Mass and species/material ledgers, R/Z momentum, electron/ion internal energy, radiation-group energy, and the enabled diagnostic kinetic-energy ledger all follow this transaction; sweep positivity remains a rollback gate.

  6. Close and commit. Project remapped momentum, apply the documented kinetic-energy closure into internal energy when enabled, rebuild corner masses from the remapped cell mass and accepted mesh, and perform thermodynamic reclosure. Acceptance commits mesh and state together as one transaction; failure restores both.

  7. Audit the feature contract. Conservation ledgers gate the accepted transaction, while ALE-disabled, never-triggered, and identity-mode runs audit bitwise equality with pure Lagrangian evolution. These OFF/bit-identity and conservation checks gate the feature as a whole.

Second-generation ReALE (reale_v2)

mesh_mode="reale_v2" (default "fixed") opts into the second-generation rezone/remap machinery. Where the families above move node coordinates on a fixed connectivity, reale_v2 runs after each accepted full Lagrangian step commits: it advects cell-centroid generators (one representative seed per cell) with the cell-averaged nodal flow, builds a constrained-Voronoi/CVT (centroidal Voronoi tessellation) target on the current outer-boundary-plus-axis domain, rebuilds the mesh topology itself by exact tessellation, conservatively remaps the hydro fields, and installs the rebuilt stride-8 CSR topology before the next step. A target, tessellation, topology, or remap rejection leaves the accepted Lagrangian state unchanged for that cycle and emits [reale_v2] SKIP; successful commits emit [reale_v2] COMMIT. The pilot scope is deliberately narrow — Main.dimension="2D_RZ", one MPI rank, exactly one material, no PLIC or per-material conservation state, hydro enabled, radiation/conduction/laser/burn disabled, and Mesh.topology_scheme="multiblock_polar_tier" with polar_tier_native_pentagon=True; every other combination is a ConfigError. The mode makes no bitwise-reproducibility claim.

Nothing in this machinery is tunable: every threshold is a machine-epsilon-derived bound or an absolute predicate. Geometric predicates (orientation, in-circle) are decided by filtered exact evaluation — a fast double-precision value is accepted through a certified error bound (Shewchuk-style A-bounds widened by a factor of two for compiler-contraction safety), uncertain cases fall through to exact expansion arithmetic and, where needed, to symbolic perturbation (SoS) keyed solely on stable generator ids; every accepted sign equals the exact sign. The subsections below describe the mechanisms that govern its operation; the tessellator itself, the conservative overlay remap, and the persistent boundary carrier (Lagrangian nodes that own the domain polyline across generations, so boundary junctions are never promoted into the domain) are specified in full in the repository's docs/NUMERICS.md §15.

Need-based rezone trigger

The rezone fires on need, not cadence. There are four reasons, in priority order: (i) first — the trigger reference (the node set installed by the last committed rezone) does not exist yet. (ii) ceiling — TENRYU_REALE_REZONE_INTERVAL steps have elapsed since the last commit; this environment-only ceiling defaults to 1, preserving the historical every-step behavior, and has no namelist or frozen-config field. (iii) dt — the hydro timestep bound has fallen to half its reference value, captured on the first step after each commit. (iv) disp — some cell corner has moved at least \(h_c/2\) since the last commit, with \(h_c=\sqrt{A_c}\) for planar cell area \(A_c\): a CFL-class half-cell displacement. The two \(1/2\) factors are derived CFL-class constants, not tunables. The displacement census is GPU-resident — one per-cell max-reduce with a deterministic atomic max — and its reference is refreshed device-to-device at every commit. Each firing logs a [reale_trigger] line with its reason, and the COMMIT line carries trigger=.

Lloyd convergence exits — the d1 certified skip and the d2 representation floor

The target builder's Lloyd/CVT refinement (moving each generator toward its cell centroid to even out spacing; iteration ceiling 4) is likewise need-based. The primary exit is the certified predicate-margin skip (d1). Before the proposal's tessellation is built, every certificate of the current warm Delaunay triangulation (the triangulation carried over from the previous rezone) — triangle orientations, interior-edge in-circle tests, and hull convexity triples — is checked in pure double arithmetic for a positive filtered slack \(s=|\tilde D|-E\) (\(\tilde D\) the double-evaluated predicate determinant, \(E\) its certified error bound) against an interval-propagated bound \(\Delta_D\) on the determinant's change under the proposed per-site displacements (the product rule \(\Delta_{xy}\le U_x\Delta_y+U_y\Delta_x\) mirrored over the predicate's own expression tree). If \(2\Delta_D\le s\) holds for every predicate, no exact predicate sign can flip anywhere along the displacement path — so the proposal's Delaunay topology equals the current one — and the iteration is structurally a no-op: the loop breaks without adopting it. When the certificate refuses (degenerate slack on exactly cocircular or collinear sub-configurations, a mismatched warm base, or TENRYU_LLOYD_D1_DISABLE), control falls to the second exit. Conservativeness only reduces skips, so correctness is unconditional.

The second exit is the representation-spacing floor (d2): when every proposed site move is below that site's own coordinate representation-spacing scale \(\delta_i=\mathrm{ulp}(|r_i|)+\mathrm{ulp}(|z_i|)\) (ulp being the spacing to the adjacent double at that value), the iteration would pay a full tessellation to polish below the floating-point noise floor of the stored coordinates — the exit is evaluated before the proposal's tessellation is built, and the loop breaks. The measured regime where d1 matters is exactly where d2 is structurally silent: relaxed configurations whose Lloyd feedback limit-cycles at a total move near \(10^{-14}\) — above the representation floor, so d2 never fires — while the certified margin (typically \(10^{-6}\)–\(10^{-4}\) of the local spacing) fires immediately and stops the loop from paying its full four tessellations every rezone. Fires log one [lloyd_d1] line and surface as lloyd_d1_fired/lloyd_d1_iteration in the target result. Neither exit introduces a tunable, and the iteration ceiling is unchanged.

Representation-conditioned weld quotient — the derived floor \(\chi_e\le4\) and the two-band fail-loud

Before exposing its final DCEL (the edge-based topology representation), the exact restricted-Voronoi core applies a deterministic degeneracy quotient: edges too short for the coordinate representation are welded on the finding that they are rounding noise, not geometry. For a represented edge \(e=(a,b)\), take \(\ell_e^2=(r_b-r_a)^2+(z_b-z_a)^2\) and \(\delta_e=\{u(r_a)+u(r_b)+u(z_a)+u(z_b)\}/2\), where \(u(q)\) is the larger of the two binary64 spacings adjacent to \(q\). From each raw pre-weld cell's exact shoelace area \(A_c\) and exact-dyadic L1 perimeter \(P_c\), the local scale is \(h_c=2A_c/P_c\); each node takes the minimum over its incident cells, \(h_a\) (ties broken by stable site id), and every measured pair uses the single canonical value \(h_e=\min(h_a,h_b)\). Candidate admission, the complete-link certificate, and the final residual audit all use this one measurement, and the areas, perimeters, and scales are frozen from the raw DCEL — contractions never redefine the acceptance scale. A pair welds exactly when

$$ \chi_e=\frac{\ell_e^2}{\delta_e h_e}\le 4, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \ell_e^2 P_c\le 8\,\delta_e A_c $$

(the right form for the node-incident cell attaining \(h_e\)). Equality welds, and the comparison is an expansion-sign decision, not a rounded quotient against a tolerance. The floor 4 is derived, not tunable: the \(\pm2\)-ulp application band on \(\ell_e\) and the attribution spread between the pair's two node-\(h\) values each carry a factor-2-class ambiguity, so a measured \(\chi_e\le2^2=4\) cannot be robustly distinguished from the floor. The underlying balance compares retaining the represented short edge, \(E_{retain}=\delta_e/\ell_e\), against collapsing it, \(E_{collapse}=\ell_e/h_e\); using the L1 perimeter (\(P_{L1}\ge P_{L2}\)) makes the rule at most \(\sqrt2\) more conservative while keeping the decision exactly dyadic.

Contractions obey the carrier classes: interior nodes may contract only with interior nodes; slaves (dependent junctions on a carrier edge) only on the same carrier or into their own carrier endpoint master; axis nodes only within the same axis carrier or into its axis master. Boundary–interior, cross-carrier, axis/off-axis, and distinct master–master pairs are forbidden; a cluster contains at most one master, and complete-link clustering blocks macroscopic transitive chains. The fail-loud invariant is two-banded: a forbidden-class or master–master pair raises weld_forbidden_class_pair/weld_master_master only in the strict band \(\chi_e\le1\), where a sub-noise edge between unweldable nodes proves broken geometry. In the extended band \(1<\chi_e\le4\) the edge is not proven noise, so such pairs are retained silently and counted (extended_band_forbidden). The post-fixpoint final audit likewise rejects the rezone (weld_residual_subfloor, through the ordinary skip path) only for strict-band residuals; extended-band residuals of any class are census-only diagnostics.

DV-CLP velocity projection and the D3 KKT certificate

After the overlay remap, the nodal velocity field recovered from cell momentum is conditioned by the DV-CLP projection: componentwise in r and z, in the nodal mass metric, subject to every accepted per-edge velocity-jump budget (a certified upper bound on the velocity difference allowed across an edge). Components with at most 256 degrees of freedom keep the semismooth-Newton second-order-cone path with its exact certificate. Larger components use a deterministic ladder. D1, mass-metric Dykstra: in stable edge-id order, each edge projects its provisional r/z jump onto the budget 2-ball and distributes the correction between its endpoints about their pair mass center (a cap of 256 full sweeps; after every sweep the exact all-edge feasibility scan permits an early exit); a pass is the feasibility-only label dykstra_feasible. On D1 exhaustion, D4 quotient translation treats the connected components of non-violating edges as rigid clusters, reduces each violating cut edge to a shifted-ball constraint, and runs the same mass-metric Dykstra on cluster masses (a cluster containing an axis node has its radial translation frozen). If the quotient exhausts its cap or a residual violation survives the post-collapse re-scan, the pre-D1 component snapshot is restored bitwise and the existing homothetic completion runs.

D3 is the active-set KKT certificate that attempts to upgrade a dykstra_feasible component's label to a certified exact projection. The active set is identified by the derived band \(\mathrm{jump}\ge b_e\,\{1-8(w+2)\varepsilon\}\) (\(b_e\) the edge budget, \(w\) the predicate's walk-segment count, \(\varepsilon\) machine epsilon — the exact mirror of the feasibility predicate's upper band); zero-budget edges are equality-active and re-verified bitwise. A dual witness is constructed by leaf elimination over a stable spanning forest of the active subgraph, with independent r and z flows; cycle-closing edges, inactive edges, and axis-edge radial multipliers are pinned to zero. The certificate verifies per-node stationarity with a Neumaier compensated sum under the derived envelope \(8(\deg+2)\varepsilon\sum|\text{terms}|\), and the Euclidean normal-cone condition per active edge — the multiplier parallel to the jump and non-negative along it, within \(16\varepsilon\) relative envelopes. The stage never modifies velocities: the paired Dykstra updates conserve component momentum to rounding, so the equality-KKT primal correction (the component-translation null mode) is sub-roundoff by construction and the witness is purely diagnostic. On a full pass the component reports solver_class=4; on any clause failure the feasibility-only label 1 is kept — never demoted — and the failing clause is counted (kkt_fail_eq/kkt_fail_stationarity/kkt_fail_cone). The [dvclp_comp] solver classes are 0 = semismooth Newton, 1 = Dykstra-feasible, 2 = homothety fallback, 3 = quotient-translation feasible, and 4 = Dykstra-feasible with a passed active-set KKT certificate (an exact projection to rounding).

Consensus-admission reason codes and LATTICE_ZERO

An edge whose budget is provably zero-equivalent is treated as a consensus constraint — its endpoint velocities are forced equal — and contracted into a single unit per connected component. The admission proof is reason-coded: exactly zero budgets (ANALYTIC_ZERO), rounding-envelope certificates (ROBUST_ZERO), and the lattice class (LATTICE_ZERO). The lattice proof: when the certified budget upper bound lies below \(g_e=\min\bigl(\mathrm{ulp}(v_{r0}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{r1}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{z0}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{z1})\bigr)\) — the smallest representation spacing of the velocities over both endpoints and both components — no representable nonzero velocity difference exists at all, so equality is the only representable feasible point. Taking the minimum over endpoints and components is the conservative floor: a pair straddling binades can realize the finer operand's spacing. Admission always stands on the proof alone — an edge is never admitted merely because the fallback family would flatten it.

The staged-dt gate — production requirement rezone_min_dt_s

After remap and carrier-velocity projection, but before any staged array is installed, the host-side staged-dt gate evaluates every staged ring edge in cell/face index order. It transcribes the base CSW98 edge-AV CFL expression with the configured Numerics.hydro.av_cfl_coefficient (the AV limiter \(\psi\) fixed to 0 — a documented deviation that conservatively biases the bound toward rejection), and applies the node-crossing bound crossing_dt_safety × edge length ÷ closing speed on edges with positive face-direction closing speed. The rezone commits only when the minimum staged bound is strictly greater than rezone_min_dt_s; otherwise it emits a staged_dt SKIP with the bound, threshold, and binding cell, and the installed mesh is untouched (nothing was installed, so no rollback machinery is needed). The default is \(10^{-14}\) s, 0.0 disables the evaluation, and negative values are a ConfigError. The threshold is a deck-owned production requirement in the same class as Numerics.dt.max_s — never a geometric weld tolerance.

Post-commit re-anchor of the dt growth ladder

After each committed rezone the timestep controller may re-anchor the growth ladder (the lineage of per-step expansion caps produced by the growth limit on the timestep page) once, upward only: the first post-commit chosen \(\Delta t\) is raised to the minimum of all non-growth bounds and the last pre-spike chosen \(\Delta t\) (the most recent chosen value whose limiter was not the hydro bound). A spike edge that the rezone has removed is not a persistent constraint, so the ladder's memory of it is stale by construction; the pre-spike ceiling makes the trigger/re-anchor pair converge to the pre-spike operating point rather than oscillate, and a genuinely degraded mesh (hydro bound still low) keeps full ladder protection because the raise is bounded by the current hydro term. The lineage limiter records rezone_reanchor when the raise applies.

GPU tessellation gates — tess_gpu_dual/tess_gpu_restrict

Two stages of the exact tessellator can be offloaded to the GPU while preserving byte identity. Both are opt-in and default False, and both require a CUDA device at rezone time when enabled — they fail loud, with no silent host downgrade. tess_gpu_dual=True offloads the per-triangle exact circumcenter construction of the dual build. The device kernel replicates the host expansion pipeline operation for operation (explicit-fma two_product, tie-preferring magnitude merge, zero elision, exact power-of-two scaling; the translation unit is compiled with fmad contraction disabled), so admitted triangles return the host term sequences and the constructed circumcenter pair byte-for-byte. A per-triangle structural certificate — overflow of the fixed capacities derived from the operation tree (numerators of at most 48 terms, denominators of at most 16, working buffers capped at 64), a zero or non-positive denominator, or a non-finite accumulate — routes that triangle to the unchanged host exact path, processed in the deterministic class-emission order, so fallback and admitted triangles alike produce the host bytes. The kernel is a pure per-triangle map with no atomics, and buffers come from the persistent scratch pools (no per-call cudaMalloc). Verification: OFF leaves the host path untouched; ON is certified by canonical-serialization byte identity — a 69,626-site production cloud and synthetic cocircular grids, a forced-fallback identity case, and a per-class witness/constructed byte shadow gate.

tess_gpu_restrict=True (requires tess_gpu_dual=True, ConfigError otherwise) additionally offloads the restrict stage — the domain classification of dual nodes. It computes a certified brute-force winding number over all domain segments from the device-resident dual term sequences, where every z-compare and orientation sign is accepted only through the replicated running-error filters widened by the standard factor of two. A certified winding equals the exact winding, and certified nodes provably touch no segment (an exact zero orientation is never certifiable), so their classification — inside/outside with empty supports — is the exact host result; every uncertain, boundary-touching, or fallback-backed node runs the unchanged host classification in node order. Output is one status byte per node; determinism and byte identity follow as above. Both gates are a pure venue choice that changes no physical or numerical semantics by construction.

Verification

  • OFF-path bitwise gates require an ALE-disabled run to remain bit-identical in fields and history.
  • Remap conservation ledgers track mass, R/Z momentum, material internal energy, radiation-group energy, and explicit floor or redistribution terms. The general remap mass gate is \(\le 10^{-14}\) relative error. Managed axis-band unit gates use \(10^{-12}\) for mass/internal/radiation energy and \(10^{-10}\) for translation-invariant kinetic energy; runtime mass/internal/radiation/cell-momentum residuals roll back at \(10^{-8}\).
  • ale_identity_mode returns from every ALE entry point after Lagrange; it is a null test against an ALE=0 run at matched timesteps.
  • I-series batteries exercise production rezoning, admissibility, conservative remap, axis, and multiblock paths in coupled configurations.
  • reale_v2 verification is fail-closed by construction: any target, tessellation, topology, remap, or staged-dt gate violation SKIPs that cycle's rezone and leaves the committed Lagrangian state unchanged. The GPU tessellation gates are certified by canonical-serialization byte identity against the OFF path.
