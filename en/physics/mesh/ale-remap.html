ALE: rezoning & remap reference

TENRYU's 2D RZ (r–z axisymmetric) ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian) separates rezoning, which changes only mesh shape after Lagrangian hydrodynamics, from conservative remap, which transfers the material state to that mesh. The legacy Winslow family, reference targets and the reference barrier, and the M1/TMOP (target-matrix optimization paradigm [3]) objective-function solver connect to a common admissibility test. Only an accepted target receives a conservative transfer of mass, momentum, internal energies, and radiation-group energies. In addition to these methods, which move the mesh at fixed connectivity, the opt-in reconnection-based ALE (ReALE [5, 6]; mesh_mode="reale_v2") rebuilds the mesh connectivity (mesh topology) itself through the advection of generators (one representative point per cell) and exact tessellation (described below). The terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • A cycle is Lagrangian step → trigger → target mesh → admissibility test → conservative remap.
  • The default solver is bit-exact legacy Winslow; M1/TMOP and reference-barrier paths are opt-in.
  • Remap transports cell integrals, not intensive values, then recovers primitive state using the new volumes and masses.
  • A candidate that fails any of the RZ-volume, corner-Jacobian (corner-J), Gauss-point-Jacobian (Gauss-J), axis-margin, or sweep-positivity checks is reduced or rolled back.
  • The opt-in connectivity-changing ALE (mesh_mode="reale_v2") is a separate mechanism that rebuilds the mesh connectivity through Voronoi re-partitioning rather than relaxing node coordinates; its convergence, welding, and projection decisions are driven by certificates derived from machine epsilon.

Physical mechanism

A mesh that follows the material keeps interfaces sharp, but shear, vorticity, and converging asymmetries progressively strain that Lagrangian mesh: cells shear, flatten, and eventually invert, while the timestep collapses with the smallest cell altitude long before the material physics itself fails. ALE is the escape. TENRYU retains Lagrangian fidelity while the mesh is healthy; when it is not, it moves the mesh without moving the material. Rezoning proposes better node coordinates, and remap conservatively transfers the state onto them. The “arbitrary” in the name refers to exactly this freedom: the mesh velocity is pinned neither to the material velocity (Lagrangian) nor to zero (Eulerian) but may be chosen at will — TENRYU's choice being the material velocity while the mesh is healthy, and the rezoner's proposal once it is not.

Rezoning is geometry, not physics: it constructs only a target mesh through the Winslow-type smoothing and its constrained variants in the solver family below, and must not silently change the answer. RZ-volume positivity, corner-J floors, Gauss-point Jacobian floors, and line search with rollback enforce that separation. An unacceptable target is rejected and the cycle simply remains Lagrangian. Cadence, mesh-quality metrics, and axis/corner-J guards decide when a rezone is attempted at all.

Remap is a transaction that moves conserved quantities between old and new meshes with the same connectivity. Exact bookkeeping uses the signed volumes swept between their faces. Each swept region takes one donor-cell state, with slope reconstruction and limiting for monotone second order (a van Leer limiter by default, or the limiters of the opt-in MS2 (Margolin–Shashkov second-order [4]) moment remap), and transfers every extensive ledger—mass, species masses, momentum, internal and kinetic energy, and radiation-group energy—with one signed volume, one donor, and equal-and-opposite cell updates. Mesh motion therefore conserves those transported ledgers to round-off by construction. The kinetic-energy closure (ke_conservation_closure; below) deposits the kinetic-energy remap/projection discrepancy (projection: the operation that returns remapped cell momentum to nodal velocity — described below) into material internal energy, and subzonal corner masses (the masses of the subzones that split a cell around its nodes — used by the hourglass control and compatible energy bookkeeping of hydrodynamics) are rebuilt only after an accepted remap—the one point where their Lagrangian invariance may change.

The production managed ALE is a banded controller rather than a global relaxation. Axis bands and Euler windows confine where respacing acts—the axis is where RZ Lagrangian meshes fail first—and band-only swept-volume remap keeps the transaction local. The managed controller also preserves bitwise equality when the feature is off: if the controller is disabled or never triggered, the result is bitwise identical to the pure Lagrangian calculation.

Terms used in this calculation

The ALE calculation has terms of its own for how the mesh is moved and for the conditions under which the result is accepted. The quantities that measure mesh quality (the admissibility test, the corner Jacobian, the Gauss-point Jacobian, and the axis margin) are defined on the Meshes & geometry page. This page describes only when and for what ALE uses them (ALE cycle and triggers, Admissibility and acceptance checks). The first half below (from transaction to the M1 objective) covers the terms of the methods that keep the connectivity fixed; the second half (from generator to the reale_v2 procedure) covers those of the method that changes the connectivity (reale_v2).

Transaction and rollback

A transaction is the procedure that decides, with rezoning and remap taken as one unit, whether to commit or reject. At the start of a transaction a copy (snapshot) of the node coordinates and the state is taken. If anything fails — target-mesh generation, the admissibility test, the conservative remap, the corner-mass rebuild, or the re-closure (definition) — both coordinates and state are restored from the snapshot (rollback), and the step stays Lagrangian. Only when everything passes are the coordinates and state committed together (acceptance). An intermediate state in which the coordinates have moved but the state has not been transferred, or the reverse, never leaves the transaction. The unit matters because a mismatch between mesh and state would break conservation when density is recovered from volume. For M1, acceptance additionally requires a test on the decrease of the objective (The M1 objective).

Axis band, row-K margin, and managed ALE

An axis band is the band of \(K\) radial rows of cells (rows \(i=0,\dots,K-1\)) that touch the symmetry axis in a single-block 2D RZ mesh. A Lagrangian RZ mesh fails first near the axis, so only the inside of the axis band is returned to a configuration of equal RZ volumes (the equation is in Axis bands, Euler windows, and corner mass), and the state is remapped conservatively. Nodes and state outside the band are unchanged. The width \(K\) defaults to axis_band_managed_remap_width (default 3) and is capped by axis_band_managed_remap_max_width (default 6).

The row-\(K\) margin is a dimensionless quantity that tells how much the outermost cell of the axis band has shrunk relative to an equal-volume configuration. It is the minimum, over all axial columns, of the ratio of the volume \(V_{\rm actual}\) of the row-\(K-1\) cell to the volume per row \(V_{\rm target}=\pi R_K^2\Delta z/K\) obtained when the axis band is respaced to equal volumes, and it equals 1 for an equal-volume configuration (\(R_K\) is the mean radius of the two row-\(K\) nodes at the ends of the column, and \(\Delta z\) is the difference between the z coordinates of the midpoints of the upper and lower faces of the row-\(K-1\) cell). It is a different quantity from the axis margin (definition; units cm²), which measures the headroom against inversion of the cells that touch the axis.

Managed means that this respacing is done through a controller (axis_band_managed_remap_enabled, default False) that decides the trigger, the width \(K\), and acceptance. The controller evaluates the trigger conditions before each step and, when the operator splitting is a Strang splitting, also after each hydro half-step (axis_band_managed_remap_every_hydro_half_step, default True). The margin floor used in the trigger test is axis_band_managed_remap_margin_trigger (default 1.0e-4), and the cap on the kinetic-energy return is axis_band_managed_remap_kinetic_return_max_fraction (default 0.1).

Euler window

An Euler window is a region of space within which the mesh is kept fixed to a reference grid (the coordinates of the initial mesh). It is given as a rectangle (shape="rectangle", with range r0, r1, z0, z1) or an annulus ("annulus", with center cr, cz, inner radius rad_in, and outer radius rad_out). Nodes inside the window are placed on the reference grid, nodes outside on the Lagrangian coordinates, and a smooth transition of width transition_width joins them. The cell weight \(\eta\) is set from the signed distance \(d\) of the cell centroid outside the window (\(d\le0\) inside) and the transition width \(\delta\) (transition_width): \(\eta=1\) for \(d\le0\), \(\eta=1-t^2(3-2t)\) with \(t=d/\delta\) for \(0<d<\delta\), and \(\eta=0\) for \(d\ge\delta\). A node's weight is the mean of the weights of the cells that meet at it. The target coordinates are \(\mathbf x^{target}=\eta\,\mathbf x^{ref}+(1-\eta)\,\mathbf x^{L}\), with \(\mathbf x^{ref}\) the reference grid and \(\mathbf x^{L}\) the Lagrangian coordinates. Inside the window the resolution stays that of the reference grid; outside, the mesh follows the material. Several windows (euler_windows) are overlaid by taking the maximum of the node weights.

An Euler window can be used for a multiblock 2D RZ mesh with CSR (compressed sparse row) connectivity when conservative_remap_enabled=True, and not together with M1. The window acts only from t_on_s (default 0) to t_off_s (default −1; no end when negative).

Reference mesh, reference target, and reference barrier

The reference mesh is the node coordinates held in the state (x_r_reference, x_z_reference). For multiblock meshes they keep the coordinates of the mesh at initialization and are not updated at each transaction. The target mesh of a remap is placed at these coordinates only for the duration of the transaction, and the original coordinates are restored after the remap. The shape measure of M1 and the relative floors of the reference barrier are taken against these coordinates.

The reference target is the mesh toward which the reference-barrier rezone moves the nodes, selected by reference_target. "none" (default) creates no displacement. "eulerian_initial" is the coordinates of the initial mesh (the Eulerian grid). "spherical_equal_angle" is the spherical mesh \((r,z)=(s_i\sin\theta_j,\;s_i\cos\theta_j)\) that keeps the radius \(s_i\) of shell \(i\) of the initial mesh (the distance of node \((i,0)\) from the origin) and spaces the nodes at equal angles \(\theta_j=\pi j/n_z\) in the axial direction.

The reference barrier is a protective rezone that starts without waiting for the cadence (reference_barrier_enabled, default False). It adds the displacement \(\Delta\mathbf x\) from the current mesh toward the reference target with a blend factor \(\lambda\). The line search halves \(\lambda\) from reference_blend_default (default 1.0), up to reference_linesearch_max_iters times (default 24), and takes the largest value at which the RZ volume, corner Jacobians, and Gauss-point Jacobian stay above floors relative to the reference mesh (default 1.0e-8). It starts under either of two conditions, both enabled by default. (a) The minimum of the cell-centroid \(\sin\theta=|r_c|/\sqrt{r_c^2+z_c^2}\) (\(\theta\) is the polar angle from the +z axis) falls below reference_trigger_axis_margin_threshold (default 1.0e-2). This \(\sin\theta\) is a different quantity from the axis margin (units cm²). (b) The minimum of the corner-Jacobian ratio (the current value of each corner divided by its value in the initial mesh) and the diagonal-corner length ratio (the ratio, to the initial mesh, of the mean \(b_{\rm eff}\) of the lengths of the two edges that meet at one corner of the cell) falls below reference_trigger_corner_j_ratio_threshold (default 0.5).

The M1 objective

The M1 objective is the mesh-quality function that the rezone solver m1_tmop minimizes. “M1” is the prefix of the keys of this objective (m1_*), and the objective follows TMOP (target-matrix optimization paradigm [3]). The equation is given in Rezone solver family. It is the sum of four terms, with default weights of 0 for the alignment weight \(\gamma_{\rm align}\) (m1_gamma_align), 0 for the tether weight \(\lambda_{\rm tether}\) (m1_lambda_tether), 0 for the polar-row spacing weight \(w_\theta\) (m1_theta_reg), and 1.0e-3 for the barrier weight \(\beta\) (m1_barrier_beta).

The shape-quality term (the M1 quality term) takes, at each quadrature point of a cell, the matrix \(T=A_{cq}W_{cq}^{-1}\), which measures the Jacobian matrix \(A_{cq}\) of the actual mesh against the Jacobian matrix \(W_{cq}\) of the reference mesh, and averages its shape measure \(\mu=\|T\|_F^2/(2\det T)\) with weights \(\omega_{cq}\det W_{cq}\). \(\mu\) is smaller the closer the cell's shape is to that of the corresponding reference-mesh cell (apart from scaling and rotation), and its minimum is 1. The alignment weight \(\gamma_{\rm align}\) adds \(\gamma_{\rm align}\|T-I\|_F^2\), which also brings the cell closer to the size and orientation of the reference cell. The tether term penalizes the movement of a node from its Lagrangian position. The polar-row spacing term penalizes the imbalance between the distances to the two neighboring nodes along a row of a polar-type block. The barrier term acts only at quadrature points where \(\det A_{cq}\) falls below 0.1 times the reference value, and strongly penalizes a cell collapsing (its determinant approaching zero).

An M1 transaction repeats the update of all nodes m1_sweeps times (default 8) and submits the resulting candidate as it is to the test. There is no search over the blend factor. The candidate is accepted only when it passes the admissibility test and the objective has decreased by more than the relative amount m1_min_j_dec_rel (default 0.0); otherwise the transaction is rolled back.

Generators and the Voronoi tessellation

A generator is the point that represents a cell in reale_v2, placed at the cell centroid (also called a site; its identifier is the stable cell number). There are as many generators as cells.

A Voronoi tessellation divides the domain into the sets of points nearest to each generator (the Voronoi cells), and each Voronoi cell becomes a cell of the new mesh. A tessellation whose Voronoi cells are cut to the region (the domain) enclosed by the current outer boundary and the axis is called restricted. The circumcenter of each triangle of the Delaunay triangulation is a vertex of a Voronoi cell and is called a dual node. The restrict stage classifies the dual nodes as inside or outside the domain by winding number and constructs exactly the intersections of the Voronoi edges with the domain boundary.

A Lloyd iteration moves each generator to the planar centroid of its Voronoi cell to even out the spacing, and the configuration to which it converges is the CVT (centroidal Voronoi tessellation). The iteration ceiling is reale_lloyd_max (default 4).

Certificate

A certificate is a check that guarantees a property holds under error bounds that include rounding, together with the record that it passed. reale_v2 uses certificates for five kinds of objects. (1) The sign of a predicate: when a double-precision orientation or in-circle value exceeds its certified error bound, its sign equals the exact sign. (2) The Delaunay triangulation: when the slack of the triangle orientations, interior-edge in-circle tests, and hull convexity triples that support the current triangulation is at least twice the bound on its change under the generator motion, the connectivity is guaranteed unchanged (used for the skip of the Lloyd iteration). (3) The weld of short edges: every pair of nodes of the groups to be merged satisfies the weld test (complete linkage; weld). (4) The velocity projection: the KKT conditions of the active set hold to rounding (projection). (5) The GPU circumcenter construction: the per-triangle structural certificate guarantees that no fixed capacity was exceeded, no denominator was non-positive, and no accumulation was non-finite (GPU tessellation). When a certificate does not hold, the result is not adopted, and the calculation proceeds on the safe side (no skip of the Lloyd iteration, the exact host path, no upgrade of the solver class, and so on).

Persistent boundary carrier, master, and slave

The persistent boundary carrier is the mechanism that keeps the domain boundary (the polyline of the outer boundary and the axis) on the same Lagrangian nodes across rezones. The boundary polyline is defined only by masters, that is, persistent Lagrangian nodes chosen from the boundary nodes of the first mesh. The junctions where the edges of Voronoi cells meet the boundary are rebuilt at every rezone as slaves. A slave lies at an exact position \(\lambda\) (from 0 to 1) on a carrier edge joining two neighboring masters A and B, and its position and velocity are given by \(\mathbf X_s=(1-\lambda)\mathbf X_A+\lambda\mathbf X_B\). In the Lagrangian phase, the mass and force of a slave are condensed onto the two masters of its edge, and its position and velocity are reconstructed from the masters.

The carrier is needed to stop the number of boundary vertices from growing. If the domain is re-extracted from the mesh boundary at every rezone, junctions on the boundary of the Voronoi tessellation are promoted to corners of the domain, the number of boundary vertices grows, and the number of vertices of a cell grows without bound (the measurement in docs/NUMERICS.md §15.3 of the repository gives about 420 vertices per rezone). With the carrier, the number of domain vertices stays at the number of masters, and the number of mesh nodes does not change.

Velocity-jump budget

The velocity-jump budget is the allowed upper bound, set for each edge of the new mesh, on the difference between the nodal velocities at its two ends (the velocity jump). It is obtained by reconstructing the velocity field of the old mesh linearly on each triangle after dividing the cells into triangles, walking along the new edge, and adding up the magnitude of the change of velocity along the edge (for each segment, the magnitude of the directional derivative of the velocity along the edge vector times the fraction of the edge that the segment covers). On an edge where the budget is exceeded, the difference of the remapped nodal velocities is larger than the amount by which the velocity field of the old mesh changed along the same edge. The velocity projection moves the nodal velocities to the nearest values in the nodal-mass metric (momentum is conserved) so that the velocity jump of every edge is at most its budget. An edge whose budget is zero to rounding becomes a consensus constraint that makes the velocities at its two ends equal (consensus constraint).

The reale_v2 procedure

After an accepted Lagrangian step commits, the procedure runs in the order below. If anything is rejected, the rest is not performed, and [reale_v2] SKIP is emitted without changing the committed state (with the name of the rejecting stage and the reason).

  1. Decide the trigger. Proceed only when one of the four reasons in the need-based trigger holds.

  2. Build the generators. Place a generator at the centroid of each cell, and take the mean of the cell's nodal velocities as the flow of that cell.

  3. Build the target. Advect the generators with the flow over \(\Delta t\), then even out the spacing with the Lloyd iteration. Generators on the axis stay on the axis.

  4. Tessellate. Build exactly the restricted Voronoi tessellation of the target generators on the domain.

  5. Weld and build the staged arrays. Weld short edges in the exact core (weld), merge short edges by reale_short_edge_collapse_rel just before installation, and assemble the arrays of the new mesh (the staged arrays, that is, the arrays before installation). Check the boundary consistency and the geometry.

  6. Remap. Cut each old cell into the pieces nearest to each new generator and move the conserved quantities to the new cells (conservative overlay remap). If reale_corner_mass_reset is True, rebuild the corner masses. Then project the nodal velocities with the velocity-jump budget (projection) and project the velocities of the boundary carrier.

  7. Check the timestep. Confirm that the timestep bound of the mesh before installation is larger than rezone_min_dt_s (check).

  8. Install. Write the assembled arrays into the state (installation; COMMIT in the log). The first timestep after installation receives the raise of the growth-limit basis.

ALE cycle and triggers

Figure A shows one cycle. The normal entry is the post-Lagrange point. The ALE stage runs on the steps set by the every_n_steps cadence (default 5; every step when it is 1 or less). For a single-block structured mesh with a Winslow-family solver (legacy_winslow, rz_full_metric_winslow), the cell quality is examined on a cadence step, and the mesh is rezoned only when quality has degraded. The quality of a quadrilateral cell, sampled with the bilinear map at the four 2×2 Gauss points, is defined as

$$ q_c=\frac{J_{\min}}{J_{\max}}, \qquad \min_c q_c<q_{\mathrm{threshold}}. $$

The default is quality_threshold=0.2; \(q_c=1\) denotes a parallelogram, while zero indicates degeneracy. The rezoning of M1 and of multiblock (CSR-connectivity) meshes does not use this threshold; it builds a target mesh on every cadence step. spacing_ratio_threshold is a parsed and validated reserved key with no runtime firing rule in the current implementation (docs/NUMERICS.md §3.3 in the repository defines none either), so it is not counted as an active trigger here.

Protective paths can bypass the cadence. The preventive axis guard (single-block meshes) forces rezoning when the minimum axis margin (definition) falls below preventive_axis_guard_fraction (default 0.1) times its initial value. The default-off pre-hydro corner-J trigger (Numerics.hydro.corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_enabled) finds where a signed corner Jacobian reaches its floor (corner_jacobian_floor_eps, default 1.0e-6) along the predicted trajectory and invokes ALE before operator splitting when the admissible fraction of the predicted one-step node displacement — how far it can proceed before a corner Jacobian reaches its floor, minimized over all corners — falls below the threshold (corner_jacobian_ale_trigger_scale, default 0.5). The reference barrier (terms) uses the cell-centroid \(\sin\theta\), the corner-J ratio, and the diagonal-corner length ratio as dedicated triggers.

Rezone solver family

legacy_winslow preserves the simplified orthogonal Winslow update [1] bit-exactly. Metric coefficients are lagged from the current logical-grid coordinates in a Jacobi iteration, and the cross derivative is omitted (here \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are the Winslow metric coefficients — unrelated to the radiation pages' symbols):

$$ r_{i,j}^{new}=\frac{\alpha(r_{i+1,j}+r_{i-1,j})+\beta(r_{i,j+1}+r_{i,j-1})} {2(\alpha+\beta)}. $$

The same equation updates \(z\). An opt-in full R-Z metric variant (rezone_solver="rz_full_metric_winslow") adds the mixed derivative and an R-weighted candidate. Its node-local line search, active when rezone_local_admissibility_linesearch=True (default False), tests incident-cell Gauss-J, corner-J, signed RZ volume, and non-axis radii.

The reference-barrier solver (terms) constructs displacements toward reference targets such as the initial Eulerian mesh or an equal-angle spherical mesh, then blends them with a global line search with reference-relative floors. The line search halves the blend factor \(\lambda\) from reference_blend_default (default 1.0), up to reference_linesearch_max_iters times (default 24), and takes the largest \(\lambda\) at which the candidate mesh's RZ volume, corner Jacobians, and Gauss-point Jacobian stay above floors relative to the reference mesh (reference_volume_floor_rel, reference_corner_j_floor_rel, reference_gauss_j_floor_rel; all default 1.0e-8). The Gauss-point Jacobian in this test is evaluated as the Jacobian at the cell center (the mean of the four corner Jacobians). In the multiblock center patch (multiblock_lagrangian_bulk_center_patch_reference_enabled, default False), a Winslow smoothing proposal is first moved by barrier optimization into the feasible set defined by the volume and corner-J floors, and then passes through the same kind of global line search.

m1_tmop is an opt-in damped Gauss–Newton solver. Its objective (the M1 objective) combines M1 quality relative to a reference metric, a tether to the Lagrangian position, polar-row spacing regularization, and a small-determinant activation barrier:

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal J(\mathbf x)={}& \frac{\sum_{c,q}\omega_{cq}\det(W_{cq})\,\mu(A_{cq}W_{cq}^{-1};\gamma_{\rm align})} {\sum_{c,q}\omega_{cq}\det(W_{cq})} +\frac{\lambda_{\rm tether}}{N_v}\sum_i\frac{\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_i^L\|^2}{h_i^2}\\ &\quad+\frac{w_\theta}{N_v}\sum_{i\in\mathcal R} \left(\frac{d_{iL}-d_{iR}}{d_{iL}+d_{iR}}\right)^2 +\beta\sum_{c,q}\left[\det A_{cq}<\sigma_b\det W_{cq}\right] \log\frac{\sigma_b\det W_{cq}}{\det A_{cq}}. \end{aligned} $$

Here \(A_{cq}\) and \(W_{cq}\) are the Jacobian matrices of the actual and reference meshes' maps at quadrature point \(q\) of cell \(c\) (for a quadrilateral, one of the 2×2 Gauss points; \(\det A_{cq}\) is the Gauss-point Jacobian there), with weights \(\omega_{cq}\) and the shape-quality measure \(\mu\) at alignment weight \(\gamma_{\rm align}\) (the reciprocal of Knupp's shape measure [2] plus an alignment term) — the M1 quality term; \(\lambda_{\rm tether}\) penalizes displacement from the Lagrangian positions \(\mathbf x^L_i\) on the local scale \(h_i\) (the root-mean-square length of the reference-mesh edges meeting node \(i\)); \(N_v\) is the total node count; the \(w_\theta\) term regularizes polar-row spacing through the left/right neighbor distances \(d_{iL},d_{iR}\) over the row set \(\mathcal R\); and the barrier, with weight \(\beta\) (a different quantity from the Winslow coefficient \(\beta\); default 1.0e-3), acts only where \(\det A_{cq}<\sigma_b\det W_{cq}\), that is, where \(\det A_{cq}\) falls below \(\sigma_b=0.1\) (a constant in the implementation) times the reference value.

Each node update caps the proposed displacement at 1/4 of the shortest distance from the node to its neighbors in the incident cells, then halves it up to 12 times until the local objective decreases. This update is repeated over all nodes m1_sweeps times (default 8). A transaction is accepted only when the candidate-mesh oracle finds the mesh admissible and

$$ \frac{\mathcal J_{before}-\mathcal J_{after}} {\max(|\mathcal J_{before}|,10^{-300})} >\texttt{m1\_min\_j\_dec\_rel}. $$

The 0.0 default requires a strict decrease while retaining small useful improvements. In the multiblock path, the M1 reference mesh is a fixed reference that keeps the coordinates of the mesh at initialization and does not move at each acceptance. The accepted coordinates are placed at the reference positions only for the duration of the conservative remap, as its target mesh, and the reference returns to its original coordinates after the remap. The configuration check rejects rezone_solver="m1_tmop" for the logical meshes rectangular_rz and cone_shell (the error message calls this restriction the scope of “M1 v1”). The polar-row spacing term acts only on rows of polar-type blocks.

Admissibility and acceptance checks

Rezoner convergence is not mesh acceptance. Acceptance is decided by the admissibility test (definition). Every active candidate cell must retain positive signed RZ volume and corner-J, satisfy the 2×2 Gauss-J floors, and, for axis cells, have a positive axis margin. The first directional-remap sweep must also keep intermediate volumes positive. For single-block meshes, the default backtracking tries the blend factor \(\lambda\) (which blends the Lagrangian mesh toward the proposed target) at 1, 1/2, 1/4, … in turn, halving at each rung for up to 12 rungs (the smallest is 1/2048), and takes the first value that passes. The opt-in safe backtracking (safe_backtrack_enabled) first validates the Lagrangian mesh at \(\lambda=0\). It then tries \(\lambda=2^{-e}\), with the exponent \(e\) running from 0 up to safe_backtrack_min_exp (default 20), from the largest value down, and takes the first value that passes as \(\lambda_{\rm lo}\). It then bisects between \(\lambda_{\rm lo}\) and twice that value (capped at 1) safe_backtrack_binary_iters times (default 8) to find the largest admissible blend. If no positive value passes, the mesh and state roll back to the pre-transaction snapshot.

The floors differ by method. In the default backtracking of single-block meshes, a candidate is rejected when any 2×2 Gauss-point Jacobian is negative, any corner Jacobian is zero or negative, or the axis margin is zero or negative (with reject_zero_gauss_j=True, default False, it is also rejected when the minimum Gauss-point Jacobian is at most \(\max(10^{-30},\,10^{-8}J_{\max})\)). The candidate-mesh oracle used by the reference barrier, M1, the center patch, and similar paths requires the RZ volume, corner Jacobians, and Gauss-point Jacobian each to stay above floors relative to the values of the reference mesh (default 1.0e-8; Rezone solver family).

Thus, a smaller objective or Winslow residual alone never commits a mesh. Reference targets, M1 targets, and managed repairs all end at the same geometry oracle and remap-positivity checks.

Swept-region conservative remap

Figure B shows the swept region and the transfer from the donor to the receiving cell in a one-dimensional strip. The default legacy_split alternates the ordering of R and Z swept-region sweeps. Revolving the meridional polygon \(P_f\) between an old and new face gives the signed volume

$$ \Delta V_f=V(P_f)=\frac{\pi}{3}\sum_k(r_k+r_{k+1}) (r_kz_{k+1}-r_{k+1}z_k). $$

Here \((r_k,z_k)\) are the vertices of the meridional polygon \(P_f\) swept between the old and new face; the prefactor \(\pi/3\) comes from the exact revolution about the axis.

For each internal face, TENRYU chooses one donor and computes one flux, then applies equal-and-opposite updates. A conservative cell integral \(\bar q=qV\) changes as

$$ \bar q_i^{new}=\bar q_i^{old}-F_{i+1/2}+F_{i-1/2}. $$

(\(F_{i\pm1/2}\) are the conserved-quantity fluxes through the two faces of cell \(i\), evaluated from the donor-side reconstructed state and the swept volume.) The transferred quantities are mass, R/Z momentum, electron/ion internal energy, material volume, and every radiation-group energy. Density is recovered as \(m^{new}/V^{new}\), velocity as remapped momentum/mass, and specific energy as remapped extensive energy/mass. Remap transports conservative integrals first, rather than directly averaging intensives such as \(\rho\), \(\mathbf u\), or \(e\).

  • Signed swept region \(P_f\): its sign records the direction of face motion, while its magnitude is the volume exchanged across that face.
  • Donor selection: the sign of the swept volume selects the upwind donor cell; that same donor supplies the reconstructed state for the single face flux.
  • MS2 limiter: the limited linear reconstruction is monotone second order and prevents the remap from creating new local extrema.
  • Exact RZ geometry: polygon revolution evaluates both the swept volume and, for MS2, the swept polygon's exact first moment rather than a near-axis first-order approximation.
  • Kinetic-energy budget: ke_conservation_closure adds the diagnostic-compatible kinetic-energy remap/projection discrepancy to material internal energy.
  • Corner-mass timing: corner masses remain invariant through Lagrangian motion and rejected proposals, and are rebuilt only after an accepted conservative remap.
  • Trigger metrics: cadence, cell quality, axis margin, and corner-J determine whether the geometry transaction is attempted.

MS2 limiters and the kinetic-energy budget

The opt-in ms2_moments path is a Margolin–Shashkov-style second-order moment remap. It reconstructs \(q(\mathbf x)=q_L+\nabla q_L\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_L)\) from a four-neighbor least-squares slope and limits it with van_leer or barth_jespersen. Using the exact first moment \(M_1(P_f)\), the donor integral is

$$ I_f=\rho_L|P_f|+\nabla\rho_L\cdot \left(M_1(P_f)-\mathbf x_L|P_f|\right). $$

(\(|P_f|\) is the revolved volume of the swept polygon \(P_f\), the same quantity as \(\Delta V_f\).) Legacy split and MS2 both use the same face flux with opposite signs on its two cells, so limiting preserves pairwise conservation. Per-sweep mass/internal/radiation positivity and final EOS re-closure remain separate checks.

Projecting cell momentum back to nodal velocity does not generally preserve kinetic energy exactly. The default-on ke_fixup is a control accepted for compatibility with legacy decks (definition); the following ke_conservation_closure actually handles the post-projection kinetic-energy deficit. ke_conservation_closure remaps \(q_K=K_c^{diag}/V_c\) using the corner-mass/nodal-velocity measure (the result being \(K_c^{remap,*}\)), then deposits \(\Delta I_c=K_c^{remap,*}-K_c^{node,*}\) into material internal energy: all of it into the single energy in one temperature, all of it into the ions in two. It is read by the single-block ALE step only (a single-block 2D_RZ mesh with ALE enabled, outside the identity mode, with band ALE off or composed with the rezone). There it is off by default in one temperature and on by default in two (an explicit False is honored with a warning), because without it the remap and the node projection change the kinetic energy and the ions never receive the change. An optional positivity redistribution draws a deficit from global positive capacity and records any unresolved remainder in a ledger.

Axis bands, Euler windows, and corner mass

axis_band_managed_remap_enabled is a default-off near-axis controller (axis band), usable only with a single-block mesh (Mesh.topology_scheme="single_block"). The band width \(K\) (number of rows) defaults to axis_band_managed_remap_width (default 3) and is capped by axis_band_managed_remap_max_width (default 6). When the row-\(K\) margin (definition) at the default width is below 1 and, for some \(K\) from that width up to the cap, the row-\(K\) margin is at least axis_band_managed_remap_margin_trigger (default 1.0e-4), the controller remaps only \(i\in[0,K-1]\), starting from the smallest such \(K\), to the equal-RZ-volume target

$$ r_i^{target}(j)=R_K(j)\sqrt{i/K},\qquad z_i^{target}(j)=z_K^{node}(j). $$

Here \(R_K(j)\) is the radius of the fixed row-\(K\) node at axial index \(j\), so the band nodes are respaced to equal revolved volumes while their axial coordinates follow row \(K\).

It widens \(K\) one row at a time from the chosen value up to the cap, trying the band remap at each \(K\). It checks positivity, the conservation ledgers, the cap on the kinetic-energy return (axis_band_managed_remap_kinetic_return_max_fraction, default 0.1, as a fraction of the internal energy of the band cell), and the re-closure through the EOS, and takes the first \(K\) that satisfies all of them. If every \(K\) fails, state is unchanged and control returns to legacy timestep reduction. Mass, momentum, electron/ion internal energy, and radiation groups are conserved; only the nodal-projection kinetic-energy delta is warning-level by construction.

euler_window.enabled is an opt-in target mesh for multiblock regions with CSR connectivity (Euler window). A rectangle or annulus uses the fixed reference grid inside and Lagrangian coordinates outside, joined by a C¹-continuous transition weight. When the blended target passes the candidate-mesh check, it is placed at the reference positions as the target mesh of the remap, by the same procedure as an accepted M1 rezone, and it enters the conservative-remap transaction. After the remap, the reference coordinates return to their original values. It requires conservative remap and is mutually exclusive with M1/TMOP.

Hydrodynamic subzonal corner masses remain fixed during pure Lagrangian motion. A rejected geometric proposal does not change them; they are rebuilt from remapped cell mass and the post-remap mesh only after an accepted rezone/remap. This lifecycle preserves the Lagrangian invariance required by compatible work and anti-hourglass/subzonal-pressure terms.

One accepted ALE cycle, step by step

  1. Evaluate triggers. At the ALE entry point, evaluate scheduled cadence together with cell-quality, axis-margin, and corner-J guards. If nothing fires, retain the Lagrangian mesh and state unchanged.

  2. Construct a target. Run the selected Winslow, reference-barrier, or M1/TMOP solver to propose node coordinates only. The managed controller confines this proposal to its selected axis band or Euler window.

  3. Test admissibility. On the candidate mesh, test for positive signed RZ volume, the corner-J and 2×2 Gauss-J floors, and the applicable axis margin. Line search reduces the displacement when needed; the opt-in safe backtracking validates \(\lambda=0\), searches \(\lambda=2^{-e}\), and refines the largest admissible blend. If no positive blend passes, restore the pre-transaction snapshot and stay Lagrangian.

  4. Build swept regions. Between every old and new face, construct the oriented polygon and evaluate its exact axisymmetric signed volume and, for the second-order path, exact first moments. The first directional sweep's intermediate volumes must remain positive.

  5. Transfer conservative ledgers. Select the donor from the swept-volume sign, reconstruct its state with the selected Van Leer or MS2 limiter, and apply each face flux once with opposite signs to its two cells. Mass and species/material ledgers, R/Z momentum, electron/ion internal energy, radiation-group energy, and the enabled diagnostic kinetic-energy ledger all follow this transaction; sweep positivity remains a rollback condition.

  6. Close the kinetic-energy budget and commit. Project remapped momentum, deposit the kinetic-energy difference into internal energy through ke_conservation_closure when enabled, rebuild corner masses from the remapped cell mass and accepted mesh, and perform re-closure. Acceptance commits mesh and state together as one transaction; failure restores both.

  7. Check equality with the feature off. Conservation ledgers are a condition for accepting the transaction, and ALE-disabled, never-triggered, and identity-mode runs are checked for bitwise equality with pure Lagrangian evolution. Passing these OFF/bit-identity and conservation checks is the acceptance condition for the feature as a whole.

Connectivity-changing ALE (reale_v2)

mesh_mode="reale_v2" (default "fixed") opts into rezone/remap that changes the mesh connectivity. Where the methods above move node coordinates on a fixed connectivity, reale_v2 runs after each accepted full Lagrangian step commits: it advects cell-centroid generators (one representative point per cell, also called a site) with the cell-averaged nodal flow (the mean of the cell's nodal velocities), builds a restricted-Voronoi/CVT target on the current outer-boundary-plus-axis domain, rebuilds the mesh connectivity itself by exact tessellation, conservatively remaps the hydro fields, and installs the rebuilt CSR connectivity, with a fixed width of 16 vertex slots per cell (stride-16), before the next step. The procedure is given in the terms section. A target, tessellation, connectivity, or remap rejection leaves the accepted Lagrangian state unchanged for that cycle and emits [reale_v2] SKIP; a successful installation emits [reale_v2] COMMIT. The scope is deliberately narrow (the error messages that report a configuration mistake call this scope “pilot”) — Main.dimension="2D_RZ", one MPI (Message Passing Interface) rank, exactly one material, no PLIC (piecewise linear interface calculation) or per-material conservation state, hydro enabled, radiation/conduction/laser/burn disabled, Mesh.topology_scheme="multiblock_polar_tier" with polar_tier_native_pentagon=True, and no armed legacy ALE lane (Mesh.motion="ale" together with Numerics.ale.enabled=True is rejected); every other combination is a ConfigError. The mode makes no bitwise-reproducibility claim.

The geometric predicates, the short-edge weld of the exact core (below), and the certificates of this method have no tunable tolerance: every threshold in them is a machine-epsilon-derived bound or an absolute predicate. Some namelist keys are tunable, however. The rezone trigger has reale_dt_trigger_factor and reale_dt_trigger_cooldown, and the Lloyd iteration ceiling has reale_lloyd_max (below); their defaults equal the former fixed values. In addition there are the short-edge merge just before installation (reale_short_edge_collapse_rel), the tolerance on the additivity of the remap overlay (reale_overlay_additivity_tol), the subdomain mode that rebuilds only the neighborhood of disturbed cells (reale_subdomain_rezone, reale_subdomain_frac_max), the reset of the corner masses (reale_corner_mass_reset), the limit on the range of nodal velocities (reale_velocity_max_principle), and the revision of the velocity-projection solver (dvclp_solver_rev). Defaults and meanings are given in the table in Namelist controls. Geometric predicates (orientation, in-circle) are decided by filtered exact evaluation: a fast double-precision value is accepted through a certified error bound (Shewchuk-style A-bounds [7] widened by a factor of two for compiler-contraction safety), and uncertain cases fall through to exact expansion arithmetic and, where needed, to symbolic perturbation (SoS, Simulation of Simplicity [8]) keyed solely on stable generator ids. Every accepted sign equals the exact sign. The subsections below describe the mechanisms that govern its operation; the tessellator itself, the conservative overlay remap, and the persistent boundary carrier are specified in full in the repository's docs/NUMERICS.md §15.

Need-based rezone trigger

The rezone fires on need or at a step-count ceiling. Four reasons, in priority order, can fire it: (i) first — the trigger reference (the node set installed by the last committed rezone) does not exist, or its node count differs from the current mesh. (ii) ceiling — TENRYU_REALE_REZONE_INTERVAL steps have elapsed since the last commit; this environment-only ceiling defaults to 1, preserving the historical every-step behavior, and has no namelist or frozen-config field (a frozen config is the configuration fixed by the freeze; definition). At the default of 1 this reason holds at every step before (iii) and (iv) are tested, so the need-based reasons (iii) and (iv) act only when the ceiling is 2 or more. (iii) dt — the hydro timestep bound has fallen to reale_dt_trigger_factor (default 0.5, i.e. half) times its reference value, captured on the first step after each commit; a positive reale_dt_trigger_cooldown (default 0) additionally requires that many steps since the commit. (iv) disp — some cell corner has moved at least \(h_c/2\) since the last commit, with \(h_c=\sqrt{A_c}\) for planar cell area \(A_c\): a half-cell displacement that follows from the CFL (Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy) condition. The displacement factor \(1/2\) is a constant derived from the CFL condition, not a tunable, and the default 0.5 of the timestep factor comes from the same derivation. The displacement census is GPU-resident — one per-cell max-reduce with a deterministic atomic max — and its reference is refreshed device-to-device at every commit. Each firing logs a [reale_trigger] line with its reason, and the COMMIT line carries trigger=.

Lloyd convergence exits — the certified skip and the representation-spacing floor

The target builder's Lloyd iteration (terms; iteration ceiling reale_lloyd_max, default 4) is likewise need-based. The primary exit is the certified predicate-margin skip. Before the proposal's tessellation is built, every certificate (definition) of the current Delaunay triangulation (the triangulation carried over from the previous rezone as a warm start) — triangle orientations, interior-edge in-circle tests, and hull convexity triples — is checked in pure double arithmetic for a positive filtered slack \(s=|\tilde D|-E\) (\(\tilde D\) the double-evaluated predicate determinant, \(E\) its certified error bound) against an interval-propagated bound \(\Delta_D\) on the determinant's change under the proposed per-generator displacements (the product rule \(\Delta_{xy}\le U_x\Delta_y+U_y\Delta_x\) mirrored over the predicate's own expression tree). If \(2\Delta_D\le s\) holds for every predicate, no exact predicate sign can flip anywhere along the displacement path — so the connectivity of the proposal's Delaunay triangulation equals the current one — and the iteration is structurally a no-op: the loop breaks without adopting it. When the certificate refuses (degenerate slack on exactly cocircular or collinear sub-configurations, a mismatched warm base, or the environment variable TENRYU_LLOYD_D1_DISABLE, which switches this exit off), control falls to the second exit. Conservativeness only reduces skips, so correctness is unconditional.

The second exit is the representation-spacing floor: when every proposed generator move is below that generator's own coordinate representation-spacing scale \(\delta_i=\mathrm{ulp}(|r_i|)+\mathrm{ulp}(|z_i|)\) (ulp being the spacing to the adjacent double at that value), the iteration would pay a full tessellation to polish below the floating-point noise floor of the stored coordinates — the exit is evaluated before the proposal's tessellation is built, and the loop breaks. The measured regime where the certified skip matters is exactly where the floor is structurally silent: relaxed configurations whose Lloyd feedback limit-cycles at a total move near \(10^{-14}\) — above the representation floor, so the floor never fires — while the certified margin (typically \(10^{-6}\)–\(10^{-4}\) of the local spacing) fires immediately and stops the loop from paying its full four tessellations every rezone. Each firing of the certified skip logs one [lloyd_d1] line and sets the fields lloyd_d1_fired/lloyd_d1_iteration of the target-builder result. Neither exit introduces a tunable, and the iteration ceiling is unchanged.

Representation-conditioned weld of short edges — the derived floor \(\chi_e\le4\) and the two bands for forbidden pairs

Before exposing its final DCEL (doubly connected edge list, the edge-based representation of the mesh connectivity), the exact restricted-Voronoi core (terms) deterministically welds edges that are too short for the coordinate representation, on the finding that they are rounding noise, not geometry. For a represented edge \(e=(a,b)\), take \(\ell_e^2=(r_b-r_a)^2+(z_b-z_a)^2\) and \(\delta_e=\{u(r_a)+u(r_b)+u(z_a)+u(z_b)\}/2\), where \(u(q)\) is the larger of the two binary64 spacings adjacent to \(q\). From each raw pre-weld cell's exact shoelace area \(A_c\) and exact-dyadic L1 perimeter \(P_c\), the local scale is \(h_c=2A_c/P_c\) (a different quantity from the trigger's \(h_c=\sqrt{A_c}\)); each node takes the minimum over its incident cells, \(h_a\) (ties broken by stable generator id), and every measured pair uses the single canonical value \(h_e=\min(h_a,h_b)\). Candidate admission, the complete-link certificate, and the final residual audit all use this one measurement, and the areas, perimeters, and scales are fixed from the raw DCEL — contractions never redefine the acceptance scale. A pair welds exactly when

$$ \chi_e=\frac{\ell_e^2}{\delta_e h_e}\le 4, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \ell_e^2 P_c\le 8\,\delta_e A_c $$

(the right form for the node-incident cell attaining \(h_e\)). Equality welds, and the comparison is an expansion-sign decision, not a rounded quotient against a tolerance. The floor 4 is derived, not tunable: the \(\pm2\)-ulp application band on \(\ell_e\) and the attribution spread between the pair's two node-\(h\) values each carry an ambiguity of about a factor of 2, so a measured \(\chi_e\le2^2=4\) cannot be robustly distinguished from the floor. The underlying balance compares retaining the represented short edge, \(E_{retain}=\delta_e/\ell_e\), against collapsing it, \(E_{collapse}=\ell_e/h_e\); using the L1 perimeter (\(P_{L1}\ge P_{L2}\)) makes the rule at most \(\sqrt2\) more conservative while keeping the decision exactly dyadic.

Contractions obey rules by node kind (including the masters and slaves of the persistent boundary carrier): interior nodes may contract only with interior nodes; slaves only on the same carrier or into their own carrier endpoint master; axis nodes only within the same axis carrier or into its axis master. Boundary–interior, cross-carrier, axis/off-axis, and distinct master–master pairs are forbidden; a cluster contains at most one master, and complete-link clustering (two groups merge only when every pair of nodes, one from each group, satisfies the weld test) blocks macroscopic transitive chains. Forbidden pairs are handled in two bands: a pair of forbidden kinds or a master–master pair fails the tessellation with the reason code weld_forbidden_class_pair or weld_master_master only in the strict band \(\chi_e\le1\), where a sub-noise edge between unweldable nodes proves broken geometry. In the extended band \(1<\chi_e\le4\) the edge is not proven noise, so such pairs are retained silently and counted (extended_band_forbidden). The post-fixpoint final audit likewise rejects the rezone (weld_residual_subfloor, through the ordinary skip path) only for strict-band residuals; extended-band residuals of any kind are census-only diagnostics.

Velocity projection with the velocity-jump budget and the active-set KKT certificate

After the overlay remap, the nodal velocity field recovered from cell momentum is conditioned by a projection with the velocity-jump budget (the identifiers of the implementation use dvclp): componentwise in r and z, in the nodal mass metric, subject to every accepted per-edge velocity-jump budget. Components with at most 256 degrees of freedom keep the semismooth-Newton second-order-cone path with its exact certificate. Larger components try a deterministic sequence of stages. The first is a mass-metric Dykstra iteration: in stable edge-id order, each edge projects its provisional r/z jump (a two-component vector) onto the disk whose radius is the budget and distributes the correction between its endpoints about their pair mass center (a cap of 256 full sweeps; after every sweep the exact all-edge feasibility scan permits an early exit); a pass gives a feasibility-only result (solver class 1). If the Dykstra iteration exhausts its cap, a quotient translation treats the connected components of non-violating edges as rigid clusters, reduces each violating cut edge to a shifted-ball constraint, and runs the same mass-metric Dykstra on cluster masses (a cluster containing an axis node has its radial translation fixed). If the quotient translation exhausts its cap or a residual violation survives the post-collapse re-scan, the component snapshot taken before the Dykstra iteration is restored bitwise and the homothety fallback runs.

The active-set KKT (Karush–Kuhn–Tucker) certificate attempts to upgrade the label of a feasibility-only component (solver class 1) to a certified exact projection. The active set is identified by the derived band \(\mathrm{jump}\ge b_e\,\{1-8(w+2)\varepsilon\}\) (\(b_e\) the edge budget, \(w\) the number of segments walked along the edge to obtain the budget, \(\varepsilon\) machine epsilon — the exact mirror of the feasibility predicate's upper band); zero-budget edges are equality-active and re-verified bitwise. A dual witness is constructed by leaf elimination over a stable spanning forest of the active subgraph, with independent r and z flows; cycle-closing edges, inactive edges, and axis-edge radial multipliers are pinned to zero. The certificate verifies per-node stationarity with a Neumaier compensated sum under the derived envelope \(8(\deg+2)\varepsilon\sum|\text{terms}|\), and the Euclidean normal-cone condition per active edge — the multiplier parallel to the jump and non-negative along it, within \(16\varepsilon\) relative envelopes. The stage never modifies velocities: the paired Dykstra updates conserve component momentum to rounding, so the equality-KKT primal correction (the component-translation null mode) is sub-roundoff by construction and the witness is purely diagnostic. On a full pass the component reports solver class 4; on any clause failure the feasibility-only class 1 is kept — never demoted — and the failing clause is counted (kkt_fail_eq/kkt_fail_stationarity/kkt_fail_cone). The solver classes, printed as solver= in the [dvclp_comp] diagnostic line (enabled by the environment variable TENRYU_DVCLP_DIAG=1), are 0 = semismooth Newton, 1 = Dykstra-feasible, 2 = homothety fallback, 3 = quotient-translation feasible, and 4 = Dykstra-feasible with a passed active-set KKT certificate (an exact projection to rounding).

Consensus admission and the velocity representation-spacing floor

An edge whose budget is provably zero-equivalent is treated as a consensus constraint — its endpoint velocities are forced equal — and contracted into a single unit per connected component. The admission proof takes one of three forms: an exactly zero budget, a rounding-envelope certificate, or the representation-spacing floor. The representation-spacing proof states that when the certified budget upper bound lies below \(g_e=\min\bigl(\mathrm{ulp}(v_{r0}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{r1}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{z0}),\mathrm{ulp}(v_{z1})\bigr)\) — the smallest representation spacing of the velocities over both endpoints and both components — no representable nonzero velocity difference exists at all, so equality is the only representable feasible point. Taking the minimum over endpoints and components is the conservative floor: a pair straddling binades can realize the finer operand's spacing. Admission always stands on the proof alone — an edge is never admitted merely because the fallback family would flatten it.

The timestep check before installation — production requirement rezone_min_dt_s

After remap and carrier-velocity projection, but before any staged array is installed, the host-side timestep check on the built but not yet installed mesh (the staged-dt check) evaluates every edge around every staged cell in cell/face index order. It transcribes the CFL expression of the base CSW98 (Caramana–Shashkov–Whalen 1998) edge artificial viscosity (AV; see hydrodynamics) with the configured Numerics.hydro.av_cfl_coefficient (the AV limiter \(\psi\) is fixed to 0, a deliberate difference from the original expression that conservatively biases the bound toward rejection), and applies the node-crossing bound Numerics.hydro.crossing_dt_safety × edge length ÷ closing speed on edges with positive face-direction closing speed. The rezone commits only when the minimum staged bound is strictly greater than rezone_min_dt_s; otherwise it emits a staged_dt SKIP with the bound, threshold, and the cell that sets the bound, and the installed mesh is untouched (nothing was installed, so no rollback machinery is needed). The default is \(10^{-14}\) s, 0.0 disables the evaluation, and negative values are a ConfigError. Like Numerics.dt.max_s, the threshold is a production requirement set by the deck — never a geometric weld tolerance.

Post-commit re-anchor of the timestep growth limit

After each committed rezone the timestep controller may re-anchor the basis of the growth limit (the cap on expansion relative to the previous step; see the timestep page) once, upward only: the first post-commit chosen \(\Delta t\) is raised to the minimum of all non-growth bounds and the last pre-spike chosen \(\Delta t\) (the most recent chosen value whose limiter was not the hydro bound). The edge that drove the hydro-bound spike has been removed by the rezone and is not a persistent constraint, so the growth limit's memory of it is stale by construction; the pre-spike ceiling makes the pair of rezone trigger and re-anchor converge to the pre-spike operating point rather than oscillate, and a genuinely degraded mesh (hydro bound still low) keeps the full protection of the growth limit because the raise is bounded by the current hydro term. When the raise applies, the history's dt_winner records rezone_reanchor as the timestep limiter name (the [dt_breakdown] log line does not show it).

GPU tessellation switches — tess_gpu_dual/tess_gpu_restrict

Two stages of the exact tessellator can be offloaded to the GPU while preserving byte identity. Both are opt-in and default False, and both require a CUDA device at rezone time when enabled: without one they raise an error rather than silently falling back to the host. tess_gpu_dual=True offloads the per-triangle exact circumcenter construction of the dual build (the stage that finds the circumcenters of the Delaunay triangles). The CUDA kernel replicates the host expansion pipeline operation for operation (explicit-fma two_product, tie-preferring magnitude merge, zero elision, exact power-of-two scaling; the translation unit is compiled with fmad contraction disabled), so admitted triangles return the host term sequences and the constructed circumcenter pair byte-for-byte. A per-triangle structural certificate — overflow of the fixed capacities derived from the operation tree (numerators of at most 48 terms, denominators of at most 16, working buffers capped at 64), a non-positive denominator, or a non-finite accumulate — routes that triangle to the unchanged host exact path, processed in a deterministic order, so fallback and admitted triangles alike produce the host bytes. This CUDA kernel is a pure per-triangle map with no atomics, and buffers come from the persistent scratch pools (no per-call cudaMalloc). Verification: OFF leaves the host path untouched; ON is certified by canonical-serialization byte identity — a 69,626-generator production point cloud and synthetic cocircular grids, a forced-fallback identity case, and a per-kind byte comparison of the GPU witness and constructed values with the host values.

tess_gpu_restrict=True (requires tess_gpu_dual=True, ConfigError otherwise) additionally offloads the restrict stage (the stage that restricts the Voronoi diagram to the domain; terms) — the domain classification of dual nodes. It computes a certified brute-force winding number over all domain segments from the device-resident dual term sequences, where every z-compare and orientation sign is accepted only through the replicated running-error filters widened by the standard factor of two. A certified winding equals the exact winding, and certified nodes provably touch no segment (an exact zero orientation is never certifiable), so their classification — inside/outside with empty supports — is the exact host result; every uncertain, boundary-touching, or fallback-backed node runs the unchanged host classification in node order. Output is one status byte per node; determinism and byte identity follow as above. Both switches are pure venue choices that change no physical or numerical semantics by construction.

Verification

  • OFF-path bitwise checks require an ALE-disabled run to remain bit-identical in fields and history.
  • Remap conservation ledgers track mass, R/Z momentum, material internal energy, radiation-group energy, and explicit floor or redistribution terms. Managed axis-band unit tests use \(10^{-12}\) for mass/internal/radiation energy and \(10^{-14}\) for the changes of kinetic and total energy under a uniform translation. At runtime the band rolls back when the mass, radiation-energy (when the band holds radiation energy), cell-momentum, or nodal-momentum residual exceeds \(10^{-8}\), or when the residual of the band's total energy (internal plus kinetic) exceeds \(\min(10^{-10},\,2\times10^{-16}\sqrt{B})\) with \(B\) the number of band cells.
  • ale_identity_mode returns from every ALE entry point after Lagrange; it is a null test against an ALE-disabled run at matched timesteps.
  • The integration tests of 2D RZ (the I-series of the test-tag legend) exercise production rezoning, admissibility, conservative remap, axis, and multiblock paths in coupled configurations.
  • reale_v2 verification is fail-closed by construction (a failure falls to the side that leaves the state unchanged): any target, tessellation, connectivity, remap, or pre-installation timestep violation SKIPs that cycle's rezone and leaves the committed Lagrangian state unchanged. The two GPU tessellation switches are certified by canonical-serialization byte identity against the OFF path.
