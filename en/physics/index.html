Physics kernels

In this section, the documented pages of the physics operators are called physics kernels. A physics operator is the stage of a step that is responsible for one physical process, such as hydrodynamics, electron conduction, or radiation; there are nine. The pages of the infrastructure that supports them, mesh generation and the domain decomposition for the Message Passing Interface (MPI), are also counted as physics kernels. A function that runs on the GPU is called a CUDA kernel, to distinguish it from a physics kernel. Each physics kernel page documents the physical model, the discretization actually implemented, the namelist controls, and the verification that applies. TENRYU has two geometries, 1D and 2D RZ. 1D is spherical, cylindrical, or planar (selected by Mesh.geometry_1d); 2D RZ is two-dimensional in cylindrical coordinates \((R,Z)\), axisymmetric about the axis (R=0). Within one step, only the hydrodynamics is split into two half steps of half the time step each, with electron conduction and radiation between them. The default execution order is laser, nuclear burn immediately after it, a hydro half step, electron conduction, radiation, and a hydro half step. Arranging the operators in this order to advance one step is called operator splitting (see Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline). By default, the ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian) rezoning and remap enter after the second hydro half step and run only when a trigger condition is met. The catalog below orders the physics kernels for ease of learning.

Lagrangian hydrodynamics

Compatible staggered hydrodynamics, time integration, shock capturing, RZ momentum schemes, and plasma viscosity.

Meshes & geometry

1D zoning and regions, 2D RZ mesh families, and the measures of mesh quality.

ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian)

Rezoning, which moves the mesh; conservative remap, which carries the state onto the new mesh; and the experimental ALE that changes the mesh connectivity.

Radiation transport

Multigroup flux-limited diffusion (FLD), coupled implicitly to matter through the Fleck linearization, and discrete-ordinates SN, which solves the matter coupling by iterations such as the Picard iteration.

Heat conduction

Electron and ion heat conduction: the local Spitzer–Härm model with flux limiting, the nonlocal SNB (Schurtz–Nicolaï–Busquet) model, the 1D and 2D RZ spatial discretizations, and time integration by super time-stepping (STS) and other schemes.

Laser drive & absorption

Refracted ray tracing and inverse-bremsstrahlung deposition.

Laser–plasma interaction (LPI)

Cross-beam energy transfer (CBET), which exchanges energy between beams, and the generation, transport and deposition of hot electrons.

EOS, opacity & ionization

A material closure (the relations that give pressure, temperature, ionization, opacity, and similar quantities from the conserved quantities): the equation of state (EOS) and the opacity are given analytically or from tables, and an ionization model is selected.

Nuclear burn

Fusion reactions, charged-particle partition, and neutron heating.
