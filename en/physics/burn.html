Nuclear burn reference

TENRYU's 1D spherical burn kernel advances Bosch–Hale DT, DD, and D³He reactions, fuel depletion, charged-product deposition or transport, and electron/ion energy partition as one explicit-source burn stage. The kernel also provides screening, multigroup-diffusion and Monte Carlo transport of the charged products, and two-line first-collision neutron heating. Terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • Burn is off by default. The core target is spherical 1D_SPH Lagrangian cells; the 2D RZ port supports scheme="local" and "diffusion" only. The point-source sphere kernel (terms) of "fraley" is 1D-specific, "mc" is not ported, and neutron heating (neutron_heating) is 1D-only, so configuration validation rejects all three in 2D. The default of scheme is "fraley", so a 2D deck cannot omit scheme and must name "local" or "diffusion".
  • Inventories (the particle numbers of the species in a cell) are stored as specific inventories \(Y_s=n_s/\rho\) [1/g], so hydrodynamic compression and dilution do not create or destroy particles (Inventory and specific inventory).
  • scheme selects charged-product treatment. Neutron heating has a separate neutron_heating switch.
  • Fusion rest-mass energy is an external source booked in the energy ledger; deposited, escaped, and in-flight energy are audited separately (In flight and the burn ledger).

Physical mechanism

The burn stage converts fusion rest mass into fast charged products and neutrons. In a two-temperature Lagrangian calculation, the kernel advances channel rates \(n_i n_j\langle\sigma v\rangle/(1+\delta_{ij})\) with Bosch–Hale reactivities, depletes the fuel, and returns the released energy as electron- and ion-heating sources. Inventories are specific inventories \(Y_s=n_s/\rho\), which are Lagrangian invariants: compression changes \(n_s\) through \(\rho\) alone, so hydrodynamics cannot create or destroy particles. Storing specific inventories removes the failure in which a carried-over birth density overburns an expanding cell.

Charged-product transport matters because a 3.5-MeV alpha has a range measured in areal density; whether that energy remains in the hot spot or escapes controls self-heating and ignition margin. TENRYU provides a deliberate hierarchy of three models: the Fraley point-sphere model [4] gives a cheap analytic locally retained fraction in spherical geometry (Point-source sphere kernel and retained fraction); multigroup diffusion solves the Corman multigroup equations [6] on a log-uniform energy grid from diffusion_E_min_keV to 15.5 MeV, slowing six products without statistical noise; and Monte Carlo CSDA (continuous slowing-down approximation) keeps straight-line particles between steps as the statistical reference. All three report through the same energy ledgers.

A fast ion initially loses energy mainly to plasma electrons; once it slows below the critical energy — the energy at which losses to electrons and to ions are exactly equal — ion losses take over. That partition matters because ion heating promotes further burn, whereas electron heating can leak through conduction and radiation. The default partition from the Li–Petrasso stopping power [5] is tabulated at initialization over \(T_e,T_i,n_e\), leaving interpolation only at runtime. The simpler Fraley law [4], which gives the fraction of the energy that goes to ions as \(f_i=1/(1+32/T_e[\mathrm{keV}])\), is retained only for DT alphas.

The neutron-heating model is single-flight, first-collision recoil heating on fuel D and T (First collision and single flight), with discrete 14.049- and 2.449-MeV lines. The first collision deposits the mean recoil locally and partitions it with the same Li–Petrasso table. Subsequent flights, kerma (the kinetic energy released in matter per unit mass by neutron interactions), (n,2n) breakup, time of flight, and spectrum width are omitted explicitly, so the resulting profile is a fuel-self-heating approximation rather than a neutronics answer. Salpeter two-temperature [2] (terms) and Chugunov–DeWitt [3] screening modify low-temperature reactivities; the two-temperature Salpeter form is a TENRYU extension. Fusion energy is an external rest-mass source audited separately as deposited, escaped, or in-flight energy.

Reactivities and fuel inventory

The available reaction channels are DT = T(d,n)⁴He, both DD branches D(d,p)T and D(d,n)³He, and D³He = ³He(d,p)⁴He. Reactivities use the Bosch–Hale (1992) [1] Padé approximants. Cell ion temperature is converted from eV to keV exactly once at the kernel boundary. The volume rate is \(r=n_i n_j\langle\sigma v\rangle/(1+\delta_{ij})\), giving the required factor 1/2 for DD. Rates are zero below each fit floor and clamped at the high-temperature ceiling. The default T_floor_keV=0.2 matches the DT/DD floor; the D³He fit begins at 0.5 keV.

fuel_materials names the non-void (defined here) materials that receive inventories. x_D, x_T, and x_He3 set their initial number fractions; all must be non-negative and sum to one. D, T, ³He, ⁴He, and proton inventories are stored as \(Y_s=n_s/\rho\), with \(n_s=\rho Y_s\) reconstructed for each rate evaluation. The specific inventory is the Lagrangian invariant, and storing it avoids the severe overburn caused by carrying a birth number density over into an expanding cell.

screening="none" is the identity (no screening). salpeter applies weak screening with an electron-inclusive, two-temperature Debye length; chugunov_dewitt uses the Chugunov–DeWitt 2009 interpolation formula (their Eq. (A4)) [3] with a rigid electron background. The two-temperature Salpeter form is a TENRYU extension (terms). For invalid thermodynamic inputs the Salpeter factor falls back to unity, and its exponent \(h\) (the screening enhancement of the reaction rate is \(e^h\)) is capped at \(h\le2\) outside the weak-screening regime.

  • \(r=n_i n_j\langle\sigma v\rangle/(1+\delta_{ij})\) — the channel reaction rate per volume; \(\delta_{ij}=1\) supplies the DD half factor for identical reactants.
  • \(Y_s=n_s/\rho\) — the Lagrangian specific inventory. Hydrodynamic compression or expansion changes \(n_s=\rho Y_s\), not \(Y_s\) itself.
  • Temperature boundary — ion temperature is converted from TENRYU's internal eV to keV exactly once before evaluating the Bosch–Hale fit.
  • Fit bounds — rates vanish below the inclusive floors (0.2 keV for DT/DD and 0.5 keV for D³He) and clamp above the Bosch–Hale fit-validity ceilings [1]: 100 keV for DT and both DD branches, 190 keV for D³He; T_floor_keV defaults to 0.2.
  • Screening — the selected factor multiplies each reaction pair's reactivity; for invalid inputs the Salpeter factor reverts to one, and its weak-model exponent \(h\) (the screening enhancement is \(e^h\)) is capped at \(h\le2\) outside the weak-screening regime.
  • Fraley retention — the point-source sphere kernel determines the locally retained fraction from the fuel-sphere areal density and the product's birth radius (terms).
  • \(f_i=1/(1+32/T_e[\mathrm{keV}])\) — the optional Fraley electron/ion split, restricted by validation to DT alphas.

Terms used in this calculation

The burn kernel builds its time advance, the bookkeeping of particle numbers, the destination of the product energy, and its treatment of neutrons from units specific to this calculation. The terms below carry meanings specific to TENRYU's implementation, so they are defined here together.

Burn stage and subcycling

A burn stage is one update that the burn kernel performs over the time step \(\Delta t\). The outer step advances by operator splitting, and burn is one operator in that sequence (Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline). Burn is placed right after the laser and before the first hydro half-step, and it advances the whole \(\Delta t\) in one go as an explicit source; \(\Delta t\) is not subdivided to alternate burn with the other operators. During a stage, each cell's density and electron and ion temperatures are held constant, and at the end of the stage the energy produced by the reactions is added to the electrons and ions as heating sources.

Subcycling divides \(\Delta t\) inside a stage into \(M\) equal subintervals (each of length \(\Delta t/M\)) and advances the five-species network of each cell with the explicit midpoint method (second-order Runge–Kutta). Cells with fast reactions need finer subintervals, so \(M\) is set per cell by

$$ M=\min\!\left[\max\!\left(\left\lceil\frac{\Delta t\,r_{max}}{\epsilon_{dep}}\right\rceil,1\right),M_{max}\right],\qquad r_{max}=\max_s\frac{\max(q_s^{+},q_s^{-})}{\max(n_s,\,10^{-9}n_{tot},\,1\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}})}. $$

Here \(\epsilon_{dep}\) is eps_deplete (default 0.1; above 0 and at most 0.5), \(M_{max}\) is subcycle_max (default 64; from 1 to 4096), \(s\) runs over the reactant species (D, plus T and ³He according to the enabled channels), \(q_s^{+}\) and \(q_s^{-}\) are the gross production and gross destruction rates of species \(s\) by the reactions [cm⁻³ s⁻¹], and \(n_{tot}\) is the sum of the number densities of the five species. Because the larger of gross production and gross destruction is used rather than the net rate, a species whose production and destruction nearly cancel while turning over quickly (trace T that DD breeds and DT consumes at once, for example) is still resolved. When the reaction count of one subcycle would exhaust the inventory of a reactant, the count is scaled back deterministically to keep positivity. When the required number exceeds \(M_{max}\) in some cell, the stage proceeds with the cap, reports the required number, and limits the burn time step of the next step (Depletion and explicit time control).

Inventory and specific inventory

The inventory is the number of particles of each of the five species D, T, ³He, ⁴He, and p that a cell holds. Neutrons are not part of the inventory; they are counted only in the ledger.

The specific inventory \(Y_s=n_s/\rho\) [1/g] is the number of particles of species \(s\) per gram. The inventory is stored as the specific inventory, not as the number density \(n_s\) [cm⁻³]. Because the cell mass does not change in pure Lagrangian motion, compression and expansion leave \(Y_s\) unchanged and change only \(n_s=\rho Y_s\); only nuclear reactions change \(Y_s\). Storing the number density would let an expanding cell carry over its number density from the time of birth and overburn the fuel; the specific inventory prevents this. The number density \(n_s=\rho Y_s\) is rebuilt from the current \(\rho\) only when the reaction rates are evaluated.

The initial inventory follows from the sum \(f_{fuel}\) of the volume fractions of the cell's fuel materials (fuel_materials) and the initial number fractions \(x_D\), \(x_T\), and \(x_{He3}\) (x_D, x_T, x_He3; the defaults are 0.5, 0.5, and 0, and the sum must be 1; set all three for any combination other than the default): \(Y_s=x_sf_{fuel}/(\bar Am_p)\) for \(s=\) D, T, ³He. Here \(\bar A=\sum_sx_sA_s\) is the mean mass of the fuel ions and \(A_s\) is the mass of species \(s\) in units of the proton mass (2.0141 for D, 3.0160 for T and ³He). ⁴He and p start at 0. A checkpoint writes the specific inventory to burn_state/specific_inventory, and a restart uses it (Outputs & analysis).

Point-source sphere kernel and retained fraction

The fuel sphere is the contiguous range from the innermost to the outermost of the cells whose summed fuel-material volume fraction exceeds vf_threshold (default \(10^{-3}\)), the fuel cells. Cells inside the range that fall at or below the threshold stay in the range. The radius of the outer edge of the outermost cell is \(R_b\).

The point-source sphere kernel gives, in closed form, the fraction of a charged product's energy that is deposited inside the fuel sphere. A product is assumed to start from a point source at the center of its birth cell, to be emitted isotropically, to travel in a straight line through a uniform medium, and to slow down under an electron drag that is linear in velocity, \(E(s)=E_0(1-s/\lambda)^2\) (\(E_0\) is the birth energy, \(s\) the flight distance, and \(\lambda\) the range). With \(r\) the radius of the birth point, \(u=r/R_b\), and \(\mu\) the cosine of the angle between the flight direction and the outward radial direction, the distance from the birth point to the surface of the fuel sphere is \(L(\mu)=-u\mu+\sqrt{1-u^2+u^2\mu^2}\) in units of \(R_b\). With \(\tau=R_b\bar\rho/(\rho\lambda)\), the fuel-sphere radius divided by the range, the deposited fraction \(f(u,\tau)\) is

$$ f(u,\tau)=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\Bigl\{1-\bigl[1-\min\bigl(\tau L(\mu),1\bigr)\bigr]^2\Bigr\}\,d\mu . $$

Here \(\bar\rho\) is the trapezoidal radial mean of the density from the center of the birth cell to \(R_b\) [g/cm³], and \(\rho\lambda\) is the product's range expressed as an areal density [g/cm²] (\(\rho\lambda/\bar\rho\) is the range as a length).

The retained fraction is this \(f(u,\tau)\). The retained part \(f\) is deposited in the birth cell (the split between electrons and ions follows partition), and the rest, \(1-f\), is booked in the charged-escape ledger as energy that leaves the fuel sphere. At the center (\(u=0\)), \(f=1-[1-\min(\tau,1)]^2\), which equals 1 for \(\tau\ge1\). At the surface of the fuel sphere (\(u=1\)), \(f\) only approaches 1/2 as \(\tau\) grows (the half of the emission that goes outward escapes). Averaged over sources distributed uniformly in a uniform sphere, \(f\) reduces to the classical expression \(f(\tau)=\tfrac32\tau-\tfrac45\tau^2\) for \(\tau\le\tfrac12\) and \(f(\tau)=1-\tfrac1{4\tau}+\tfrac1{160\tau^3}\) for \(\tau\ge\tfrac12\).

The range of the 3.54-MeV DT alpha is given by the Fraley fit [4]

$$ \rho\lambda_\alpha=\frac{1.5\times10^{-2}\,T_e^{5/4}}{1+8.2\times10^{-3}\,T_e^{5/4}}\cdot\frac{1+0.17\ln T_e}{1+0.17\ln\bigl(T_e\sqrt{\rho_0/\rho}\bigr)}\ \ [\mathrm{g/cm^2}],\qquad \rho_0=0.213\ \mathrm{g/cm^3} $$

(\(T_e\) in keV and \(\rho\) in g/cm³; \(T_e\) is limited to at least 0.1 keV and each of the two logarithmic factors to at least 0.15). The ranges of the other products are scaled from the alpha by \(\lambda_s=\lambda_\alpha\sqrt{A_sE_s/(A_\alpha E_\alpha)}\,(Z_\alpha/Z_s)^2\) (\(A\) is the mass, \(E\) the birth energy, and \(Z\) the charge). The electron term and the ion term of the fit are corrected for the composition of the cell (fuel and ash); the correction is 1 for equimolar DT. Because the kernel assumes straight flight, a uniform medium, and a radial mean density, it does not represent the layering of the density along oblique chords.

The kernel estimates, cheaply in a 1D spherical calculation and without tracking the flights, how much of the charged-product energy heats the fuel and how much escapes. The default scheme="fraley" (1D only) uses it, and the retained fraction fixes the energy deposited in electrons and ions and the energy that escapes, and so the strength of self-heating and the ignition margin. The smaller \(\tau\) is (the longer the range compared with the fuel sphere), the larger the escaping fraction. For example, \(f=0.75\) for \(u=0\) and \(\tau=0.5\), and \(f=0.94\) for \(u=0.5\) and \(\tau=1\).

In flight and the burn ledger

The in-flight energy \(E_{inflight}\) is the energy carried by charged products that have been produced but have neither been deposited in a cell nor escaped from the system. Multigroup diffusion carries it into the next step as the specific spectra \(Y_g=N_g/\rho\) [1/g] (\(N_g\) is the particle number density of group \(g\), stored per unit mass like the specific inventory), and Monte Carlo carries it as the particles kept across steps. With fraley and local, a product is either deposited or escapes within the step that produces it, so \(E_{inflight}=0\).

The burn ledger books where the energy released by burn goes, and is part of the overall energy ledger. For the charged products, each step enforces the following identity to within rounding (released = deposited + escaped + change in in-flight energy).

$$ E_{rel}=E_{dep}+E_{esc}+\Delta E_{inflight}. $$

Here \(E_{rel}\) is the energy released by the charged products, \(E_{dep}\) the energy deposited in electrons and ions, \(E_{esc}\) the energy that leaves the system (the part leaving the fuel sphere for fraley, the part leaving through the outer surface for diffusion and mc), and \(\Delta E_{inflight}\) the change of the in-flight energy over the step. For neutrons, all the released energy counts as escaped when neutron_heating is off; when it is on, the identity emitted = deposited + degraded + escaped is enforced (First collision and single flight). The overall energy ledger receives only the energy deposited in the fluid, as an external source; escaped energy and neutrons never enter the fluid, so they are not counted as losses. The history channels burn/released, burn/dep_e, burn/dep_i, burn/esc_charged, burn/esc_neutron, the cumulative channels such as burn/E_released_cum, and burn/E_inflight (diffusion and mc only) are this ledger (History channels).

First collision and single flight

The first collision is the first elastic scattering of a neutron on a fuel D or T.

Single flight is the treatment in which each neutron flies once, along one straight chord from its emission point to the outer surface of the sphere, and the neutron scattered in the first collision is not followed any further.

Neutrons are emitted in two lines, 14.049 MeV from DT and 2.449 MeV from DD. From the radius of the center of the emitting cell, they are emitted isotropically, in direction cosines \(\mu\) discretized by an even-order Gauss–Legendre quadrature (the number of \(\mu\) points is neutron_heating_n_mu; default 16, an even number from 2 to 64), and the chord of each direction is followed shell by shell. The fraction that collides in a shell interval \(\Delta s\) is \(T_{rem}(1-e^{-\Sigma\Delta s})\), where \(\Sigma=n_D\sigma_D+n_T\sigma_T\) is the macroscopic elastic-scattering cross section of fuel D and T (the number densities \(n_D\) and \(n_T\) are built from the inventory \(\rho Y_s\)) and \(T_{rem}\) is the transmission up to that point. Materials other than the fuel, ⁴He, and p are treated as transparent. The mean fraction \(f\) of the colliding neutron's energy that goes to the recoiling nucleus is deposited in the cell of that shell and partitioned between electrons and ions with the recoil entries of the Li–Petrasso table. The remaining \(1-f\) is carried away by the scattered neutron, is not followed, and is booked in the ledger as degraded. The part that does not collide is booked as escaped.

The cross sections \(\sigma\) and the mean fractions \(f\) are the following values, fixed in the code.

Two-temperature Salpeter form

The two-temperature Salpeter form is the enhancement factor applied to the reactivity when screening="salpeter". For each pair \((i,j)\) of reacting species, \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\to F\langle\sigma v\rangle\) with \(F=\exp h\). The screening length \(\lambda\) is a Debye length in which the ions and the electrons have separate temperatures,

$$ \lambda^{-2}=4\pi e^2\left[\frac{\sum_sn_sZ_s^2}{k_BT_i}+\frac{n_e}{k_BT_e}\right],\qquad h=\frac{Z_iZ_je^2}{\lambda\,k_BT_i} $$

where \(e\) is the elementary charge, \(n_s\) are the number densities of the five species built from the inventory, \(Z_s\) their charges (1 for D, T, and p; 2 for ³He and ⁴He), \(n_e=\bar Z\rho/(Am_p)\) is the electron number density, and \(Z_i\) and \(Z_j\) are the charges of the two reacting species. Salpeter's formula [2] assumes an equilibrium in which ions and electrons share one temperature; TENRYU extended it to \(T_i\ne T_e\). The weak-screening approximation holds only for \(h\ll1\), so \(h\) is capped at 2 (an enhancement of at most \(e^2\approx7.4\)), with a warning the first time the cap acts. When an input (\(T_i\), \(T_e\), or \(n_e\)) is non-positive or not finite, \(F=1\). The option chugunov_dewitt uses the Chugunov–DeWitt interpolation formula [3] with a rigid electron background, not the two-temperature form defined here.

Depletion and explicit time control

Each burn stage holds density and temperature constant and advances the per-cell species ODE with explicit-midpoint RK2 subcycles. The required number of subcycles is based on the larger of gross production and gross destruction for every reactant in an enabled channel, keeping the relative inventory change per subcycle below eps_deplete. Gross control resolves cases where DD breeds trace T while DT consumes it almost as quickly, even though the net T rate is small. A deterministic scale-back of reaction counts preserves positivity and the stoichiometric balance.

If the required number of subcycles exceeds subcycle_max, the current step uses the cap, reports saturation, and folds \(0.9\,dt\,M_{max}/M_{req}\) into the next burn time-step limit (\(M_{req}\) is the required number of subcycles and \(M_{max}\) the subcycle_max cap). Deposited power also imposes \(\Delta t\le f_E e_{cell}/P_{dep}\), where \(f_E=\)explicit_source_limit and \(e_{cell}=\rho_cV_c(e_e+e_i)\) is the cell's total internal energy — the same limiter form as the identically named key of the hot-electron physics kernel, with the electron-plus-ion reservoir here (there it is the electron energy). It is a limit on the source that keeps self-heating small without updating the temperatures inside the stage.

One burn stage, step by step

  1. Hold each cell's density and electron/ion temperatures constant for the duration of the burn stage.
  2. Compute the enabled channel rates, then the required number of RK2 subcycles from gross production and gross destruction for every reactant, targeting a relative inventory change below eps_deplete per subcycle. Gross rather than net control resolves the trace-tritium DD-to-DT turnover case.
  3. Advance the five-species network with explicit-midpoint RK2. If a proposed reaction count would exhaust a reactant, deterministically scale the counts back before reconstructing inventories, preserving positivity and the stoichiometric balance.
  4. If the required number of subcycles saturates subcycle_max, report it and fold \(0.9\,dt\,M_{max}/M_{req}\) into the next burn time-step limit. Independently impose \(\Delta t\le f_E e_{cell}/P_{dep}\) on deposited source power.
  5. Hand charged products to the selected treatment (fraley, diffusion, or mc in 1D; local or diffusion in 2D RZ), and send neutron products through the optional first-collision heating path (1D only).
  6. Partition deposited energy into electron and ion sources, update the burn ledger (deposited, escaped, and in-flight energy), and expose the combined burn time-step limiter to the driver.

Product transport and electron/ion partition

The default scheme="fraley" combines the Fraley alpha-range fit with a point-source spherical kernel [4] (Point-source sphere kernel and retained fraction). It computes the locally retained fraction from the fuel-sphere areal density and the birth radius, deposits that fraction in the birth cell, and sends the remainder to the charged-escape ledger. scheme="diffusion" uses the Corman 1975 [6] multigroup equations to track the in-flight specific spectra of six products — the charged products of each channel: the DT alpha; the DD proton, triton, and helion (the ³He nucleus); and the D³He proton and alpha — and deposits the energy lost during group-to-group slowing. scheme="mc" keeps straight-line CSDA particles between steps, of the same type as the Monte Carlo tracking of Yuan et al. [8], and samples mc_particles_per_cell particles per product, cell, and step. MC tallies are statistical, but the counter-based random-number streams (Philox4x32-10) remain independent through particle global IDs. local is for 2D RZ only (1D rejects it); it transports no product energy and deposits it entirely in the birth cell (the electron/ion partition is shared with the other schemes).

The default local-deposition split, partition="li_petrasso", tabulates Li–Petrasso stopping over \(T_e,T_i,n_e\) during initialization; runtime performs only interpolation. The alternative Fraley law, \(f_i=1/(1+32/T_e[\mathrm{keV}])\), is restricted to DT alphas; when scheme is fraley or local, it is rejected with DD or D³He. It is also rejected with neutron heating (neutron_heating), because the law is a fit for the DT alpha and cannot partition the energy of the recoiling D and T between electrons and ions. Multigroup diffusion and Monte Carlo each include a stopping partition of their own and do not use this key.

Neutron heating

With neutron_heating=True (1D only), DT-n at 14.049 MeV and DD-n at 2.449 MeV are emitted isotropically. A neutron_heating_n_mu-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature traces shell chords. Attenuation uses the elastic-scattering cross sections of fuel D and T only. The first collision deposits the mean recoil energy locally (First collision and single flight), and the Li–Petrasso table partitions the recoil D/T energy between electrons and ions. Residual scattered-neutron energy is recorded as degraded; energy crossing the outer boundary is recorded as escaped.

This model is single-flight. It omits subsequent flights of degraded neutrons, shell-material kerma, (n,2n) breakup, time-of-flight retardation, and recoil-spectrum width. Its spatial heating profile should therefore be read as a first-collision fuel-self-heating approximation.

Verification

  • The unit test test_burn_reactivity pins the Bosch–Hale reference reactivities (their Table VIII) at eight temperatures for four reactions to a relative \(1.5\times10^{-3}\), catching eV/keV errors. The unit test test_burn_network covers closed-form DT/DD depletion, coupled bred-species chains, gross-turnover subcycling, and reaction-count stoichiometry.
  • In the unit test test_burn_deposition, sections G-K0, G-K1, and G-K2 (test tags) cover the Fraley range anchors, the point-source sphere kernel identity, and the sphere-average closed form, and sections G-P1 and G-P2 cover the published Li–Petrasso stopping-power reference values (their Table I [5]) and the intentionally different Fraley partition. The six-run microsphere test burn_rung2_microsphere combines the fuel-pair bound, rate scaling, and early alpha-recapture self-heating.
  • Disabled-versus-baseline A/B twins matched 53/53 field and 164/164 history datasets bitwise, and the GXII (GEKKO XII) golden reference showed a relative difference of 0 in six runs. The burn-stage ledger test and the diffusion and Monte Carlo transport tests require released = deposited + escaped + Δin-flight within floating-point closure.
  • The unit test test_burn_neutron_heating checks the constants fixed for neutron heating (cross sections and recoil fractions), the thin limit, analytic spherical attenuation, and emitted = deposited + degraded + escaped. MC separately gates fixed-seed CV ≤0.1% (CV: coefficient of variation, \(\sigma/|\mu|\)), cross-seed CV ≤5%, and Philox stream independence.
