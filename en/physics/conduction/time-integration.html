Time integration

By default the time integration of heat conduction is explicit super-time-stepping (STS). In a hot corona the explicit stability limit of thermal diffusion is far smaller than the global time step, but STS uses multistage explicit updates (a three-term RKL2 recursion in 1D; stages of different lengths such as Chebyshev stages in 2D RZ) and covers the global step in a small number of stages. 1D also has an implicit solver that solves a backward-Euler tridiagonal system (solver="implicit"), and 2D RZ has an implicit solver that satisfies the discrete maximum principle (solver="dmp_implicit"). This page describes the terms and the procedure of STS, the two implicit solvers, and the keys of the time integration.

Terms used in this calculation

The time steps of STS use several words in the sense of TENRYU's implementation: the global step, the explicit stability limit, the substep, the superstep, and the stage. They are defined together here.

Time steps of STS

The global step is the time step \(\Delta t\) that the driver advances in one outer step; how it is chosen is described on the time-step control page. In both 1D and 2D RZ, conduction proposes as the upper limit of the global step the Chebyshev-stage formula \(s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\) (820 times \(\Delta t_{exp}\) by default; see the conduction section of that page). In 1D, however, the driver uses only the cell estimate for that limit; inside the conduction call, 1D electron STS incorporates the face-conductance bound into \(\Delta t_{exp}\) and plans RKL2 stages and subcycles to cover that step.

The explicit stability limit \(\Delta t_{exp}\) is the time step at which an explicit update of heat diffusion with the forward Euler method is stable, to within a safety factor. Because κ is proportional to \(T_e^{5/2}\), \(\Delta t_{exp}\) is several hundred times smaller than \(\Delta t\) in the corona. In 1D, the driver time-step candidate and diagnostics use only the cell width/diffusivity estimate, whereas inside the 1D electron STS call \(\Delta t_{exp}\) takes the smaller of the cell estimate and the flux-limited face-conductance bound; in 2D RZ it is obtained from the Gershgorin bound of the operator used in the update (step 3). The safety factor \(C_{cond}\) is the key Numerics.dt.cfl_cond (default 0.25, range (0, 1]). The ratio \(\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\) sets the number of STS stages.

A substep is one explicit update of size \(\Delta t_{exp}\) in the hypothetical case that STS is not used and \(\Delta t\) is divided into explicit updates. The number needed is \(N_{sub}=\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\rceil\), which is 130–340 in the GXII corona. TENRYU does not run substeps; \(N_{sub}\) is the reference quantity that measures the benefit of STS.

A superstep / subcycle is the interval that STS advances in one sequence of stages. For 2D RZ Chebyshev stages, when \(\Delta t\) is at most \(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\), the global step is one superstep (\(\Delta t_{sup}\)); above that, \(\Delta t\) is divided into \(n_{sup}\) equal supersteps. Here \(s_{max}\) is the ceiling on the number of stages in one superstep (sts_max_stages, default 40, range 1–200) and \(\eta\) is the coefficient that decides the division (sts_subcycle_eta, default 0.9, range (0, 1]). By default in 2D RZ, one superstep covers up to about 738 times \(\Delta t_{exp}\) (\(0.9\times820\)). In 1D electron STS (RKL2), the stage cap is the largest odd integer \(s_{cap}\) ≤ \(s_{max}\) (39 by default) with stability factor \(c(s_{cap})=(s_{cap}^2+s_{cap}-2)/4\) (389.5 by default; 1 when \(s_{cap}\) = 1), and \(\Delta t\) is divided into \(n_{sub}\) equal subcycles of length \(\tau=\Delta t/n_{sub}\) when \(\Delta t\) > \(\max(\eta,10^{-6})\,c(s_{cap})\Delta t_p\) (about 350.55 \(\Delta t_{exp}\) initially under the defaults).

A stage is one update inside a superstep or subcycle. In 2D RZ Chebyshev STS, a superstep consists of \(s\) forward-Euler updates whose lengths \(\tau_j\) differ from stage to stage (formula in step 5) and sum to \(\Delta t_{sup}\), requiring about \(\sqrt{2\Delta t_{sup}/\Delta t_{exp}}\) stages with lengths set by the damping \(\nu\) (sts_damping, default 0.01, range (0, 1)). 1D electron STS uses neither Chebyshev stage lengths \(\tau_j\) nor sts_damping; it advances \(s\) stages (odd \(s\) ≥ 3, or 1 forward-Euler stage when \(\tau/\Delta t_p\) ≤ 1/2) with the three-term second-order Runge–Kutta–Legendre (RKL2) recursion [3] and re-evaluates coefficients at every stage.

Super-time-stepping

Because Spitzer conductivity scales as \(T_e^{5/2}\), marching the global step \(\Delta t\) in explicit substeps of size \(\Delta t_{exp}\) would require \(N_{sub}=\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{exp}\rceil\) CUDA kernel launches. In a representative GXII corona — the same \(T_e\approx2\) keV, \(\rho\approx10^{-3}\) g/cm³, \(\Delta l\approx50\,\mu\)m conditions quoted in the physical mechanism section of the overview page — \(N_{sub}\) reaches 130–340. STS widens the stability interval as \(O(s^2)\), covering the same interval in 17–27 stages for 2D RZ Chebyshev stages (\(s\approx\lceil\sqrt{2N_{sub}}\rceil\)) and 23–37 stages for 1D RKL2 stages. These terms are defined in the terms section. The procedure is as follows.

One conduction step, step by step

  1. Build every face κ. In 1D, on every active face (including material interfaces), kirchhoff_same_material evaluates the width-weighted harmonic prefactor multiplied by the Kirchhoff secant, and harmonic evaluates the width-weighted harmonic mean of cell κ (spatial-discretization page). In 1D electron STS, besides the entry evaluation used for the stability bound, each RKL2 stage re-evaluates cell and face κ from its floored input temperature \(\widehat Y=\max(Y,T_{floor})\). In 2D RZ there is no face κ, and the node-local operator is assembled from the cell κeff. The conductivity that goes into the operator for each cell is κeff/Te5/2 at the entry temperature when the secant is used, and κeff otherwise.

  2. Apply the flux limiter to every face. In 1D, form the SH face estimate with the same face κ (using the unfloored difference \(Y_R-Y_L\)), evaluate \(q_{max}\) at the floored face temperature \((\widehat Y_L+\widehat Y_R)/2\), and form the limiting factor \(\lambda_f=1/(1+|q_{raw}|/q_{max})\). In 1D electron STS, this flux limiter is also re-evaluated at every stage. In 2D RZ, \(\kappa_{eff}=\theta\kappa_{SH}\) is formed per cell (local model page), and this is the limited conductivity.

  3. Find the explicit stability limit. In 1D, under both values of face_kappa_policy, each owned active cell contributes the smaller of the cell estimate and the flux-limited face-conductance bound (\(\Delta l_c\) is the cell width and \(C_c=(\rho c_vV)_c\) is the cell heat capacity):

    $$ \Delta t_{exp}=C_{cond}\min_c\!\left(\frac{(\Delta l_c)^2}{D_{eff,c}},\ \frac{2C_c}{g_c}\right),\qquad g_c=\sum_{f\ \mathrm{open}}\lambda_fG_f,\qquad C_{cond}=0.25\ \text{(default)}. $$

    Here \(G_f=A_f\kappa_f/\Delta r_f\) is the conductance of an open face \(f\), \(\kappa_f\) is the face κ, \(\lambda_f\) is the face flux-limiter factor, and \(\Delta r_f\) is the distance between cell centers (cells with no open faces contribute only the cell estimate). If no rank has any open face after entry checks, 1D STS returns immediately with 0 stages, keeping entry temperatures unchanged with no floor clamp or energy booking.

    In 2D RZ, \(\Delta t_{exp}=4C_{cond}/\lambda_G\), where \(\lambda_G\) is the Gershgorin bound of the operator that the stages apply.

    $$ \lambda_G=\max_c\frac{w_c\left|\sum_dG_{cd}+b_c\right|+\sum_dw_d\left|G_{cd}\right|}{(\rho c_vV)_c}. $$

    Here \(G_{cd}\) is the pair coefficient of cell \(c\) and its neighbor \(d\), and \(b_c\) is the sum of the conductances of the cell to the half-edges held at a fixed temperature (0 when no boundary is held). The sum over \(d\) leaves out void cells and cells with \(\rho c_v\le0\). The temperature weight \(w\) is \(w_c=T_{e,c}^{5/2}\) when the Kirchhoff secant is used (then \(G_{cd}\) is assembled from \(\kappa_{eff}/T_e^{5/2}\)) and \(w_c=1\) otherwise. The operator of the stages is the matrix \(M^{-1}K\) with \(M=\mathrm{diag}(\rho c_vV)\), \(K_{cd}=-G_{cd}\), and \(K_{cc}=\sum_dG_{cd}+b_c\). By the Gershgorin circle theorem, \(\lambda_G\) bounds every eigenvalue of \(M^{-1}K\), so \(\Delta t_{exp}\) guarantees by construction that \(\lambda\Delta t_{exp}\le4C_{cond}\) for every eigenvalue \(\lambda\). When the secant is used, the bound holds at the temperatures where it was computed, and every stage checks it again (step 6).

  4. Choose the stage and subcycle counts. In 1D electron STS (RKL2), from the initial plan bound \(\lambda_{plan}\) and margin \(m_b\) (sts_bound_margin, default 0.1), the base step \(\Delta t_p\), subcycle count \(n_{sub}\), and stage count \(s\) are planned as follows:

    $$ \begin{aligned} \lambda_{plan}&=\frac{4C_{cond}}{\Delta t_{exp}},\qquad L=\lambda_{plan}(1+m_b),\qquad k=\max\bigl(1,\,2C_{cond}(1+m_b)\bigr),\qquad \Delta t_p=\frac{4C_{cond}}{k\lambda_{plan}},\\ c(s)&=\begin{cases}1 & (s=1),\\ (s^2+s-2)/4 & (\text{odd }s\ge3),\end{cases}\qquad \Delta t_{cap}=\max(\eta,\,10^{-6})\,c(s_{cap})\Delta t_p,\\ n_{sub}&=\begin{cases}\lceil\Delta t/\Delta t_{cap}\rceil & (\Delta t\gt\Delta t_{cap}),\\ 1 & (\Delta t\le\Delta t_{cap}).\end{cases} \end{aligned} $$

    With defaults \(C_{cond}\) = 0.25 and \(m_b\) = 0.1, \(k\) = 1 and \(\Delta t_p=\Delta t_{exp}\). Here \(s_{cap}\) is the largest odd integer not above sts_max_stages, \(\eta\) is sts_subcycle_eta (default 0.9, floored at 10−6 during planning), and \(n_{sub}\) is incremented by 1 if rounding leaves \((\Delta t/n_{sub})/\Delta t_p\gt c(s_{cap})\). For subcycle length \(\tau=\Delta t/n_{sub}\) and ratio \(R=\tau/\Delta t_p\), choose 1 forward-Euler stage (\(s\) = 1) when \(R\) ≤ 1/2 (or \(s_{cap}\) ≤ 2), and otherwise the smallest odd \(s\) ≥ 3 satisfying \(s^2+s-2\ge4R\).

    In 2D RZ Chebyshev STS, the stability interval grows as \(O(s^2)\). Let \(\Delta t_{sup}\) be the interval that one superstep advances; the stage count is

    $$ s=\min\!\left(s_{max},\ \max\!\left(1,\left\lceil\sqrt{2\Delta t_{sup}/\Delta t_{exp}}\right\rceil\right)\right). $$

    Here \(s_{max}\) is the stage ceiling (sts_max_stages, default 40). When \(\Delta t\) exceeds \(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2\), it is divided into \(n_{sup}=\lceil\Delta t/(\eta\,s_{max}(s_{max}+1)\Delta t_{exp}/2)\rceil\) equal supersteps with \(\Delta t_{sup}=\Delta t/n_{sup}\), where \(\eta\) is sts_subcycle_eta (default 0.9). Otherwise \(n_{sup}=1\) and \(\Delta t_{sup}=\Delta t\). The stage count and the division for the RKL2 option of sts_scheme in 2D RZ are given in the sts_scheme row of the key table.

  5. Build the stage weights or stage lengths. 1D electron STS (RKL2) does not construct stage lengths \(\tau_j\); instead it computes the RKL2 recursion weights from \(s\) and \(\tau\) [3]:

    $$ \begin{aligned} b_0&=b_1=\frac13,\qquad b_j=\frac{j^2+j-2}{2j(j+1)}\ \ (j\ge2),\qquad a_j=1-b_j,\qquad w_1=\frac{4}{s^2+s-2},\qquad \tilde\mu_1=b_1w_1,\\ \mu_j&=\frac{(2j-1)b_j}{jb_{j-1}},\qquad \nu_j=-\frac{(j-1)b_j}{jb_{j-2}},\qquad \tilde\mu_j=\mu_jw_1,\qquad \tilde\gamma_j=-a_{j-1}\tilde\mu_j\ \ (2\le j\le s). \end{aligned} $$

    In 2D RZ Chebyshev STS, with the default damping ν=0.01 (the Chebyshev damping parameter, sts_damping — distinct from the collision frequency \(\nu_{ei}\) on the overview page and the ion-acoustic damping rate \(\nu_a\) on the CBET page), construct

    $$ \tau_j=\frac{\Delta t_{exp}} {\nu^2+(1-\nu^2)\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi(2j-1)}{4s+2}\right)}, \quad j=1,\ldots,s, $$

    then rescale all τj uniformly so the stage lengths sum to the superstep, \(\sum_j\tau_j=\Delta t_{sup}\). With the shipped defaults the sum before rescaling exceeds \(\Delta t_{sup}\), so this rescale shrinks the τj.

  6. Apply all stages and check admissibility. In 1D electron STS, heat capacity \(\rho c_v\), geometry, density, charge, and mass stay at step entry while each stage re-evaluates conductivities and flux limiters in the operator \(L_j\) from \(Y_{j-1}\) and advances the RKL2 increment \(D_j\) (\(Y_j=Y_0+D_j\), \(F_0=L_1(Y_0)\), \(D_0=0\); or \(D_1=\tau F_0\) for \(s\) = 1). In 2D RZ, \(D_{eff}\), κeff, and ρcv stay at their entry-\(T_e^n\) values (only the Kirchhoff secant is evaluated at the stage temperature) under the Chebyshev update:

    $$ \begin{aligned} D_1&=\tilde\mu_1\tau F_0\quad(\text{1D RKL2}),\\ D_j&=(\mu_jD_{j-1}+\nu_jD_{j-2})+(\tilde\mu_j\tau L_j(Y_{j-1})+\tilde\gamma_j\tau F_0)\quad(2\le j\le s,\ \text{1D RKL2}),\\ T_{e,c,j}&=T_{e,c,j-1}+\frac{\tau_j}{(\rho c_vV)_c}\sum_dG_{cd}S_{cd}\,(T_{e,d,j-1}-T_{e,c,j-1})\quad(\text{2D RZ}). \end{aligned} $$

    \(G_{cd}=-(a_{cd}+a_{dc})/2\) is the pair coefficient built from the matrix \(a\) of the node-local operator (\(G_{cd}=G_{dc}\)), and \(d\) runs over the neighbors that share a node with \(c\) (void cells and cells with \(\rho c_v\le0\) are left out). \(S_{cd}\) is the \(T^{5/2}\) secant over the two cell temperatures, \(\tfrac27(T_d^{7/2}-T_c^{7/2})/(T_d-T_c)\), and \(S_{cd}=1\) for face_kappa_policy="harmonic". In 1D, each face heat flow enters the two adjacent cells with opposite signs in identical arithmetic order. In 2D RZ, the pair power \(G_{cd}S_{cd}(T_d-T_c)\) likewise differs between \(c\) and \(d\) only in sign. Both updates are therefore conservative (unless a temperature-floor clamp intervenes). In 1D, no floor clamp is applied on intermediate stages; the floor is applied and booked only at the end of each subcycle. The per-material conservation mode applies the corresponding update of each dimension.

    Because coefficients vary per stage in 1D electron STS, three admissibility checks run under both values of face_kappa_policy: (a) the per-stage row rate \(r_i=2g_i/C_i\) must stay at or below \(L=\lambda_{plan}(1+m_b)\); (b) in each insulated region \(r\) connected by open faces, every stage output must stay within \([lo_r,\,hi_r]\) (\(lo_r=\min_{i\in r}T_i^0-T_{floor}\), \(hi_r=\max(\max_{i\in r}T_i^0,T_{floor})+T_{floor}\)); and (c) at the end of each subcycle (before flooring), the heat-capacity-weighted RMS deviation \(\sigma_r(Y)=\sqrt{\sum_{i\in r}C_i(Y_i-a_r)^2/\sum_{i\in r}C_i}\) from the call-entry heat-capacity-weighted region mean \(a_r\) must satisfy \(\sigma_r(Y_s)\le\sigma_r(Y_0)+10^{-9}T_{s,r}\) with \(T_{s,r}=\max(-lo_r,hi_r)\). If a row-rate violation occurs at or before a range or dissipation violation, a non-finite rate (\(\lambda_{seen}=+\infty\)) rejects immediately without replanning, and a finite rate restarts from \(T_e^n\) with a replanned \(\lambda_{plan}\) (\((1+m_b)\max(\lambda_{seen},\lambda_{plan})\) after attempt 1 and \(2\max(\lambda_{seen},\lambda_{plan})\) thereafter, up to sts_bound_retries_max times); if a range or dissipation violation occurs earlier, the subcycle is halved (if the plan is already 1 forward-Euler stage \(s=1\), it rejects without halving). When replanning or halving fails — or when the host path's planned stages across started attempts plus the next planned attempt would exceed a positive sts_total_stages_max (rejected before starting that attempt) — owned temperatures are restored bitwise to their entry values, and the host path requests a full-step retry from the driver (the persistent kernel zeroes floor bookings, flags an error, and stops the run at the end of the step).

    When the secant is used in 2D RZ, the spectrum of the operator changes between stages. Each stage therefore recomputes the row bound \(\lambda_G\) at its input temperatures and checks that it does not exceed \(1+\)sts_bound_margin times (default 1.1 times) the value the plan used. If a stage exceeds it, the step restarts from \(T_e^n\) on a replanned set of stages. The first attempt runs all stages to the end to measure the largest bound and replans for \(1+\)sts_bound_margin times that value. Later attempts stop at the stage that exceeds the plan and double the plan. At most sts_bound_retries_max restarts are made (default 4). If the plan is still exceeded, the step asks the driver for a full-step retry (the outer step is redone with the time step halved). When the retry is disabled (the default of Numerics.hydro.driver_full_step_retry_enabled), or the retry attempt limit (driver_full_step_retry_max_attempts, default 3) or the time-step floor is reached, the run stops.

  7. Re-close once. After all stages, the electron-energy change that corresponds to the temperature change, \(c_{v,e}(T_e^{n+1}-T_e^n)\) (with \(c_{v,e}\) at the step entry), is booked in ee, and for materials with an EOS table Te, Pe, and Cv,e are recomputed from \((\rho,e_e)\). For ideal-gas materials without a table, ee and Pe are computed from the updated temperature. This is the default Numerics.hydro.eos_closure_mode="energy_authoritative"; with "legacy", materials with a table also get ee and Pe from the updated temperature.

Implicit solvers

solver="implicit" solves, in 1D, one backward-Euler tridiagonal system instead of STS. The face conductivity (face_kappa_policy), the flux-limiter coefficient φ, and the cell-center spacing Δr are evaluated once at the step-entry temperature Ten and frozen. Both end boundaries have zero heat flux, and cell_is_void cells become identity rows. The system is solved with cuSPARSE cusparseDgtsv2. This solver adds no conduction restriction to the global time step.

$$ -w_{j-1/2}\,T_{e,j-1}^{n+1} +\left(\frac{\rho c_{v,e}V_j}{\Delta t}+w_{j-1/2}+w_{j+1/2}\right)T_{e,j}^{n+1} -w_{j+1/2}\,T_{e,j+1}^{n+1} =\frac{\rho c_{v,e}V_j}{\Delta t}\,T_{e,j}^{n},\qquad w_{j+1/2}=\frac{A_{j+1/2}\,\kappa_{j+1/2}\,\phi_{j+1/2}}{\Delta r_{j+1/2}}. $$

Here j is the cell, ρcv,eVj is the electron heat capacity of the cell, Δt is the global step, Aj+1/2 is the face area, κj+1/2 is the face conductivity, and φj+1/2 is the flux-limiter coefficient. The solved temperatures are booked by passing the face heat flow wj+1/2(Te,j+1−Te,j) to the two neighboring cells with opposite signs. The booked energies therefore sum to zero up to round-off, whatever the residual of the tridiagonal solve.

Setting solver="implicit" on a 2D single-block mesh issues a warning and solves with STS; on a multi-block mesh the input check rejects it. In 2D the input check rejects "hypre"; in 1D it is accepted only in a build with hypre enabled, but no implicit solve is run, and a warning is issued and STS is used. SNB requires "implicit" in 1D.

solver="dmp_implicit" solves the 2D RZ electron heat conduction in one backward-Euler step of a nonlinear two-point flux that satisfies the discrete maximum principle. The inputs are those of the node-local operator preparation (κ, ρcv,e, void cells, held boundaries). The nonlinear equation is solved by Picard iteration. In each iteration, every cell row is a sum of non-negative coefficients times temperature differences, so the linear system is a strictly diagonally dominant M-matrix and the discrete maximum principle holds. The linear system is solved by GMRES on the GPU, and the Picard iteration is accelerated by Anderson acceleration of depth dmp_anderson_depth (default 5).

The iteration stops when the largest change of the face fluxes is at most dmp_picard_rel_tol (default 10−10) times the largest flux. A step that does not converge within dmp_picard_max_iterations (default 50) iterations, and a step that fails the acceptance check (the booked temperatures must be finite, lie within the range, and agree with the iterate), is retried with a smaller time step without changing the state. The electron conduction does not limit the global step. It cannot be combined with SNB. When ion heat conduction is used together with it, the ions are still solved with STS (or RKL2). The details of the algorithm are in docs/NUMERICS.md §4.3.2 of the repository.

Verification

  • 1D standard-problem verification against analytic solutions: the 1D heat-conduction verification against analytic solutions page tests convergence orders and energy conservation, and records stage and subcycle counts, of 1D RKL2 (with per-stage coefficients) on a DT–Au–DT contact and planar and spherical Barenblatt–Pattle heat sources.
  • STS comparisons: 1D zero-flux eigenmodes with analytic solutions test the spherical, planar, and cylindrical STS computations against the corresponding implicit and per-material computations, checking the spatial order of accuracy and closed-domain electron-energy conservation. In addition, test_conduction_pattle_1d checks the propagation of the \(T^{5/2}\) heat front against the Pattle nonlinear-diffusion similarity solution [2].
  • Implicit solver satisfying the discrete maximum principle (test_conduction_dmp_2d): this test checks that a field linear in z is kept on a distorted mesh, the discrete maximum principle and energy conservation, the booking of the held-boundary energy and that a uniform field is kept, bitwise reproducibility and independence of the winding of the cell vertices, the implicit solution of cells with very different heat capacities, and the acceptance check of the booked temperatures.
