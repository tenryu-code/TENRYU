Laser–plasma interaction (LPI)

Laser–plasma interaction (LPI) refers to processes in which laser light couples to plasma waves and transfers energy by routes other than inverse-bremsstrahlung (IB) absorption. TENRYU treats two LPI effects: cross-beam energy transfer (CBET), which redistributes light power between crossing beams through an ion-acoustic wave, and the generation, transport, and material deposition of hot electrons driven by instabilities near the quarter-critical density. Both run as internal stages of the laser operator: CBET modifies the ray powers, and hot-electron transport uses power from the laser capture records to deposit energy in matter. TENRYU does not solve wave growth or saturation, nor does it model reflected light from backscatter. This page describes the scope of the two effects, their exchange with the ray trace, their place in the operator sequence, support in 1D, and the entry-point keys. The child pages present the equations and algorithms of each effect.

Effects included and not included

Included. CBET and hot electrons.

  • CBET: The beat of two beams drives an ion-acoustic wave through the ponderomotive force, and power moves from one beam to the other through that wave. TENRYU evaluates the exchange with the gain of a linear, steady, strongly damped local plane wave [1]. In the legacy reduction (geometry_mode="legacy"), pairwise exchanges are antisymmetric; with wavelength detuning, TENRYU records the power transferred to the ion-acoustic wave (the wave-action deficit) in the signed ledger E_cbet_iaw. The port_section mode uses the actual port layout and evaluates gain separately at each state's sample points, leaving a conservation residual in the exchange. When contributions near caustics are present, a pump multiplier reduces that residual, and the ledger records the remaining residual as a numerical loss. In port_section, detuning shifts only the resonance location, and E_cbet_iaw remains zero.
  • Hot electrons: Energetic electrons produced by two-plasmon decay (TPD) and stimulated Raman scattering (SRS) are prescribed through a calibrated conversion efficiency and temperature [2]. TPD and SRS differ in their default capture density, temperature, and angular band in the source model. In 1D, transport projects electron motion onto straight chords, and collisional slowing and scattering shorten the projected range. Electrons reaching the outer target surface can be reflected back into the target by the outer sheath.

Not included. The following processes are omitted.

  • The growth and saturation of TPD and SRS waves. The hot-electron conversion efficiency η(t) is a calibrated input; even with eta_mode="model", it follows a reduced relaxation formula driven by local plasma quantities and solves no wave equation.
  • Reflected light lost to SRS and SBS (stimulated Brillouin scattering) backscatter. TENRYU has no key for scattered-light reflectivity and does not trace reflected rays grown from noise. CBET uses the same three-wave coupling as SBS only to exchange power between already traced crossing beams, with one beam acting as the seed for the other.
  • Filamentation, self-focusing, speckle, and wave optics (interference, diffraction). Laser propagation uses geometric-optics rays and excludes these effects.
  • Intensity corrections near caustics in the default legacy mode. A caustic is the envelope of a family of rays, where the geometric-optics intensity diverges [3]. The legacy mode uses no port layout and averages over the azimuth between two rays. The port_section mode caps the intensity near caustics and applies a pump multiplier to reduce the conservation residual when near-caustic contributions are present. Neither mode solves the wave field itself near a caustic.
  • Speckle statistics, polarization dynamics, and broadband illumination (see the CBET page for the reasons).

Interpret the results as the plasma response to the specified CBET coefficient and hot-electron source closure (conversion efficiency and temperature). They are not a first-principles prediction of LPI.

Relation to the ray trace

CBET uses the ray records from Laser drive and absorption. Hot electrons use capture records from the selected laser path, including the radial absorption calculation.

  • Received (CBET): the path length and direction cosine of each ray in each cell it crosses, the initial ray power, and the beam group. From the hydrodynamic state, CBET also reads the ion temperature, flow velocity, and mean mass number. The electron density, electron temperature, and mean ionization are fields already used by the ray trace.
  • Received (hot electrons): the source position and the power selected by the capture rule. The ratio of capture density to critical density defaults to 1/4, with SRS channel defaults of 0.20 in model mode and 0.18 in legacy mode. In the ordinary 1D ray trace, IB absorption from segment entry to the capture position is applied before capture; in a segment that reaches critical density, capture uses the segment-entry power. In radial_absorption_1d, the code tests the density at cell midpoints and uses the first qualifying cell's outer face and entering power.
  • Returned (CBET): the ray power after exchange, from which the subsequent IB deposition is computed. With wavelength detuning in legacy, CBET also returns the ledger E_cbet_iaw of power transferred to the ion-acoustic wave.
  • Returned (hot electrons): with the default subtract_from_laser=True, capture subtracts the converted power from the ray and adds cell-wise hot-electron deposition power to optical deposition power. The 1D transport ledger also tracks escaped, unresolved, and in-flight energy.

CBET and hot electrons can run together only when Laser.cbet.geometry_mode="port_section" and Laser.hot_electron.eta_mode="model" are specified together. With those settings, hot-electron capture uses the ray power after CBET. Enabling both in any other combination fails input validation.

In 1D, the laser power ledger closes in one of two ways depending on the configuration. When port_section and eta_mode="model" run together and the time step has an active capture channel, the optical ledger closes at hot-electron capture after CBET replay (leaving hot-electron deposition, escape, and in-flight energy to the separate transport ledger), comparing the input laser power with the sum of optical deposition power, unabsorbed light power, power captured as hot electrons, power transferred to the ion-acoustic wave, and the CBET exchange residual.

In all other 1D time steps, including those with only CBET enabled, the ordinary laser ledger closes optical and hot-electron power together in a single balance. It compares the input laser power with the sum of total deposition power (optical deposition plus hot-electron deposition), unabsorbed light power, the sum of escaped hot-electron energy, unresolved energy, and the signed change in in-flight energy divided by the time-step duration, power without a receiving cell (power whose destination hydrodynamic cell is void or supercritical and therefore cannot receive it), power transferred to the ion-acoustic wave, and the CBET exchange residual.

Position in the operator sequence

CBET and hot electrons are internal stages of the laser operator rather than separate operators. The default outer step (Strang-type) runs the laser, nuclear burn, the first half of hydrodynamics, conduction, radiation, and the second half of hydrodynamics, in that order. The laser operator handles ray tracing, CBET exchange, and hot-electron capture, transport, and deposition, adding both IB deposition power and hot-electron deposition power to the electron energy source term. When CBET and hot electrons run together, both the initial ray trace and the replay after the CBET solve deplete ray power at the capture position (with zero optical depth from segment entry on a critical-crossing segment), and the final replay after the CBET solve supplies the capture records to hot-electron transport. See Operator splitting and the re-closure discipline for the splitting of the full sequence.

The explicit source limit "hot_electron" bounds the next time step using the ratio of cell electron energy to hot-electron deposition power. In 1D, the limit takes the minimum over cells with positive deposition power, volume, density, and electron energy. Both CBET and hot electrons run on a single MPI rank.

Support in 1D

Effect1D (Main.dimension="1D_SPH")
CBETRequires Mesh.geometry_1d="spherical" and Laser.mode="raytrace_2d". geometry_mode is "port_section" (a reconstruction that rotates a single reference trace to the real port directions) or the conventional "legacy" (exchange between beam pairs through rays; default). port_section is 1D only.
Hot electronsLaser.mode is "radial_absorption_1d" or "raytrace_2d". The angular model is "cone" (default) or "radial". "cone" is not available in the cylindrical geometry (Mesh.geometry_1d="cylindrical"). A planar cone deck requires an explicit planar_inner_face.
