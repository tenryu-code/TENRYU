CBET verification against analytic solutionsreference

Problems with analytic (closed-form) solutions are used to confirm that the 1D spherical CBET (the port_section mode; CBET page) solves the equations it is meant to solve. Two beams enter a uniform plasma sphere without refraction, the same laser time-step function as in production is run once on the GPU, and the exit power of each beam and the exit power of each impact-parameter bin are compared with the analytic solutions. The problems are a check of the incident field without exchange and three problems with exchange: opposed flat-top beams, crossed Gaussian beams, and opposed beams in a linear flow. The decrease of the errors over three resolution levels and its order are also checked. This page explains the setup, the analytic solutions, the compared quantities, the results, the origin of the errors, and what the test does not check, with figures.

At a glance

  • What is compared: the exit powers produced by the production path (ray tracing, rotation of the reference trace, copies into sections, construction of the coupling coefficients, and the Newton solution of the exchange) are compared with integrals of the analytic solution of the same problem. The test sets only the plasma state (uniform density, temperature, and flow) and the switch that turns off absorption.
  • Problems: the incident field (no exchange), depletion and a detuning scan of opposed flat-top beams (Tang's formula), depletion of crossed Gaussian beams (the solution of McKinstrie et al.), and opposed beams of equal frequency passing the resonance points of a linear flow (where the magnitude of the flow component along the chord equals the sound speed).
  • Results: at the finest level, the relative error of each beam's exit power is 1.8×10-4 (flat-top) and 4.1×10-3 (Gaussian), and the relative error of the power at the inbound end is 5.9×10-4 (linear flow), below the acceptance bound of 1 %. The errors decrease each time the resolution is doubled, with observed orders of 1.2–1.9.
  • Wrong references: thirteen references, each with one coefficient made wrong, lie farther from the production output than the larger of the acceptance bound and five times the production error. The comparison therefore detects errors in those coefficients.
  • Not checked: refraction, caustics and the field limiter near them, cases in which the pump multiplier acts, the interaction of absorption and exchange, the in-flight frequency shift, and depletion of three or more beams are outside this test (last section).

Purpose and approach

Ray-based CBET models differ considerably between codes in their implementation details. Follett et al. [1] name, as one reason why the accuracy achievable by ray-based codes is unclear, that very few analytic results are available for use as test cases. TENRYU's port_section contains many approximation stages: one reference trace is rotated onto each port, and the rays copied into sections receive gain at each point from the fields of the other beams (reconstruction section of the CBET page). Confirming that these stages reduce to the correct equations as the discretization is refined requires problems whose solutions are known in closed form.

The test therefore creates, inside the production path, the conditions under which analytic solutions hold: parallel rays through a uniform plasma sphere without refraction. Under these conditions neighboring rays do not cross, so there is no caustic and the field limiter does not act. Each point is crossed by only one ray of each beam, so there is no contribution near a caustic, and the pump multiplier stays 1. The own-sheet exclusion removes nothing. What remains is the discretization error of cells, rays, impact-parameter bins, and sections, together with the iteration tolerance and rounding. The output must therefore approach the analytic solution as the resolution increases.

The test is tests/laser/test_cbet_physics_closed_forms.cu. It fills the plasma state with uniform values and calls the same laser time-step function as production (laser_step) once. The exit powers and the powers of each ray's records are read from the CBET workspace, and the convergence of the iteration and the pump multiplier are read from the [cbet] log line that production writes.

Common setup

The medium is a uniform plasma sphere of radius \(R=0.02\) cm. The normalized electron density is \(\hat n_e=n_e/n_c=0.2\) (\(n_c\) is the critical density), the ionization is \(Z=3.5\), the mean mass number is 6.5, the electron temperature is 2000 eV, the ion temperature is 1000 eV, and the flow is zero except in the linear-flow problem. The laser wavelength is 351 nm and the ion-acoustic damping rate is \(\alpha_{iaw}=0.2\). The inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption coefficient is replaced by 10-30 through a test setting, which makes it effectively zero.

There are two beams; the power weight of port A is 0.1 and that of port B is 0.9. Below, the lower-power port A is called the seed and the higher-power port B the pump. Port A keeps the laser wavelength, and the angular frequency of port B is raised by \(\Delta\omega\) (the detuning; the higher-frequency beam gives power). The focus is placed far away at \(10^7\) cm, so the rays of each beam are parallel, with tilts of at most \(2\times10^{-9}\). In the uniform medium the rays do not bend, and each ray crosses the sphere along a straight chord. The half-length of the chord of a ray with impact parameter \(b\) (its distance from the beam axis) is \(\sqrt{R^2-b^2}\).

The gain law is the same as in the gain model of the CBET page: the intensity \(I_A\) of a seed ray grows under the pump intensity \(I_B\) as

$$ \frac{dI_A}{ds}=\kappa\,I_B\,I_A,\qquad \kappa=K_0\,\frac{\hat n_e}{1-\hat n_e}\,H\,P(g),\qquad K_0=\frac{\lambda_0e^2}{m_ec^3}\frac{Z}{ZT_e+3T_i},\qquad H=\frac{1+(\hat d_A\cdot\hat d_B)^2}{4}, $$

and the pump loses the same amount. Here \(s\) is the distance along the seed ray, \(\kappa\) the gain coefficient, \(\lambda_0\) the laser wavelength, \(e\) and \(m_e\) the electron charge and mass, \(c\) the speed of light, \(T_e,T_i\) the electron and ion temperatures (erg), \(H\) the polarization-average factor (calibration factor f_cbet = 1), \(\hat d_A,\hat d_B\) the unit vectors of the propagation directions of the two beams, and \(P(g)\) the ion-acoustic response function of the normalized detuning \(g\) (the formula on the CBET page). Intensities are power per area.

On resolution level \(\ell=1,2,3\), the number of cells is \(16\cdot2^\ell\), the number of rays per beam \(64\cdot2^\ell\), and the number of impact-parameter bins \(2\cdot2^\ell\); all are doubled together. The number of sections is fixed at 16 in the problems whose two beams share an axis (by the symmetry about the axis, the result does not depend on the number of sections) and is \(4\cdot2^\ell\) for the crossed Gaussian beams. The CBET iteration tolerance is \(10^{-9}\) and the iteration limit is 200.

Compared quantities and acceptance

The analytic solution gives the exit intensity \(I^{\rm out}(b,\varphi)\) per ray (or per plane). To make it the test's reference, the exit power of each beam and the exit power of each impact-parameter bin and section are computed as integrals of the analytic solution. The reference of bin \(\beta\) is \(\int I^{\rm out}\,2\pi b\,db\) over the range \([b_\beta,b_{\beta+1}]\) of impact parameters that the bin covers (for the crossed Gaussian beams, the value at the azimuth \(\varphi_m=2\pi m/N_s\) of section \(m\), divided by the number of sections \(N_s\)). The integrals use eight-point Gauss quadrature, with the intervals halved until the change is at most \(10^{-12}\) times the beam's input power. Three quantities are compared:

$$ E_T=\max_{\text{port}}\left|\frac{P^{\rm out}}{P^{\rm out}_{\rm ref}}-1\right|,\qquad E_D=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{\text{port},\,m,\,\beta}\bigl|P^{\rm out}_{m\beta}-P^{\rm out}_{{\rm ref},m\beta}\bigr|,\qquad E_M=\max_{\text{port}}\left|\frac{\sum_\beta P^{\rm in}_{\beta}}{\sum_\beta P^{\rm in}_{{\rm ref},\beta}}-1\right|. $$

Here \(P^{\rm out}\) is the exit power of a beam from TENRYU, \(P^{\rm out}_{\rm ref}\) its analytic value, \(P^{\rm out}_{m\beta}\) the sum of the exit powers of the rays of section \(m\) and bin \(\beta\), and \(P\) the sum of the input powers of the two beams. \(E_T\) is the larger relative error of the two beams' exit powers, and \(E_D\) is the error of the distribution over bins and sections normalized by the input power. \(E_M\) is used only in the linear-flow problem: \(P^{\rm in}_\beta\) is the sum over bin \(\beta\) of the power of each ray at the end of its inbound sheet, the point closest to the center (the reason is given in the linear-flow section). In the linear-flow problem \(E_D\) is also computed from the powers at the inbound end.

The acceptance conditions on the finest level \(\ell=3\) are \(E_T\le0.01\) (and \(E_M\le0.01\) in the linear-flow problem), \(E_D\le0.05\), and a sum of the exchanges (conservation residual) \(|\mathcal R_0|\le0.01P\). In addition, \(E_T\) and \(|\mathcal R_0|\) on \(\ell=3\) must not exceed their values on \(\ell=1\) (for very small values, up to a floor set by the error estimate), and the observed order \(p=\log_2(E_D^{(\ell)}/E_D^{(\ell+1)})\) from the ratio of \(E_D\) on two neighboring levels must be at least 0.45 (the basis of the order is in the section on the origin of the errors). A run on \(\ell=4\) is added only when all the premises of the next paragraph (converged iteration, a pump multiplier of 1, and the others) hold on \(\ell=3\) and one of two cases occurs. In the first, \(E_T\) on \(\ell=3\) exceeds 0.01, or exceeds the larger of its value on \(\ell=1\) and the floor above (in the linear-flow problem the same applies to \(E_M\); for the opposed beams with \(g=0.5,1.5\), computed on one level only, only the comparison with 0.01 applies). In the second, the pair of levels selected for the order is \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\), and its order is below 0.45. The pairs are examined from the fine side (first \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\), then \(\ell=1\) and \(\ell=2\)), and the first pair that meets the conditions given in the section on the origin of the errors is selected. When \(\ell=4\) is added, the bounds are judged on \(\ell=4\) (the comparison that must not exceed is then made with \(\ell=3\)), and the order is taken from a pair selected again among the four levels. When the pair \(\ell=1\) and \(\ell=2\) is selected with an order below 0.45, or when no pair meets the conditions, the test fails without adding \(\ell=4\). In this run \(\ell=4\) was not needed.

Each run checks that the premises of the analytic solutions also hold in the production output. The log line must show that the iteration converged, that the pump multiplier is 1 and was found in one iteration, that the contribution near caustics (the sum of \(Q^{\rm cp}\)) is zero, that the field limiter acted at no point, and that no fallback of the pump multiplier occurred. The deposition into the plasma must be at most \(10^{-12}\) times the input power. All runs met these conditions, and the deposition was about \(3\times10^{-32}\) times the input power.

Incident-field check (no exchange)

Before the exchange, the incident field that the exchange uses is checked. The calibration factor f_cbet is set to 0 to switch off the exchange, and the pair of flat-top beams and the pair of Gaussian beams are computed on \(\ell=2\) (64 cells, 256 rays per beam, 8 bins). Two quantities are compared. One is the exit power of each ray (ray tube): with no absorption and no exchange, it must equal the launched power times the port weight to a relative error of \(10^{-12}\). The other is the power path integral per cell \(c\), sheet, and bin (the tally summed over sections). This is the \(L_{g,c}\) of the exchange formula, which sets the size of the pump field. The reference is the sum over straight chords,

$$ L^{\rm ref}_{\beta,c}=\sum_{k\in\beta}P_k\Bigl[\sqrt{r_{c+1}^2-b_k^2}-\sqrt{r_c^2-b_k^2}\Bigr]. $$

Here \(k\) runs over the rays of bin \(\beta\), \(P_k\) is the ray's power, \(b_k\) its impact parameter, and \(r_c,r_{c+1}\) are the radii of the inner and outer faces of cell \(c\); a square root of a negative argument is read as 0. The bound is \(10^{-6}\) times the largest tally of that port and sheet.

The largest relative error of the rays' exit powers is \(8.8\times10^{-15}\), and the largest tally difference, relative to the largest tally, is \(1.5\times10^{-8}\) for the flat-top beams and \(1.4\times10^{-14}\) for the Gaussian beams (Figure A). Without exchange, the rotation of the reference trace, the copies into sections, and the tallies per cell reproduce the straight-chord geometry with errors more than two orders of magnitude below the bound.

Three problems with exchange and their analytic solutions

The top row of Figure B shows the analytic solutions of the three problems computed on a fine grid; these curves are not TENRYU output. The middle and bottom rows compare TENRYU's output on the finest level \(\ell=3\) with the reference values per impact-parameter bin (per bin and section for the crossed Gaussian beams). The reference value is the analytic exit intensity (for the linear-flow problem, the intensity at the inbound end) integrated over the bin (section on the compared quantities). The power of each bin is divided by the power launched into that bin (the port power times the bin's share of the cross-section, divided by the number of sections). The difference between TENRYU and the reference is at most 0.1 % in every bin for the opposed flat-top beams and the linear-flow problem (largest values \(4.3\times10^{-4}\) and \(9.6\times10^{-4}\)) and grows toward the outer bins. For the crossed Gaussian beams it is at most 2.9 %, and its sign changes with the section azimuth.

Opposed flat-top beams

The two beams face each other on one axis (\(\hat d_A\cdot\hat d_B=-1\), \(H=1/2\)) and have flat-top cross-sections of radius \(0.8R\). The angular frequency of port B is raised by \(\Delta\omega=2\bar kc_ag\) (\(\bar k\) is the light wavenumber in the plasma and \(c_a\) the sound speed). For opposed beams the beat wavenumber is \(|\mathbf{k}_a|=2\bar k\), so this \(g\) is exactly the normalized detuning of the CBET page. In a uniform medium, the difference \(I_B-I_A\) of the pump and seed intensities on one chord is constant along the chord, and the gain law is solved in closed form (Tang's formula; Eq. (2) of Randall et al. [2]). With the seed's incident intensity \(I_{A0}\), the pump's incident intensity \(I_{B0}\), the intensity ratio \(B=I_{A0}/I_{B0}\), and the gain exponent \(G=2\kappa I_{B0}\sqrt{R^2-b^2}\), the exit intensities are

$$ I_A^{\rm out}=(1-\delta)\,I_{B0},\qquad I_B^{\rm out}=(\delta+B)\,I_{B0},\qquad (1-\delta)(\delta+B)=B\,e^{G\delta}, $$

where \(\delta\) is the root \(0<\delta<1\) of the last equation (\(\delta I_{B0}\) is the difference \(I_B-I_A\), constant along the chord). The test finds this root by bisection to \(2^{-60}\). The pump intensity is chosen so that \(G=2\) on the central chord. With \(B=1/9\), the seed grows to about 4.5 times its incident intensity on the central chord and the pump falls to about 0.62 times (Figure B, top left). Without pump depletion the seed would reach \(I_A^{\rm out}=I_{A0}e^G\) (about 7.4 times), so the depletion is a large effect.

Three detunings, \(g=0.5,1,1.5\), are computed. Changing \(g\) changes the response \(P(g)\), but the test rechooses the pump intensity so that \(\kappa I_{B0}R\) stays constant, so the three analytic solutions have the same shape. In the production path, \(g\) is obtained from the frequency difference and enters \(\kappa\) independently of the pump intensity. The errors of the three cases agree to the printed digits, so the conversion from frequency difference to detuning and the value of the response agree with the reference for every \(g\).

Crossed Gaussian beams

The two beams cross at an angle \(\Theta\) (\(\cos\Theta=0.2928\), about 73°), and both have Gaussian cross-sections of waist radius \(w=R/4\) (cut at a beam radius of \(0.75R\)). The angular frequency of port B is higher by \(\Delta\omega=\bar k\,|\hat d_A-\hat d_B|\,c_a\), and the normalized detuning is \(g=1\) (resonance). In a uniform medium without refraction, the two beams exchange power independently in each plane that contains both propagation directions (the coordinate normal to these planes is \(y\)). McKinstrie et al. [3] gave the solution of this problem in closed form for arbitrary incident cross-sections. In the notation of this problem, the exit intensity of a ray at transverse position \(t\) in plane \(y\) is

$$ I_A^{\rm out}=\frac{I_{A0}f\,e^{\kappa I_{A0}E}}{e^{\kappa I_{A0}E}-1+e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E_{\rm tot}}},\qquad I_B^{\rm out}=\frac{I_{B0}f\,e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E}}{e^{\kappa I_{A0}E_{\rm tot}}-1+e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E}}, $$ $$ E(t,y)=\frac{E_{\rm tot}(y)}{2}\Bigl[1+{\rm erf}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt2\,t}{w}\Bigr)\Bigr],\qquad E_{\rm tot}(y)=w\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,e^{-2y^2/w^2},\qquad \kappa=\frac{K_0}{\sin\Theta}\,\frac{\hat n_e}{1-\hat n_e}\,\frac{1+\cos^2\Theta}{4}\,P(1). $$

Here \(f=e^{-2b^2/w^2}\) is the cross-section shape at the ray's position, \(E\) is the integral of the cross-section shape of this beam's rays that a ray of the other beam has crossed before reaching this ray, and \(E_{\rm tot}\) is the whole integral of the cross-section shape in that plane. The factor \(1/\sin\Theta\) in the gain coefficient comes from the path length \(dt/\sin\Theta\) through a transverse width \(dt\) of the other beam. The pump intensity is chosen so that \(\kappa I_{B0}E_{\rm tot}(0)=2\). Seed rays on the small-\(t\) side meet an undepleted pump and grow by about 7.4 times (\(e^2\)), while rays on the large-\(t\) side meet a pump depleted by the rays before them and grow by only about 3.3 times (Figure B, top center). Because the two beams point in different directions, the pump field varies between sections (azimuths about the beam axis). Only this problem checks convergence in the number of sections.

Opposed beams in a linear flow

The two beams face each other on one axis and have equal frequencies (\(\Delta\omega=0\)). The plasma flows outward from the center with speed \(u=2c_ar/R\) (\(r\) is the radius). With position on a chord measured by the signed distance \(z\) from the point closest to the center, the flow component along the chord is \(u_z=2c_az/R\), and the normalized detuning is \(g=\pm2z/R\) independently of the impact parameter. The resonance \(|g|=1\) occurs on both sides at \(|z|=L_e=R/2\), where the magnitude of the flow component along the chord equals the sound speed (\(|u_z|=c_a\)). Exchange through resonance in such a flow gradient was treated by Kruer et al. [4]. In a uniform medium the intensity difference \(D=I_A-I_B\) is constant along the chord, and the seed intensity is

$$ \begin{gathered} I_A(z)=\Bigl[\frac1D+\Bigl(\frac1{I_{A0}}-\frac1D\Bigr)e^{D\sigma(z)}\Bigr]^{-1},\qquad I_B=I_A-D,\\ \sigma(0)=-\kappa_0L_e\,F\!\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{R^2-b^2}}{L_e}\Bigr),\qquad F(x)=\int_0^x P(g)\,dg. \end{gathered} $$

Here \(\kappa_0=K_0\hat n_e/(2(1-\hat n_e))\) is the gain coefficient without the response, \(\sigma(z)\) is the integral of \(\kappa_0P(g)\) from the seed's entry point to \(z\), and \(F\) is the antiderivative of the response function (the test evaluates it in a closed form with an arctangent). Because \(P\) is odd in \(g\), in the first half of the chord the seed (light moving toward the center) loses power to the pump moving outward, and in the second half the roles reverse and the seed regains the same amount; the same holds for the pump. The exit power after the whole chord therefore equals the input (Figure B, top right), and the comparison of exit powers \(E_T\) does not test the exchange in this problem (measured: \(3\times10^{-9}\)). The test therefore uses \(E_M\), which compares the power at the end of the inbound sheet (\(z=0\)). On the central chord the seed has fallen there to about 0.38 times its input and the pump to about 0.93 times. The pump intensity is chosen so that \(\kappa_0L_eF(2)I_{B0}=1\).

The production path does not include the change of light frequency by the time variation of the plasma density (the in-flight frequency shift; see the CBET page). The analytic solution of this problem also keeps the frequencies constant.

This problem has no caustic, so the pump multiplier stays 1 and the residual is booked as a numerical loss as it is.

Origin of the errors and their order

Under the conditions of the analytic solutions, the difference between the production output and the analytic solution comes from the discretization. There are two main sources.

  • The pump field per cell: the pump field seen by a seed sample point represents depletion by the ratio \(L_{q,c}/L^0_{q,c}\) of the power path integrals of the pump state (exchange formula of the CBET page). This ratio is one value per cell and state, and it averages the change of the pump's depletion within the cell. Where the pump rays are tangent to a spherical shell, the extent of a state along the ray reaches about \(\sqrt{2r(\Delta r+\Delta b)}\) (\(\Delta r\) is the cell width and \(\Delta b\) the bin width), so the error there is first order in the cell and bin widths; elsewhere it is second order.
  • The path near the point closest to the center: integrating the length of a straight chord over radius, the integrand \(ds/dr=r/\sqrt{r^2-b^2}\) diverges at the point closest to the center, \(r=b\). The production path integrates the two sub-intervals next to that point with the substitution \(r=r_t+u^2\) and four-point Gauss quadrature, and uses two-point Gauss quadrature beyond them (placement of sample points on the CBET page). The error that remains in the sub-intervals without the substitution is proportional to \(h^{1/2}\) in the sub-interval width \(h\). Its coefficient is small, and the test estimates the size of this term in each run. The relative error of the chord lengths, weighted by ray power, is \(4.2\times10^{-5}\) (\(\ell=1\)) to \(2.2\times10^{-5}\) (\(\ell=3\)) for the flat-top beams, and its estimated contribution to \(E_D\) is 3–17 % of \(E_D\) (5–18 % for the Gaussian beams and 1–8 % for the linear-flow problem).

Because of the second term, the formal order (the order at sufficiently high resolution) is 1/2. The test sets the lower bound of the order at 0.45, ten percent below 1/2. The observed orders of 1.2–1.9 are higher because, at these resolutions, the first- and second-order terms make up most of the error and the share of the \(h^{1/2}\) term is small. The order is computed on a pair of levels whose \(E_D\) are both at least 100 times the estimate of the other errors (reference quadrature, iteration tolerance, and rounding, about \(10^{-8}\) together) and for which the uncertainty of the order derived from the estimate of the \(h^{1/2}\) term is narrower than 0.45. In this run the pair \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\) qualified for all three problems.

The compared quantity is \(E_T\) for the flat-top and Gaussian beams (\(E_D\) for the section-orientation control only) and \(E_M\) for the linear-flow problem.

What the test does not check

The test uses only conditions under which analytic solutions hold, so it does not check the following.

  • Refraction by density gradients and the caustics it creates; the field limiter at caustics, the contributions near caustics, and cases in which the pump multiplier acts (other tests listed in the verification section of the CBET page check these individually).
  • The interaction of absorption and exchange (this test turns off absorption).
  • The in-flight frequency shift by the time variation of the plasma (also absent from the production path).
  • The treatment of the power of the frequency difference (the Manley–Rowe deficit) and a kinetic ion-acoustic response.
  • Simultaneous depletion of three or more beams.

The values per bin and section are not judged one by one; the judgment uses only the distribution error \(E_D\). When the environment variable TENRYU_CBET_CLOSED_FORMS_DUMP names an output directory, the test writes TENRYU's values and the reference values per bin and section on each level to CSV files. The middle and bottom rows of Figure B are drawn from this output.
