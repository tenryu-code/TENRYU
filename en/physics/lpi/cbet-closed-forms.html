CBET verification against analytic solutionsreference

Abstract

The 1D spherical cross-beam energy transfer (CBET) calculation of TENRYU (the port_section mode; CBET page) was verified against three problems with known analytic (closed-form) solutions. Two beams enter a uniform plasma sphere without refraction or absorption; the same laser time-step function as in production is run once on the GPU, and the exit power of each beam and the exit power of each impact-parameter bin and section are compared with the analytic solution integrated over the bin. The problems are depletion of opposed flat-top beams (PA), depletion of crossed Gaussian beams (PB), and opposed beams passing a resonance in a linear flow (PC). On the finest resolution level \(\ell=3\), the relative error of the beams' exit powers is \(2.0\times10^{-4}\) in PA and \(3.9\times10^{-3}\) in PB, and the relative error of the power at the inbound end in PC is \(4.5\times10^{-4}\), below the 1 % accuracy reported in the literature. The errors of the distribution over bins and sections are \(1.5\times10^{-4}\) (PA), \(6.8\times10^{-3}\) (PB), and \(3.6\times10^{-5}\) (PC); they decrease each time the resolution is doubled, with observed orders of 1.61, 1.17, and 2.11. With exchange switched off, the incident field agrees with an independent sum along straight chords to within \(1.5\times10^{-8}\). Thirteen references, each with one coefficient made wrong, all fall outside the acceptance range.

Introduction

CBET is the exchange of power between two laser beams that cross in a plasma, mediated by an ion-acoustic wave. TENRYU's 1D spherical calculation rotates one reference ray trace onto the direction of each beam, divides each beam's cross-section into impact-parameter bins (the impact parameter is the distance of a ray from the beam axis) and azimuthal sections, and lets every point of a ray receive gain from the fields of the other beams (reconstruction section of the CBET page). The amounts exchanged are solved by Newton's method so that the gains of all rays hold simultaneously (solution of the exchange).

Ray-based CBET models differ considerably between codes in their implementation details. Follett et al. [1] name, as one reason why the accuracy achievable by ray-based codes is unclear, that very few analytic results are available for use as test cases. To show that TENRYU's calculation reduces to the correct equations as the discretization is refined, the size of its error and the rate at which the error decreases with resolution must be checked on problems whose solutions are known in closed form.

Analytic solutions hold when parallel rays travel straight through a uniform medium without refraction. The rays then follow chords, and neighboring rays of the same beam do not cross. There is therefore no caustic (the envelope of a family of rays, where the geometrical-optics intensity diverges [6]). The field limiter and the pump multiplier that TENRYU's CBET uses near caustics do not act, and what remains is the discretization error of cells, rays, bins, and sections, together with the iteration tolerance and rounding. Conversely, refraction, caustics and their treatment, and the interaction of absorption and exchange are outside this verification (limitations).

The test is tests/laser/test_cbet_physics_closed_forms.cu. It fills the plasma state with uniform values and calls the same laser time-step function as production (laser_step) once. The exit powers and the powers of each ray's records are read from the CBET workspace, and the convergence of the iteration and the pump multiplier are read from the [cbet] log line that production writes. The numbers on this page are the output of this test run on 12 November 2026 on one GPU (NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q); all four test cases passed.

Setup

Geometry

The medium is a plasma sphere of radius \(R=0.02\) cm. Two beams (ports A and B) enter from outside the sphere. Figure A shows the arrangement of the three problems.

In PA and PC, the axis of port A is the unit vector \((0.36,0.48,0.80)\) and that of port B is its opposite, so the two beams face each other on one axis (\(\hat d_A\cdot\hat d_B=-1\), where \(\hat d\) is the unit vector of a beam's propagation direction). The cross-section is flat-top with radius \(r_b=0.8R\). In PB, the axis of port B is \((0.60,-0.64,0.48)\), and the propagation directions cross with \(\cos\Theta=\hat d_A\cdot\hat d_B=0.2928\) (\(\Theta=72.97^\circ\)). The cross-section is Gaussian, with intensity proportional to \(e^{-2b^2/w^2}\), radius \(w=R/4\), and cut off at \(r_b=0.75R\). The power outside the cut-off is \(1.5\times10^{-8}\) of the total. The focus is placed far away at \(10^7\) cm, so the rays of each beam are parallel, with tilts of at most \(1.6\times10^{-9}\) rad.

The power weight of port A is 0.1 and that of port B is 0.9. Below, the lower-power port A is called the seed and the higher-power port B the pump. In PA and PB, port A keeps the laser wavelength of 351 nm, and the angular frequency of port B is raised by \(\Delta\omega\) (the detuning; the higher-frequency beam gives power). In PC both beams have the same frequency. The path of each ray is divided at its turning point into an inbound and an outbound sheet (terms on the CBET page). On a straight chord, the inbound sheet runs from the entry point to \(z=0\) and the outbound sheet from \(z=0\) to the exit point.

Plasma

The plasma state is uniform in the sphere (Figure B). The normalized electron density is \(\hat n_e=n_e/n_c=0.2\) (\(n_c\) is the critical density; \(n_e=1.81\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}\)), the ionization is \(Z=3.5\), the mean mass number is \(A=6.5\), the electron temperature is \(T_e=2000\) eV, and the ion temperature is \(T_i=1000\) eV. The sound speed is \(c_a=[(ZT_e+3T_i)/(Am_p)]^{1/2}=3.84\times10^{7}\) cm/s (temperatures are converted to erg in this formula; \(m_p\) is the proton mass), and the ion-acoustic damping rate is \(\alpha_{iaw}=0.2\). The flow is zero in PA and PB; in PC it is the linear outward flow \(u_r=2c_ar/R\).

Resolution levels

On resolution level \(\ell\), the number of cells is \(16\cdot2^\ell\), the number of rays per beam \(64\cdot2^\ell\), and the number of impact-parameter bins \(2\cdot2^\ell\); all are doubled together (Table 1). Cells are equally spaced in radius and bins in impact parameter. The number of sections is fixed at 16 in PA and PC, whose beams share an axis: by the symmetry about the axis the pump field does not depend on azimuth, and the result does not depend on the number of sections. In the crossed problem PB the number of sections is also doubled, \(4\cdot2^\ell\). The CBET iteration tolerance is \(10^{-9}\) and the iteration limit is 200.

Analytic solutions

Common gain law

The gain law is the same as in the gain model of the CBET page. The intensity \(I_A\) of a seed ray grows under the pump intensity \(I_B\), with distance \(s\) along the ray, as

$$ \frac{dI_A}{ds}=\kappa\,I_B\,I_A,\qquad \kappa=K_0\,\frac{\hat n_e}{1-\hat n_e}\,H\,P(g),\qquad K_0=\frac{\lambda_0e^2}{m_ec^3}\frac{Z}{ZT_e+3T_i},\qquad H=\frac{1+(\hat d_A\cdot\hat d_B)^2}{4}, $$

and the pump loses the same amount. Here \(\kappa\) is the gain coefficient, \(\lambda_0\) the laser wavelength, \(e\) and \(m_e\) the electron charge and mass, \(c\) the speed of light, and the temperatures \(T_e,T_i\) are in erg. \(H\) is the polarization-average factor with the calibration factor f_cbet = 1. \(P(g)=\alpha_{iaw}g/[(\alpha_{iaw}g)^2+(1-g^2)^2]\) is the ion-acoustic response function of the normalized detuning \(g\) (the formula on the CBET page). \(P\) is odd in \(g\) and takes the values \(\pm1/\alpha_{iaw}=\pm5\) at \(|g|=1\). Intensities are power per area. Because the medium is uniform and the rays are straight, the cross-section of a ray tube is constant, and a ray's power is proportional to its intensity. In each problem the pump intensity is chosen so that the gain exponent takes a set value (given with each solution).

PA: opposed flat-top beams

Equations. On the chord of impact parameter \(b\), take the coordinate \(s\) with the seed's entry point at \(s=0\) and its exit point at \(s=\ell_b=2\sqrt{R^2-b^2}\). The seed travels toward \(+s\) and the pump toward \(-s\). The pump decreases along its own direction by the gain, so

$$ \frac{dI_A}{ds}=\kappa I_AI_B,\qquad \frac{dI_B}{ds}=\kappa I_AI_B. $$

These are Eq. (1) of Randall et al. [2] without absorption. The boundary conditions are \(I_A(0)=I_{A0}\) and \(I_B(\ell_b)=I_{B0}\). \(H=1/2\), and the detuning is set by the angular-frequency shift of port B, \(\Delta\omega=2\bar kc_ag\) (\(\bar k=(\omega_0/c)\sqrt{1-\hat n_e}\) is the light wavenumber in the plasma and \(\omega_0\) the laser angular frequency). For opposed beams the beat wavenumber is \(|\mathbf k_a|=2\bar k\), so this \(g\) is exactly the normalized detuning of the gain model. \(\kappa\) is constant along the chord.

Assumptions. Uniform medium, straight rays, no absorption, steady state, constant \(\kappa\) (Table 2).

Derivation. Subtracting the two equations shows that \(I_B-I_A\) is constant along the chord. Writing it as \(\delta I_{B0}\) gives \(dI_A/ds=\kappa I_A(I_A+\delta I_{B0})\); separating the variables and integrating from \(s=0\) to \(\ell_b\), the increment of \(\ln[I_A/(I_A+\delta I_{B0})]\) equals \(\kappa\delta I_{B0}\ell_b\). At the exit point \(I_A+\delta I_{B0}=I_B(\ell_b)=I_{B0}\), and at the entry point \(I_{A0}+\delta I_{B0}=I_B(0)\).

Solution. With the intensity ratio \(B=I_{A0}/I_{B0}=1/9\) and the gain exponent \(G=\kappa I_{B0}\ell_b\),

$$ I_A^{\rm out}=(1-\delta)\,I_{B0},\qquad I_B^{\rm out}=(\delta+B)\,I_{B0},\qquad (1-\delta)(\delta+B)=B\,e^{G\delta}, $$

where \(\delta\) is the root \(0<\delta<1\) of the last equation. Setting Randall et al.'s \(R\) to \(1-\delta\) and their \(Q\) to \(G\), the last equation is their Eq. (2) (Tang's formula) [2]. The test finds the root by bisection to \(2^{-60}\). The pump intensity is chosen so that \(\kappa I_{B0}R=1\), that is, \(G=2\) on the central chord. On the central chord the seed grows to 4.45 times its incident intensity and the pump falls to 0.62 times. Without pump depletion the seed would grow by \(e^G=7.39\), so the depletion is a large effect.

Three detunings, \(g=0.5,1,1.5\), are computed. Changing \(g\) changes the response \(P(g)\) (\(P(0.5)=0.175\), \(P(1)=5\), \(P(1.5)=0.181\)), but the test rechooses the pump intensity so that \(\kappa I_{B0}R=1\) holds. The three analytic solutions therefore have the same shape. In the production path, \(g\) is obtained from the frequency difference and enters \(\kappa\) independently of the pump intensity.

PB: crossed Gaussian beams

Equations. The two beams cross at the angle \(\Theta\). In each plane that contains both propagation directions (the coordinate normal to these planes is \(y\)), the seed grows at the rate \(\kappa I_AI_B\) along its own direction and the pump decreases at the same rate along its own direction. McKinstrie et al. [3] wrote these steady equations (their Eqs. (3)–(4)) in coordinates along the two beam directions and gave the solution in closed form for arbitrary incident cross-sections (Eqs. (5)–(9)). In a uniform medium without refraction, the exchange is independent between planes of different \(y\). The detuning is \(\Delta\omega=\bar k\,|\hat d_A-\hat d_B|\,c_a\), so \(g=1\) (resonance).

Assumptions. As for PA, and in addition that the region where the beams overlap lies inside the sphere. The Gaussian radius \(w=R/4\) is a quarter of the sphere radius, so most of the power meets this condition.

Solution. In plane \(y\), let \(t\) be the transverse position of a seed ray (the coordinate in that plane normal to the seed direction). In the notation of this problem, Eqs. (8)–(9) of McKinstrie et al. [3] read

$$ I_A^{\rm out}=\frac{I_{A0}f\,e^{\kappa I_{A0}E}}{e^{\kappa I_{A0}E}-1+e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E_{\rm tot}}},\qquad I_B^{\rm out}=\frac{I_{B0}f\,e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E}}{e^{\kappa I_{A0}E_{\rm tot}}-1+e^{-\kappa I_{B0}E}}, $$ $$ E(t,y)=\frac{E_{\rm tot}(y)}{2}\Bigl[1+{\rm erf}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt2\,t}{w}\Bigr)\Bigr],\qquad E_{\rm tot}(y)=w\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,e^{-2y^2/w^2},\qquad \kappa=\frac{K_0}{\sin\Theta}\,\frac{\hat n_e}{1-\hat n_e}\,\frac{1+\cos^2\Theta}{4}\,P(1). $$

Here \(f=e^{-2b^2/w^2}\) is the cross-section shape at the ray's position, \(E\) is the integral of the cross-section shape of this beam's rays that a ray of the other beam has crossed before reaching this ray, and \(E_{\rm tot}\) is the whole integral of the cross-section shape in that plane. The factor \(1/\sin\Theta\) in the gain coefficient comes from the path length \(dt/\sin\Theta\) through a transverse width \(dt\) of the other beam. \(H=(1+\cos^2\Theta)/4=0.271\). The pump intensity is chosen so that \(\kappa I_{B0}E_{\rm tot}(0)=2\).

Seed rays on the small-\(t\) side meet an undepleted pump and grow by up to nearly \(e^2\). Rays on the large-\(t\) side meet a pump depleted by the rays before them and grow less. Because the two beams point in different directions, the pump field differs between sections (azimuths about the beam axis). Only this problem checks convergence in the number of sections.

PC: opposed beams in a linear flow

Equations. The two beams face each other on the same axis as in PA and have equal frequencies (\(\Delta\omega=0\)). Position on a chord is measured by the signed distance \(z\) from the chord's midpoint (the turning point). The component of the flow \(u_r=2c_ar/R\) along the chord is \(u_z=2c_az/R\), so the normalized detuning is \(g=2z/R=z/L_e\) (\(L_e=R/2\)) independently of the impact parameter (Figure B(c)). The resonance \(|g|=1\) lies on both sides at \(z=\pm L_e\). Exchange through resonance in such a flow gradient was treated by Kruer et al. [4], whose Eq. (7) gives the gain integral in a linear flow in an arctangent form. The equations are those of PA, with the gain coefficient \(\kappa=\kappa_0P(z/L_e)\), \(\kappa_0=K_0\hat n_e/(2(1-\hat n_e))\), varying with \(z\).

Assumptions. As for PA, and in addition a steady flow and light frequencies that do not change in flight.

Derivation. As in PA, \(D=I_A-I_B\) is constant along the chord. Taking \(\sigma(z)=\int\kappa_0P\,ds\), measured from the seed's entry point, as the independent variable and setting \(y=1/I_A\), the equation \(dI_A/d\sigma=I_A(I_A-D)\) becomes the linear equation \(dy/d\sigma=Dy-1\), which gives

$$ I_A=\Bigl[\frac1D+\Bigl(\frac1{I_{A0}}-\frac1D\Bigr)e^{D\sigma}\Bigr]^{-1},\qquad I_B=I_A-D. $$

Because \(P\) is odd, \(\sigma\) over the whole chord is zero. The seed therefore gives power to the pump in the first half of the chord (moving toward the center), and in the second half the roles reverse and it regains the same amount. The exit power equals the input, and \(D=I_{A0}-I_{B0}\) holds exactly.

Solution. At the end of the inbound sheet, \(z=0\),

$$ \sigma(0)=-\kappa_0L_e\,F\!\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{R^2-b^2}}{L_e}\Bigr),\qquad F(x)=\int_0^x P(g)\,dg=\frac{1}{d}\Bigl[\arctan\frac{2x^2-2+\alpha_{iaw}^2}{\alpha_{iaw}d}+\arctan\frac{2-\alpha_{iaw}^2}{\alpha_{iaw}d}\Bigr],\qquad d=\sqrt{4-\alpha_{iaw}^2}. $$

The pump intensity is chosen so that \(\kappa_0L_eF(2)I_{B0}=1\). At \(z=0\) on the central chord, the seed has fallen to 0.383 times its input and the pump to 0.931 times. Because the exit power equals the input, a comparison of exit powers does not test the exchange in this problem. The test compares the power at \(z=0\) instead (\(E_M\) in the next section).

Quantities compared and acceptance

TENRYU's output. The test reads the power of each ray's records from the CBET workspace. The exit power is a ray's last record, and the power at the inbound end in PC is the last record of the inbound sheet. Their sums per section \(m\) and impact-parameter bin \(\beta\) are \(P^{\rm out}_{m\beta}\) (in PC, \(P^{\rm in}_{m\beta}\)), and the sum over all rays is the beam's exit power \(P^{\rm out}\).

Reference. The analytic solution gives the exit intensity \(I^{\rm out}(b,\varphi)\) per ray (or per plane). The reference of bin \(\beta\) is the integral \(\int I^{\rm out}\,2\pi b\,db\) over the bin's impact-parameter range \([b_\beta,b_{\beta+1}]\). In PB, the integral at the azimuth \(\varphi_m=2\pi m/N_s\) of section \(m\) is divided by the number of sections \(N_s\). The integrals use eight-point Gauss quadrature, with the intervals halved until the change is at most \(10^{-12}\) times the beam's input power. The reference is an integral of the analytic solution itself and does not use TENRYU's discretization (ray positions or cells).

Error measures. Three quantities are compared:

$$ E_T=\max_{\text{port}}\left|\frac{P^{\rm out}}{P^{\rm out}_{\rm ref}}-1\right|,\qquad E_D=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{\text{port},\,m,\,\beta}\bigl|P^{\rm out}_{m\beta}-P^{\rm out}_{{\rm ref},m\beta}\bigr|,\qquad E_M=\max_{\text{port}}\left|\frac{\sum_{m\beta} P^{\rm in}_{m\beta}}{\sum_{m\beta} P^{\rm in}_{{\rm ref},m\beta}}-1\right|. $$

Here \(P\) is the sum of the input powers of the two beams. \(E_T\) is the larger relative error of the two beams' exit powers, \(E_D\) the error of the distribution over bins and sections normalized by the input power, and \(E_M\) the relative error of the power at the inbound end in PC. In PC, \(E_D\) is also computed from the powers at the inbound end. The sum of the exchanges (conservation residual) \(\mathcal R_0\), the difference between the power gained by the seed and the power lost by the pump, is read from the production log line.

Acceptance. On the finest level \(\ell=3\), the test requires \(E_T\le0.01\) (also \(E_M\le0.01\) in PC), \(E_D\le0.05\), and \(|\mathcal R_0|\le0.01P\). The 1 % values follow the conclusion of Follett et al. [1] (p. 14) that ray codes can achieve 1 % accuracy with 1 % uncorrected energy-conservation error. The 5 % for \(E_D\) has no literature value and is TENRYU's choice. For runs with three levels, \(E_T\) on \(\ell=3\) (also \(E_M\) in PC) must not exceed the larger of its value on \(\ell=1\) and its floor estimated from the other errors. The bound on \(|\mathcal R_0|\) is the larger of its value on \(\ell=1\) and one millionth of the input power. Also, the observed order \(p=\log_2(E_D^{(\ell)}/E_D^{(\ell+1)})\) must be at least 0.9. The bound 0.9 lies 10 % below the formal order 1 (convergence), following the criterion of Oberkampf and Roy [5] (p. 342) that an observed order within 10 % of the formal order verifies it. Pairs of levels are examined from the fine side, and the first pair whose \(E_D\) are both at least 100 times the estimate \(e_o\) of the other errors (reference quadrature, iteration tolerance, and rounding) is chosen. Requiring the discretization error to exceed the other errors by a factor of 100 also follows Oberkampf and Roy [5] (p. 201). Level \(\ell=4\) is added only when the convergence, deposition, field-limiter, and pump-multiplier checks of Table 2 hold on \(\ell=3\), and either a power error fails its absolute bound or its comparison with the coarse level, or a three-level run selects the pair \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\) and finds an order below 0.9. The added level is then compared with \(\ell=3\), and the order is judged again. Exceeding the distribution-error or conservation-residual bound alone does not add a level. No added level was needed in this run.

Incident-field check (no exchange)

Before the exchange results, the incident field that the exchange uses is checked. This check is called PZ. The calibration factor f_cbet is set to 0 to switch off the exchange, and the flat-top beam pair of PA and the Gaussian beam pair of PB are computed on \(\ell=2\). Two quantities are compared.

One is the exit power of each ray (ray tube). With no absorption and no exchange, it must equal the launched power times the port weight to a relative \(10^{-12}\). The other is the power path integral per cell \(c\), sheet, and bin \(\beta\) (summed over sections). This is the \(L_{g,c}\) of the exchange formula, which sets the size of the pump field. The reference is the straight-chord sum computed independently by the test,

$$ L^{\rm ref}_{\beta,c}=\sum_{k\in\beta}P_k\Bigl[\sqrt{r_{c+1}^2-b_k^2}-\sqrt{r_c^2-b_k^2}\Bigr]. $$

Here \(k\) runs over the rays of bin \(\beta\), \(P_k\) is the ray's power, \(b_k\) its impact parameter, and \(r_c,r_{c+1}\) are the radii of the inner and outer faces of cell \(c\); a square root of a negative argument is read as 0. The bound is \(10^{-6}\) times the largest value of that port and sheet.

This reference is not an analytic solution. The ray positions \(b_k\) and powers \(P_k\) are built by the test with the same rule as TENRYU (rays equally spaced in impact parameter, powers from the ring areas and the cross-section shape), so the reference is the continuous closed form \(\int_{b_\beta}^{b_{\beta+1}}I(b)\,2\pi b\,[\sqrt{r_{c+1}^2-b^2}-\sqrt{r_c^2-b^2}]\,db\) evaluated by the midpoint rule at the ray positions. The check therefore confirms that the rotation of the rays, the copies into sections, and the per-cell tallies reproduce the straight geometry; it does not test the discretization error in the number of rays (the \(E_D\) of the exchange problems tests that).

Results

The premises of Table 2 thus hold in the production output. Table 4 lists the errors on each level.

PA

Figure C compares the exit power of each bin directly with the analytic solution. In the upper panel, TENRYU's values (open circles) lie on the references (short black segments) in every bin, and each reference is the bin average of the analytic curve.

On \(\ell=3\) the relative differences are \(+2.3\times10^{-6}\) to \(+4.5\times10^{-4}\) for the seed and \(-1.8\times10^{-6}\) to \(-1.9\times10^{-4}\) for the pump, and on every level they grow toward the outer bins. The seed grows slightly more than the analytic solution, and the pump loses slightly more. The growth toward the outer bins is consistent with the error near the turning point described under convergence. The length of a chord inside the cell that holds its turning point is about \(\sqrt{2b\Delta r}\) (\(\Delta r\) is the cell width); relative to the half-chord \(\sqrt{R^2-b^2}\), on \(\ell=3\) it grows from 2 % in the innermost bin (\(b=0.025R\)) to 17 % in the outermost bin (\(b=0.775R\)).

In the runs with \(g=0.5\) and \(g=1.5\) (on \(\ell=3\) only), the relative difference of each bin agrees with that of \(g=1\) to within \(2\times10^{-13}\), and \(E_T=2.02\times10^{-4}\) and \(E_D=1.51\times10^{-4}\) are the same. Because the test chooses the pump intensity so that the analytic solutions have the same shape, this shows that the production path's conversion from frequency difference to detuning and the value of the response \(P(g)\) agree with the reference for all three \(g\).

PB

In PB the pump field depends on azimuth, so both the values per section and the values summed over sections are shown (Figure D).

Per section (top left), the seed varies strongly with azimuth, reaching 6.5 times its incident power at 67.5°, 2.2 times at 157.5°, and 3.4 times at 247.5°, and TENRYU's values follow this variation. The variation with azimuth reflects where the rays of a section cross the other beam (the value of \(E\)). On \(\ell=3\), the relative differences per section and bin range from \(-2.5\times10^{-2}\) to \(+2.9\times10^{-2}\), two orders of magnitude larger than the \(10^{-4}\) level of PA. Their sign changes smoothly with azimuth, and they cancel partly in the sum over sections. The relative differences of the section sums (bottom right) are \(-7.7\times10^{-4}\) to \(+9.1\times10^{-3}\) for the seed and \(-7.2\times10^{-3}\) to \(+5.3\times10^{-4}\) for the pump.

The setup of the test shows two differences that can make the errors of PB larger than those of PA. First, PB is the only problem that includes the discretization of the pump field between sections; in PA and PC the beams share an axis and the pump field does not depend on azimuth. Second, the cross-section of the PB beams varies on the scale of the Gaussian radius \(w=R/4\), which the bin width \(0.047R\) on \(\ell=3\) resolves with only about five bins; in the flat-top beams of PA, the depletion varies on the scale of the sphere radius. The test output does not separate the error into these parts, so these data cannot decide which one dominates.

In exit power, the seed is \(+0.39\) % and the pump \(-0.16\) % off on \(\ell=3\), giving \(E_T=3.9\times10^{-3}\). On the coarse levels the exit power of the seed differed from the analytic value by more than 1 %: \(1.931\times10^{17}\) erg/s on \(\ell=1\) against an analytic \(1.879\times10^{17}\) erg/s (\(+2.8\) %), and \(+1.3\) % on \(\ell=2\). The conservation residual \(\mathcal R_0\) decreases with resolution from \(-3.2\times10^{-3}P\) to \(-3.0\times10^{-4}P\). Each ray's gain is taken from the field at its own position, so the power gained by the seed and the power lost by the pump are computed at different points, and a difference remains in the crossed problem (pump multiplier section of the CBET page). This problem has no caustic, so the pump multiplier stays 1 and the residual is booked as a numerical loss as it is.

PC

In PC the power at the inbound end \(z=0\) is compared (Figure E).

At \(z=0\) the seed has fallen to 0.38–0.42 times its input and the pump to 0.93 times. Outer chords are shorter, so the integration range of \(F\) is narrower and the seed decreases less. Every chord in the beam passes the points \(|g|=1\) (even the outermost half-chord, \(0.6R\), exceeds \(L_e\)). On \(\ell=3\) the largest relative difference is \(7.6\times10^{-4}\) for the seed and \(3.8\times10^{-5}\) for the pump, and as in PA it grows toward the outer bins. The pump difference is likely smaller because the pump changes less (7 %) than the seed (60 %). The exit-power error \(E_T\) is \(3\times10^{-9}\): TENRYU reproduces the analytic result that the exit power equals the input, to the level of the iteration tolerance \(10^{-9}\).

Convergence

Figure F shows the errors against the resolution level. Every measure of the three problems decreases from level to level.

The observed orders, from the pair \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\), are 1.61 for PA, 1.17 for PB, and 2.11 for PC, all at least 0.9. The estimate \(e_o\) of the other errors was \(5.9\times10^{-9}\) to \(9.5\times10^{-9}\); even the smallest \(E_D\) (PC on \(\ell=3\), \(3.6\times10^{-5}\)) is about 6000 times larger, so the orders are hardly affected by the other errors (even at a factor of 100, the order moves by at most \(\log_2(1.01/0.99)=0.029\)).

Under the conditions of the analytic solutions, the difference between TENRYU's output and the analytic solution comes from the discretization. There are two main sources.

  • The pump field per cell: the pump field seen by a seed sample point represents depletion by the ratio \(L_{q,c}/L^0_{q,c}\) of the power path integrals of the pump state (exchange formula of the CBET page). This ratio is one value per cell and state, and it averages the change of the pump's depletion within the cell. Where the pump rays are tangent to a spherical shell (near the turning point), the extent of a state along the ray reaches about \(\sqrt{2r(\Delta r+\Delta b)}\) (\(\Delta b\) is the bin width), so the error there is first order in the cell and bin widths; elsewhere it is second order.
  • The path near the turning point: integrating the length of a straight chord over radius, the integrand \(ds/dr=r/\sqrt{r^2-b^2}\) diverges at the turning point \(r=b\). The production path obtains the integral along the path of each seed sub-interval in closed form, with the density represented as \(\eta_1/r^2+\eta_0\), and gives half of it to each of the sub-interval's two sample points (placement of sample points on the CBET page). The density of this problem is uniform, so the closed form is the chord length times a constant, and the integral along the path carries only rounding error, also in the sub-interval that holds the turning point. The test estimates this rounding error as the unit roundoff of double precision times the condition number of the sub-interval formulas near the turning point; in this run the estimate was at most \(5.0\times10^{-13}\) (relative). What remains is the error of averaging the quantities evaluated at the sample points, such as the pump field, with two equal weights. It is proportional to the square of the sub-interval width \(h\) in a sub-interval where those quantities vary smoothly in radius, and can be proportional to \(h\) in the sub-interval that holds the turning point.

Both sources have a part that is first order in the resolution, so the formal order is 1. The observed orders above 1 are likely because the second-order part is still large on \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\). That PB has the order closest to 1 suggests that its first-order part is relatively large, but the test output cannot separate the contributions.

Limitations

The verification uses only conditions under which analytic solutions hold, so it does not check the following.

  • Refraction by density gradients and the caustics it creates; the field limiter at caustics, the contributions near caustics, and cases in which the pump multiplier acts (other tests listed in the verification section of the CBET page check these individually).
  • The interaction of absorption and exchange (this test turns off absorption).
  • The in-flight frequency shift by the time variation of the plasma (also absent from the production path).
  • The treatment of the power of the frequency difference (the Manley–Rowe deficit) and a kinetic ion-acoustic response.
  • Simultaneous depletion of three or more beams.

The values per bin and section are not judged one by one; the judgment uses only the distribution error \(E_D\). The test does not output the intensity along a ray or the per-cell depletion ratio of the pump, so the error of PB cannot be split into the discretization in sections and the discretization in bins. When the environment variable TENRYU_CBET_CLOSED_FORMS_DUMP names an output directory, the test writes TENRYU's values and the reference values per bin and section on each level to CSV files. Figures C–E are drawn from this output.
