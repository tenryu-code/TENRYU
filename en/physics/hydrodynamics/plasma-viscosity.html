Plasma viscosity reference

TENRYU adds unmagnetized Braginskii shear stress to Lagrangian hydrodynamics. In planar, cylindrical, and spherical 1D, the model uses the ion species present in each cell, applies the stress through corner forces, and returns their work to internal energy. The ion and electron channels can be used separately or together.

At a glance

  • The module is off by default. species="ion" is the default channel.
  • species="both" adds the ion and electron viscosities.
  • The 1D driver combines acoustic and viscous restrictions on the time step. Both hydro stages check stability.

Physical mechanism

Coulomb collisions transfer momentum across a velocity gradient. In the classical single-species closure the viscosity is proportional to pressure times collision time. The collision time scales as temperature to the power 3/2 divided by density and the Coulomb logarithm. At fixed charge and logarithm, the resulting viscosity scales as temperature to the power 5/2.

Plasma viscosity damps resolved shear and acoustic modes. Artificial viscosity supplies shock dissipation when the shock width is smaller than the cell size (Manheimer & Colombant 2007, pp. 542–543).

Braginskii coefficients and channels

In 1D, the following expressions give the unbounded coefficients for a single ion species and the electron channel, and the ion–ion Coulomb logarithm for any pair of ion species.

$$ \begin{aligned} n_i&=\rho/(A m_p),\qquad n_e=\bar Z n_i,\\ L_{jk}&=\max\!\left(2,23-\ln\frac{Z_jZ_k\sqrt{n_e(Z_e+T_i/T_e)}}{T_i^{3/2}}\right),\\ \eta_i&=f\,0.96\,n_i kT_i\tau_i,\qquad \tau_i=k_i\frac{\sqrt A\,T_i^{3/2}}{n_i\bar Z^4 L_{ii}},\\ \eta_e&=f\,\eta_{00}^e(Z_e)n_e kT_e\tau_e,\qquad \tau_e=k_e\frac{T_e^{3/2}}{n_e Z_e L_{ei}}. \end{aligned} $$

The density is ρ, A is the cell's effective ion mass in amu with a floor of 1, and the proton and electron masses are denoted by mp and me. Ion and electron temperatures are in eV and use their respective temperature floors. The factor k converts eV to erg, f is eta0_scale, and the collision times are τi and τe, and their prefactors ki and ke are 2.0852 × 107 and 3.4409 × 105 in these units. The viscosities ηi and ηe are in poise. The cell mean charge Z̄ has a floor of 1 and sets the electron density and the ion charge in the single-ion formula. The number densities are ni and ne. The indices j and k label the ions in a collision pair. Their charges in the logarithm have a floor of 1. Ljk is the pair logarithm, with Lii denoting the single-species value. Every pair uses the same cell-wide screening term. The electron–ion logarithm Lei follows NRL Plasma Formulary (2023), p. 34, item (b), using the electron collision charge defined below and a floor of 2. Positive lnlambda_fixed replaces every logarithm. [1, 10, 12]

The electron factor η00e is the fit of Velikovich et al. (2001), p. 4525, Eq. (3), evaluated at the electron collision charge. The fit gives 0.7331 at charge 1 and tends to 1.81. Braginskii (1965), p. 253, Eq. (4.45) gives 0.733, and p. 218, Eq. (2.25) gives 0.73. [1, 3]

The ion list includes the species of all non-void materials with positive volume fraction in the cell. A material contributes in proportion to its volume fraction divided by its mean ion mass in amu, with a mass floor of 10−12. Composition keys or TMAT EOS species data give the individual masses and number fractions. A material with at most one entry contributes its bulk mass and mean charge. The multi-ion closure solves for two Laguerre coefficients per retained ion species, using its mass, charge, number fraction, and pair collision frequencies (Simakov & Molvig 2016, pp. 032115-3, 032115-8, Eqs. (5), (55)–(58), and Eqs. (D16)–(D18)). Up to 16 entries are supported across the non-void materials of the deck, counting at least one per material. The cell omits entries below 10−30 of the largest cell number fraction. [11]

$$ P=\sum_j y_j m_{1j},\qquad Q=\sum_j y_j m_{2j},\qquad Z_e=\begin{cases}\max(1,Q/P),&P>0,\\1,&P\le0.\end{cases} $$

The sums run over the cell's retained ion species. The weight yj is the number fraction of entry j, and m1j and m2j are the first and second charge-state moments. TMAT EOS files with /ionization provide both moment tables, even for single-entry materials. Each table is interpolated on the logarithmic ion-density and electron-temperature axes with endpoint clamps. Where a single charge is available, the moments use the charge and its square. Multi-entry materials use TMAT first moments, fixed composition charges or TMAT nuclear charges under the fixed model, or species Thomas–Fermi charges. Single-entry materials without moment tables use their material mean charge. These moments use the charges before the ion-closure floor. The ratio of the second sum to the first gives the electron collision charge with a floor of 1.

Terms used in this calculation

Channel

A channel is the ion or electron contribution to the shear stress. The coefficients are selected by species. The constant model assigns all of eta_const to the selected channel, or half to each when both are selected.

The history group /diagnostics/plasma_viscosity_history/ records the physical ion and electron coefficient maxima and their ratio statistics using classical coefficients without cap, scaling, or fixed logarithms. The effective coefficient and the instantaneous heating rates reflect the run settings.

Mean-free-path cap

The mean-free-path cap replaces each single-ion or electron collision time by the smaller of its classical value and the cap length divided by the species thermal speed. The cap length is mfp_cap_cells times the cell width in 1D. Each thermal speed is the square root of the temperature in erg divided by the particle mass. The same factor applies to ion and electron channels. Mason et al. (2014), p. 022705-1, applies this rule to ions. The electron cap extends that rule to the electron mean free path. [7]

In the multi-ion closure, each species has a mean free path from its thermal speed divided by its collision frequency summed over all partners. Each species contributes a cap factor equal to the smaller of 1 and the cap length divided by that mean free path. The multiplier on the multi-ion coefficient is the weighted mean of these factors. The weights are proportional to species partial pressure divided by total collision frequency (Vold et al. 2017, p. 042702-6, Eq. (15)). [13]

The factor defaults to 20, and zero removes the cap. When a cap acts, the coefficient depends on the mesh spacing. Refinement at fixed cap factor does not recover the classical coefficient. The constant-viscosity model has no cap.

Stage stability check

The stage stability check evaluates each active cell with positive width at the start and midpoint of the 1D hydro call.

$$ q=\frac{\Delta t}{D}\bigl(c+\sqrt{c^2+Ga^2}\bigr),\qquad c=\sigma W+c_d,\qquad G=\frac{(A_l^2+A_h^2)D^2}{2V^2}. $$

The call takes a time increment denoted by Δt. The face-area factor G accounts for geometry. The cell width and volume are D and V, and Al and Ah are its face areas. The speed c combines the artificial-viscosity speed (zero in expansion) with the viscous speed cd defined below. The speed a is the acoustic speed, with its radiation contribution to the squared speed evaluated at call entry for both stages. A cell fails if its dimensionless measure q exceeds 1 + 10−12 or is non-finite. A failure rejects the trial. The full-step retry policy determines whether the driver halves the increment and retries or stops (Benson 1992, pp. 311–312, Dobrev et al. 2012, preprint p. 24). [14, 15]

Stress, work, and time step

In 1D the cell exerts one force on each of its two nodes. The force uses the trace-free strain rate (Braginskii 1965, p. 218, Eq. (2.20)).

$$ \begin{aligned} e&=(u_h-u_l)/D,\qquad \theta=e+\alpha h,\\ W_{rr}&=2e-2\theta/3,\qquad W_{tt}=2h-2\theta/3,\\ F_l&=\eta_{\rm eff}S(W_{rr}-gW_{tt}),\qquad F_h=-\eta_{\rm eff}S(W_{rr}+gW_{tt}). \end{aligned} $$

The subscripts l and h label the lower and upper nodes. u is nodal velocity and D is their separation. The radial strain is e, the trace is θ, and the tangential strain h is the sum of the two velocities divided by the sum of their radial positions. The geometry factor α is 0 for planar, 1 for cylindrical, and 2 for spherical geometry. The factor g is α times the cell width divided by the sum of the radii. The tangential strain and g are zero in planar geometry or when the radius sum is non-positive. The ratio S is cell volume divided by width, and the effective viscosity is the sum of the selected coefficients. At an active interior node with positive mass, the viscous acceleration is the sum of the neighbouring corner forces divided by nodal mass. Node zero, inactive nodes, and fixed or reflecting outer nodes receive no viscous force. A free or pressure-driven outer node receives only the force from the inner cell. Void and inactive cells and cells with non-positive width contribute zero force.

$$ \Delta E_s=-\Delta t(F_{s,l}^h\bar u_l+F_{s,h}^h\bar u_h), \qquad \dot E_s=V(\eta_s/2)W{:}W . $$

The superscript h marks forces evaluated at the midpoint state. The work uses the mean of the start and end velocities, denoted by an overbar. The sign transfers the kinetic energy lost to a channel's nodal forces to the cell's internal energy. The cell volume is V. The species index s selects the ion or electron coefficient in the same force formula. A two-temperature update sends each channel's work to its own energy. A one-temperature update adds their sum. The predictor uses the entry-state force and the predicted midpoint velocity with a half increment. Both stages use the call-entry charges. In spherical geometry the work is in erg, while the diagnostic cell rate on the right is in erg/s and uses the output-state coefficient and strain. The cylindrical and planar measures are per unit axial length and per unit area. The double contraction sums the squares of the strain-rate tensor components. The work can have either sign in the coupled step (Caramana et al. 1998, p. 234, Eq. (12), and p. 236, Eq. (20)). [16]

$$ \begin{aligned} \Delta t_{\rm PV}&=\min_{\text{active cells},\,D>0}\frac{D}{c'+\sqrt{c'^2+a'^2}},\\ c'&=\frac{\kappa W}{C_{\rm CFL}}+\frac{c_d}{\phi},\qquad a'=\frac{a}{C_{\rm CFL}}. \end{aligned} $$

The 1D candidate uses the start-of-step state. D is the cell width, and c prime and a prime are scaled speeds. The artificial-viscosity speed in the first numerator and the acoustic speed a come from the same calculation as the acoustic time-step candidate. The factors are cfl_hydro and dt_safety, respectively. The viscous speed cd is the cell width times the largest absolute row sum of the cell's two-node viscous matrix, divided by twice the cell mass. Rows of nodes without viscous force are omitted. In planar geometry with a retained row, this speed is 4/3 times the effective viscosity divided by density and width. With both scaled speeds zero, the cell imposes no bound. Non-finite scaled speeds or a candidate outside the finite positive range in a constraining cell stop the run before the step with plasma_viscosity_stability_1d.

The driver compares this candidate with the hydro and other time-step candidates (see the global time-step rule). The selected constraint is recorded as braginskii when it sets the step. With only the viscous contribution present, inverse time steps add across channels within each cell. The grid-wide limit takes the minimum over cells, and the joint limit is at least the reciprocal of the sum of the inverse grid-wide channel limits. With the cap enabled, the lower bound on the viscous-only time step depends on both selected channels, the coefficient multiplier, and the cell geometry (NUMERICS §3.1.13).

One viscosity application, step by step

  1. Build the ion list and collision charges. Use the cell state at call entry.

  2. Evaluate coefficients and forces. Use each stage's temperatures and geometry and the call-entry charges. Evaluate the stage stability check at both stages.

  3. Return work to internal energy. Use the forces and time centering described in Stress, work, and time step.

Where it matters

The classical coefficients assume weak coupling, non-degenerate electrons, and mean free paths short compared with gradient lengths. Strongly coupled heavy ions and degenerate electrons fall outside this regime. The electron Fermi temperature is 7.86 eV at an electron number density of 1023 cm−3 and scales as density to the power 2/3 (Atzeni & Meyer-ter-Vehn 2004, p. 367, Haines 2024, pp. 050501-6–8). The floors on mean charge, ion closure charges, electron collision charge, and Coulomb logarithms regularize separate parts of the model. Neutral collisions are outside the closure. A free-path cap regularizes the time step at the resolved scale. [17, 9]

Namelist controls

These are all the keys under Numerics.hydro.plasma_viscosity. The defaults are the same in Studio's input fields and in the parser when a deck leaves a key unset. Studio writes the block into 1D decks only.

KeyDefaultPurpose
enabledFalseMaster switch for plasma viscosity.
model"braginskii"Braginskii coefficients or verification-only "constant".
species"ion"The channels that enter the effective viscosity: ion, electron, or additive both.
eta_const0.0 PUniform dynamic viscosity for the constant model. With species="both" each channel gets half, so the effective viscosity stays eta_const.

mfp_cap_cells20.0Number of cell widths \(C\) in the mean-free-path cap; zero disables it. Studio's input field accepts positive values only.
lnlambda_fixed0.0Zero evaluates the Coulomb logarithms. A positive value fixes all ion–ion and electron–ion logarithms.
dt_safety0.3The 1D resolved time-step factor must be in (0, 2] with hydro and plasma viscosity enabled. See SPECIFICATION §6.4.7 for input errors.

Materials.materials[*].composition_A supplies the ion mass in amu for each composition entry. Every mass must be finite and positive. The list must have the same length as composition_Z. The difference between its number-fraction-weighted mean and the material mass is measured relative to the material mass. A difference above 5% is rejected, and one above 1% triggers a warning. The key is required for a non-void material with two or more composition entries when 1D hydro runs with plasma viscosity enabled after TENRYU_BRAG_ENABLE, or with av_heat_to="viscosity_ratio". The check applies to all viscosity models and channel settings. TMAT EOS species masses come from the file.

Each 1D checkpoint stores /metadata/plasma_viscosity/species_signature. The restart compares the material count and the file-derived species and charge signatures under those same conditions on 1D hydro and the switches. It refuses an absent dataset, a different material count, or a differing material signature. Checkpoints rejected by the earlier 1D hydrodynamics transition report that refusal first. The configuration and deck hash checks also apply to composition_A (SPECIFICATION §6.4.3 and §7.4).

Verification

The braginskii_planar_wave_decay, braginskii_cylindrical_wave_decay, and braginskii_spherical_wave_decay checks compare measured 1D acoustic decay rates with the analytical rates. The braginskii_electron_wave_decay check repeats the planar comparison at constant viscosity with the electron channel alone and with both channels.
