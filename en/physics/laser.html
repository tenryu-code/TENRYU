Laser drive & absorption reference

TENRYU constructs ray bundles from beam optics and time waveforms, solves plasma refraction and inverse-bremsstrahlung (IB) absorption, and heats the electron system. The default 1D spherical path tracks chords in an \((R,Z)\) plane with radial field lookup; 2D RZ tracks 3D rays through an axisymmetric field. Terms specific to this calculation are defined in the terms section.

At a glance

  • Beam power callables are frozen into tables at initialization; no Python runs during the simulation.
  • 1D raytrace_2d follows one chord per ring (terms); 2D RZ raytrace_3d follows 3D rays. In 1D the default characteristic integration advances each ray segment by segment (terms) by quadrature from its conserved angular momentum and reflects rays at the critical radius.
  • IB segment losses are deposited conservatively; the power left at termination enters the balance according to the selected termination rule.
  • An underdense corona is required to start a cold-solid drive. It is placed either in the initial fluid mesh or, as a ghost corona, on the LaserMesh only (practical requirement below).

Physical mechanism

An electromagnetic wave in plasma obeys the dispersion relation \(\omega^2=\omega_{pe}^2+c^2k^2\). The plasma frequency \(\omega_{pe}\) rises with electron density, so to the laser the plasma is a refractive medium with index \(n=\sqrt{1-n_e/n_{\rm crit}}\) — smaller the denser the plasma. The critical density \(n_{\rm crit}\) is where the local plasma frequency reaches the laser frequency and propagation stops; it scales inversely with the square of the laser wavelength (\(\lambda^{-2}\)), so frequency-tripled 351 nm light reaches nine times higher density than the 1053 nm fundamental. As in ordinary optics, light bends toward the higher refractive index — here the low-density side — so rays bend down the density gradient, away from the dense core. Light cannot propagate beyond the critical surface: a normal-incidence ray reflects there, while an oblique ray turns around earlier, at its turning point (the radius of the turning point is the Bouguer radius, defined below). Geometric optics — treating the wave as a bundle of rays — applies because the wavelength is tiny compared with the corona's density-gradient lengths.

The plasma absorbs through inverse bremsstrahlung (IB): electrons quiver collectively in the wave field, and electron–ion collisions randomize that ordered quiver energy into heat. The conversion rate is set by the electron–ion collision frequency, giving the absorption coefficient \(\kappa_{\rm IB}\propto \hat n\,\nu_{ei}/\sqrt{1-\hat n}\) (where \(\hat n=n_e/n_{\rm crit}\) is the electron density normalized to critical) with \(\nu_{ei}\propto \bar Z n_eT_e^{-3/2}\ln\Lambda\). Since \(\nu_{ei}\) is itself proportional to \(n_e\), absorption grows as the density squared (\(\kappa_{\rm IB}\propto\hat n^2\) apart from the near-critical factor), and the \(T_e^{-3/2}\) makes cold plasma absorb more strongly. The factor \(1/\sqrt{1-\hat n}\) is the group-velocity slowdown near critical: light that travels more slowly lingers near the critical surface and absorbs more. An underdense corona represented on the grid is therefore a physical prerequisite, not a numerical convenience: at a hard vacuum–solid jump, rays reach critical with no low-density path along which absorption could have happened, and reflect essentially unabsorbed. The practical-requirement box below quantifies the consequence.

The v1 default drive is collisional IB along refracted geometric rays. On the 1D spherical raytrace path, the Langdon factor — the IB reduction from non-Maxwellian distributions — is applied by default (it turns on automatically when all beams share one gaussian / super_gaussian / flat_top profile, resolves itself to off otherwise, and can be switched off explicitly with Laser.ib.langdon_model="off"). Wave optics (interference and speckle) are not default terms. The collisional charge \(Z_{\rm eff}=\sum_sn_sZ_s^2/\sum_sn_sZ_s\) for mixed plasmas (\(n_s\) and \(Z_s\) are the number density and charge number of each ion species; Laser.ib.zeff_model) is also resolved automatically: it is on when a material supplies an ionization-stage table or, in 1D spherical runs, a known multi-species composition (e.g. CD), and off otherwise. The remaining extended-absorption options — the Coulomb logarithm at the laser frequency (Laser.ib.coulomb_log_model) and resonance absorption at the turning point (Laser.ra) — are opt-in and off by default. All of these extensions are 1D-only; separate kernels for cross-beam energy transfer (CBET; see the CBET page) and for hot electrons (see the hot-electron page) cover the laser–plasma-interaction channels that TENRYU does model.

The implementation keeps two promises: determinism and conservation. On the determinism side, beam-waveform callables are frozen into tables at initialization so no Python runs during the simulation, and per-ray deposits pass through fixed-order per-beam reductions, making the results bitwise reproducible. On the conservation side, every call closes the energy ledger "incident = absorbed + unabsorbed" exactly; how each ray ended — critical-surface escape or deposit, mesh exit, or intensity cutoff — decides which side of that ledger receives its residual power.

Terms used in this calculation

A 1D fluid carries only radial profiles, but the laser follows each ray along its own path in a beam-fixed \((R,Z)\) plane and counts the absorption on the way (next section). TENRYU builds, advances, and reuses this bundle of rays in the units below. These terms carry meanings specific to this calculation, and to TENRYU's implementation in particular, so they are defined here together.

Chord, ring, and launch plane

A chord is the path of one ray in the \((R,Z)\) plane that contains the beam axis and the target center (the meridional plane). \(R\) is the distance from the beam axis, and \(Z\) is the coordinate along the beam axis, with the target center as the origin and the source side positive. In vacuum a chord is a straight line (a chord of the sphere); in the plasma the ray refracts and the path curves. The 1D fluid quantities depend only on the radius \(r=\sqrt{R^2+Z^2}\), so each ray needs to be followed in this one plane only.

A ring is one of the concentric rings of equal width into which the aperture on the launch plane (next paragraph) is divided about the beam axis. The aperture radius is \(R_{\rm beam}=|Z_{\rm init}-z_f|/(2F)\), where \(F\) is the F-number, \(Z_{\rm init}\) is the position of the launch plane, and \(z_f\) is the position of the focus (next subsection). Dividing \(R_{\rm beam}\) into \(N_{\rm ray}\) equal widths \(\Delta R=R_{\rm beam}/N_{\rm ray}\) gives the rings \(k=0,1,\dots,N_{\rm ray}-1\). For each ring one ray is launched from the point at the ring-center radius \(R_k=(k+1/2)\Delta R\), along the straight line through the focus. The ring area \(\Delta A_k=\pi[(k+1)^2-k^2]\Delta R^2\) and the intensity profile set the weight \(w_k\) of the ring's power, which the ray carries (beam optics). The division into rings and the direction of the rays are shown in the top panel of Figure B.

The number of rings \(N_{\rm ray}\) is Laser.rays_per_beam; its 1D default is 1000 (Studio's default is also 1000), and it must be at least 10. Finer rings improve the resolution of the intensity profile and of the refraction and absorption that differ from ray to ray, but the cost grows in proportion to the number of rays traced. In the 1D cylinder and slab, each ring is also divided in azimuth about the beam axis into Laser.raytrace.azimuthal_rays parts (default 16), and that many rays are traced per ring. In 2D RZ, rays_per_beam (default 128) is not a ring count but the number of points per side of the 2D array that covers the beam cross-section.

The launch plane is the plane \(Z=Z_{\rm init}\), perpendicular to the beam axis, from which the rays are launched. It lies \(Z_{\rm init}\) from the target center toward the source, at the outer end \(R_{\rm max}\) of the LaserMesh. The LaserMesh is rebuilt at every call to follow the fluid state, so the launch plane moves outward as the corona expands. Between the launch plane and the plasma surface is vacuum, where a ray travels in a straight line; it refracts after it enters the plasma.

Impact parameter, launch radius, and radius on the plane through the target center

The impact parameter is the distance \(b\) between the target center and the line of a ray when the ray is taken as a straight line without refraction. For a ray entering from vacuum it equals the Bouguer invariant \(B\) (next section), which is conserved along the path. In a spherically symmetric plasma with a given profile, the impact parameter alone fixes the radius at which the ray turns (Figure A).

In 1D, besides the impact parameter, two radii measure a distance from the beam axis for each ray. They are easily confused, so they are distinguished here for the ray of ring \(k\).

The launch radius \(R_k=(k+1/2)\Delta R\) is the distance from the beam axis of the point from which the ray of ring \(k\) is launched.

The radius on the plane through the target center, \(R_{{\rm ref},k}\), is the distance from the beam axis of the point at which the line of the ray crosses the plane \(Z=0\), which passes through the target center perpendicular to the beam axis. The intensity profile is evaluated at this radius. Unlike the launch plane, this plane does not move, so the weights do not change as the corona expands. Figure B compares the three quantities, with the focus off and on the target center and with the launch plane moved.

A ray travels from the point at launch radius \(R_k\) along the straight line through the focus \((R,Z)=(0,z_f)\) on the axis, so the radius on the plane through the target center and the impact parameter are

$$ R_{{\rm ref},k}=R_k\frac{|z_f|}{|Z_{\rm init}-z_f|},\qquad b_k=\frac{|z_f|\,R_k}{\sqrt{R_k^2+(Z_{\rm init}-z_f)^2}}. $$

Here \(z_f\) is the position of the focus, measured from the target center along the beam axis with the source side positive. For a beam that travels toward \(-z\) (Studio's default) it equals the z coordinate of Laser.beams[].focus. Studio's default is 0 µm, the target center.

Neither \(R_{{\rm ref},k}\) nor \(b_k\) depends on the position \(Z_{\rm init}\) of the launch plane: \(R_{{\rm ref},k}=(k+1/2)|z_f|/(2FN_{\rm ray})\) and \(b_k=|z_f|\sin\theta_k\) with \(\tan\theta_k=(k+1/2)/(2FN_{\rm ray})\), where \(\theta_k\) is the angle between the ray and the beam axis, so they are set by the position of the focus, the F-number, and the ring number alone. When the focus is not at the target center (\(z_f\neq0\)), both increase monotonically with \(k\). In general \(b_k\) equals neither the launch radius \(R_k\) nor \(R_{{\rm ref},k}\).

If the focus is at the target center (\(z_f=0\)), every ray passes through the center, so \(b_k=0\) and \(R_{{\rm ref},k}=0\) for all rings, and the intensity profile does not change the weights (the ring areas alone set them). The input validation warns when an intensity profile is given in this case.

Marching and segment

Marching is the solution of the ray equation (next section) in steps of length \(\Delta s\) (Laser.raytrace.integrator="leapfrog"). The step is based on the spacing of the radial nodes at the ray's position multiplied by Laser.raytrace.cfl_ray (default 0.8) and is adapted along the path (step 3 of the procedure); one ray takes at most Laser.raytrace.max_steps steps (default and upper limit 100000). Marching is the general method: it also advances the 3D rays of 2D RZ. In 1D spherical geometry the default is the characteristic integration (its section), which has neither the step-size error of marching nor its step refinement near turning points.

A segment is one part of a ray's path over which the optical depth \(S\) and the deposition are evaluated. In marching, the path advanced in one step is a segment, and the loss of a segment that crosses a face of a fluid cell is deposited in the cell of its midpoint. In the characteristic integration, a segment is the path between break points such as the radial nodes and the faces of the fluid cells, and each segment lies in one fluid cell.

LaserMesh

The LaserMesh is the structured \((R,Z)\) grid that the laser keeps separately from the fluid mesh for the ray tracing. The fluid density, electron temperature, and mean ionization \(\rho,T_e,\bar Z\) are mapped onto its nodes to form the electron density normalized to critical, \(\hat n\), and its gradient, and a ray reads these fields at its position by interpolation between nodes. The outer end \(R_{\rm max}\) of the LaserMesh is the position \(Z_{\rm init}\) of the launch plane. A ray that leaves the LaserMesh ends its trace, and its remaining power goes to the unabsorbed term (mesh exit).

In 1D spherical geometry the radial nodes are rebuilt at every call. A fine band of uniform spacing \(dR_{\rm fine}\) surrounds the critical radius, with a target width of \(64\,dR_{\rm fine}\) on each side, and outside the band the spacing grows geometrically (ratio 1.08 toward the core, 1.05 toward the corona). \(dR_{\rm fine}\) is the smallest fluid cell width near the critical surface multiplied by Laser.lasermesh.mesh_factor; the default of mesh_factor is 0.5, and the value that Studio writes together with the ghost corona is 0.1. The \(Z\) nodes mirror the \(R\) nodes about 0 (\(n_z=2n_r\)). The outer end is Laser.lasermesh.r_max_factor (default 1.5) times the outer face of the outermost cell with \(\hat n\) at or above Laser.lasermesh.rmax_n_hat_threshold (default 0.001), and the number of radial cells \(n_r\) is held at or below Laser.lasermesh.nr_max (default 4096) by enlarging \(dR_{\rm fine}\) when it would be exceeded. In 2D RZ one LaserMesh of \(n_r\times n_z\) cells (Laser.lasermesh.nr and nz; default 128 × 256) is built along the fluid symmetry axis, and its node geometry is fixed after initialization.

Because the LaserMesh is independent of the fluid cells, it can place nodes outside the outermost material cell (ghost corona and vacuum). Up to the outermost material cell, the radial reference array that the 1D ray kernel reads is built on nodes fixed to the faces and centers of the fluid cells (LaserMesh and marching), so changing mesh_factor does not move the nodes in that region. The LaserMesh spacing therefore affects the node spacing in the outer region (the base of the marching step and the number of segments in the characteristic integration) and the position of the launch plane; the GPU memory and the cost of mapping the fields grow with the node count \((n_r+1)(n_z+1)\).

Beam group

A beam group is a set of beams that share one trace in 2D RZ. Beams form one group when their polar angles (the angle between the beam direction and the symmetry axis) differ by less than \(10^{-6}\) rad and they have the same F-number, the same intensity profile, and the same focus. The focus is compared as a position relative to the beam direction, and positions that coincide under a rotation about the axis or a mirror reflection count as the same. In an axisymmetric field, beams with the same polar angle give the same absorption pattern when rotated about the axis, so only one representative beam per group is traced, and its rays carry the group power (the sum of the powers of the members). When CBET is on, only beams with the same wavelength offset \(\Delta\lambda\) are grouped. In 1D spherical geometry each beam is its own group, and the mechanism by which beams of identical conditions share one trace (the chord section) works separately from this.

Reuse cache

The reuse cache (Laser.raytrace_skip) stores the result of the last trace and uses it, without tracing the rays, while the plasma has changed little. It stores the deposition profile per unit power \(\hat f_{g,c}\) (the deposition in cell \(c\) divided by the power that produced it), together with the density \(\rho\), electron temperature \(T_e\), and mean ionization \(\bar Z\) of every cell at that time. There is one profile per beam in 1D (\(g\) numbers the beams) and a single profile in 2D RZ (the sum of the depositions of all beam groups divided by the total power). On a reused step the deposition in cell \(c\) is formed as \(\sum_g\hat f_{g,c}P_g\Delta t\), with the current power \(P_g\) (the power of beam \(g\) in 1D, the total power of all beams in 2D RZ).

The cache is off by default (Laser.raytrace_skip.enabled=False). When it is on, the trace is reused while the plasma change \(\delta\) since the cache was taken stays below threshold (default 0.01), for at most max_consecutive consecutive steps (default 10); the other conditions that force a new trace are listed in step 7. With norm="max_relative" (default), \(\delta\) is the largest relative change from the cached value of \(\rho\), \(T_e\), and \(\bar Z\) over all cells; with "l2_relative", it is the root mean square over all cells and the three quantities of the relative change from the current value. The denominators have lower limits that avoid division by zero (the floors for \(\rho\) and \(T_e\), and 0.01 for \(\bar Z\)).

A reused step skips the cost of tracing the rays. Rescaling by the power is exact as long as the absorption coefficient does not depend on the power. The Langdon factor depends on the intensity, so while it is on the rescaling is approximate, and the limit of 1% on the change of the total power bounds the error. The 2D RZ profile is a single one for all beam groups, so a change in the ratio of the group powers does not update it, and the test sees only the change of the total power. The deposition profile stays that of the time the cache was taken while the plasma changes by up to \(\delta\).

Tail closure

The tail closure closes the remaining absorption of a ray that approaches the critical surface with an analytic expression instead of numerical integration, and ends the ray there. Near critical \(\kappa_{\rm IB}\propto(1-\hat n)^{-1/2}\) diverges, so tracing the path to the critical surface in finite steps and cutting it off underestimates the absorption of the last step. The tail here is the last stretch before the critical surface, where \(\kappa_{\rm IB}\) grows quickly. For a ray that climbs the density gradient toward the critical surface, \(\hat n\) is taken to rise linearly to 1 locally, and the remaining optical depth is

$$ \tau_{\rm tail}=\frac{2A_0}{|\nabla\hat n|_0}\left(u_0-\tfrac23u_0^3+\tfrac15u_0^5\right),\qquad u_0=\sqrt{1-\hat n_0}. $$

The subscript 0 denotes the value at the point where the closure starts, and \(A_0\) is the coefficient \(A\) in \(\kappa_{\rm IB}=A\hat n^2/\sqrt{1-\hat n}\). The absorbed power \(\Delta P=-P\operatorname{expm1}(-\tau_{\rm tail})\) is deposited in the fluid cell that contains the starting point (\(\tau_{\rm tail}\) is limited to 700), and the remaining power follows the termination mode (terminate_mode; see termination).

The closure starts when the ray moves up the density gradient (\(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\hat n>0\)), \(\hat n\ge0.5\), \(1-\hat n<A_0/|\nabla\hat n|\), and the local turning gap \((1-\hat n)\sin^2\theta\) is below \(4\times10^{-4}\) (\(\theta\) is the angle between the ray direction and the density gradient); a more oblique ray is not closed and keeps being traced until it turns by itself. When a ray reaches the critical radius (\(\hat n=1-\varepsilon_{\rm crit}\); Laser.absorption.eps_crit, default \(10^{-4}\)), it is closed with the same expression whatever the other conditions. In a linear layer at normal incidence the expression equals the analytic integral, so the total absorption follows the 16/15 law (verification). Marching and the characteristic integration with critical_handling.terminate=True use this closure; the default characteristic integration (terminate=False) reflects the ray at the critical radius and does not evaluate it.

The 1D spherical chord raytrace

The fluid state depends only on radius, but ray kinematics are solved in a beam-local \((R,Z)\) plane to retain oblique path length and refraction. The radius \(r=\sqrt{R^2+Z^2}\) selects density, temperature, and ionization; absorbed power goes directly to the corresponding hydro cell. By spherical symmetry, the spatial direction of a beam does not change the absorption pattern. Only when all lit beams agree exactly in their power over the time step, F-number, focus on the axis, wavelength offset \(\Delta\lambda\), and intensity profile (kind, \(w_0\), \(m\), and the samples of a table profile) is one beam traced and its deposition added once per beam. If any one of these differs, each beam is traced separately with its own power. Each beam is also traced separately with the verbose log (Main.verbosity="verbose"), with ray output (Laser.ray_output_count above 0), on the steps that write a snapshot (the ray-density diagnostic needs them), and when CBET is on.

The plasma refractive index and ray equation are

$$ n_{\rm refr}=\sqrt{\max(\varepsilon_n,1-\hat n)},\qquad \hat n=\frac{n_e}{n_{\rm crit}},\qquad \frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}=-\frac{c^2}{2}\nabla\hat n. $$
  • \(\varepsilon_n\) (namelist Laser.absorption.eps_n, default \(10^{-4}\)) floors \(n_{\rm refr}\), keeping both the index and IB denominators finite at critical; the CBET prefactor uses the same floor.
  • \(d\mathbf v/dt=-\tfrac{c^2}{2}\nabla\hat n\) is the time-parametrized ray equation. It is motion in the effective potential \(c^2\hat n/2\), so symplectic steppers can integrate its trajectories.
  • \(B=n_{\rm refr}(r)r\sin\alpha\) is the spherical Bouguer invariant, expressing conservation of ray angular momentum in a radial index. Its drift is the trace-quality audit used by the verification checks (\(\alpha\) is the angle between the ray and the radial direction — defined in the next paragraph).
  • In \(\kappa_{\rm IB}=\hat n\nu_{ei}/[c\sqrt{1-\hat n}]\), \(\hat n\nu_{ei}\) is collisional conversion of quiver energy, \(1/\sqrt{1-\hat n}\) is group-velocity slowdown, and \(\nu_{ei}(T_e,\bar Z,\ln\Lambda)\) is evaluated in cgs + eV.
  • \(\Delta P=-P\operatorname{expm1}(-S)\) computes the absorption of the ray power \(P\) from the segment optical depth \(S\) (trapezoidal in marching, by quadrature in characteristic integration). Evaluating with expm1 instead of subtracting \(1-e^{-S}\) directly avoids catastrophic cancellation in optically thin (\(S\ll1\)) segments. Each segment's loss is deposited (at its midpoint in marching) and the power updates by difference, so summing the losses along one ray telescopes — adjacent terms cancel — and reproduces the entry-minus-exit power exactly.
  • \(w_k\) is the profile value times the exact area of the rings that partition the aperture (terms), normalized over all rings, so the ray bundle carries the specified beam power. In 1D the profile is evaluated at the radius \(R_{{\rm ref},k}\) on the plane through the target center (terms). This plane does not move, unlike the launch plane, so the weights do not change as the launch plane moves out with the corona.

For a spherically symmetric refractive index, the Bouguer invariant \(B=n_{\rm refr}(r)\,r\sin\alpha\) (with \(\alpha\) the angle between the ray and the radial direction) is constant along the path — the optical analogue of angular-momentum conservation in a central force field. At the turning point (where the radius of the ray is smallest) the ray runs perpendicular to the radius, \(\alpha=\pi/2\), so the radius of the turning point — the Bouguer radius \(r_B\) — is the solution of \(B=n_{\rm refr}(r_B)r_B\). Rays with different impact parameters \(b\) turn smoothly at different depths and undergo inverse-bremsstrahlung absorption on both the inbound and outbound legs; only a nearly head-on \(b\approx0\) ray reaches the critical surface without turning; the default characteristic integration reflects it there (marching and terminate=True terminate it). Example paths are shown in Figure A.

Beam optics, spot profile, and waveform

The F-number \(F=f_{\rm focal}/D_{\rm lens}\) gives half-angle \(\theta_{\rm beam}=\tan^{-1}(1/2F)\), and the aperture radius on the launch plane is \(R_{\rm beam}=|Z_{\rm init}-z_f|/(2F)\) (\(Z_{\rm init}\) is the position of the launch plane and \(z_f\) the position of the focus; terms). In 1D the aperture is divided without gaps into \(N_{\rm ray}\) rings; the ray of ring \(k\) is launched from the launch radius \(R_k=(k+1/2)\Delta R\) along the line through the focus, with \(\Delta R=R_{\rm beam}/N_{\rm ray}\) and ring area \(\Delta A_k=\pi[(k+1)^2-k^2]\Delta R^2\) (the geometry is in Figure B).

The Gaussian or super-Gaussian relative intensity \(I\) and the weight \(w_k\) of ring \(k\) are given in terms of the radius \(R_{\rm ref}\) on the plane through the target center (terms):

$$ I(R_{\rm ref})\propto \exp\!\left[-2\left(\frac{R_{\rm ref}}{w_0}\right)^{2m}\right],\qquad w_k=\frac{I(R_{{\rm ref},k})\Delta A_k}{\sum_j I(R_{{\rm ref},j})\Delta A_j}. $$

Here \(R_{{\rm ref},k}=R_k|z_f|/|Z_{\rm init}-z_f|\) is the distance from the beam axis of the point where the line of ray \(k\) crosses the plane through the target center perpendicular to the beam axis. \(w_0\) is the radius at which the intensity falls to \(e^{-2}\) on this plane. \(m=1\) is Gaussian; larger \(m\) flattens the top. If the focus is at the target center (\(z_f=0\)), \(R_{{\rm ref},k}=0\) for all rings and the profile does not change the weights. In 2D RZ, the profile is sampled on a circular array normal to the beam axis.

LaserBeam.power(t_s) accepts seconds and returns watts. Initialization samples it through Main.t_end into a frozen linear table. Studio generates a square pulse with optional rise/fall, a Gaussian from peak or energy plus FWHM and center, or a piecewise-linear point/CSV table.

LaserMesh and marching

In 1D spherical geometry, one beam-local \((R,Z)\) structure is induced from the radial nodes of the LaserMesh: its \(Z\) nodes mirror the \(R\) nodes about zero. The ray kernel reads radial lookup arrays built on nodes anchored to the fluid cell faces and centers (beyond the fluid region, on the radial nodes of the LaserMesh). In 2D RZ, the single LaserMesh is aligned with the fluid symmetry axis and its node geometry remains fixed after initialization. Rays move on the LaserMesh either by the default characteristic integration in 1D (next section) or by marching (terms); the processing steps for these methods are listed in the procedure section.

Characteristic integration (the 1D default)

By default in 1D (Laser.raytrace.integrator="auto"), rays are not marched in steps but integrated segment by segment along their characteristics (the marching of step 3 below is selected with "leapfrog", 1D spherical only). In a spherically symmetric field a ray conserves its angular momentum, the Bouguer invariant \(B\) defined above (the impact parameter of a ray entering from vacuum), so with \(\varepsilon=1-\hat n\) and \(Q(r)=r^2\varepsilon(r)-B^2\) (\(Q\ge0\) along the path)

$$ \frac{ds}{|dr|}=\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,r}{\sqrt{Q}},\qquad \frac{d\phi}{|dr|}=\frac{B}{r\sqrt{Q}},\qquad d\tau=\kappa_{\rm IB}\,ds $$

are integrated over the radius (\(\phi\) is the polar angle in the meridional plane). The path is cut into segments at the nodes of the radial reference array, the hydro cell faces, and a few other radii; the turning point (\(Q=0\)), the critical surface, and the center are found exactly as events inside a segment. Each segment's path length, angle, and optical depth come from adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature, with \(u=\sqrt{r-r_B}\) as the variable near the turning point at radius \(r_B\) to remove the \(1/\sqrt Q\) singularity. Every segment lies in one hydro cell, so its absorption goes directly into that cell. The method has neither the step-size error of marching nor its step refinement near turning points.

With this integrator a ray that reaches the critical radius (\(\hat n=1-\varepsilon_{\rm crit}\), with \(\varepsilon_{\rm crit}\) given by Laser.absorption.eps_crit) is reflected by default (Laser.absorption.critical_handling.terminate=False) and absorbs on the way in and out along the same segments; terminate=True closes the critical-layer tail analytically (the tail closure) and terminates the ray, as marching does. The 1D cylinder and slab (Mesh.geometry_1d) use the same form: the in-plane angular momentum and the axial velocity are conserved in the cylinder, the transverse velocity in the slab (a beam's power is per unit length in the cylinder and per unit area in the slab).

On the GPU one ray is traced by 32, 64, 128, or 256 lanes, each lane taking one segment along the path (Laser.raytrace.lanes_per_ray; the default 0 selects the largest lane count for which the number of rays times the lane count fits in the threads the GPU can run at once, else 32). The summation order depends on the lane count, so the rounding of the result does; for the same GPU and ray count, repeated runs are bitwise identical. Fix the lane count when comparing bitwise with a different GPU model.

A ray crosses the same segments on its way in and on its way out, and a segment's path length, angle, and optical depth depend only on its radius interval, so the outward segments take the values the inward leg computed (Laser.raytrace.reuse_inward_integrals, default True). For a ray reflected at the critical radius the results are bitwise those of integrating again. A ray that turns integrated a segment away from its turning point in \(r\) on the way in and in \(u=\sqrt{r-r_B}\) on the way out, so the reuse replaces one value of the same integral by another at the same tolerance (\(10^{-7}\) of the segment), and the absorption centroid at which the Langdon factor is evaluated moves at second order in the segment width. In 1D implosions of GXII (GEKKO XII) the results differ by as much as runs whose initial time step or laser energy differs in the 12th digit, and the runs take 7 to 12 % less time. The table takes 72 bytes of GPU memory per segment and ray (about 100 MiB for 300 cells and 1000 rays). With False the outward segments are integrated again.

One laser call, step by step

  1. Rebuild and map. Rebuild the LaserMesh (terms) for the current fluid state. Around the critical surface place a fine band whose spacing is mesh_factor (namelist Laser.lasermesh.mesh_factor, default 0.5) times the smallest nearby hydro width, then coarsen geometrically toward both the core and corona. Map \(\rho,\bar Z,T_e\), form \(n_e\), and use the linear interpolant between nodes whose \(d\hat n/dr\) is its exact derivative.

  2. Build rays. Look up each beam's power from its frozen waveform table; in 1D this is the exact average \(\bar P_b\) of the piecewise-linear waveform over the time step \([t,t+\Delta t]\), so the step energies sum to the pulse integral. In 1D, launch from each ring (exact area and profile weight) a ray from its launch radius \(R_k\) along the line through the focus. When all beams have identical conditions, only one beam is traced and the others receive the same deposition (conditions: the chord section).

  3. Advance each ray. The 1D default characteristic integrator (section above) advances rays segment by segment by quadrature; the following describes marching (integrator="leapfrog") and 2D RZ. The 1D path uses variable-step velocity Verlet, re-centering the previous half-kick and current half-kick at the current position with no seed kick. The ray equation is Hamiltonian, so a symplectic integrator keeps the conserved quantity — the Bouguer invariant, in spherical symmetry — free of systematic drift. The earlier staggered leapfrog, which stores velocity a half-step apart, loses that property once the adaptive controller changes \(ds\): the phase centering breaks and each substep dissipates the Hamiltonian by \(O(|\nabla\hat n|\,|\Delta(\Delta s)|)\) (proportional to the product of the density-gradient magnitude and the change in step size) — measured as a turning point too shallow by 0.027 in \(\hat n\) and a 22% loss of round-trip optical depth. Re-taking both half-kicks at the current position removes the mismatch; the check against the analytic solution measures a turning-point error of \(1.1\times10^{-5}\) in \(\hat n\) and a 0.008% round-trip optical-depth error (the 2D RZ raytrace_3d also launches at speed \(\sqrt{1-\hat n}\) and advances by the same variable-step velocity Verlet). Gradient and optical-depth metrics can grow \(ds\); only the turning-angle target can shrink it. Vacuum legs end at exact chord–sphere intersections, and a vacuum-to-material hand-off pins the next lookup interval to the material side.

  4. Deposit each segment. In marching, form the trapezoidal optical depth \(S\), evaluate \(\Delta P=-P\operatorname{expm1}(-S)\), and deposit that loss at the segment midpoint. Characteristic integration evaluates the same expression with each segment's quadrature optical depth and deposits the loss in the segment's cell.

  5. Terminate. At \(\hat n\ge1-\varepsilon_{\rm crit}\) (Laser.absorption.eps_crit, default \(10^{-4}\)), apply the tail closure (the analytic closure of the near-critical remainder), then assign residual power according to the critical escape/deposit mode. The 1D default characteristic integrator instead reflects the ray at the critical radius and keeps tracing it. Mesh-exit and intensity-cutoff residuals go to unabsorbed.

  6. Reduce and check the balance. Reduce per-ray deposit rows in fixed order per beam, then heat radial hydro cells in 1D or the four bilinear neighbors in 2D RZ. Assert incident = absorbed + unabsorbed.

  7. Use or invalidate the reuse cache. When the reuse cache (terms) is enabled (Laser.raytrace_skip.enabled=True; off by default) and none of the conditions below holds, the rays are not traced: the deposition is formed from the stored deposition profile and the current power. If any of the conditions holds, the rays are traced again and the cache is taken anew.

    • It is the first time step (step number 0), or no cache has been taken yet.
    • The plasma change \(\delta\) since the cache was taken reaches the threshold (threshold, default 0.01).
    • The cache has been reused for max_consecutive consecutive steps (default 10).
    • The total power, or in 1D the power of any single beam, changes between zero and nonzero.
    • The total power has changed by more than 1% since the cache was taken.
    • Any beam direction (the length of the unit-vector difference) changes by more than \(10^{-10}\), the focus position by more than \(10^{-10}\) cm, or the defocus (defocus_DR) by more than \(10^{-12}\).
    • An ALE (arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian) rezone or remap has just been performed (2D RZ only).
    • The test \(\hat n>\hat n_{\rm margin}-\varepsilon_{\rm crit\_guard}\) gives a different result in any cell than at the cache time, i.e. that cell's \(\hat n\) crossed the band edge in either direction (\(\hat n_{\rm margin}\) is the critical clip value of the LaserMesh, Laser.lasermesh.critical_margin; the guard, Laser.raytrace_skip.crit_guard, defaults to 0.01).
    • CBET is on (1D only; 2D RZ runs with CBET do not use the cache) and the power of any beam has changed by more than 1% since the cache was taken (the exchange between beams makes the absorption nonlinear).

    radial_absorption_1d, runs with hot electrons on, and 2D RZ runs with CBET on never use this cache.

IB deposition, the critical surface, and termination

The IB coefficient \(\kappa_{\rm IB}=\hat n\nu_{ei}/[c\sqrt{1-\hat n}]\) includes the slowing group velocity; \(\nu_{ei}\) uses \(T_e\), \(\bar Z\), and the Coulomb logarithm in cgs + eV. For marching, \(S=(\kappa_n+\kappa_{n+1})\Delta s/2\) (characteristic integration uses the quadrature value of each segment),

$$ \Delta P=-P_n\operatorname{expm1}(-S),\qquad P_{n+1}=P_n-\Delta P, $$

avoids thin-segment cancellation; \(P_n\) is the ray's carried power at segment \(n\) (distinct from the beam-profile intensity \(I\) above). \(\Delta P\) is deposited at the segment midpoint in marching and in the segment's cell in characteristic integration. Because the power updates by difference, summing the losses along one ray telescopes — adjacent terms cancel — so \(\sum\Delta P=P_0-P_{\rm final}\) holds exactly up to rounding.

In marching, and in characteristic integration with critical_handling.terminate=True, a ray terminates at \(\hat n\ge1-\varepsilon_{\rm crit}\). The 1D tail closure closes the remaining optical depth analytically. Default terminate_mode="escape" records residual power as unabsorbed reflection/scatter; "deposit" dumps it into an allowed critical-adjacent receiver. This dump is not path-integrated IB and should be intentional. Mesh-exit and intensity-cutoff remainders are unabsorbed, closing incident = absorbed + unabsorbed. With the 1D default characteristic integrator, terminate defaults to False (it stays True when terminate_mode="deposit" is chosen): rays reflect at the critical radius, and the quadrature counts the absorption up to it. terminate=False cannot be combined with "deposit".

Practical requirement: a low-density corona layer

Do not model a cold solid surface as one density jump. Without an underdense corona layer, 351 nm rays reach critical with almost no absorbing path and reflect. With the corona layer removed, the GXII regression deck absorbed only about \(4\times10^{-4}\) of the pulse over 1 ns (measured in 2026-07), too little to bootstrap a corona. There are two ways to supply the layer.

  • The corona ramp is a layer of exponentially falling density placed in the initial fluid mesh; it enters the fluid as cells with mass. The GXII regression deck places a 10 µm layer that seeds a preplasma. The deck's cells are 2.5 µm wide, so the layer is represented by 4 cells, and the critical density lies inside the first cell that touches the outer surface. Studio's Geometry section can generate the same layer.
  • The ghost corona (Laser.lasermesh.ghost_corona) is a virtual corona placed only on the LaserMesh, outside the outermost material cell; it does not enter the fluid density, temperature, or mass (1D only). It is off by default in the solver and in Studio, and every laser-drive preset of Studio turns it on.

The two are compared on the 1D mesh page.

2D RZ and the simple radial mode

In 2D RZ, raytrace_3d follows \((x,y,z)\), reading axisymmetric \(\hat n(R,Z)\) at \(R=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) and transforming its gradient by the chain rule. A circular 2D ray array represents each beam. Deposition maps through \((R,Z)=(\sqrt{x^2+y^2},z)\) to four bilinear neighbors. Beams of the same beam group (terms) share a trace.

radial_absorption_1d instead attenuates \(P_{\rm total}(t)=\sum_bP_b(t)\) as one inward radial flux. Direction, F-number, focus, profile, and ray count do not affect deposition; there are no trajectories. In 1D cylindrical and planar geometries (Mesh.geometry_1d), raytrace_2d runs with the characteristic integrator (marching is spherical-only).

Verification

  • Linear-profile end-to-end checks compare with two closed forms for a density profile rising linearly to critical over thickness \(\ell\) [11]: the radial-pencil “Beer 16/15” law — the one-way IB optical depth is \(\tau=C\ell\int_0^1\hat n^2/\sqrt{1-\hat n}\,d\hat n=(16/15)C\ell\), with \(C\) the profile's IB coefficient scale and the check subtracting the \(\sqrt{\varepsilon_n}\) floor cut — and the beam-integrated oblique “\(\cos^5\)” law, round-trip absorption \(A=1-\exp[-(32/15)C\ell\cos^5\theta]\) at incidence angle \(\theta\).
  • Several impact parameters check the analytic Bouguer radii (the radii of the turning points) and bound the drift of the invariant.
  • test_ray_trace_characteristic compares characteristic integration with closed forms for chords in a uniform medium and for radial rays through piecewise-linear profiles, and with an independent reference (a host Dormand–Prince integration of the ray equations) for the optical depth, exit point, and per-cell deposition of turning rays. It also checks agreement with a converged fine marching run, the 16/15 law, the round-trip absorption of reflection at the critical radius, closed forms in the cylinder and the slab, agreement across 32 to 256 lanes, and bitwise-identical repeated runs.
  • Interior \(\hat n\) and \(d\hat n/dr\) checks independently verify the fidelity of the field and the force.
  • verify_gxii_1d_fld_regression checks the golden absorption (about 96% under the regression deck's broad-spot twelve-beam irradiation — the golden absorbed energy, \(9.53\times10^{10}\) erg, divided by the deck's incident energy, \(9.9\times10^{10}\) erg (the integral of a trapezoidal pulse of total power \(10^{13}\) W); not comparable with the other absorption figures quoted in this documentation; the Examples page lists all three with their conditions) and implosion observables of an integrated deck that seeds a 10 µm corona layer (4 cells of 2.5 µm).
  • Of the L1 tests (single-physics laser tests for refraction, absorption, the energy ledger, and convergence in the ray count; see test tags and terms), test_l1_oblique_incidence_refraction.cu and test_l1_beer_lambert_inverse_bremsstrahlung_slab.cu only record that the deck features they need (setting the beam angle, setting the absorption coefficient in a uniform slab) are not implemented, and perform no check. test_l1_laser_energy_balance_in_absorbed_escaped_reflected.cu requires a relative energy-ledger error \(\le10^{-6}\), and test_l1_ray_count_noise_convergence.cu requires the relative difference (in the 2-norm) of the deposition map from the 257-rays-per-axis reference map to fall by a factor \(\ge1.2\) when the rays per axis increase from 65 to 129.
  • For L2 (laser–hydro coupling), test_l2_planar_ablation_front_benchmark.cu records the intended pressure comparison within 50% plus monotone front recession, and test_l2_laser_to_hydro_energy_partition.cu records the intended energy-partition criterion within 5% with \(f_{\rm thermal}>0.3\) (the fraction of absorbed laser energy found in matter internal energy at measurement time); both remain explicit diagnostic feature gaps. test_l2_oblique_beam_target_offset_symmetry.cu likewise records the current offset-control feature gap.
