Examples & presets reference

TENRYU Studio's one-dimensional presets provide three entry points: a radiation heat wave, a direct-drive laser implosion, and an indirect drive set by radiation temperature. Each configuration was run to completion on an RTX 4090 on 2026-07-31 and checked against representative physics metrics. A separate deck library under examples/verification/ reproduces the verification gates.

At a glance

  • The three presets were checked beyond schema validation: each completed on an RTX 4090 and produced an interpretable result.
  • The slab demonstrates heat-front propagation, the laser capsule a direct-drive implosion, and the indirect case stagnation and rebound under Tr(t).
  • Use per-step history metrics for sharp implosion peaks that sparse snapshots can miss.
  • The canonical regression reference is examples/verification/gxii_1d_fld_regression.py — the GXII benchmark deck (GXII: the GEKKO XII laser at ILE, The University of Osaka).

Run-validated presets

Execution procedure: apply the preset in TENRYU Studio, save the deck (namelist), and pass it to tenryu run following the steps on Running.

The values below are observation targets for deciding whether a preset forms its intended problem. They are not universal goldens for a deck changed to use another GPU, binary, resolution, stopping condition, or material model. Run the distributed setup unchanged first and inspect both its history and snapshots before beginning a parameter study.

Reading the results

For slab_radiation, examine the full temperature gradient rather than only the wall cell. The front turns near 35 µm and leaves cold material farther into the slab, distinguishing a propagating diffusive heat wave from uniform heating. Opacity, density, heat capacity, and thickness jointly set the optical depth and thermal capacity; changing any one of them need not preserve either 104 eV or 35 µm.

For laser_capsule, obtain absorption from the ratio of deposited to incident laser energy, then evaluate compression and bang from the maximum of implosion/rho_peak and its time. Removing the corona ramp leaves no useful inverse-bremsstrahlung path outside the cold solid surface: rays reflect near critical density and the intended drive does not form. The ramp is a physical part of this initial condition, not cosmetic mesh padding.

For indirect_tr, the foot organizes the initial shock and the rise to 200 eV drives the main compression. Confirming that the shell radius grows again after its minimum distinguishes a captured rebound from a run that stopped just before stagnation. Because peak density persists only briefly, use the per-step history rather than the largest value among cadence-limited snapshots.

Three absorption figures, three different conditions

This documentation quotes three laser-absorption numbers that are not comparable with one another, because they belong to different irradiation geometries and opacity models:

  • ≈83–84% — the GXII golden-regression deck: twelve f/3 beams with broad 200 µm super-Gaussian spots overfilling the 250 µm-radius target, constant-opacity ideal-gas benchmark physics.
  • ≈86% — the Studio laser_capsule preset: the same GXII-class 10 TW × 1 ns drive on a preset target whose layer thicknesses, corona ramp, and mesh differ from the regression deck.
  • ≈45% — the HELIOS matched-irradiation LTE campaign: a deliberately narrower ≈113 µm Gaussian spot from a nearly collimated f/191 beam, with LTE table opacities (see Verification & benchmarks).

Absorbed fraction depends strongly on spot size, beam aperture, and the opacity model, so agreement between these numbers is neither expected nor meaningful.

Verification deck library

examples/verification/ is the deck library for reproducing analytic, conservation, single-operator, and integrated regression gates; this page does not enumerate every case. The canonical practical 1D reference spanning direct laser drive, two-temperature matter, hydrodynamics, conduction, and multigroup FLD is gxii_1d_fld_regression.py. In addition to defining the golden-regression input, its comments preserve the corona-ramp lesson. When developing a laser deck, consult those comments together with the pulse, material interfaces, and output history rather than copying key names alone.

Presets primarily teach and seed new configurations; verification decks reproduce contracts and detect changes. A plausible preset metric and a passing gate are complementary evidence, and neither substitutes for the other.
