Examples & presets reference

TENRYU Studio's one-dimensional presets cover basic configurations (a pressure-driven shock, a radiation heat wave, a laser-driven planar foil, a direct-drive shell implosion and an indirect drive set by a radiation temperature) and configurations that use optional kernels (S_N transport, nonlocal electron transport (SNB), CBET with hot electrons, fusion burn, material tables with multigroup radiation, and the cold-equilibrium constitutive branch). Every preset but the CBET one was run to its end time on an RTX 4090 on 2026-10-01 and checked against a run on a twice finer mesh. Commissioned decks for typical experiments are in examples/laser_plasma_1d/; a separate deck library that reproduces verification items is in examples/verification/.

At a glance

  • Every preset but the CBET one was run to its end time and compared with a run on a twice finer mesh; Studio shows the result on each card, under "Verification run".
  • The meshes come from the mesh skill (tenryu-mesh-1d): a recommended mesh for the laser-driven presets, a per-layer table for those driven by a radiation temperature or a pressure.
  • Known problems are stated with the results: the outermost boundary cell that keeps accelerating on finer meshes under a spherical Marshak drive, the mesh dependence of the absorbed energy in the SNB foil, the clamped values in the table foil, and the 0.6 % density difference behind the pressure-driven shock.
  • The canonical regression reference is examples/verification/gxii_1d_fld_regression.py — the GXII benchmark deck (GXII: the twelve-beam GEKKO XII laser at the Institute of Laser Engineering, The University of Osaka).

1D laser-plasma example suite (examples/laser_plasma_1d/)

This suite collects commissioned one-dimensional decks modeling typical laser-plasma experiments.

  • Its ten decks cover foil shock breakout, two-pulse shock timing, impedance matching, an exploding foil, a radiative shock in Kr, a cylindrical liner, solid-sphere convergence (with a 600/900/1350-cell study), a D2-filled shell implosion, a CBET + hot-electron corona setup, and Marshak-driven DT burn.
  • The suite README records each deck's status, measured commissioning metrics, and caveats.
  • The solid materials use tmat EOS tables that are not distributed because they derive from licensed PrOpacEOS data. Users must generate them with tools/tmat/propaceos_to_tmat.py from their own PrOpacEOS/SESAME workflow; one deck additionally requires a SESAME ASCII library.

Execution procedure: apply the preset in TENRYU Studio, save the deck (namelist), and pass it to tenryu run following the steps on Running.

The values below are observation targets for deciding whether a preset forms its intended problem. They are not universal goldens for a deck changed to use another GPU, binary, resolution, stopping condition, or material model. "Mesh difference" is the difference from the same setup run on a twice finer mesh. In the planar presets the rear face (r = 0) is a rigid wall: the mid-plane of a foil driven from both sides.

Reading the results

The pressure-driven shock is the model of a basic check: measure the shock speed from the shock position over time and compare it, with the density and the flow velocity behind the shock, against the exact Rankine-Hugoniot values. In the radiation heat wave, look at the whole temperature profile, not only at the wall; the front position does not survive a change of the density, the opacity or the heat capacity.

In the laser-driven presets, read the absorbed fraction from the ratio of the deposited to the incident laser energy, and the ablation pressure as its mean until the shock reaches the rear wall. The rear face of a planar foil is a rigid wall, so the compression after the shock reaches it comes from the reflected shock; it is not an implosion's stagnation. In the spherical implosions, read the peak density and the stagnation time from per-step history metrics such as implosion/rho_peak: the peak lasts briefly, and the maximum over sparse snapshots misses it. With a cold solid surface alone — no ghost corona and no corona ramp — the rays have no path along which they are absorbed and reflect near the critical surface, so the drive does not form.

Whether a result depends on the mesh is checked by running the same setup on a twice finer mesh (the convergence pair of Studio's mesh section); the mesh differences above are the result of that comparison. The cases whose difference exceeds the target, and the defects that appear only on the finer mesh, are stated in the table and on the Studio cards.

Absorption figures and their different conditions

This documentation quotes several laser-absorption numbers that are not comparable with one another, because they belong to different irradiation geometries and opacity models:

  • ≈93% — the GXII golden-regression deck (golden of 2026-09-25): twelve f/3 beams with broad 200 µm super-Gaussian spots overfilling the target of radius 250 µm, with constant-opacity ideal-gas benchmark physics.
  • 89% and 67% — the Studio planar-foil preset (planar irradiation at 351 nm, 1e14 W/cm²) and the shell-implosion preset (a 527 nm Gaussian pulse on a CD shell of 250 µm outer radius), both from the runs of 2026-10-01, with wavelengths, intensities and targets that differ from the regression deck's.
  • ≈45% — the cross-code benchmark against HELIOS (LTE, matched irradiation; measured in July 2026): a deliberately narrow ≈113 µm Gaussian spot from a nearly collimated f/191 beam, with LTE table opacities (see Verification & benchmarks).

Absorbed fraction depends strongly on the wavelength, the intensity, the spot size, the beam aperture and the opacity model, so these numbers are not expected to agree, and their disagreement carries no significance.

Verification deck library

examples/verification/ is the deck library for reproducing analytic, conservation, single-operator, and integrated regression gates; this page does not enumerate every case. The canonical practical 1D reference spanning direct laser drive, two-temperature matter, hydrodynamics, conduction, and multigroup FLD is gxii_1d_fld_regression.py. The deck defines the golden-regression input, and its comments record why the initial corona ramp is needed. When developing a laser deck, consult those comments together with the pulse, material interfaces, and output history rather than copying key names alone.

Presets primarily teach and seed new configurations; verification decks reproduce contracts and detect changes. A plausible preset metric and a passing gate are complementary evidence, and neither substitutes for the other.
